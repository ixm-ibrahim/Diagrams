## What an entry looks like

Every entry is one note file holding eight parts, in this order, exactly as he settles them: ID (the section's initials plus a number — PP1, PP2, and so on), Title, Motivation, Definition, Inherits From, Terms Introduced, Notes, and Examples. Two bookkeeping lines sit at the top of the file (a status and a date); everything else stays out — no commentary and no mentions of where the words came from, because these notes are the source of truth for everything built later, so they explain themselves and cite nothing.

One part needs special explanation. Terms Introduced is the list of ordinary words that, from this entry onward, point back to this entry's concept whenever a later definition uses them. For example, the first entry introduces the word "what": in the question "what is that?", the "what" is asking about a particular phenomenon that occurred, so the word refers back to the entry about phenomena. A word on such a list can still have other meanings in everyday life — "feeling" can also mean an emotion — but its place on the list covers only the use that points back to its entry. The practical consequence for your checking: whenever a later definition uses one of these words, make sure it is being used in the sense that points back to its entry, and not in one of its other everyday senses.
