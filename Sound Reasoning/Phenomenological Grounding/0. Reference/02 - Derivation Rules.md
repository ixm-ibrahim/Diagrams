# 02 Derivation Rules

How new nodes get derived. Everything here is the author's ruling unless marked as a thread's working reading (those are open to his veto). New rules get added as they are discovered.

## Deriving nodes

- The core inferential principle governing everything (his words): a method of reasoning that justifies falsehood can't distinguish what is true from what is not.
- Sound, clear logic is the highest priority of the whole effort (his ruling). The concept layer — each argument actually establishing what it claims — comes before the language layer; see the two-layer rule in [[03 - Demystification]] and the logic helper in [[01 - Processing Queries]]. This applies to all pages and all parts of a page.
- Children nodes are a specific manifestation of a parent node.
- Children nodes can only introduce one new term.
- Children nodes must use terms from ancestral nodes.
- A node's observations should diversely encompass its children nodes.
- Inheritance lists only the nearest connections: if A -> B -> C, then C inherits from B only, because B already includes A in its inheritance.
- The Definition property and the Conclusion's concluding statement must match word for word.
- Nodes grow over time — going back to add links or material is proofreading, not new derivation; the work itself stays one node at a time.
- Two structures, two pairs of properties (his design). Inherits From / Leads To carry derivation — what builds on what, the chains. Nested In / Encompasses carry zoom — which node a node sits inside, and which nodes unpack it — so a reader can walk the record at any depth (his naming). A section's higher-level node is an ordinary node, general enough to encompass its nested nodes; any node can gain nested nodes later, at any depth, without anything renumbering. Once nesting exists, an ID's number no longer promises reading order — reading order lives in the chains, and reader-facing decimal numbering (like the old website's 1.1.2) is computed for display, never stored. Both directions of each pair are kept consistent, like pending links.
- The author is the initial author of everything in the record: he writes and accepts improvements, or explicitly accepts a suggestion. Suggestions are always welcome — from the master thread and every section thread, about the current piece or anything else noticed.
- Terms Introduced are listed alphabetically.
- An introduced term covers its grammatical forms — "appear" brings "appearance" and "appearing" along with it.
- Node text is written in standard markdown, so words can be emphasized and the files can later be consumed by the website project.

## The relation between nodes (thread's working reading, revised with his corrections and checker repairs — his to ratify, reword, or veto)

The whole idea, plainly (his summary): every concept is built out of other concepts — "distinct" is said of things that occur, so it needs "occur" first. Follow any concept's parts down far enough and you hit blocks that are not built out of anything: concepts you get just by looking. Something occurs; things differ. This record exposes those blocks, one per node, in the order the concepts need each other — the block every other block needs comes first — and proves each one by looking, not by argument from another block. The links between nodes say one thing only: this block needs that block first. Truth comes from looking, at every node separately. Walk the links backward from any claim and you see every block it is made of, each proven by a look, nothing assumed. In his words: the effort is conceptual grounding — exposing the primitive building blocks of all concepts, in the order of their coverage, starting with the concepts that just appear; the section name "Phenomenological Primitives" already says it. The bullets below are the fine print of this paragraph, kept for the checkers.

- All nodes describe one thing: what occurs. The observations are not split per node — PP2 (distinction)'s "red is not hot" could have sat in PP1 (phenomena)'s Observations list, and PP1's list already shows the differences PP2 claims. Every look already shows everything; what changes from node to node is only how much of it gets claimed.
- No justification travels between nodes. PP2 is not deduced from PP1, and PP1's claim is not a premise for PP2's: each claim's support is what occurs, looked at again — which is exactly the fact PP1 states. So every justification in the record rests on the fact PP1 names — occurrences — and none on PP1's say-so. ("Justification" here means premise-support: warrant drawn from another claim's having been established. There is a bookkeeping sense in which any general claim raises the odds of the specific claim that contains it — that is just the containment named below, and it carries nothing once PP1's denial test has run; adversarial check.)
- What a finished node passes to the next is the subject, the words to state the next claim with, and the question — its Unknowns — that the next node answers. The parent is needed as the lens, not as a premise: without its concept, the child's feature cannot be claimed or tested. (The raw noticing needs no words — an infant tells "strawberry" from "ouch" long before it has words for them; PP2's reply to its criterion objection gives the example. What needs the parent is the claiming.) The lens-not-premise line is re-earned at every node, not assumed: the checkers ask whether the child's new feature is displayed in what occurs independently of the concept — PP2 has the infant case; a future node whose subject is itself conceptual (claims, definitions, awareness) will need the line argued fresh there (adversarial check).
- The grounding is double, and naming the two halves dissolves the "grounded in PP1" puzzle (resolved with him). Every node is grounded twice over: its truth in what occurs, its meaning in the nodes before it. Reality grounds truth — distinctions occur whether or not any node was ever written, and PP2's truth could be sourced from what occurs with no PP1 anywhere. The ancestors ground meaning — the claim "Distinct phenomena occur" is made of terms, and in this record a term means something only where a node has granted it; without PP1 the same words would still be true, but they would not yet be a claim of the record, because "distinct" would be said of occurrences no node has exposed. That is the whole sense in which PP1 "allows" PP2: it grounds the saying, not the fact. Even PP2's own machinery runs on it — its Proof by Contradiction states PP2's opposite with PP1's concept ("all occurrences would appear identical"), so the denial test itself speaks in the parent's meaning. The record's name states both halves: phenomenological (truth grounded in what appears) grounding (every meaning traced to its node).
- What "PP2 is grounded in PP1" means, exactly (resolved with him): two different things get grounded, in two different places. Truth is grounded in reality at every node, directly — PP1 holds none of PP2's truth. Meaning is grounded in two steps: a node's one new concept takes its meaning from what occurs — the new label is attached to something there, shown rather than defined in other words (a philosopher's name: ostensive definition; "distinct" labels the difference between red and hot itself) — while the node's remaining words take their meaning from the nodes that grounded them earlier, and their whole job is to say what the new label attaches to ("one phenomenon not like another" — without PP1's words, the attachment cannot be said). So inside "Distinct phenomena occur": "phenomena occur" has its meaning from PP1 and its truth from reality; "distinct" has its meaning from reality directly. This is the record's name made literal: phenomenological grounding — every concept's meaning grounded in what occurs rather than in other words alone, so the record's dictionary ends at reality instead of running in a circle.
- Each node makes the claim about what occurs one concept more specific: "something occurs" → "what occurs is not all alike" → "each is something specific". The claims run from the least said toward ever more said, each read directly off what occurs, each checked by the denial test — the If Rejected section's Proof by Contradiction: assume the opposite, and see what breaks. That is the only place deduction appears: inside a node, as the check's tool — not between nodes.
- Two containments, running in opposite directions — both real; which direction depends on what is being contained (resolved with him). Take "something is in the box" and "a red ball is in the box". In what is said, the second contains the first: it says everything the first says and more, so the specific claim entails the general one — content grows downward, and denying a parent takes all its descendants down. In what is allowed, the first contains the second: the first allows every filled box, the second only the red-ball ones — territory shrinks downward, so the parent's set contains the child's, exactly the subset picture (PP1 allows every way things could be except nothing occurring; PP2 allows all of those except the perfectly uniform one; each child cuts one more position away, and the Eliminates section records exactly this). The two directions are forced to be opposite: each thing more said is one more way things could have been ruled out (for a philosopher: extension and intension vary inversely). Nothing new is ever added to what occurs — it was all already there; what grows is only what has been said about it, and what shrinks is what the claims leave open.
- Entailment and justification must still not be confused: the child's entailing the parent is containment of content, not a source of support — the child's support is its own look and its own denial test; no node stands on an inference from its parent. The reverse entailment is not declared impossible, only unused: contents can overlap enough that a parent entails some thin reading of a child (the adversarial check flagged the planned "each is something specific" as a possible case). The redundancy objection polices the rest: a child that claims no more than its parent entails adds no claim of its own — PP2's objection 6 shows how that is tested.
- Why one concept per node, and why the record does not simply open at "distinctions occur" or any later claim (his question): truth alone would allow it — reality gives everything at once (the red ball was in the box before anyone walked closer), and a later claim is read off what occurs as directly as the first. What forces the order is that the earlier concept lives inside the later one: "distinct" is said of occurrences, so a record that opened with "distinctions occur" would not have skipped the phenomena node — it would carry that node inside its first claim, unstated, untested, its words ungranted. The one-concept rule then gives every concept one address: its own denial test (deny a bundled claim and the proof must split into exactly the smaller nodes' proofs), its own objections (each lands at the level it actually attacks — the objection index runs on this), and its own dictionary entry (every word traced to the node that grants it). The order is discovered, not chosen: it follows which concept contains which — forced along a containment line, free to branch where none runs. PP1 opens the record because its claim is the one every observation whatsoever satisfies — total coverage, minimal content — and every step after it trades coverage for content, one concept at a time.
- The form of reasoning, named (his word — working reading): grounding. A claim enters the record only when everything it is built from is already on the record: each borrowed concept traced to the node that granted it, and the claim's own warrant on the page — at this stage, the look (the Observations) plus the denial test. "Grounded in reality" and "grounded in PP1" are two different relations wearing one word: reality is where a claim's truth comes from; a parent node is where its borrowed pieces were granted. PP1 does not make PP2 true — reality does; PP1 makes PP2 fully traceable. And "distinct" is granted by PP2 itself, from reality: a parent gives what the new concept is said of, not the concept.
- Why the form survives the record's whole future (his projection — working reading): when enough is exposed to state rules of reasoning — induction (using the seen to say the unseen), later deduction — each such rule arrives as a grounded node before any node uses it. A later inductive or deductive node is then the same act PP2 was: one new thing, every borrowed piece traced, its warrant on the page — only the warrant has changed, from a direct look to a grounded rule applied. Looking comes first because it is the only warrant that needs nothing prior. So the transition he foresees is seamless by construction: inference does not replace grounding — it enlists in it, once grounded itself.
- The two names within it (pending his ratification) — one per arrow, so neither swallows the other (philosopher check). Specification names the relation between the claims: each node is a specification of its parent's claim, the way a species specifies its genus — one differentia added ("distinct"), with the specific entailing the general and not the reverse; the existing rule already half-says it ("children nodes are a specific manifestation of a parent node"), and the one-new-term rule is a differentia rule. Two guards stand: the specification is discovered, not chosen — read off what occurs, not written the way an engineer writes a spec; and this is not the logician's "specification" (deriving an instance from a general truth), where support flows the reversed way. Expose names the act, and its object is fixed: a node exposes, in what occurs, one more concept — it does not expose its parent. For a philosopher, the cited relatives: the determinable–determinate relation ("red" to "colored"), holding here in exactly two respects — the specific entails the general, and both are seen in one look with no inference between them — and Husserl's explicative contemplation (one subject unfolded, determination by determination), kept clear of the later sense of "explication" as a chosen replacement of a concept. And the umbrella word is his own title's (his catch): grounding — exposure is how a claim gets grounded, specification is the shape the grounded claims form, and the grounding itself is double, per the bullet above. (One caution for philosopher readers: metaphysicians today use "grounding" for a relation between facts — what makes what obtain; the record means the two epistemic halves named here, so the double formulation should travel with the word.)
- The grounding relations, disentangled (with him — what "grounded in" means each time it is said). (1) Truth: every node's claim is grounded in reality directly — PP2 needs nothing from PP1 to be true. (2) The feature, in reality: what PP2 exposes is a feature of what PP1 exposes — a distinction is between occurrences, so without phenomena there is nothing for a difference to be between. This dependence is in what occurs, not only in our concepts, and it is the reason the order is discovered rather than chosen. (3) The terms, in the record: each node grounds exactly one new concept directly in reality (PP2 grounds "distinct" in "red is not hot") and borrows every other concept from ancestors that already grounded them — PP2's own Conclusion defines its new term with PP1's terms ("Phenomena are labelled "distinct" when one is not like another"). "PP2 is grounded in PP1" means the second and third relations, and only those; the first belongs to reality alone. The whole method — taking claims from reality one concept at a time, with every reused term already grounded — is what the project's own name says: phenomenological grounding (his naming of the form, pending his ratification alongside "exposure" for the act and "specification" for the shape).
- Why this order comes before axioms and theorems: the first claims have no earlier claims to lean on, so their support can only be what occurs, directly — and what occurs (phenomena, distinctions) must already be there before any axiom can be written down at all. Not because deductions escape observation — a deduction is observed like anything else — but because deduction's support runs from claim to claim, and here no support runs between claims at all.
- What the record's own support-words mean, given all this (the gloss that keeps the older passages consistent — adversarial check): "derivation" and "what builds on what" in the rules above name the order of specification — what hands subject, words, and question to what — not a transmission of support; and "the bottom of the structure" in PP1's objection 2 names the claim whose fact every other claim presupposes and whose denial would take everything down — not a premise the structure draws warrant from. PP1's "on which all other ideas are built" reads the same way: built out of what it names, not resting on its say-so.
- The word "grounded" carries two jobs here, and splitting them dissolves most of the knots (resolved with him). Every claim is truth-grounded in reality: what occurs is what makes it true — PP2's claim as directly as PP1's. And every claim is sense-grounded in the record: every concept in it except its one new one traces to an earlier node. PP1 contributes no truth to PP2; it contributes the concept the new one is a feature of. "Distinct" itself comes from reality, noticed there exactly as "occur" was — what PP2's sentence takes from PP1 is the material the new concept is said of (his lego picture: the parent supplies pieces for saying, not for being true). So the form of reasoning, named with the project's own title (pending his ratification): phenomenological grounding — truth anchored in what occurs at every node, sense anchored in what is already exposed; exposure is its act, specification is its trail. Guard for philosophers: this is not "metaphysical grounding" (a theory of what makes reality itself be as it is) — it is a record's discipline about where truth comes from and where each concept comes from. His framing of the act: "just as we roam the earth and discover new science, we're roaming our own minds and discovering its features" — the terrain is what occurs, the mind's features included; the record is the map, and mapping adds no land.
- Whether the transition to induction and deduction, projected for far-future nodes, is seamless (his question): structurally yes — the rules of induction (using the seen to uncover the unseen) and deduction arrive as ordinary nodes, one concept at a time, noticed in how reasoning itself behaves and checked by the same denial test (the core inferential principle at the top of this file is a claim of exactly that kind). What changes is only a claim's support: once a rule-node is ratified, later claims may cite it — "derived by that rule from these claims" — and every such citation traces back through the rule-node to a direct look. One bedrock form, then: exposure — which at some node exposes inference itself, after which inference runs as a grounded tool, not as a second bedrock. The standing rule this implies, logged now for that day: a rule of reasoning may be used only downstream of the node that ratifies it.

## What "Terms Introduced" means

A node's introduced terms are the words that, used in later sentences, refer back to this node's concept. The author's example: in "what is that?", the "what" asks to describe a particular phenomenon that occurred, so it refers back to the distinction node (the example's landing was updated when PP2 introduced "what" — his ruling). Homonyms keep their other meanings elsewhere ("feeling" can also mean an emotion); a term's listing covers only the usage that refers back to its node. The check this creates: when a later node uses an introduced word, verify it is used in the sense that points back to its node, not in a homonym sense. Thread's working reading: the text sections are everyday-language explanation, not term-granting — only Terms Introduced grants words, and only the Definition is bound to build from ancestral terms.

His grounding for that reading: the writing is "us looking back a posteriori — we already have all of these terms/concepts/definitions ingrained in us, and we're just exposing and discovering them here". So the Motivation, Conclusion, and every other text section speak with full ordinary language (PP1's Motivation says "or it does not" although "not" is introduced at PP2); what the nodes put in order is which concept exposes which, not which words the prose may use.

## Process

- The full working routine — what to read at thread start, how to handle each of the author's messages, when to consult which reference file, how to verify and when to loop — lives in [[01 - Processing Queries]]. Threads follow it, and re-read it whenever unsure.
- Section threads may update the rules files whenever the work needs it; anything added as a thread's own reading is marked as such.
- Threads do not record snapshots in the file history; the master thread reviews finished work and records the snapshot after the author's ok.
- Temporary working files — handoffs, changes lists, anything made for the AI process rather than for the record — live in their own folder, "Temporary Files" at the vault root, so nothing process-shaped is mistaken for part of the digital brain (his ruling). The PP1 changes list and the standing handoffs moved there.
- All writing — nodes, prompts, returns, chat — follows [[03 - Demystification]]: everyday words, full flowing sentences, examples where a point is invisible without one, bite-sized bullets, no ambiguity, no bare references. Writing and checking both consult [[04 - Common Biases]] — a shared bias does not look like a bias.
- [[04 - Common Biases]] grows with the work: any thread that meets a new bias, or a new example of a listed one, adds it to that page.
- When checking a finished node, spawn one fresh helper per kind of effort — one for format against these rules, one for the writing against [[03 - Demystification]] and [[04 - Common Biases]], one for the logic — the concept layer: each argument establishes what it claims, each objection's commitments genuinely support it, each reply answers the commitment it names, nothing assumes what it concludes (his instruction) — one hunting for points that need an example and lack one, one for consistency across the reference files — each helper seeing only what its check needs, and each disclosed in one line. A fourth helper runs earlier, at the end of the first complete draft: it hunts for common AI words and phrases not yet on the banned list and reports them to the author with examples, per [[01 - Processing Queries]] (his instruction).
- When finishing any node, add its objections to [[07 - Objection Index]]: under the right category, numbered, with the general objection (the form anyone might raise about any claim) as the title and the node's specific form beneath it, linked. Consult the index first when a new objection arrives — a recurring objection may already have its answer at another level. Objections are organized pedagogically (his ruling): a point fully explained once is not re-explained — later spots refer to it in the ruled shape; and the same objection reframed does not get several entries — one sub-bullet naming each reframing is enough, so long as the commitments exposed apply to each of them. This holds across pages too; the index is the cross-page tool for it.
- Every thread pastes the blank node template, unfilled, each time it opens a new entry.
- Any issue a check finds is stated to the author in chat, plainly, with the problem described. This binds all threads.
- When finishing any node: check [[05 - Pending Links]] (add the waiting links if this node is the one being waited for; log new entries when this node points at future nodes), and update [[06 - Phenomenological Dictionary]] (each introduced term, its one-line meaning, its node).
- The old project website is a source to mine when working any node — for objections especially: `Comparative Religion\Final Draft\Website\data.json` holds his old tree, with per-node observations, objections, eliminates, and unknowns. His instruction: "you know you can investigate the old project website for the objections". Material from it follows the same inspiration rules as everything else: quoted word for word, source named, checked against the file — inspiration stays in chat, and enters the record only through his acceptance.
- All his old projects are "a source of ideas", never "a source of truth" (his correction): nothing is settled by where a word or claim sat in an old file — an argument must stand on what occurs and on the current rules, and the old material only supplies candidates to consider. Arguing "the old scheme placed it there" is the mistake; see the matching entry in [[04 - Common Biases]].
- The snapshot is recorded at the moment the author gives his ok on a node.
- Rulings and additions are recorded in the reference files without dates (his instruction).

## The node format

Properties: ID (section initials plus a number — PP1, PP2,...), Title, Definition, Inherits From (nearest parents only, as links), Leads To (nearest children, as links, added as they get made), Nested In (the encompassing node this one sits inside, as a link), Encompasses (the nodes nested inside this one, as links, added as they get made — his naming), Terms Introduced (alphabetical), plus one bookkeeping field filled by the thread: metadata_date.

Text sections, in this order, any section absent when empty:

1. Motivation — the question or short statement that gives the node its point.
2. Observations — the set of things pointed at; not exhaustive unless the node's set is small and finite. Examples live here. A bracketed type tag, like "[taste] strawberry", is optional: "observations don't need the type" — PP1 carries them only in case readers got confused about what a bare observation meant, like whether "the earth is flat" is a thought that occurred or a claim being made (his ruling).
3. Conclusion — the definition again, word for word, in bold, with any relevant explanations under it.
4. If Rejected — what you have to accept if you reject the node, as one or more outcomes; an outcome may carry "Proof by Contradiction" and "Consequences" sub-parts. The Proof by Contradiction header carries the reminder "(assume the opposite, and show how it breaks itself)" — the "how" is his wording; the reminder belongs to the Obsidian page format only — the brainstorming format goes without it (his ruling). Consequences are ordered with the one most directly related to the Proof by Contradiction at the top — "it seems more intuitive like this" (his ruling).
5. Unlocks — two guaranteed subsections, Concepts first: Concepts (the doors this node opens), then Vocabulary (introduced terms in bold, each with a one-line meaning and italic synonyms — the word "Synonyms" sits inside the italics; a term may sit inside another entry's italic synonyms instead of carrying its own bold entry). Vocabulary entries are listed alphabetically, like Terms Introduced (his ruling). A grouped entry that is not a term by itself (like PP2's singling-out and comparing words) comes after all the vocabulary terms are done, in a separate alphabetically ordered section, as needed — not required in every node (his ruling).
6. Eliminates — what the node rules out.
7. Unknowns — what the node deliberately does not answer, each with a pointer to the answering node once it exists.
8. Objections — objections at this node's level, with their refutations; an objection belonging to a different level goes to [[08 - Unresolved Objections]] until its node exists. An objection may refer to the result of an objection on another page, but concisely — the shape is "see ___, which addresses ___" (his ruling). An objection may be referred to by its number, with a brief header as needed — like "see objection 2 ("made, not found")" (his ruling); the rule against pointing by number alone keeps applying to everything else, like commitments. Objections must be genuinely different objections: before adding one, name the commitment it stands on that no listed objection already carries — two objections sharing their core commitment get merged, keeping the strongest form. More objections is not more rigor; padding a node with restatements of one doubt is hyperskepticism wearing the process as a disguise (see [[04 - Common Biases#Hyperskepticism]]) — checkers verify this on every node (his instruction). Each objection's parts, in order: Objection Basis (the objection at full strength), Objection Commitments (what the objector must stand behind), Shared Ground (only what both sides already agree on — the reply starts from listening, and everything after works outward from that footing; the disagreement itself belongs to the later parts, never here), What's Missing, Correction.
9. Relevant Links — a basic list of relevant nodes the reader can navigate to: an id plus definition as a link, with the relevancy stated for each link, like "(parent)" (his ruling). Always present, even before its nodes exist — the header may sit empty until then: "no placeholder is necessary, as long as we know that it's there" (his ruling), with what it waits for tracked in [[05 - Pending Links]]. It fills in as those nodes are created.
10. Notes — as long as needed, or absent. Not a place for development thoughts or process material — open rulings, term questions, and working notes live in chat and the reference files, never in the node (his correction).

Formatting: a blank line follows each section header and separates a paragraph from a following list (optional after a short bold lead-in line, like the Conclusion's concluding statement). Bullet counts everywhere are dynamic: any section or list, in any part of a node, holds as many items as it needs — more or fewer; no count is a pattern to copy. Inside a list, a blank line separates labeled sub-blocks (like "Proof by Contradiction" from "Consequences", or "Concepts" from "Vocabulary"); otherwise no blank lines inside a list. The long dash () marks sentence breaks and asides; the short dash (-) appears only inside compound words. Double quotes for every quoted word, label, or claim; single quotes only inside double quotes. The same kind of thing gets the same mark every time. Where a sentence's point is invisible without an example, the example goes right there, in parentheses or a sub-bullet ("a three-sided shape has three sides"). No sentence should point at an earlier item by number alone — restate in a few words what the item said ("the second commitment — that X — is true, but..."). Exception (his ruling): objections may be referenced by their number, with a brief header as needed.

Naming: section folders are numbered, like "1. Phenomenological Primitives"; node files are named like "PP1 - Phenomena"; the numbered reference files live in the "0. Reference" folder and are named like "06 - Phenomenological Dictionary".
