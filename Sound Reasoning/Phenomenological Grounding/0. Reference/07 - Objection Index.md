# 07 Objection Index

Every objection the project has met, in one place — like the dictionary, but for objections. The same objection tends to recur at different levels, and its full answer lives at the level that properly handles it; this index says where. Objections are grouped by category and numbered. Each title states the general objection — the form anyone might raise about any claim — and beneath it sits the specific way it appeared at each node, with a link and the answer in one line. Each objection is a heading, so any note can link straight to it. New entries get added as nodes meet them; an objection still waiting for the node that answers it lives in [[08 - Unresolved Objections]] until that node exists, then moves here.

## The claim is empty

### 1. True-by-definition statements say nothing about the world

The general objection: a statement whose truth comes from word-choices was never checked against the world, so it carries no information and cannot support claims about the world.

- At [[PP1 - Phenomena]], objection 1, as: "'Phenomena occur' is trivially true — a tautology, like 'a three-sided shape has three sides'." Answered there: it is not guaranteed by any choice of words — what makes it true is that something does occur — and taking it away removes the possibility of any claim at all, including the objection.

### 2. A claim that rules nothing out says nothing

The general objection: a real claim must draw a line between how things are and how they are not; a claim that draws no line is empty.

- At [[PP1 - Phenomena]], objection 2, as: "describe what things would be like if 'Phenomena occur' were not so — there is nothing to describe." Answered there: the line is real but drawn among positions rather than situations — the opposite cannot even be held — and the claim is checked by denial, not exempted from checking.

## The claim assumes too much

### 3. Nothing can occur without someone it occurs to

The general objection: the idea of a happening drags along the idea of someone it happens to, so occurrence with nobody there is not a coherent idea.

- At [[PP1 - Phenomena]], objection 3. Answered there: the node speaks from before the words for an experiencer exist; whether one exists stays an open unknown, answered by later nodes.

## The claim is false

### 4. Consciousness is an illusion — nothing is actually happening

The general objection: experience is the brain misdescribing itself, and even genuine experience is the brain reconstructing what the senses receive — not reality as it truly is.

- At [[PP1 - Phenomena]], objection 4. Answered there: the illusion, the theorizing, the denying, and the reconstructing are themselves happenings; what they reveal about anything beyond them is a later question.
