## Motivation

How do phenomena occur?

## Observations

- Light is not dark.
- Red is not hot.
- Pressure is different than hunger.
- Ouch is not strawberry.
- 2 is not an arm.
- *"2 + 2 = 4"* is not a flying pig.
- *"The earth is flat"* is not *"The earth is round"*.

## Conclusion

**Distinct phenomena occur.**
- Phenomena are labelled "distinct" when one is not like another.

## If Rejected

- **Without distinctions, all things would appear identical in every way — even in the definitions between "distinction" and "phenomenon".**
    - **Proof by Contradiction** (assume the opposite, and show how it breaks itself)
        1. Distinctions do not occur.
        2. Then all occurrences would appear identical in every way.
        3. Red, orange, ouch, ah, 2, 3, happy, sad, loud, quiet, "earth is flat", "earth is round", up, down, "phenomenon", "distinction", etc. all occur.
        4. Red is not orange, ouch is not ah, 2 is not happy, etc. — each of these is a distinction.
        5. Contradiction: all occurrences would appear identical in every way — yet red is not orange, ouch is not ah.

    - **Consequences**
        1. Everything collapses into one featureless happening:
            - Red would be no different from orange, hot no different from ouch, ouch no different from a flying pig — nothing to single out, nothing to point at, nothing to compare.
            - Even PP1's observations could not be listed relative to PP2's, because listing things means telling them apart in at least one way.
        2. The rejection defeats itself:
            - To say "distinctions do not occur", the speaker needs words that are not each other — "distinction" is not "occur", "no" is not "yes". A denial that is not any different from an agreement says nothing.
            - So the denial is built out of distinctions — it is itself an example of the very thing it denies.

## Unlocks

- **Concepts:**
    - What occurs is varied, not uniform — there are unique, different things to discover, label, and investigate.
    - Singling out: a phenomenon can be pointed at rather than any other — "this, not that" — which is what makes asking "what is that?" possible.
    - The next observation: each distinct phenomenon is something specific — what the distinctions consist of.

- **Vocabulary:**
    - **"Distinct"** — not like another. *Synonyms: different, other, separate*
    - **"Multiplicity"** — the occurrence of distinct phenomena: one and another, not one alone. *Synonyms: count, number*
    - **"Not"** — the marker of a distinction: "red is not hot" points at the distinction between red and hot.
    - **"Uniform"** — without distinctions: everything alike in every way. What this node's observations rule out.
    - The singling-out and comparing words — each gets its meaning from distinctness:
        - **"a"** / **"one"** / **"each"** — pick out one phenomenon rather than the others.
        - **"the"** / **"that"** — point at a particular one.
        - **"what"** — asks which one occurred.
        - **"which"** / **"than"** — separating one from another.

## Eliminates

- "What occurs is one uniform happening, alike in exactly every way" — every observation above is a counterexample, and this very position cannot even be stated without using distinct words.
- "We invent the differences ourselves, after the fact — they are not in what occurs" — but nobody adds the difference between red and hot: the moment both occur, they are already somehow not the same. Whether anything makes the differences before you become aware of them is a separate question, left open (see the objection that distinctions are made rather than found, and Unknowns).

## Unknowns

- What the distinctions consist of — what each phenomenon specifically is.
- Whether distinct phenomena also connect — likenesses, belonging together.
- How many distinct phenomena occur — no count is taken.
- Whether a given distinction is always there or only sometimes — that question needs words for time and repetition that no node has introduced yet.
- Whether anything — a mind, a language — helps make the differences before you become aware of them, or whether nothing makes them at all. Raised by the objection that distinctions are made rather than found; this node does not answer it.
- Whether there are boundaries between distinct phenomena at all, where they are, and whether they are ever sharp (as raised by the objection that experience is a continuous flow) — questions this node does not answer.
- Whether anything is beneath the many distinct phenomena — something they all share or come from. The observations above already show that the many occur; what may be beneath them is a question for later nodes. (Raised by the objection that reality is one.)

## Objections

### 1. You say that phenomena differ — but you haven't said what makes them different.

**Objection Basis**

- "Red is not hot" — in what respect? Saying two things are distinct without specifying what differs between them says nothing: someone claiming a difference is expected to say what the difference is.
- A claim asserting that two things differ cannot be checked while no criterion is given. Until you say what differs, "distinct" is just a word floating over the observations.

**Objection Commitments**

1. Noticing a difference and knowing what differs them are one act — you cannot have the first without the second.
2. Saying that two things differ means nothing until what differs between them is specified.

**Shared Ground**

- The objector is right that "what makes them different?" is a good question that can be investigated — this project asks it too, and answers it in future nodes.
- Both sides agree a claim should not stay vague: every word in this project has to trace to something that occurs.

**What's Missing**

- The first commitment — that noticing a difference and knowing what differs are one act — inverts the actual order: you notice that red is not hot before you can say why. The awareness of the difference itself comes first; the understanding of why comes after.
    - A child tells "strawberry" from "ouch" long before it has any words for taste or pain.
- The second commitment — that saying two things differ means nothing until what differs between them is specified — would disqualify nearly every observation ever made: almost everything anyone has learned was noticed first and specified later. And the claim means something even before the respect is given: "red is not hot" already says the two are not alike in every way.
    - What the distinctions consist of is still a good question — and it is exactly what the future nodes are for. It is already listed under Unknowns.
    - Deepening the demand into "explain what you observed" makes it no stronger: an explanation is itself something observed that has no explanation of its own yet. Explaining an observation moves the ignorance one level deeper — it does not remove it.
        - Things fall — explained by gravity. But what is gravity? Explained by mass bending space and time. But why does mass do that? Each answer is a new observation to explain: the ignorance moved deeper, and it never vanished.
        - That is the direction of all knowing: it starts from the observations and moves toward what explains them — it does not start from knowing what reality is, with the observations read off from that. Nobody has that starting point; it is what the explaining slowly closes in on.
        - So the deepened demand — that an observation is not meaningful until explained — could not be satisfied even once: every explanation would wait on the explanation beneath it, without end.

**Correction**

- This node exposes that differences occur, not what they are made of.
- "In what way do differences occur?" is a question for the future nodes. An open next question does not undo the observation that raised it.

### 2. The distinctions are made, not found — attention, language, or culture draws the lines.

**Objection Basis**

- A philosopher named William James described infant experience as a "blooming, buzzing confusion" — perhaps experience is a continuous field until attention carves it into pieces, so what this node calls "distinct phenomena" only become distinct when someone pays attention to them.
- Languages carve experience differently: some split colors where others don't, some have no word separating "emotion" from "sensation". If the lines moved with the language, the lines are made by us, not found in the world.
- Philosophers following Kant (a philosopher who argued the mind adjusts and adds to experience before you actually become aware of it) add: the mind organizes raw input into distinct items, so the distinctness is contributed by the mind, not observed.

**Objection Commitments**

1. Reality is one undivided whole: without you to tell this from that, nothing is actually this rather than that.
2. Attention comes first, distinctions second: things become distinct because you pay attention to them in your awareness.
3. Whatever varies from culture to culture cannot be an intrinsic part of reality.

**Shared Ground**

- The objector is right that attention and language affect what gets noticed and what things get called — a wine-taster distinguishes flavors a beginner cannot, and languages really do draw the color lines in different places.
- Both sides agree the labels come from us: no word appears on its own authority — someone points it at something — and this project itself treats every term as a label pointed at something.

**What's Missing**

- The first commitment — that nothing is actually this rather than that without you — describes something nobody has ever observed: all observing happens within experience, and the claim is about reality apart from it. That is a model of reality put in front of the observations, not an observation (like saying "everything is really just atoms" — a picture of what things are, offered in place of what occurs).
- The second commitment — that attention comes first and distinctions second — inverts the observable order: attention needs something to select. You pay attention to red rather than hot because they already differ — if nothing differed from anything, there would be no edge for attention to land on.
    - James's "buzzing confusion" describes too many distinctions at once — not the absence of distinctions.
    - And even if babies learn over time to tell things apart, that only says people get better at noticing. Whether what they come to notice was waiting there all along, or is partly made by us, is exactly the question this node leaves open — either way, once someone notices, a distinction occurs, and that is all this node claims.
- The third commitment — that whatever varies with culture cannot be an intrinsic part of reality — confuses the labels with what they sort. Languages disagree about where "red" ends and "orange" begins — but no language's speakers find a world where red and loud are one thing. The lines move; the differences they sort do not vanish.
- And the node actually takes no side on where the distinctions come from: it says distinctions occur — it does not say who or what, if anything, makes them before you become aware of them. That question is listed under Unknowns.

**Correction**

- Attention selects among distinctions already occurring, and language labels them — neither can begin unless distinctions are already there.
- Whether anything makes the differences beforehand is a question for later, listed under Unknowns. That some difference occurs is confirmed every time one thing is noticed rather than another.

### 3. Experience is a continuous flow — not a collection of separate items.

**Objection Basis**

- A philosopher named Bergson argued that lived experience is a continuous flow (he called it duration), and that thought falsifies it by chopping it into pieces.
- A sunset's colors run into each other with no line where red ends and orange begins; one mood shades into the next. So the "distinct phenomena" here are artifacts of the chopping our minds do, not what actually occurs.

**Objection Commitments**

1. Real experience is continuous; separateness is imposed by analysis after the fact.
2. If there is no sharp boundary between two things, they are not really distinct.
3. Describing experience as items falsifies it.

**Shared Ground**

- The objector is right that experience often flows: colors do shade into one another, and no bell rings where one mood ends and the next begins.
- Both sides also agree that experience does not come as a list of items cut apart from one another, lined up for inspection.

**What's Missing**

- The second commitment — that no sharp boundary means no real distinction — confuses being distinct with being sharply bounded. You may not find the line where red becomes orange — yet red is not orange. The difference is plainly there even where the line is not.
    - Twilight has no sharp edge either, and yet day is still not night.
- A flow with no distinctions in it would not be a flow: nothing in it would differ from anything else, so no one could tell that anything in it was moving or changing. A flow is only a flow because this stretch is not that one, this shade is not the next. A river flows, and its speed is still not its temperature.
- And the objection's own words pick things out: "flow", "duration", "continuous" each label a feature the objector distinguishes from its opposite. Stating the objection uses the distinctness it denies.

**Correction**

- "Distinct" here means "not like another" — it does not mean "cut apart with sharp borders". Continuous flow is full of distinctions; that is how anyone can tell that it flows.
- Where one phenomenon ends and another begins, and whether that edge is ever sharp, are further questions this node does not take up — they are listed under Unknowns.

### 4. All distinctions are ultimately unreal — reality is one. (This position is called monism.)

**Objection Basis**

- Old and serious traditions hold that multiplicity is an illusion: Advaita Vedanta in India (which calls the illusion "maya"), the Greek philosopher Parmenides, some Buddhist schools. On their view, what appears as many distinct things is one undivided reality, and the appearance of distinction is a product of ignorance.
- Some people in deep meditative states report exactly that: all distinctions dissolving into one.

**Objection Commitments**

1. Whether experienced distinctions count as real depends on ultimate reality — if reality is one, then experienced distinctions are errors.
2. Observing a difference commits you to that difference being ultimately real.
3. An illusory distinction disqualifies the observation of it.

**Shared Ground**

- Both sides agree that "what is ultimately real?" is a valid question to ask — one this node lists under Unknowns rather than answers.
- The monist traditions themselves grant that the world appears as many — that is exactly the thing they set out to explain away.

**What's Missing**

- The second commitment — that observing a difference commits you to that difference being ultimately real — asks the observation to say more than it says. "Red is not hot" is a statement about what occurs, not about what is ultimately real (what is there on its own, not just as a form of something else) — a dream's red is still not the dream's hot, and a dream's red is not even "normal" red.
    - Call all of it an illusion if you like: the illusion still contains distinctions. An illusion with nothing distinct in it would not even be an illusion — there would be nothing to it. (This is the same answer PP1 gave [[07 - Objection Index#4. Consciousness is an illusion — nothing is actually happening|the objection that consciousness is an illusion]] — an illusion is also something happening — now aimed at distinctions.)
- The position also needs distinctions to state itself: the one versus the many, the real versus the illusory, the awakened view versus the ignorant one. A teaching that all distinctions are unreal, applied to itself, erases the distinction between itself and its own denial.
    - As a result, if this objection were true, it could no longer even say that appearance misleads about reality — that difference would be erased with all the rest.
- Even the person who says all distinctions dissolved describes the state through distinctions: stillness rather than noise, bliss rather than pain — some contents rather than others. And such an account is itself distinct from an account of a toothache.

**Correction**

- This node claims that distinctions occur — not that they are ultimately real (which has not been defined yet), and not that reality is many.
- A monist can hold "ultimate reality is one" and still grant that within what occurs, red is not hot. The main monist traditions say so themselves: Advaita Vedanta splits its teaching into an everyday level, where distinctions hold (its own classic example — mistaking a rope for a snake — needs the rope to not be the snake), and an ultimate level, where all is said to be one; Buddhist schools call the same split "the two truths". What this node claims belongs entirely to the everyday level.

### 5. A distinction needs someone to draw it — differences require a differentiator.

**Objection Basis**

- Telling two things apart is an act, and an act needs an actor — or at least a standpoint from which the difference shows.
- So "distinct phenomena occur", with nobody there doing the distinguishing, has not been shown to make sense. (This is [[07 - Objection Index#3. Nothing can occur without someone it occurs to|PP1's objection 3]] — "something can only occur if it occurs to something" — aimed here at distinctions.)

**Objection Commitments**

1. A difference exists only in the act of telling two things apart: no act of comparing, no distinction.
2. So a someone — or at least a standpoint — must already exist before any distinction can occur.

**Shared Ground**

- The objector is right that every distinction anyone has ever noticed was noticed in experience — this project builds on that rather than disputing it.
- Both sides agree that "who or what experiences these differences?" is a real question this project must eventually answer.

**What's Missing**

- The first commitment — that a difference exists only in the act of telling two things apart — has it backwards. Telling two things apart is only possible when the two are already unlike each other: if red and hot were alike in every way, there would be nothing for the comparing to find. So the act of comparing does not produce the difference — it depends on it. (See objection 2, "made, not found", which answers the same claim about attention: attention needs something to select.)
    - Comparing as an act — a someone doing the comparing — needs words for acts and actors that no node has introduced yet. (The comparing words this node introduces point at differences that are already there; they do not do the telling-apart themselves.)
- See [[07 - Objection Index#3. Nothing can occur without someone it occurs to|PP1's objection 3]], which addresses "something can only occur if it occurs to something": the node does not deny that there is a someone — it comes before any words for a someone have been introduced. Its silence about a differentiator is not a denial of one.

**Correction**

- The node says that distinctions occur. Who or what experiences them — and whether telling things apart is the act of a self — are questions for the future nodes on awareness and selfhood, already waiting in PP1's Unknowns.

### 6. This adds nothing — it is already inside "Phenomena occur", or it is the next node said vaguely.

**Objection Basis**

- Look at the sequence: "phenomena occur", "distinct phenomena occur", then a node about what each phenomenon specifically is. That reads like one thing said three ways, sliced thin — the slicing looks forced.
- PP1's own observations already listed a dozen different things — red, hot, ouch, a flying pig. The variety was already on the table. What does this node add that the plural "phenomena" didn't?

**Objection Commitments**

1. PP1's varied observations — and its plural "phenomena" — already assert distinctness.
2. A node must add something that can be denied on its own to deserve its place.
3. Whatever this node does add really belongs to the next node.

**Shared Ground**

- The objector is right that steps should not multiply without need: a node has to add something of its own, or it goes.
- Both sides agree PP1's observation list was already encompassing variance — and that variety is exactly what this node points back at.

**What's Missing**

- There is a position that accepts PP1 and rejects this node: "something occurs — one uniform, featureless occurrence, alike in every way". Stating that position shows the node adds a claim of its own.
    - PP1 says that something occurs. This node says what occurs is not all alike. PP1 deliberately left that open — its Unknowns ask whether what occurs is uniform or varied.
    - A question one node leaves open and the next node answers is not redundancy — it is exactly how the nodes are meant to follow one another.
- The first commitment — that PP1's varied list and plural already assert distinctness — mistakes showing for claiming.
    - PP1's claim used none of the differences its list displayed: deny every difference, and the claim degrades to "something occurs" and still stands.
    - PP1's own Conclusion defines the singular ("'Phenomenon' is our label for anything that occurs") — so the plural was a manner of speaking, not a claim that many occur.
    - This node is where the differences themselves get claimed, and so become checkable and usable.
- The second commitment — that a node must add something that can be denied on its own — is satisfied, not violated: this node can be denied by itself (deny it and you get exactly that uniform position), so by the objector's own standard it deserves its place.
- The third commitment — that whatever this node adds really belongs to the next node — runs two questions together: "distinct" says the phenomena are not alike; it does not yet say what each one is. That second question is real and different, which is why it gets its own node.

**Correction**

- The steps are the order in which each observation is claimed and labelled — not one thing re-said three times: something occurs (PP1); what occurs is not all alike (this node); each is something specific (the next node).
- Each step can be denied on its own, so each deserves its place on its own.
