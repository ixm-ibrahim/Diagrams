# Observation

## The question this node must answer

What is an observation, in his words — and what separates an observation from a phenomenon? The word carries [[Scope]] ("a set of observations"), [[Context]] ("the scope of observations the statement is about"), and [[Ontological]] ("whatever is not directly observed") — three ratified definitions standing on this hole.

## Inspiration — not live wording

- **His raw notes** (2026-07-07, verbatim, `Comparative Religion\claim-structure\archive-v2\raw-notes-2026-07-07.md`, note 3): "This is in logic too: deductions from inductions, inductions from observations, observations from phenomena." — his own ordering puts observations one step above phenomena.
- Same notes, note 6, verbatim:
  > We start with observing basic things - red, anger, 2, etc. - and call "reality" all that (ie. whatever we didn't will, what is independent of it).
- Same notes, note 3's aside, verbatim:
  > Like if you see an apple, then you objectively saw an apple - that doesn't necessarily mean that you saw a "real" apple or not ... it's a categorization/interpretation problem.

## Open flags
