# Handoff — The case that every outside cause has a will behind it

A plain-language record, written so a fresh chat can pick it up cold. No prior reading needed and no names to look up; only the reasoning is here.

This document does two things, and only two:

1. It lays out, in plain terms and at full strength, **the case** that outside causes have a will behind them — stated as the person who holds it means it.
2. It gathers the **questions that naturally come up** about the case — some asked to clarify, some resting on an assumption that hasn't yet been checked against the argument — and shows what the argument already says to each.

It is written one-sided on purpose: it presents the case, and the questions that belong to understanding it. The aim of the second part is to save a reader from re-walking a question the argument has already settled.

---

## Words used here, in plain terms

- **the will (as a command)** — the one thing you experience directly, from the inside. By the starting definition below, a will just *is* "the thing that commands a change." So "doing" is not something extra you have to catch the will performing; it is already part of what a will is.
- **the facilitating mechanism** — the middle you don't control, which takes the command and turns it into the effect *somehow*. Even your *ability* to will sits in this uncontrolled layer: you can't will to will otherwise, or to stop being able to will.
- **the somehow (the doing)** — the actual change-making between two correlated steps. It is never observed, in *any* chain, inside you or outside you. That is a limit on what can be known, not a gap you could close with a better look.
- **ease / resistance** — your awareness of whether a will lands. Will your hand: it moves (ease). Will the rain, or the air past your fingers, or a lost limb: nothing (resistance). This is awareness of success-or-failure — not a sensation of "producing" anything.
- **the starter slot** — the first position in a chain. In your own action it holds your will; in a plant it holds the unknown "?" before water, soil, and light do their work.
- **outside / an outside cause** — "outside" means outside direct experience, which is everything but your own me-ness; you never experience arm-ness or rain-ness, only their effects on you. An outside cause is any not-you thing that shows the same chain shape your willing shows.
- **best explanation (abduction)** — when a thing can't be seen, you go with the account that best fits what you *can* see, rather than shrugging. This isn't leaning on science as an authority; it's that refusing it would throw out nearly all knowledge of what we can't directly observe, which defeats itself (see move 8).
- **will-with-awareness** — what gets carried into the unknown starter slot: a commander with awareness bound to it (in your own case you perceive, you will in response, and you aim at the effect). Explicitly *not* a human mind, and not "a person behind reality."

## 0. The question, and where it stands

**The question.** The one piece of causing you are on the inside of is your own: you will, and your body answers. Every other "cause" you meet only through its effects on your senses. So when you carry the word "cause" out into the world, does a **will** come along with it?

**Where it stands.** The case reaches a clear, bounded claim: *every outside cause has a will-with-awareness behind its starter slot; whether those are one will or many is left open.* This document lays out that case, then collects the questions that naturally come up about it.

---

## 1. The case, at full strength

The method comes first, because the case leans on it: **observe → name and define → match patterns → draw out what follows → check by prediction where you can, and where you can't, compare explanations and take the best.** The one move ruled out is the reverse — starting from an answer, then filtering the evidence to protect it. Everything below runs forward from observation, never backward from a conclusion.

1. **The first observation, and the first definition.** The one bit of causing you are inside of is your own. You observe that your body's movement *corresponds to* your will, and you name that observed correspondence "cause" or "doing." It stands apart from merely wanting — willing the rain, where the correspondence fails. So at root, "cause/doing" is an observed **correlation**. The will is the one thing experienced directly; the correlation, the mechanism, and the effect are all met from the outside.

2. **A new observation forces a refinement.** Willing alone isn't enough — sometimes nothing moves, and the body also does things you can't control. So a middle piece has to sit between will and effect. Update the definition: a cause is **{will-command, facilitating mechanism, effect}**. The mechanism takes the command and turns it into the effect *somehow*, and that somehow is never fully exposed. This change comes from an observation, not from convenience — the only honest reason to change a definition.

3. **What is actually experienced — and what isn't.** Not "production." You are aware of the will as a command, and of *ease or resistance* — whether the will lands. And the will is known through several channels at once (proprioception, sight, sound, the felt result), not one shaky inner glimpse — the way a bee sting gets pinned down by pain and sight and sound all landing on the same answer. What you do **not** experience is the sub-personal machinery. But that is no special gap: the somehow was never tied to any one level (tissue, cell, atom) in the first place, so "do you feel the mechanism?" has no clean answer to give.

4. **"Outside," defined without smuggling anything in.** You only ever directly experience your own me-ness. Your arm, heart, and nerves are met through the senses exactly as the rain is — so your own body already counts as "outside." An **outside cause** is any not-you thing that shows the same chain shape your willing shows: {unknown commander, apparent mechanism, apparent effect}. "Outside" points at *where*; it adds no ingredient, so the definition isn't circular.

5. **Line the chains up by matching positions, not by what we loosely call "the cause."** Don't compare *willing* to *water* — water is the plant's middle step, the counterpart of your nerve and muscle. Compare like to like:
   - you: **[will]** → [nerve, muscle] → [arm moves]
   - plant: **[?]** → [water, soil, light] → [growth]
   The "?" is the **starter slot** — the same position your will fills. Your will lines up with the plant's unknown first thing, not with the water.

6. **The doing is never observed — anywhere.** You never watch the somehow itself: not in water, and not in your own nerves. So asking to "see the doing" can't pick out the will, since nothing passes that test.

7. **"Doing" is part of what a will is — by the starting definition.** Because "cause/doing" was *defined* (move 1) as the will being the start of a change, the will doesn't have to be caught separately "bringing about" anything. "Will" already means "the thing that commands an effect." So the will is the one commander you are acquainted with; every other commander, and the somehow itself, you only ever infer.

8. **So take the best explanation — and notice the method isn't optional.** When a thing can't be directly observed, the only way to reach it at all is to take the account that best fits what you can see. This isn't leaning on science as a final authority. It's that refusing the move doesn't stay contained: refuse it, and you don't just lose this one conclusion — you lose almost everything anyone counts as knowledge of what they can't directly see, science included. A rule for what counts as knowledge that ends up discarding nearly all knowledge saws off the branch it sits on; you'd have no footing left to assert the rule either, so you can't consistently hold it. Science is simply one place the move is already running in plain view. Apply it here: the only thing you have any acquaintance with that *does* anything is your will, so the best explanation for the unknown doer is that it is **will-like**.

9. **And leaving the slot empty is not a free default.** Insisting on staying agnostic, or on a doer with no inside at all, quietly treats *no-will* as the safe starting point — and that is its own unobserved assumption, no freer than the other. Observation can't tell the two apart, so "no-will" has no special claim to being the baseline. The will, as the only doer you actually know, comes out ahead in the comparison rather than even with the blank.

10. **Carry it outward in stages — and stop where the evidence stops.**
    - **Scope: every outside cause, near enough by definition.** Anything you'd call an outside cause already has the shape, so the projection covers all of them, not a chosen few.
    - **Knowing: yes.** In your own case, will and awareness come as a pair — you perceive, you will in response, and you aim at the effect you command — so carrying the will over carries its awareness along. The limit is marked plainly: this is *will-with-awareness*, **not** a human mind, and not "a person behind reality."
    - **One vs. many: not claimed.** Even one plant's growth isn't "1 will"; it is an incalculable run of transitions, each its own will-manifestation. Whether reality is *one* commander or *many* is a downstream question to investigate, settled by checking whether the patterns of all those manifestations reduce to a single pattern or stay plural. The case leaves this open on purpose.

**Strength claimed.** This is the best read of what you can see — not as certain as the direct experience of willing itself, but the best explanation by the same standard used everywhere else. "Strong" means *best-explanation here*, not *certain*.

## 2. Questions that come up, and what the argument says

These are the questions a reader naturally brings. Some are asked to clarify; some rest on an assumption that hasn't been checked against the argument yet. For each: the question, what the argument says, and — where one is hiding — the assumption worth pausing on.

### "Where exactly is the doing, and do you feel it?"
**The questions.** Is the will just along for the ride, with the real doing happening in the middle step? You feel the willing but not the carrying-out — so isn't the part you feel separate from the part that does the work? Do you feel the *mechanism*? Can you point to the will actually producing the effect?

**What the argument says.** It never claims you feel or observe the doing. The somehow is unobserved in every chain, the will's own included, and the argument grants that from the start. Its claim is narrower: the will *is* the commander, because "doing" is part of what "will" means (move 7). The arm doesn't move without the will, so the will is needed; the middle step is only more steps-and-effect, so the doing isn't sitting "in there"; and "the mechanism" isn't at any one level, so there's no single thing to feel or fail to feel.

**The assumption worth pausing on.** That there is a separate "doing" you could inspect, set apart from the will-command — so the will would have to split into a felt front-end and an unfelt producer. The argument doesn't split it that way, so this whole cluster is already answered by move 7.

### "Shouldn't a claim like this meet a higher bar?"
**The questions.** Shouldn't you have to show the doing for the will in particular? Shouldn't a claim this big be as certain as the experience of willing itself?

**What the argument says.** No cause anywhere shows the doing, so asking it only of the will sets a bar nothing meets. And certainty that strict would rule out science along with the claim. The bar the argument actually uses is best-explanation by its own kind of reasoning (move 8).

**The assumption worth pausing on.** That the will-claim should clear a higher bar than ordinary claims about causes.

### "Can this even be tested?"
**The questions.** Isn't this a claim no observation could ever touch? And couldn't it be that the will actually trails the movement rather than starting it?

**What the argument says.** It can be tested in principle: if your own controllable movements turned out to run with the will as a mere passenger, the claim would fail. And naming a bare possibility ("maybe it trails") isn't evidence — unsupported maybes sit on both sides equally, so they don't tip a best-explanation either way.

**The assumption worth pausing on.** That a claim about an unseen inside must be untestable, and that simply naming an alternative counts against the best explanation.

### "Aren't you comparing the wrong things?"
**The questions.** When you set your willing beside a plant, shouldn't *willing* line up with the *water*? And isn't felt warmth a fair mirror for the will?

**What the argument says.** Line the chains up by position. Water is the plant's middle step — the counterpart of your nerve and muscle, not of your will. And warmth is an effect *on you*, which sits on the far side of the chain, not at the starter. Match starter to starter (move 5).

**The assumption worth pausing on.** That you compare chains by what we loosely call "the cause," rather than by matching positions in the chain.

### "But the rock plainly has no will — doesn't it?"
**The questions.** Isn't the boulder a cleaner case of plain cause-and-effect? Surely you wouldn't credit a rock with a will?

**What the argument says.** Calling the rock will-free assumes the very thing in question. Move 9 is built right here: "no-will" isn't a free starting point, because observation can't tell a will-filled starter from a will-free one — so neither can simply be assumed at the outset.

**The assumption worth pausing on.** That "the rock has no will" is common ground. In this argument it isn't; it's the open question.

### "Why this style of reasoning, and not another?"
**The questions.** Isn't it equally likely either way, so why favor a will? Isn't the maker comparison really about contrast? Isn't a single example too thin to build on? And doesn't a concept like this form the way it does for everyday objects you can see from both sides?

**What the argument says.** The route is best-explanation, not a coin-flip on odds. The maker comparison runs on *likeness* (I make things by willing; this resembles what I make), not contrast. The point was never the count of examples — direct experience isn't the kind of evidence you tally, and more cases wouldn't change it. And the everyday-object model doesn't fit: with cups and boxes you see both sides directly, whereas here you live one side and meet the other only through its effects.

**The assumption worth pausing on.** That the argument must use a particular favored style of reasoning, or fit a model drawn from a case that isn't parallel.

### "Two quick assumptions worth slowing down on"
**The questions.** Does filling in what a thing is on the inside really bear on what does the producing? And isn't a doer with no awareness simply the simpler assumption?

**What the argument says.** On the first: yes, it bears on it — in this argument "doing" is part of what a will is, so a guess about the inside *is* a guess about the doing (move 7). On the second: a will felt from the inside has a character of its own, while a doer named only by its effect has none, so "no awareness" isn't automatically the leaner option (move 9).

**The assumption worth pausing on.** That either of these can be set aside in passing, when each actually turns on a move the argument has already made.
