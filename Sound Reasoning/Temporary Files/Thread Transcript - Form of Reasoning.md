## The answer, in short

The term: **unlocking** — the record's own word, already a section of every node. The relation between nodes is that the parent unlocks the child: it makes the child's question askable and its claim sayable as a claim of the record, and contributes no part of its truth — the way opening a door moves nobody through it.

The telling, in one line: **each node takes its question from its parents and its answer from reality.** And this is not a doctrine — it can be read off the pages, in two facts about PP2:

1. **PP1 appears nowhere among PP2's supports.** Walk PP2's Proof by Contradiction step by step: no step says "phenomena occur, therefore...". Even step 3 — "Red, orange, ouch ... all occur" — is PP2's own fresh observation, asserting more than PP1's claim could hand it (PP1 says only that *something* occurs; step 3 says *these specific things* do). Nothing in PP2's warrant draws on PP1's having been established.
2. **PP1 appears everywhere in PP2's words.** The claim ("Distinct **phenomena occur**"), the definition ("Phenomena are labelled "distinct" when one is not like another"), and the proof's own steps ("all occurrences would appear identical in every way") all speak with PP1's concept.

Nowhere as support, everywhere in the words. And the handing-down is literally on PP1's page: its Unlocks says "The next observation: seeing if what occurs is uniform or varied — whether or not distinctions occur" — PP1 hands PP2 its question outright, along with the words to answer it. Reality hands PP2 the answer, at PP2's own observation, exactly as it handed PP1 its answer.

## The one picture, and his seven questions as views of it

**Q1 — both nodes come from reality, so what is the link?** The link passes a question and words; truth is not passed because it does not have to be — in the primitives, what occurs is present to every node alike. His "in different ways, exactly" has an exact answer inside "Distinct phenomena occur": the words "phenomena occur" take their **meaning** from PP1 and their **truth** from reality (PP2's own observations re-verify them — every observed difference is itself an occurrence, which is also why the child entails the parent and not the reverse). The word "distinct" takes its meaning from reality directly — PP2 attaches the label to the difference between red and hot itself — while PP1's words say what the label attaches to ("one is not like another"). And his wrinkle — that PP1's own list already displays distinct things — is answered on PP2's own page, objection 6: "PP1's claim used none of the differences its list displayed: deny every difference, and the claim degrades to 'something occurs' and still stands." Displaying is not claiming; a node is where one displayed feature becomes claimed. Three levels have to be told apart: observation shows features together; prose (proofs included) speaks our full ordinary language, looking back; only claims and definitions are bound to granted terms — and there, and only there, the order runs one way ("distinct" is defined through "phenomenon", "phenomenon" through "occur", not the reverse). That is how the link can be one-way while every observation shows everything together and all prose uses everything.

**Q2 — if the links hold no truth, what are they for?** Errors hide inside words. An ordinary argument leans silently on the meanings of its terms, so a falsehood can lodge inside a word nobody ever proved. The links are the record's refusal to lean silently: walk any claim backward, definition by definition, and every load-bearing word ends at a node with its own observation and its own denial test — the record's dictionary ends at reality instead of running in a circle, and a dispute about a claim lands at exactly one node. The "algebra": grants accumulate downward (PP3 will use PP2's words, which use PP1's); entailments accumulate upward (each child says everything its parent says, and more); only the downward direction is ever used in a proof. And the damage accounting is exact: if a node somehow fell, its ancestors would stand untouched (no claim drew truth from it), while its descendants would fall twice — false, because each says everything the fallen node said, and unsayable, because their definitions would trace through a word no node any longer grants. That double fall is the upward entailment at work — unused in proofs, active only in the accounting.

**Q3 — which contains which, and what is the "one more thing"?** Both containments are real and forced to run opposite because two different things are contained. In what is said, the child contains the parent — everything the parent says plus one thing more, so denying a parent takes its descendants down. In what is left open, the parent contains the child — each claim cuts one position directly (PP1: "Nothing whatsoever is happening"; PP2: that what occurs is one uniform happening), so each added claim strictly shrinks what is left standing. The "one more thing" is one displayed feature promoted from shown to claimed, with one new **concept** minted to hold it — however many words the concept brings (PP2 mints seventeen, all tracing to distinctness). His box example, placed exactly: walking closer added no contents to the box — and between PP1 and PP2 not even the walking happens, since PP1's own list had already displayed the differences. His instinct that the newness is "the new context" is right in this form: "an apple is in the box" answers a finer question inside "something is in the box" — a question the first sentence's own words made askable. The sharpening is in the claims, not in what occurs. One correction to the lego picture, to match his discovery feeling: the features occur fused in every observation — nobody assembles them; what is assembled one piece at a time is only the saying.

**Q4 — why one concept per node, and why this process at all?** Two separate answers. The **order** is forced: a record opening at "distinctions occur" could not really skip the phenomena node — it would only hide it, since "distinct" is defined through "phenomenon" and "phenomenon" through "occur"; the opening claim would hold the parent inside itself, unstated, untested, its words granted by no node. Forced, that is, given the one discipline the record runs on — no claim enters with ungranted load-bearing words — and that discipline is not a taste: it is what the Q2 audit is bought with. The **one-concept format** is what a node is: one concept's address — its own observation, its own denial test, its own objection level, its own dictionary line. A sentence introducing two concepts is two unlockings wearing one sentence: its denial still breaks as one statement, but the breakage does not say which concept broke — the proof must split into exactly the two smaller proofs, so the format performs that split in advance. (One concept, not one word: PP2's seventeen words all hold one concept.)

**Q5 — discovery, and the blank.** Discovery is the right word, and the needing lives in what occurs, not only in our vocabulary: a difference with no two occurrences to differ is no difference — the dependence is found there, and the concepts, read off what occurs, inherit its order. Your own words already fill the blank: **in the order the concepts need each other** — the block every other block needs comes first. "Conceptual coverage" is the same order seen from the side of reality: the less a claim says, the more it covers — PP1 says the least and covers everything, and every step after it trades coverage for content. One correction: the record is a frontier, not a single line — PP1's Unknowns hold four open questions, so between parent and child the order is forced, while which open question to take up next is free.

**Q6 — is the transition to inference seamless?** First, why this link is not inference: PP1 lends PP2 no support of any kind. Not entailment — "phenomena occur" says nothing about variety (PP1's own Unknowns leave it open, and PP2's objection 6 states the position that accepts PP1 and rejects PP2: one uniform, featureless occurrence); the only entailment between them runs child to parent, the reverse of the direction support flows in an inference. And not evidence — PP2's proof cites no part of PP1. A sentence contributing no support to a claim is not functioning as a premise for it; the link is not a weakened inference but no inference at all. Your two "because the parent" feelings split accordingly: an inference's conclusion is *true* because its premises are; PP2 is *sayable* because of PP1, and true because of what occurs. Then the continuity you sensed, exactly: every claim leans two ways — on the record for its words, and on its stated support for its truth. In the primitives, the stated support is the node's own observation plus the denial test, the only support that needs no earlier claims. Once nodes have exposed what induction and deduction are — each ratified by its own observation and denial test, like everything else — a claim's stated support may also be a proven way of reasoning applied to proven claims: truth traveling between claims for the first time, needed where observation cannot go directly (the unseen). And the unlocking runs unchanged inside both: a deduction's premises must be sayable before they can support — **inference always had this half inside it, with no label of its own**. The record's first phase runs that half pure, because in the primitives it is the only thing that needs to pass.

**Q7 — the term.** Unlocking. It is record-native (no school owns it, so no trained framing rides in), it is operational (PP1's Unlocks already performs the relation), a newcomer gets the door image at once, and a philosopher lands with three guarded one-liners: the parent is an *enabling condition, not a premise*; the child *presupposes* the parent rather than draws on it — "presupposes" meaning only "needs in place to be said in the record", with no sentences left neither true nor false and none of that semantics literature, since here every presupposed claim is itself proven; and the order is what Aristotle called *prior in account* — the account of "distinct" uses "phenomena", not the reverse — with no doctrine of essences. Where the earlier pieces land inside this picture: "specification" described the trail's shape, "exposure" the act at a node, and the "sourcing"/"grounding" split the two channels — what all three lacked was the mechanism, the question handed down and the words granted, which is the part that dissolves rather than labels. If "unlocking" does not land for you, the fallback is **the grant** — the dictionary's own verb — but I recommend one term, not two, and unlocking covers both the question and the words.

Terms tested and set aside as the lead: "presupposition" (the everyday ear hears "assumed without proof" — the opposite of the record's practice — and the philosopher's ear hears a semantics doctrine), "definitional dependence" (an existing term of art about essences), "articulation" (the Latin word where an everyday one works), "spelling out" (your case-14 register ruling already rejected it), "making explicit" (a school's flag).

## What the tree is for, when it comes to logic

Logic moves truth from statement to statement when reality has already supplied it; it cannot make truth, and it cannot see inside a word. A deduction delivers a true conclusion only if two things were secured before the first step ran: the premises are true, and every word keeps one meaning for the whole trip. Logic verifies neither — it sees only the shapes of sentences. Pipes move water; they do not make water, and they cannot tell water from poison.

So an argument goes wrong in three ways, and logic catches only one: a step can be invalid (logic catches this); a premise can be false (logic is blind to this); a word can shift meaning mid-argument, or be attached to no occurrence at all (logic is blind to this too). The tree exists for the word failures, which it blocks directly — and for false premises it does something different but just as concrete: it keeps every claim traceable to observations, so the false premise is handed to the judge that can catch it, instead of hiding behind other sentences. Four stories:

**The shifting word.** "Experience is an illusion; illusions aren't real; so experience isn't real" (04's own example of semantic drift). "Real" means "being what it seems to be" where the argument needs "illusions aren't real" to sound plausible, and "occurring at all" where the conclusion wants to shock. Force one meaning from start to finish and the argument dies on the table: with "being what it seems to be", the conclusion only says experience may not be what it seems — it no longer denies that experience occurs; with "occurring at all", the second premise is false, because illusions do occur. In the record this argument cannot be assembled at all: "real" would be a homonym with two dictionary entries, each tracing to its own node, and the argument needs both entries inside one word.
