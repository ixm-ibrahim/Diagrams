# The Complete Question — the form of reasoning between the nodes

Assembled from the reasoning-form thread, in the author's voice. Double quotation marks hold his verbatim words, except where a passage is marked as the thread's wording or a page's wording. The final test in section 4 was added by him on accepting this assembly. This document does not answer the question; it only states it.

## 1. The main question

Just like how formal logic analyzes the structure of an argument, analyze the structure of this tree — PP1, PP2, and all the projected future nodes: "what is the form of reasoning that is the structure of this tree, and what is it/how does it manifest as a link between two nodes?"

The question has three parts that one answer must cover at once:

- **The link's necessity** — the part I restated most, and the part still unanswered. The significance of a node's reference to reality is extremely clear; "the significance of its reference to its parent is not." The test any answer must pass: "remove the link, and something significant [like to its truth-value] changes." "Why is there the 'demand that every chain end ... at the level of words'? Obviously the parent is a 'reference' for the child, but in what significant/necessary sense?" (Note that "reference" here touches the source observations in section 3.) So far, "all you[r] answers don't actually provide this, they only answer the unnecessary."
- **The coincidence requirement.** "Whatever the answer is, it would need to coincide with whatever the first nodes will be in the logic/reasoning/whatever this is section of the tree, as we're utilizing that form of reasoning right now to build the tree." Made concrete later: "I am asking you to draft the nodes that come before grounding and consistency, or in other words, the primitives/first nodes of the 'logical section' of this tree... and these logical primitive nodes are the labels for the answer to this whole question." Keep the target distinct: PP1 and PP2 "are the foundation in some sense to all other nodes — a different kind of foundation than the logical primitive nodes I've been asking about."
- **The deliverable: a term and a telling.** A name that a person new to the project and a seasoned philosopher would both grasp immediately, and a plain, human-friendly explanation of what the reasoning is, why the one-concept requirement follows from it, why the order is what it is, and how it relates to the induction and deduction that come later — all in demystified everyday language, with examples. "All these questions are related to each other for sure — I want the one picture they are all views of."

## 2. My motivation for asking it

- The link is asserted everywhere and exposed nowhere. PP2 lists PP1 under "Inherits From", and we keep saying PP2 is "grounded in" PP1 — "whatever that means." "In what sense does PP1 'allow' PP2, if I can just source PP2 in reality anyways?"
- The answers so far label the confusion instead of dissolving it. The main thread's pieces — "specification", "exposure", the "sourcing" (claim → reality) against "grounding" (concept → concept) division — "help some, but none of it has landed as the exposed thing itself; it feels like my confusion getting labeled rather than dissolved." Those answers felt stale, and the thread was told not to re-serve them.
- An arbitrary answer here makes every later form of reasoning arbitrary. "What is the purpose/benefit gained from organizing observations (or, our labels for observations) like this, if it seems to boil down to a choice in organization? (Then again, even logic is a choice in organization too...) Like why do I need to 'unlock' anything when I just have access to it and its label already? It seems arbitrary — and if this is arbitrary, and this form of reasoning is the foundation for all future forms of reasoning, then they are arbitrary as well..."
- The answer must show the whole method right — or show it wrong — from outside it. "You're supposed to be answering in such a way that anyone who doesn't have the knowledge of this project and its content can still understand why it's needed — like I'm expecting an answer that doesn't look like a circular, retroactive explanation. I suppose it could be the case that I'm doing all this wrong, and from how you are answering, I would never know. As a result, if this process is right, and I'm directly asking what makes it right, then you're not really addressing what makes it correct in whatever answer you give."
- The demystification is part of the question, not a delivery preference. "What benefit is it if we have a map/tree of meaning — like what problem does it solve/avoid when it comes to logic?" — with examples showing "the distinguishing factor between a true and false statement", "like what distinguishes a truth about something unseen vs. a falsehood about something unseen, or seen vs. seen, etc."

## 3. What I observed that bears on it

**Both nodes come from reality — and the wrinkle.** PP1 was grounded in/sourced from reality. PP2 was also grounded in/sourced from reality. "Is the link just conceptual — like the concept of PP2 uses the lego piece of PP1 to be valid? But then where did 'distinct' come from, if not from PP1? And here's a wrinkle: in a way, 'distinct' is also a thing that PP1 exposes too — PP1's own observation list (red, hot, ouch) already displays distinct things, and noticing a distinction is itself something that occurs, so distinctness was already inside 'phenomena' somehow — if that makes sense." So: "'phenomena occur' in PP1 comes from reality/how our minds work; the 'distinct' part of PP2 comes from reality/how our minds work; and the 'phenomena occur' inside PP2 comes from both PP1 and reality… in different ways? What ways, exactly?"

**The containment observation.** If each node is the set of all observations that satisfy its claim, the parent contains the child — PP1 encompasses all observations, PP2 a subset. Yet the child "says everything the parent says, plus one more thing" — and that one more thing "is still encompassed by the previous nodes, a manifestation of them." My box example: "something is in the box" said of a far-off blur, against "an apple is in the box" said after walking closer — the second may encompass the terms of the first plus an additional term, "but the first sentence encompasses all observations that could ever be made about what's in the box. And the 'newness' of the second isn't really a new word — it's the new context the observation manifests in." Later, from the scope side: "it's interesting that, in this tree, the scope of a parent encompasses the scopes of its children... I wonder if this is related to the question I've been asking, on what underlying form of reasoning is occurring here, since the tree will naturally shift to start using these forms of reasoning anyway (but it will be an extension of the form of reasoning we're doing now)." And the boundary form of the same picture, from the apparent-contradiction observation below: a parent defines a boundary around what occurs — a scope — and the child a sub-boundary within it.

**The apparent contradiction in the tree's structure** — added after the first full answer, and possibly "one of the core issues". I say that the parent nodes are the building blocks for their children (or, the children are broken down into their more primitive concepts as the parents) — "so this gives the impression that I won't find anything 'new' in the children that didn't come from the parents. But then, each new node introduces something 'new', which seems to contradict that intent." And it must be faced away from PP1 → PP2, which was consuming the approach: from "qualities occur" to "will occurs", or "physical phenomena occur" (like sight, hearing, touch), or "mental phenomena occur" (like thoughts, imagination), or "emotions occur" (like happiness, sadness). Where did "will" come from, if a child is supposed to break down into its parents with no leftover? The containment puzzle and the wrinkle are this contradiction's two earlier faces.

And my own reading of where the resolution may live, stated after: "The new concept that a child node introduces is already within the parent node, just not [yet] exposed or defined — like 'will occurs' for example. That could have been one of PP1's observations, after all. It's like, all of what occurs exists in PP1, and the children nodes narrow it down." Interestingly, the subject of scopes surfaces again right here: "a parent node defines some boundary around reality (and by 'reality', I mean 'what occurs') — ie. a scope — and the child defines a sub-boundary within the parent's scope — ie. a narrower scope in some sense."

**The proof observation.** Everything inside a node, like the Proof by Contradiction, is verifying the truth of the node — "and note how the exposed contradiction is a contradiction with reality, the same source that supplied the concept represented by the nodes (maybe exposing the 'reference' that each form of reasoning has to make it true or false will expose something important...)." Deniability seems relevant — "but whether this is the foundation or the side effect/justification is what I'm talking about."

**The understanding observation.** The tree seems to track understanding. "Try to explain 'distinct' without 'phenomena', and you can't — the listener won't be able to understand what PP2 actually means or refers to. But if you try to explain 'phenomena' without 'distinct', you can... You can understand in one direction, but not the other, so that's why this particular organization is necessary." That gives some intuition on what "grounded" means — "like whether or not you are qualified to understand a topic? Like let's say someone claims 'flying pigs exist in reality'. Hmm idk if understanding is the right word here, but I'll use it anyways: if you attempt to ground that sentence, you expose that the source (interesting this showed up here, I wonder what the relationship between all this is) of 'flying pigs' is imagination, not reality, so to claim that they exist in reality is unwarranted (interesting this showed up here too...). So this tree exposes/tracks... understanding? Do you see what I'm trying to get at? The study of phenomenology, as I'm doing it in this tree, is doing this fundamental form of reasoning." A node for "understanding" may need projecting — I said "to understand what __ means or *refers to*", which touches my earlier inquiry about references.

**The source observation.** "One of the projected inferences I know we'll eventually get to is 'all information has a source', which is what a reference is."

**The discovery feeling.** "It's almost like, just as we roam the earth and discover new science, we're roaming our own minds and discovering their features" — exposing the primitive, fundamental building blocks of all concepts, in some order, starting with the concepts that just appear, where PP1 covers all possible concepts and everything downstream of PP2 will ultimately require the concept of "distinction". "I keep reaching for 'conceptual grounding' or 'conceptual coverage'. Is that right? And what fills the blank in 'in ___ order'?"

**The circularity observation.** A dictionary can define "occur" by "happen", "happen" by "take place", "take place" by "occur" — in one sense that is not circular reasoning, "because each term is assigning a label to reality so reality is the actual prior link, not another term. But having said that, it's still circular reasoning in a PP2 sense — what is the distinguishing factor between 'occur' and 'happen'? Some context. Where does that context come from? Using the same words as 'occur' and 'happen'... terms still need to be grounded to avoid circularity — this point exposes that circularity (as well as grounded in imagination rather than reality) is the problem this tree resolves."

**What "logic" has to mean here.** The mainstream cutoff — formal logic as the study of argument shapes only — is not the frame. "We have the 3 pipelines about a claim, which are respective to what a claim essentially is — exposed by its phenomenological reduction. Exposing issues in the inferential structure of a sentence should only be one aspect of 'logic'. If this is a mainstream philosophy thing, then document it as appropriate." The verdict on a claim going right or wrong is the outcome of the whole assessment.

**The meta observation.** Is the purpose of this whole organization to ground our concepts in reality? "If that's the case, then the meta-discovery is that grounding in reality is more primitive than analysis for consistency — but idk about that... navigating the meta is difficult as we're implementing the same nodes that we will later discover, and the more primitive one should be reflecting of the effort this project should be doing…"

**The fundamentally unseen.** "God is an unseen concept. Gravity is an unseen concept. Intrinsic causes are an unseen concept. I don't mean something that could be seen if you just walked over there, but something fundamentally unseen." Atheists and mainstream philosophy argue that God doesn't exist — "or agnostics will say that it's unknowable whether He does" — the same way "a fire behind a hill" is argued, or "a ball exists in this closed box": you have to open it to find out, and no theist ever opened some box (some sense, some discovery) to see God — therefore they made it up. And the double standard this exposed: "when it comes to God, atheists demand grounding... but not necessarily for the existence of gravity or intrinsic causes or something." Underneath it, a genuine sub-question about where unseen concepts come from at all: "where did the concept come from (ignoring the discovery of intuitive theism) if not from their own minds, as they didn't discover it?"

**The warrant observation.** "What does 'warrant' mean? It sounds like this concept of warranting is the fundamental form of reasoning that exposes everything, right? Like what 'warrants' C's truth if A is true and B is true (deduction)? What 'warrants' gravity existing if we can't see it? What 'warrants' PP2 after PP1? Or am I misidentifying the root here? I mean, does 'warranting' supply whatever is needed for 'grounding' and 'consistency'? Are those potential child nodes context-based manifestations of the potential parent node?"

**The scope family.** "It seems like the act of 'deduction' is scope intersection, 'induction' is scope identification (including expansion and reduction), and 'abduction' is... scope selection/preference?" Deduction: "all dogs are mammals" assigns "mammal" to the scope of dogs, "my pet is a dog" places my pet in that scope, the scopes intersect and "mammal" transfers. Induction: the pattern behind a set of observations — the sun rose every day, so it will rise tomorrow — identifying the scope for a set of observations; and those scopes are what deduction then uses, "meaning induction is the foundation/grounding for deductive arguments." Inductions may divide into correct (zero counterexamples so far), incomplete (a counterexample exists but doesn't contradict the rule somehow — Newtonian and Einsteinian physics), and incorrect (a counterexample that contradicts the rule). Abduction sorts rival induced patterns — "in any abductive preference, both of rival cases would have had to be induced before one of them could be selected." A hypothesis worth keeping: "I wonder if abduction is akin to those statements that their content represent the claim's structure — in which they are the primitive/first nodes that start everything else." And the consolidation: "all forms of reasoning, including induction and deduction, are really just discussions of grounding and consistency — at least at this level of understanding, as we haven't gotten to what's behind those two concepts yet... Whatever those nodes are, that's really what all forms of reasoning is [dealing with]."

**The boundary observation.** "The primitive concepts of any category are the boundary between the scope of that topic and the not-that-topic, and in the case of logic, it has to pass itself — that's how you know it's sound (kind of like godel's theorem or whatever)." And when cautioned off Gödel: "'a strong enough formal system provably cannot prove its own consistency from inside' is just one of the horns of the trilemma, is it not?"

**Everything is imagined anyway.** "When we say everything is sourced from imagination vs. reality, we mean something specific — something to do with whatever 'grounding' in reality means. Something to keep in mind."

**The need observation.** The link relation is a kind of need. "If 'necessary'/'need' means something like 'can't exist without' [which is some induction btw], then this is a specific form of that with logic — does it not have a name?"

## 4. Every scenario, sub-question, and counterexample an answer must pass

An answer that fails any of these is not the answer.

1. **Removing the link.** Remove the link between a node and its parent, and show that something significant — like to its truth-value — changes. Alongside it: "Why is there the 'demand that every chain end ... at the level of words'?" and in what significant/necessary sense the parent is a "reference" for the child. Explaining what the link does without this is answering the unnecessary.
2. **The counter that saves PP2** — the standing refutation of the best attempt so far. 3. **The wrinkle.** PP1's own observation list already displays distinct things, and noticing a distinction is itself something that occurs. Any story about what PP1 hands PP2 must account for distinctness already being inside "phenomena" somehow.
4. **The sweep of other starting nodes.** "Why start from PP1?
