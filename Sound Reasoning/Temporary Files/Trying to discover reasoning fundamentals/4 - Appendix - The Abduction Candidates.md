## His point, at its strongest

### Two things that fix the sort, said before any answer

- **What the speaker held.** "Held" in this answer means one thing only: present in the speaker's awareness at the time — his own framing, the line "drawn in awareness, as both obviously occur in reality regardless of awareness". A preferring needs two or more held scopes that fit what was seen; where one was held, no preferring occurred, whatever else exists. The video's speaker held one statement. (The earlier candidate drew this line at speech — rivals "said" or "unsaid" — and supplied the speaker with parrots and kangaroos he had not held. That is dropped.)

Or as a pattern over a label fixed by other properties: "every human walks on two legs", with two legs claimed of every human without helping fix the label (file 0's "rides along" — that gloss is what the phrase means wherever it appears below). Under each reading the step is a different move; both verdicts are given, and which the video meant is his to say.

### The short answer

The video's step is not his abduction, and it is not a new form. As spoken, one statement was held, so no preferring occurred, and the step is a placement. Under the defining-clause reading it is a sound placement under the label as the video fixed it — the fault the second version repairs is the label (everyday "human" is fixed by more than two legs), not the move. Under the pattern reading it is a placement into "two-legged walker" plus an expecting of Polly's unseen human properties: the fit to "two-legged" was noticed, the fit to "human" was not, so the statement outruns its noticing. His feeling that it "sounds like induction" is right by his broad line for induction. And the two uses of "abduction" are two current senses of one word, documented below, not one move seen twice.

### What occurs, below words

File 0's depth is noticing (a fit or sameness present, not willed) against expecting (the held pattern held out toward what has not occurred). Two things are added at that depth, and every sort below rests on them:

- **Expecting has two directions.** Toward the next thing of the kind — the mouth watering at the next red round thing. Or toward the same thing looked at further — the red round thing in hand, sweet expected on biting. Below words, what is awaited is either another thing or more of this thing.
- **Preferring is expecting with rivals present.** Two or more held scopes are present as fitting the same seen things at once, and the expecting attaches to one of them. A rival, throughout, is a second held scope that also fits what was seen. What makes the expecting attach to one is abduction's open criterion; this answer does not close it.

Worded, this gives the moves:

- **Placement** — the properties seen are the ones that fix the label (the ones that make a thing count as a member); the fit is given; no unseen property is awaited. "Polly is a parrot", beak and feet and call seen.
- **Projection** (induction by his refined line) — the pattern held out toward the next member. "The next swan will be white."
- **Expecting turned toward one item** — the pattern held out toward this thing's unseen properties. "Polly is a human" from two legs: her hands, speech and count awaited through the scope. This is the video's step under the pattern reading; it is his induction by his broad line; a philosopher named Peirce called it "hypothesis" (documentation below).
- **Selection** (his abduction) — preferring among held rivals. Among projections: "matter attracts matter everywhere" preferred over "every unsupported thing near the ground falls". Among placements of one item: "parrot" preferred over "human" and "kangaroo" for Polly, when all three are held.

## Mainstream context (documentation only)

- A philosopher named Peirce (1878, "Deduction, Induction, and Hypothesis") introduced the third form under the word "hypothesis", later "abduction" and "retroduction": from a general statement ("all the beans from this bag are white") and a seen result ("these beans are white") to a case ("these beans are from this bag") — the video's shape.
- Peirce's "hypothesis" — an unseen fact that would make the seen fact a matter of course — is his broad line for induction ("an 'unseen' observation that explains those 'seen' observations") nearly word for word; the two vocabularies meet there, not at the word "abduction".
- Peirce's later statement (1903 lectures): a surprising fact C is observed; if A were true, C would follow as a matter of course; so there is reason to suspect A — one guess, put forward for testing.
- Peirce also wrote on choosing among hypotheses — prefer the one cheapest and quickest to test (his "economy of research") — a preference layer one step after the guess.
- Logic textbooks name the video's first step, read as a deduction, "affirming the consequent": taking the "then" part of an if-then (if human, then two-legged) as if it gave back the "if" part — sharing what all members share does not make a thing a member, since others share it too. ### What forces, and what only fits

A move is forced when the region the dot is already in lies wholly inside the circle the child puts it in. Deduction differs from placement in one thing: the child's circle. In placement, the child's circle is one whose fixing features were seen on the dot ("Polly is a bird" — feathers, beak, wings seen on her). ### His "forced induction? or forced statement?" — both, by case

A deduction's child says only what the held containments already fix. When both premises are containments, the child is a containment: "birds lay eggs" and "egg-layers reproduce" put "birds" inside "egg-layers" inside "reproducers", so "birds reproduce" — an "all" over every bird, seen or not, forced. That is a forced projection, which is what his "forced induction" names. So his first option is right when the premises are two containments and his second when one premise is a particular. ### Why it blurs

All four are moves on one picture. They share the circles, the dots, the containment, and the noticing of a fit; they differ in what is done on the picture and in what the child still owes. 1. **Is a circle being drawn around dots and past them, or is a dot being put into a circle already drawn?** Drawn: induction, or abduction of the first shape. Put: placement, deduction, or abduction of the second shape.
2. **Is one statement in view, or several that each fit the seen, one held over the others?** One: induction, placement, deduction. Several: abduction, either shape.
3. **Is the child's circle one whose fixing features were seen on the dot, or one the dot is in only through a held containment?** Seen features: placement. Held containment, no other place for the dot: deduction. Held containment, one inner circle picked among several: abduction of the second shape.

## Mainstream context (documentation only)

- A philosopher named Peirce, in his early work (1860s to 1870s), laid out the three forms as three rearrangements of one three-line argument: deduction (all M are P; S is M; so S is P), induction (S is M; S is P; so all M are P), and what he first called hypothesis and later abduction (all M are P; S is P; so S is M) — the video's shape exactly; documentation that the video's use is a recognized one, not the verdict on it.
- The same philosopher, around 1903, restated abduction as: a surprising fact C is observed; if A were true, C would follow as a matter of course; so there is reason to suspect A — stated openly as a guess owing further test, not as a forced conclusion.
- A philosopher named Harman (1965) coined "inference to the best explanation" for the pick among rival explanations — the preference sense he uses; a philosopher named Lipton wrote the standard book on it (1991).
- Logic textbooks name the video's shape, when read as a deduction, "affirming the consequent" — the recognized shape of a conclusion that does not follow (if human then two-legged; two-legged; so human).
- A philosopher named Kant divided judgment into placing a particular under a held concept and finding the concept for held particulars — the source of the "categorization" pull he felt in the video's example; already in file 0.
- A mathematician named Euler drew containments as nested circles and a logician named Venn drew them as overlapping ones; the picture his own example used is the nested kind.
