## His third message, represented back

He divides two things every statement has. Its context: "the conceptual requirements to understand" it. Its coverage: "what parts of reality it covers". He catches a circle in his own definition (context through understanding, "understanding is the occurrence of meaning, and meaningfulness is concept + context") and notes the thing is right even if the definitions loop. For "day comes after night" he lists context {day, night, chronology, pattern, projection} and coverage {earth, calculations based on physical models, etc.}, and says 'Both are "parents" in a different sense'. He asks what this round answers: what is the context of projection, and what is the "first projection" in the tree, the one "in which its context = coverage", so the sequence of inductions has a first step. He notes that for PP1 "it's context is nothing while its coverage is everything". He proposes that projection "can only be defined after reality is defined", that the nodes from PP1 to "reality is ___" are the first form and the nodes after are induction, and that the first projection may be "reality is the set of all objects", a node in its pipeline, or "that things occur without observation, or more precisely, independent of observation". He closes with the tension's resolution: "induction's children are coverage scopes to the parents, where the parents are the conceptual context to the child, because of the projection mechanism".

## The one picture in everyday words

Every statement in the tree has two lists. The first holds the concepts the statement is built from: the concepts its words label, and the concepts those are built from in turn, down to PP1. For "day comes after every night" that is day, night, after and every. These are its parents, and the tree's links join a node to this list; that is what his "a child is a scoped manifestation of its parents" says. (This reads the links as context only. 02's line that "a node's observations should diversely encompass its children nodes" reads the link through observations as well, so the reading is proposed and waits on his verdict; see Still open.) The second list holds the occurrences the statement is about: last night, the night before, tonight, every night to come. The seen part of this list is the node's Observations. For this sentence no item is on both lists: the concept "night" is not one of the nights the sentence is about, and last night is not needed to understand the sentence (someone who slept through it understands it). The two lists are not two kinds of thing, though. A concept is an awareness, and so an occurrence; his own line puts projections inside PP1's coverage "as those are other awarenesses". The lists are two relations, what must be had to understand against what is spoken of, and one item can be on both. One clean case: "every concept is an awareness" is built from the concepts "concept" and "awareness", and it speaks of the concepts, those two among them, so each of the two is on both of its lists. (The node for "projection" covers projectings, and none of those is in its context, so it is not a case of sharing.) A different thing, and not a shared member: at the node that grants a new concept, 02 has the new label "attached to something there, shown rather than defined in other words" ("distinct" labels the difference between red and hot itself), so the Observation the label is shown on must be had to possess the concept; that Observation is an occurrence and not a concept, so it is on the coverage list only, and it is not a parent node: PP2's parent is PP1. His tension came from "parents" being used for both lists. Every node, PP1 included, is within its concepts and around its observations; induction adds one thing: its second list goes past what was seen, taking the cases not yet seen to be like those seen. So the four forms of reasoning differ only in how the second list stands to the cases already seen. The first form of reasoning says only what was seen; induction says it of unseen cases too; deduction passes what holds of a whole set to a case already inside it and adds no unseen case; abduction prefers between two patterns that fit everything seen. The line between seen and unseen lives in the coverage list, between the Observations and the rest, and is drawn in awareness, not in reality (file 0: a case can happen unobserved; reality holds no sign separating observed from unobserved).

His own reading in file 1 of what is new in a child, that the child's new concept is already within the parent, "just not [yet] exposed or defined", with "all of what occurs exists in PP1, and the children nodes narrow it down", comes out as right for one list and wrong for the other when taken list by list. Right for coverage: every occurrence under "will occurs" was already an occurrence under PP1, so the child's coverage was inside the parent's all along. Wrong for context: the concept "will" is not inside the concept "phenomena"; it is a new awareness, gathered in the child's Observations, and only then placed within the concepts already had. What the step he calls exposing adds is that awareness, and that is the thing new at each node. His box in file 1 says the same once the two lists are kept apart: "something is in the box", said of a far-off blur, and "an apple is in the box", said after walking closer, share a coverage (whatever is in the box) and differ in context by one concept, "apple"; the new thing is the awareness of the apple, which agrees with his own line there that 'the "newness" of the second isn't really a new word'.

Three things more. First, the projected part is not only the "every": when "gravity" is made the label for what makes the apple fall, a feeling from inside us (my will moving my body, my sense of effort) is put into an outside case where none is felt. That transfer is a second kind of unseen; it is in his first message and in file 4's item on what is projected, and all six threads dropped it. Second, "unseen" itself has two kinds: a case not yet seen (tomorrow's night), and a case that cannot be seen at all (the wall while no one looks). "Every" can cover both, as "every night" covers the nights no one is awake for, and "while I am not aware of it" speaks of the second only; the two are not one word in longer form, and the missing node below is of the second kind. Third, the apple's two traces (file 2's first test, the tension dissolved with labeled examples). Downward, by context: "the apple falls" is built from "apple" and "falls", "falls" from "moves" and "toward the ground", and so on down to "occurs", PP1, whose context holds no concept. Upward, by coverage: "the apple falls" is one occurrence in the coverage of "things fall" (on that node's Observations list, not among its parents, which answers the puzzle he left in file 2), "things fall" is one case in the coverage of "masses attract", and each wider statement explains the narrower one, the climb ending at the widest so far, which has no explanation of its own yet. His suspicion that the pedagogical order and the tree's order are the same sorting is right: a topic is explained by the concepts it is built from, which is its context list, and the tree's parent links are that list; teaching may also climb coverage (the apple, then things fall, then masses attract), which the tree does not do.

## His questions, each answered

In his order, his words quoted.

1. "Context is the conceptual requirements to understand (wait this is circular ...), while coverage is what parts of reality it covers - is that right?" Right in the division, with the circle dissolved by order of definition: context = the concepts already had that a statement is built from, leaving out the one new concept its own node grants on an Observation (no appeal to understanding; the words list has the full line); meaning = concept + context (his line); understanding = that meaning occurring in someone (his "understanding = occurrence of meaning", read so). His aside "although, ontologically, this is still correct" is right: only the definitions were in the wrong order. One audit is owed, whether "built from" picks the same concepts as his "conceptual requirements to understand"; on "day comes after every night" they agree. One repair to "what parts of reality it covers": coverage is occurrences, not "reality", since "reality" is a later node and a statement can have a coverage no occurrence has filled outside imagination. "Flying pigs exist" is understood completely, since flying, pig and exist are all understood; the imagined pig is seen, but in imagination, and the statement says the pig exists in reality; understanding lives in context, truth in coverage.

2. "each node has a context and a coverage - context is the scope of forming concepts together, and coverage is the scope of that concept representing reality." Right: each node has both, and the seen part of coverage is already on its page as Observations. He uses "scope" for both sides, so "context" and "coverage" are what keep them apart.

3. His lists for "day comes after night": context {day, night, chronology, pattern, projection [this is required - without it, it wouldn't be an induction]}, coverage {earth, calculations based on physical models, etc.}. Two catches. "Projection" does not belong there: a child understands the sentence with no concept of projection, as PP2's infant tells "strawberry" from "ouch" with no concept of distinction. What makes the statement an induction is a quantity word in its content, "every", read as speaking of nights not in awareness. An "every" or "any" can also be a label over whichever occurrences there are, as PP1's "anything that occurs" is (his PP1 remark, item 7 below), so which reading a given "every" has is the open item on the two readings of "every" in Still open; the catch on his list is only that the member is a quantity word, not the word "projection". "Chronology" is "after" in a longer word. "Pattern" is the seen recurrence itself, the first form row's own child (the awareness of the pattern), so it belongs in the context without making the statement an induction; only "every" does that. Projecting is the step, done before it has a word. And {earth, calculations based on physical models} is not the coverage: the coverage is the nights followed by day, seen and unseen. "The earth turns" is a wider statement that explains the dawns (the apple's upward trace); the calculations are tests; each is a statement with lists of its own.

4. 'Both are "parents" in a different sense'. Context items are parent nodes (the tree's links). Coverage items are not parent nodes in any sense: PP2's parent is PP1, not "red is not hot", which is in PP2's Observations. But every tree node grants one new concept shown on an Observation (02), and that Observation must also be had to possess the concept, so his "different sense" is right for tree nodes and wrong only for extensions whose concepts are all already had, like "day comes after every night": forget last night and the sentence is still understood, one observation thinner; forget "night" and it cannot be understood at all. This also answers the counter that saves PP2 (file 1's second test): tracing "phenomena" to what occurs saves PP2's truth, as it saves PP1's, but the link to PP1 holds PP2's understanding, not its truth; "distinct" with "phenomena" forgotten is "not like another what?", so the save that gives PP2 its truth does not give it its meaning. Where a coverage item is needed to understand, it is the Observation the new label is shown on, and even there it is not a parent node.

5. 'What is the context of projection? Like what is the "first projection" ... in which its context = coverage (otherwise, how would we avoid the regress problem here? is this the right way to think about this, like what to look for?)' The context of the concept "projection", when its node is written (late, where reasoning itself becomes the subject): occurrence, awareness, "not in awareness", pattern, same; none is "projection", and its coverage is the projectings, which occur (expecting is seen). No induction has "projection" in its context, so the sequence does not need itself to start; it looked as if it did only because the step had been put into the content. "Context = coverage" is not what to look for, though not because the lists are of different kinds: a concept is an awareness and so an occurrence, and a statement about reasoning has the occurring of concepts in its coverage, so the two lists are two relations, what must be had to understand and what is spoken of, and one item can be on both (the clean case in the one picture above). Equality is still not what makes a first: PP1, the agreed first, has no concept in its context and every occurrence in its coverage (label sense). What let PP1 be first is a context holding none of the form's own kind: PP1's context holds no concept; the first projection's holds no concept that was itself formed by projection. That is what "first projection" means (no ancestor projects), so it is not a test to apply but two things to show: that the candidate's Definition speaks of an occurrence no Observation can display, and that each concept in its context was formed without doing so; in a branching tree it picks a first on its branch, not the one first. His "regress problem" takes file 4's verdict: say "at the first step of the sequence", and keep "regress" for the skeptic's problem (the old skeptic named Agrippa, documented in the Common Biases reference, 04).

6. "if there are better names for them, it may be wise to use them". Keep both words. One collision needs his verdict first: his line "context = the scope of observations the statement is about", which file 0 records as ratified in thread 2 of the old sequence, uses "context" for what he now calls coverage, and 04's heading "Exporting a claim beyond its context" does the same (Newton's physics true within everyday speeds is a region of cases). If he wants two words with no history, one thread offered "parts" and "cases". Mainstream philosophy has had a similar pair since the 1600s (comprehension and extension, later intension and extension; 02's bullet on the two containments already has "extension and intension vary inversely"); recorded for reference, with no say in the verdict.

7. "for PP1, it's context is nothing while its coverage is everything - including projections (as those are other awarenesses)". Right, and it is the case that shows the two lists as far apart as they get at the first node; right too that a projecting is itself an occurrence, so PP1 covers it, which is why the lists are told apart by relation and not by kind. One thing under this needs his verdict: PP1's "anything that occurs" is a label applying to whichever occurs and projects no unseen case, while "every night" says that nights no one has in awareness are a certain way. "Coverage" is being used in both senses, and the line between the first form and induction rests on the difference (see Still open).

8. '"projection" can only be defined after reality is defined ... the set of nodes from "reality is ___" to "<some node>" corresponds with induction'. Defining projection comes late; doing it does not, and it comes before "reality is ___" (next item). The picture of stretches (one form of reasoning per stretch of the tree) is wrong: forms are steps usable at every node once their own node exists. After "reality" the tree still has first form steps (a seen pattern labeled over the drops watched, "falling", projecting no unseen case; "gravity" is not the example, since labeling the cause puts a feeling from inside us into the outside case, which projects), and deduction's pattern is already in use at PP1 (its Proof by Contradiction: "supposing" is inside "occurring"; the Derivation Rules reference, 02, already places deduction there, "inside a node, as the check's tool — not between nodes"). What is right in it: the first long stretch uses one step only, so a stretch of the tree looked like a form.

9. 'Perhaps the "reality is ____" *is* that first projection? ... "object is a structure with nature" <-- "structure is set of limits" & "nature is set of features for a limit" <-- "limit is what is independent of want". Are any of these the first projections?' None of those listed. "Limit is what is independent of want" is seen: I want the wall gone and it stays (with file 0's caveat that nightmares and intrusive images also come unwanted, so this alone does not show a limit is outside imagination). It is read here as a label applied case by case (this stayed when I wanted it gone, so this is a limit), not as "stays across every wanting", which would already project over wantings not yet had; if he reads it the second way, the first projection is the limit node itself. "Structure", "nature" and "object" are first form steps built from seen limits and features. A node is missing: "a limit is also there when I am not aware of it", branching from "limit" beside "structure" and "nature", before "reality". "Reality is the set of all objects" stands on it rather than being it, if its "all" speaks of objects no one sees; if its "all" is only the label applying to whichever objects there are, as PP1's "anything that occurs" does, it projects no unseen case. Either way the missing node is the tree's first projection, on the conditions stated in its own section; whether "reality" projects is open.

10. "Could the first projection be that things occur without observation, or more precisely, independent of observation? This does sound like the potential foundation for all possible inductions/projections". Yes in the tree sense, specified as the missing node. It is a projection: it covers the cup during the gap when no one saw it, and the gap showed no cup. "Foundation for all possible inductions" is right for natural laws (they say the pattern holds where no one looks, ever, the second kind of unseen) and for any "every" that covers cases no one sees (the nights no one is awake for, in the induction row), and not for the bare expecting ("day will come after this night too" speaks of a case that will be seen, the first kind, and does not need the missing node); whether the expecting already uses a concept of an occurrence outside awareness is for him to look at in his own awareness.

11. "the resolution to the tension is here: induction's children are coverage scopes to the parents, where the parents are the conceptual context to the child, because of the projection mechanism". Right in outline, two repairs. The child is a scope around its observations, not its parents: "day comes after every night" is around the nights, not around the concepts "day" and "night". And "because of the projection mechanism" is misplaced: every node is a scope around its observations (PP2 around "red is not hot", with no projection; file 0 said this first, "neither containment direction separates the forms"); projection only extends the boundary past the cases already seen.

From his first message, the items this round touches (file 4 already has the rest): 2.2, the tension between a child inside its parents and a child around them, dissolved above; 3.1, whether the principles of the forms are derived before the forms can be defined ("in parallel or intertwined"), and 4.1, the rival deduction pattern, both answered under the principles section; his "regress level", file 4's verdict.

## The first projection: two senses, and the node the pipeline is missing

First in a life: the bare expecting that the next will be like the last (the mouth watering at the next red round thing; "day will come after this night too" before any word for it). It comes unwilled, as distinguishing does. Its context, as far as anyone has listed it: the concepts "this", "again" and "not yet". Whether one of those, or "want", is itself a concept formed by projection is open and only he can look; if one is, the tree node below is first in both senses. The expecting speaks of a case that will be seen (the first kind of unseen), so it does not need the tree node below, which speaks of the second kind.

First in the tree: the first node whose Definition speaks of an occurrence no Observations entry can display. His candidate, specified: "a limit is also there when I am not aware of it". Walked:
- Below it, "limit is what is independent of want": seen. Observations: I want the wall gone, it stays; I want the pain gone, it stays. Coverage = Observations. File 0's caveat: nightmares and intrusive images come unwanted too, so this node alone does not show a limit is outside imagination. "Independent of want" is read here as a label applied case by case (this stayed when I wanted it gone, so this is a limit), not as "stays across every wanting", which would already project over wantings not yet had; if he reads it the second way, the first projection is the limit node itself.
- The missing node. Context: limit, want, awareness, "not", each from the first form, none formed by projection. Why "not aware" is not itself a projected concept: it is the seen gap, noticed afterward. ## The principles behind the forms, and where each stops
