## His derivation, represented back

Deduction passes truth along and makes none of its own — "d_m = continues the truth-value" — so the truth in any chain of deductions was put in at the first premises by something that is not deduction (D1), and the patterns deduction follows came from somewhere that is not deduction either (D2, D3). Two working assumptions, to be paid for later by whether the answers hang together: everything starts from what is not yet it ("chickens from an a-chicken"), reasoning included; and truth tracks reality. Every piece of reasoning has one shape, "R = o_x --[m_x]--> c_x": something observed, a step, a conclusion. The first premise is made from observations by a step t_m; a pattern of deduction is itself a statement, so the same t_m makes it from some observations o_d_m; and whatever sorts the right pattern from the wrong one is part of t_m, since sorting truth from falsehood is t_m's job. Opened up, t_m has four parts: concepts occur from what occurs, unwilled (c_m, "intellectual causality"); a concept is tied to reality by projecting what is felt onto the patterns observed, with reality as "the set of all occurrences that occur independent of will/want" (v_m); among grounded rivals the one more consistent with reality is kept, since "competing" already needs consistency (p_p_m); and the person accepts or refuses (p_a_m). So "t_m = c_m --> v_m --> p_p_m --> p_a_m", and what remains is to say which observations the patterns are read off, which pattern comes first, and how the rest are traced and kept consistent. The standard throughout: "anyone can do it, and we should all reach the same conclusion".

## The breaks, and the repair for each

1. **Acceptance inside the truth step (T3).** "You decide what you accept as 'truth'" is inside the step he calls "the source of truth for all deductions", so what deduction receives is what was accepted: an accepted falsehood becomes a first premise and a refused truth does not. The fault is p_a_m's place, against his own T1 line "this is different than choosing to accept or deny what you end up recognizing - we're ignoring this bit for now" — not a contradiction in his words (his T2 says what is real is independent of will, and his p_a_m line puts "truth" in quotes). On the strict reading of his principle in 02 — a method that justifies a falsehood cannot tell true from false — this is the method the principle forbids; which reading he meant is file 5's item 15, his verdict. Example: PP1 lists "[thought] 'the earth is flat'"; accept it, and its truth-value is as it was; two people with the same look could end at two truths, against "we should all reach the same conclusion". Repair: p_a_m moves outside t_m; accepting fixes what is held, not what is true.
2. **Reality defined by want (T2).** "And this is self-consistent with what I discovered earlier, in that 'reality' can only be defined as 'the set of all occurrences that occur independent of will/want'" repeats a line already refuted (file 2 §5) and leans on it as support. Example: on the case by case reading the test counts the nightmare's tiger in — it comes unwanted, and there is no tiger — and the willed arm out; whether the "across every wanting" reading stands is file 5's open item, closed by the draft of reality's own node and his verdict on the reading. T3's "more consistent with reality" inherits whatever that verdict gives. Repair: "what occurs" stands in for "reality" in the pipeline until that verdict; the definition is not used as support meanwhile.
3. **Meaning in truth's slot (T2).** "Grounding in phenomenological projection" tells whether a concept is traced to what occurs — his own "context"; 02 asks a node to be both understood and true, and grounding gives the first — not whether a statement is true. Example: the flying pig is grounded (pig and flying both occur) and "flying pigs exist" is false; his energy sentence says energy "can only be described and understood" by comparing it to felt energy — understood, not found true; and PP1 and PP2 are true by the look with no projection anywhere, so "this is actually what my attempt at the phenomenological tree is doing" is against 02's "Truth comes from looking, at every node separately". Repair: v_m is the look — a statement's truth read off the Observations it refers to — and projection moves to induction's node.
4. **A truth-value on a concept, and no step that makes a statement (S10, S14).** "t_m both assigns truth-value to some concept" — and the T2 paragraph itself says both "you can have both true and false concepts" and "phenomena/qualia aren't 'true' or 'false' - they just are". No contradiction in his words: the T2 line is about qualia, the o_x, and his "concept" is declared to cover deductive and inductive conclusions ("some_observations can be any kind of concept [deductive, inductive, something else, whatever]"). Example: "red" is neither true nor false, "red occurs" is one or the other, and D1 asked for a premise, which is a statement — "red" alone shows the gap. Repair: a step that joins concepts into a statement, between c_m and v_m; truth-values live there; p_p_m becomes the denial test and is s_m.

Two smaller faults that do not change the result: "iff" (S1) claims truth passes backward too — "all dogs are cats; all cats are animals; so all dogs are animals" — and "The latter is easier to deal with" (S9) is convenience offered as a reason, where the reason at hand is that the rival falls to the test every statement gets.

## D2 — what the patterns of deduction are read off

o_d_m are occurrences among statements one holds, seen the way red is seen — not cases in reality, and not a model of reality. One example throughout, every element labeled.

Three balls in a bag. Look one: ball 1 is red. Look two: ball 2 is red. Look three: ball 3 is red. These three looks are o_x. "Every ball in this bag is red" is a first premise, d_a: the three looks said together, covering no unseen ball, so no projection is in it. A ball is drawn; look four: it came from the bag — the second d_a, "this is one of them". The step d_m gives d_c: "so this ball is red". The bag is chosen so that "every ball is red" is three looks said together and containment is itself a look. Whether the bag's step is a deduction or a placement read off a gathered statement is file 5's open item ("Placement from a definition against deduction"), left as file 5 has it.

Three kinds of o_d_m, all in the bag:

- **Statements occur.** PP1 lists "[thought] 'the earth is flat'" among what occurs, so a held statement is an observation in the sense o_x needs.
- **Containment seen.** "Every ball is red" was made of three looks, and one of them was the look at this very ball, so "this ball is red" was already among the parts of the premise before the step; the conclusion was inside the premise (02's "something is in the box" and "a red ball is in the box"). This is what the pattern "what is said of every member is said of each member" is read off.
- **The clash.** Hold "every ball is red", "this is one of them" and "this ball is not red" together: the clash occurs, unwilled, and in no look was a ball both red and not red. This is what the excluding "not" is read off — PP2's "'The earth is flat' is not 'The earth is round'" sharpened to "at most one holds".

"If P leads to Q and P, then Q" is then no more than: Q was already said when "P leads to Q" and P were held, and denying Q is holding Q and not-Q together.

Where the pattern adds something the look did not give: in "every swan is white" the "every" is projected, "this swan is white" was in no look, and the containment is in the word "every" — that is what the pattern adds beyond the look. So the pattern is read off the bag, where it adds no case, and is used on the swans, where it does.

Denial test. Say the patterns are read off something else. Off cases in reality: then the pattern is about balls and does not cover swans — yet the same pattern forces "this swan is white" from "every swan is white" and "this is a swan"; what is shared is only the statements and their containment. Chosen: "...then not-Q" is as good a pick, but hold it with its own first clause and the clash occurs — the clash drops it. Deduced: a deduction needs the pattern first (02: usable only downstream of its node).

Relation to the standing candidates. His first message already had the material — "various 'true statements' and 'false statements'" — and this makes it specific: what occurs when statements are held together. File 4's "the rival contradicts its own premise" and file 5's "every rival deduction pattern contradicts its own first line" are the clash seen on the rival. 02 line 42 asks for the patterns to be "noticed in how reasoning itself behaves". His S8 route is right; what it needs is the statement step his c_m --> v_m skipped. The only new piece in this D2 answer is the bag. The nodes it waits on — statement, the excluding "not", true, false, every and each, "leads to" — are unwritten (files 4 and 5).

## D3 — the first pattern, and how the patterns are grounded and kept consistent

Candidates, the bag on each: (a) the excluding "not" — of a statement and its denial, at most one holds; (b) what is said of every member is said of each member; (c) "P leads to Q" means every case of P is inside Q. (b) and (c) are one pattern under two wordings — a reading new in this answer: "every ball from the bag is red" and "being from the bag leads to being red" are built from the same looks, give the same conclusion, and break the same way under denial — so by his own same-example test there are two candidates.

**The first d_m_x is (a).** It is his own line twice: "'reasoning ... must be self-consistent' is the more primitive" (first message) and "the underlying form of any logical consistency is self-consistency" (T3). The standing candidate (files 4 and 5) puts it earlier in the order of need than his principle that a method which justifies a falsehood cannot tell true from false, since "tells true from false" already needs true and false to exclude each other.

Denial test on (a): "a statement and its denial can both hold." But "its denial" means the statement that says it does not hold; if both hold, the second left the first standing, so it was not a denial. The denial of (a) says there is a denial that does not deny — it contradicts itself in its own words. This is the second kind of ending, not PP1's: stating "a statement and its denial can both hold" is not holding S and not-S together, so no clash occurs in the stating; the clash is (a)'s Observation, not the fate of (a)'s denial. Whether this ending counts as firmly as PP1's is file 4's and file 5's open item, and the standing candidate makes it turn on whether "denial" is attached to an observed pattern or is a chosen word use — his verdict.

What (a)'s test uses. "If it were a denial, S would not hold; S holds; so it was not a denial" is a step of (b)/(c) rearranged, plus (a) for "holds and does not hold cannot both be". So the first pattern is defended by itself and by a step pattern — the test uses what it tests, as PP1's test also does when it uses "occurs" to watch its own denial, and this is the thing his question "is it deduction, in that we use deduction to justify deduction?" asks about; it is said here rather than hidden. Denial test on (b): "every ball is red; this is one of them; this is not red" — "every ball" was said over this ball, so the denial says of one ball both "red" and "not red", which (a) forbids. Each pattern's test uses the other. (a) is put first not because its test stands alone but because "competing" (his T3) and "tells true from false" already need "not both". (a) forbids a pair rather than stepping to a new statement; the first pattern that is a step is (b), and the later step patterns are (b) and (a) rearranged (PP1's Proof by Contradiction has the shape "every case of P is inside Q" read from the outside).

Truth from the look — at the clash for (a), at the premise's parts for (b) — with no projection: a pattern of deduction makes no claim about balls and holds for false statements too ("all fish fly; whatever flies is an animal; so all fish are animals" is a good deduction with a false premise), so reality does not enter, and his D3 wording "consistent with reality" is aimed wrong; "consistent with itself" is the test. **How they are consistent:** by (a) applied to the set of patterns — no pattern may deny another or itself. That his principle (a method which justifies a falsehood cannot tell true from false) is (a) applied to methods is a proposed reading, on the strict reading of that principle (file 5's item 15, his verdict).

**Is s_m deduction?** No, and — on the strict reading of his principle, a proposed reading — not a selection among standing rivals either. The rival to (b) — "every ball is red; this is one of them; so this is not red" — is not removed by a deduction from (b), which would be the circle he feared ("we use deduction to justify deduction"), and not by weighing it as an option; it is removed by a look at what its own premise is made of — which is (b)'s content: "every ball is red" was made of the look at this ball, so the rival's conclusion denies a part of its own premise, which (a) forbids. So s_m is the denial test, inside t_m as S9 concluded by the wrong reason. File 4: a preferring "needs two things present", and once stated the rival is not present as a candidate. Denial test on "s_m is deduction": then the first pattern was picked by a deduction whose pattern was picked by a deduction — a sequence with no first step, which his own first assumption refuses. Denial test on "s_m is a selection among standing rivals": a rival that stands has not been produced; his own "if P leads to Q, then if P is true, then Q is false" does not; on the weaker reading of his principle a preference among the principles may still be needed (file 5's open item).

**What (a) does not do:** it removes rivals that deny themselves or a seen case; it does not pick between gravity and invisible pixies, which both fit every seen fall — abduction's criterion stays open as files 4 and 5 have it, and T3 did not close it. Applied to itself, this answer passes his test: the method applied to its own patterns uses only a look, (a), and a step of (b), and none of these denies itself.
