# The answer that started from his third message: the context-against-coverage distinction

## Its one page

**What you said, at its strongest.** Each node has two lists: its context — the concepts you must already have to understand it — and its coverage — the occurrences it is true of. For PP1 the first list is bare and the second is everything. The first form puts a child under its parent in the context sense: no understanding "distinct" without "phenomena". Induction's child covers more than its parents. You propose there is no contradiction: induction's parents are its context, its child is a wider coverage, and projection is why. You ask for the first projection, and whether "things occur independent of observation" is it.

**The picture.** Your two lists are right, and they pass your own test: at PP1 one list has no member and the other has every occurrence, so they cannot be one relation under two names (the fallen "scope of a concept / scope of a claim" had the same members at every node). On one example: the concept "day" is in the context of "day comes after every night" and not in its coverage; last night's dawn is in its coverage and not in its context. The two containments passage in 02 is the same picture in other words: "what is said" is context, "what is allowed" is coverage. Keep both names; one repair: 04's "Exporting a claim beyond its context" uses "context" for what you now call coverage.

Every statement has the two lists. Its context is always built one way — understood through its parents, your first form, at work inside every other form. The four forms differ only in how the coverage stands to what was looked at:

- **Description** (the first form; its move: describing) — one more feature said of the occurrences already looked at; coverage within the look. "Day and night occur."
- **Induction** (projection) — coverage past the look. "Day comes after every night" — tonight's included.
- **Abduction** (selection) — rival projections over the same seen cases differ in coverage elsewhere; look there; the rival whose coverage holds a counterexample is cut. "The sun circles the earth" against "the earth turns": the long pendulum's swing turns, so the first is cut.
- **Deduction** (inclusion, not intersection) — what is inside an inner coverage is inside the outer. "Every night is followed by a day" + "it is night" → "a day will follow".

**Your tension.** "Parent" was covering two lists. A child is a scoped manifestation of its parents *in context* — true of every form. Induction's child is a scope around its parents *in coverage* — true of projection alone. Different lists, so no clash; your resolution holds once "parents" is split: the observations are in the child's coverage, the concepts in its context.

**Your four areas.**
1. Observations: agreed — they happen unwanted; no false awareness. Falsehood begins at description: "flying pigs are in the sky" puts an imagined occurrence under a coverage it was not in. An observation's context is bare and its coverage is itself — PP1 in the small.
2. Inductions come from descriptions by projection; the doing of induction is itself an occurrence (your white birds), so PP1 covers it. The first projection is the bare expectation "the next will be like these" — it needs only likeness and sequence, and an infant has it before any word. Its denial, "the next will not be like these", is itself a projection; that is where the regress stops. "Independent of observation" is a projection and the first one about reality — every law borrows it — but it needs "awareness" and "independent", so it is not first. "Independent of want" is seen (the pain stays when I want it gone): a description.
3. Abductions: selection by counterexample. Its first principle is your own line — a method that justifies a falsehood cannot tell true from false — which is not the first induction (its context needs method, truth, falsehood) but the first principle of selection, held by its denial breaking, not by a track record: a description. Where no differing look exists yet, both rivals stand; preference is not a verdict.
4. Deductions: the principle "inside stays inside" is a description — try to draw a case inside P, P inside Q, and outside Q. Your rival system changes what "leads to" means. The premises are inductions, so your regress ending at inductions is right for them; the principle ends at a look. Your bachelor example goes the wrong way: "bachelors are unmarried" + "John is unmarried" does not give "John is a bachelor" — John may be a widower. Valid: "John is a bachelor" → "John is unmarried".

**The video.** Its "abduction" — "humans walk on two legs; Polly does; so Polly is human" — is the same shape as your bachelor error: guessing an item's label from a shared feature. A philosopher named Peirce called that abduction; later writers use the word for picking the best explanation, your sense. Here the video's move is a projection onto one item (claiming Polly's unseen chromosomes from her seen legs); your abduction sorts such guesses. The chromosome version is deduction (Polly lacks a defining feature) and description (John shows them all).

**"Context = coverage".** It cannot hold literally — one list is concepts, the other occurrences. What you are after, in your first message's words, is an induction that "would satisfy itself": a statement whose coverage holds its own saying, so its denial falls inside it. That is PP1's test, and it is what to look for.

**In the tree:** Inherits From is context; Observations and Eliminates are coverage; "use ancestral terms" and "observations encompass the children" are your two lists, already in 02. No node yet covers past the look — which is why you see no induction.

**Confirm or veto:** (1) "description" as the first form's word and move; (2) the tension dissolved by splitting "parents" into the two lists; (3) "the next will be like these" as the first projection, with your falsehood principle moved to abduction.

- "The parents are the conceptual context to the child" in the resolution: in his own first message the parents of an induction are observations (the white birds, the falling apples), and an observation is in the child's coverage, not its context — last night's dawn is covered by "day comes after every night", and someone who slept through it understands the sentence. - "Context = coverage" cannot hold literally — one list is concepts, the other occurrences, and at PP1 one is bare and the other total. What he is after is what his first message said better: a statement that "would satisfy itself" — its coverage holds its own saying, so the denial falls inside it.
- His candidate first induction — a method that justifies a falsehood cannot tell true from false — needs method, reasoning, justify, true and false in its context, so it cannot be phenomenologically first; it cuts rivals, so it is abduction's first principle; and it is held by its denial breaking, not by a track record, so it is a description rather than an induction.
- "Independent of observation" as the first projection: it is a projection and the first one about reality, but the bare expectation "the next will be like these" needs fewer concepts and an infant has it before any word (his own strawberry example in PP2). And "independent of want" is seen (the pain stays when wanted gone), so his limit line is a description, not a projection.
- In the day/night example, "calculations based on physical models" are concepts (or rival inductions), so they belong to context, not coverage; the coverage of "day comes after night" is the dawns.
- "The child is a forced induction" for deduction: the conclusion adds no unseen case beyond what the premises projected, so his other option, "forced statement", is the right one.
- His wish to define projection only after "reality is ___": expecting happens before "reality" is a concept, so the node for projection fits earlier; what waits for "reality" is projection about standing structure, like "independent of observation".

## Its short dictionary

- scope — A boundary drawn around a set of occurrences (his word).
- coverage — The occurrences a concept or statement is true of.
- context — The concepts a concept or statement is understood through.
- meaning — Concept + context (his ratified line).
- understanding — The occurrence of a meaning (his line); the recursion ends because context runs down to concepts with no context — PP1's, the infant's "ouch" — which are had by a look, not through other concepts.
- explain — To place something inside a wider scope it fits: a concept inside its context ("distinct" inside "phenomena"), an occurrence inside a wider coverage ("the apple fell" inside "things fall").
- grounded — Every concept in a statement traced down its context to looks, and its stated coverage holding the occurrence it is said of.
- source — Where the occurrence a statement is said of happened — sight, imagination, memory.
- trace — To walk a statement's context down to the looks its concepts came from.
- warrant — What holds a statement's coverage: a look (description), seen cases with no counterexample (induction), a counterexample in the rival (selection), the premises' coverages (inclusion).
- justification — A warrant written down.
- need — A concept needs another when the other is in its context — it cannot be understood without it.
- consistency — No occurrence both inside and outside one stated coverage.
- deniability — A statement is deniable when its opposite can be said without doing what it denies; PP1 and the first projection are not.
- correspondence — A description's stated coverage holding the occurrence it is said of.
- observation — A distinct awareness (his line); context bare, coverage itself.
- description — A new label for a feature the occurrences already looked at show — coverage within the look.
- projection — Coverage past the look — saying of occurrences not looked at what the seen ones showed.
- selection — Cutting a rival projection when a look lands in its coverage against it.
- inclusion — What is inside an inner coverage is inside the outer.
- counterexample — An occurrence inside a statement's coverage that does not show what the statement says of it.
- fundamental property — A feature that fixes a label's coverage.
- superficial property — A feature projected onto a label's cases from the seen ones.

# The answer that started from where the principles of deduction and abduction come from

## Its one page

**Your points, at their strongest.** Observations happen in awareness and cannot be picked; projections happen unbidden too, drawing a scope around what was seen; abduction prefers between rival projections; deduction forces what the preferred scopes already contain. Below all four is one first form — a child is a scoped manifestation of its parents — and the tension is that induction's child is a scope *around* its parents. Your third message gives each node a context (the concepts needed to understand it) and a coverage (the occurrences it is about), and asks for the first projection — perhaps "things occur independent of observation" — looking for a place where context equals coverage so the regress ends.

**The one picture.** Every form of reasoning places a new scope (everything a label fits) against scopes already had. The first form draws it *inside* a parent's: **narrowing** ("white" inside "color" inside "quality"). Induction pushes it *past* its source: **projection** ("all swans are white", from the white swans seen). Deduction passes what covers an outer scope down to what is inside it: **nesting** (this bird inside swans, swans inside white, so this bird is white). Abduction keeps one of two projections that both fit the seen: **preference**. One principle is not a projection and tests all the rest — yours: a method that justified a falsehood has shown its stamp does not sort true from false. That is what "tells true from false" *means*, unpacked — the first form, not an induction. Its denial ("it stamped the false one true, and it sorts") says and unsays one thing.

**1. Observations.** Agreed: they occur, are not picked, and no awareness is false. Falseness first appears when a label is placed on an occurrence it does not fit — so the first form is already at work in every labeled observation.

**2. Inductions.** The move is not reasoned to; it occurs, like noticing — your white birds "defined as white". So the first induction is not a principle but expectation: a label held open for an occurrence that has not occurred yet ("again"), needing only likeness, before/after, and want. Your principle is not an induction: a counterexample already seen pushes onto no unseen case. The tension dissolves once "parents" is divided: the induction's *concept* parents (swan, white, all) contain it in understanding; its *source* (the swans seen) is contained by its coverage. No one thing is both inside and around.

**3. Abductions.** At the root there is no preference to make: the rival to your principle cannot be held, and a projection with a counterexample is removed by the principle, with no rival weighed. Preference starts only where two projections fit every seen case — "this black bird is no swan" against "swans are white or black" — and its principles are projections about projections ("features that stay together define a label; color rides along"; the bird breeds with swans, so the second). Each is testable like any projection. So the regress has a shape: every preference principle is a projection; every projection is tested by the one principle that is not; that principle is a meaning read down to occurrences. There is no first abduction.

**4. Deductions.** "P leads to Q, P, so Q" is neither induction nor deduction: it is what "leads to" means — every case of P is inside Q. The perverse system, "so Q is false", denies its own first line ("this bird is inside swans, swans are inside white, so it is outside white"), or it renames "leads to" and is ordinary nesting under another word. Your bachelor example does not follow: "bachelors are unmarried" and "john is unmarried" put both inside "unmarried"; sharing a container is not nesting — john may be an unmarried woman. Valid: john inside bachelors, bachelors inside unmarried, so john unmarried. That is why the mechanism is nesting, not intersection.

## Its short dictionary

- scope — The boundary a label draws: where it fits and where it stops.
- coverage — The occurrences inside a label's scope, seen or not ("white" covers this cloud, that swan, and every white thing).
- context — The concepts a concept is understood through ("white" needs "color" and "quality").
- source — The occurrences a statement was read off (the N white swans).
- meaning — Concept + context (his line, kept).
- understanding — The occurrence of a meaning — it recurses through context and ends at a concept that labels an occurrence directly, whose context has no items (PP1).
- explain — To give a concept's context, or the wider scope an occurrence falls inside ("apples fall" explained by "things fall").
- grounded — Understood down to occurrences (context complete) and read off occurrences (source given); the flying pig has the first and lacks the second where its stated coverage is reality.
- warrant / justification — A statement's source and the move that placed it, laid out — one job, two words.
- reference — What a label refers to: the occurrences it covers; a parent is a reference for a child only as context.
- need — A needs B for understanding when B is in A's context; for truth when A's coverage is inside B's.
- true / false — A label placed where the occurrence is inside its scope / outside it.
- narrowing — The first form's move: a label drawn inside what a parent already covers.
- projection — Induction's move: a label's coverage pushed past its source to occurrences that have not occurred.
- expectation — A label held open for an occurrence that has not occurred yet; the first projection.
- nesting — Deduction's move: a scope inside a scope, so what covers the outer covers the inner.
- preference — Abduction's move: keeping one of two projections that both fit every seen case.
- consistency — No statement both stamped and unstamped by one method — his principle where the falsehood is exposed by the method's own output.
- deniability — Whether a statement's opposite can be held without breaking itself; the test of a node, not what makes it true — the occurrences do.
- trace — To follow a concept's context down to occurrences, or a statement's source back to them.
- correspondence — A label's scope and an occurrence coinciding — the everyday word for a true placement.

- explain — to show a wider scope whose coverage holds the thing explained ("things fall" explains "the apple fell")
- seen / unseen — occurred in awareness / not occurred in awareness (yet, or ever)
- fitting — a new awareness seen as one way its parents occur (the first form's move)
- projection — drawing a scope's coverage past what has occurred — the unseen taken to be like the seen (induction's move)
- preference — keeping one of several scopes that fit the same occurrences (abduction's move)
- transfer — what holds of a scope holds of whatever lies inside it (deduction's move)
- grounded — a statement whose every concept traces through its context to what occurs, and whose coverage is drawn around what occurred or marked as projected
- source — the region of coverage in which the occurrence behind a concept happened (a flying pig's source is imagining; the misstatement "flying pigs exist in reality" places that occurrence in a region where it did not happen)
- need (between concepts) — a concept needs another when the other lies in its context
- consistency — no scope says X where another says not-X (PP2's "not" across scopes)
- correspondence — a statement's coverage holding the occurrence it names
- trace — following a concept's context downward until it ends at what occurs
- deniability (the denial test) — how a seen node is tested — deny it and the denial breaks against what occurs or against the parent; it is the test of the look, not a second source
- warrant / justification — a statement's standing: for a seen node, the look plus the denial test; for a projection, kept while no seen occurrence breaks it
- fundamental / superficial property — a property the label's scope is drawn by / one the label does not move with

**The one picture.** Every node has three things. Context: the concepts it is said within ("day comes after every night": day, night, after, pattern). Coverage: the occurrences it speaks of (every night). Observations: the occurrences that were in awareness (the nights seen so far). In every form the child falls under its context and its coverage lies inside its parents' (every night is a night) — a scoped manifestation. The forms differ in how coverage stands to observations:
