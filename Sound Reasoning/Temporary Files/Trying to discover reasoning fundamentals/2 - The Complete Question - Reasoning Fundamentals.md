## 1. The main question

File 1's question has a standing answer — mine: **"children are scoped manifestations of their parents"** (offered with "or something like that"; the wording is mine to fix, and file 0 opens with the line and the four things that ride with it — those four are thread wording, unratified). This file's question is that answer's natural follow-up — the main tension:

"perhaps this 'first' form of reasoning is where a parent is the scope of its children, while I mean 'induction' as the resulting child is actually a scope of its parents - but induction also has to utilize this primitively form of reasoning under the hood, so in some sense that child is within the scope of the parents too... I think this is the main tension I'm trying to work through."

So, in one sentence: **if children are scoped manifestations of their parents, then what exactly is induction — where the child is a scope around its parents — and what are we really doing in the tree?**

That follow-up holds the earlier framing, which the answer still owes: the most fundamental form of reasoning, stated at the phenomenological level, with its own word and its own move, and with induction, abduction, and deduction defined through it. The working statement of the form: "a child is a new awareness that satisfies/fits within the scope of its parents" — with "child", "awareness", and "parents" used loosely, "closer to a phenomenological reduction of the definition than the actual one we'd use". "Inference" is penciled in as a possible word for it, "as an inference is (loosely) when a new statement comes from another. This seems to be a little different from how it's used in mainstream philosophy, but it's just a feeling so far."

The form has two jobs that one answer must cover:

- It is "the 'first' or most primitive form of reasoning that occurs before any other form of reasoning (in the sense that, no other form of reasoning can be valid except if this has happened beforehand or under the hood)".
- It is "a 'parent' to all other forms of reasoning, so it is the underlying 'form' that builds the structure for each of those forms of reasoning". My list of the forms, in outline: "[before reasoning occurs]: naming labels to phenomenological experiences"; phenomenological inference/reasoning ("understanding on concept within the context of another" — typo his, kept); induction ("identifying the pattern/scope around a set of observations - where the child is a scope around its parents"); abduction ("grounded in reality chains of scopes/understanding are preferred over others"); deduction ("intersection of the chosen scopes, the intersection being the parent scope of the child conclusion").

And the answer owes a word: "we need a word for the mechanism for the first form of reasoning (similar to how 'projection' is the unique mechanism for induction), but without contradicting the underlying 'reasoning' that both do" — together with my note that the two may need different words: "there may or should be different names needed for the both of these (ie. the 'first' form of reasoning ... vs. the underlying structure for all other forms of reasoning)".

## 2. My motivation for asking it

- File 1's question — the form of reasoning that is the link between nodes — found its one-line answer, and this question is what that answer opens: the tension between it and what induction is, and what we are really doing in the tree. The forms themselves are still owed, since "these logical primitive nodes are the labels for the answer to this whole question" (file 1), and the tree's own building must turn out to be one of the forms it will later state as its own nodes.
- The realms were blurring into the forms: "right now in the tree, we are traveling from the epistemological primitive PP1 to some node that will be the ontological primitive (ie. the node that defines what reality is, intrinsically), and then from there it branches into the 'type' of reality (like discovering how our own minds work, ontologically, or what happens right now, physically/ontologically, or what happened from the beginning of reality, ontologically, etc.). So these realms were conflating with the fundamental types of reasoning I was trying to figure out."
- The pedagogy is part of it: topics must be ordered so "the more fundamental concepts are addressed first before the next ones (so that the conclusions/understandings from them can be used to explain the later concepts as needed)" — and I suspect this teaching order and the tree's own order are the same sorting: "I wonder if, when I ask for the 'pedagogical' ordering of things, that this is actually in alignment with how the tree itself organizes its nodes."

## 3. What I observed that bears on it

(The unquoted glosses in this section are the thread's summaries of my words, kept short.)

- **"Reality" means awareness of reality.** "when we say 'reality makes a node true', what we're really saying is that we didn't decide that awareness of actual reality, it just happened."
- **The tree tracks awareness — and maybe belief or understanding.** Awareness "would be PP5 - 'the occurrence of a quality or association'"; and "you need to believe in the parent nodes in order to validly believe in or understand a child node (or maybe both, but in different ways - things often are like this, since one is a more primitive ancestor for the other)".
- **Children are direct observations that fit their parents' scopes.** "child nodes are direct observations/awarenesses that fit within the scope of the parent nodes" — and the parent's coverage takes in the child's observations automatically: "'qualities occur' encompasses the observations within 'will occurs' as well", "as a side effect of the structure".
- **Seen against unseen.** "I can't use one of my senses to 'see' a thought or imagination, the same way I 'see' light and 'hear' sounds. This is the distinction I mean". Single events are "seen" even when intellectual: "I can visualize '2+2=4' but can't visualize '2+2=5', so these are 'seen' observations - and then I induce that 'intellectual causality exists' ... and that induction is 'unseen'". And the sweeping thought: "almost as if our ability to reason *is* a kind of vision into the unseen (ie. rules of how reality actually works, including our own selves)".
- **The main tension.** "perhaps this 'first' form of reasoning is where a parent is the scope of its children, while I mean 'induction' as the resulting child is actually a scope of its parents - but induction also has to utilize this primitively form of reasoning under the hood, so in some sense that child is within the scope of the parents too... I think this is the main tension I'm trying to work through."
- **The two traces of "apple falling".** One: "apple falling" ← "apple" & "fall", where "apple" ← "red" & "apple shape" & "apple smell" & "apple taste", each reducing toward "qualities occur" and PP1. The other: "apple falling" ← "things fall" ← "matter attracts" ← deeper physics. "That trace is clearly different - but why?" And the puzzle inside it: "According to the tree, 'apple falling' would be one of the observations that contributes to the 'things fall' node, meaning that that node is actually a child of... well idk, not 'apple falling', as that's not how PP1 and PP2 were made."
- **The same-example test** — the test that broke the thread's proposal of two scopes: "'the scope of a concept' covers everything a label fits (so the concept of 'gravity occurs' for example, covers 'apple falls' and 'moon orbits'), while 'the scope of a claim' covers the observations the claim is about (so the claim 'gravity occurs' for example, covers 'apple falls' and 'moon orbits') - see how I just copied the same example and it satisfies both sides?"
- **"Claim", reduced.** My ratified line: "claim = a statement with a truth-value". "You could phenomenologically reduce this to something like 'claim = a concept with a truth-value' (idk if this is true in a rigorous sense, but loosely it seems to be so)." And the depth complaint: "the phenomenological level which scopes, meaning, and understanding occur are more primitive than claims".
- **Understanding.** "Understanding = awareness/occurrence of meaning?" — "if you 'understand' what anything is, you have to be able to grasp the concept itself and in what context that concept exists". Gravity is understood at levels: "things fall to the ground" explaining "apples fall", or "mass attracts" explaining "apples fall" and "moon orbits". Understanding looks recursive, "or perhaps, 'understanding' has a limit, with the proper limit tracing back to PP1". And the old definition placed: "I would define 'understanding' through an 'internal model'... but this 'model' definition comes from having 'reality' already defined so you're 'modeling reality'. But phenomenologically, we don't need to define 'reality' yet to know what 'understand' means."
- **The grounding catch.** The flying pig "depends on the 'flying with wings' and 'pigs' concepts, and intellectual causality allows us to combine both concepts - and 'intellectual causality' is a part of 'reality' - so technically that concept is in fact grounded in reality. But obviously, that isn't what is meant by that."
- **The transition hypothesis** — my synthesis late in the thread: the divide between the first form and induction is [tentatively] "scoping around what occurred vs. what has not occurred (in awareness, as both obviously occur in reality regardless of awareness)".
- **No induction is yet visible inside the tree.** "the child in both of these framings don't encompass the instances of the parents - so no induction that I see... So I don't see how the observed/unobserved distinction even fits within this framing." Future nodes will naturally transition to using induction (as well as abduction and deduction) so the current structure of the tree is not representative of the final product.
- **The swan aside.** Inductions get framed as "fundamentally 'incomplete' or 'prone to error' in a way deduction can't, but this seems off to me". Seeing white swans and then a black swan-like bird leaves "two options: changing the scope of 'swan' to encompass both 'white' and 'black', or placing that under a different label/scope other than 'swan' - both technically valid, right?" — "This also seems to reflect a concept discovered in an old project about 'fundamental' and 'superficial' properties." The fact that a scope can change shouldn't mean that it was "wrong" - like whatever was observed previously is still true, and framing it as "wrong" seems more inflammatory and reflective of mainstream philosophy's alignment with the enlightenment's attack on reason like this.
- **The meta.** "whatever we derive needs to be reflected in the tree's structure itself. PP1 is the widest epistemological scope - nothing is wider than that." — with my own dissatisfaction attached: "I feel like I'm not doing this subject justice with the above comment, like so what?"

## 5. The answers proposed in this thread that fell, and why

(The quotes in this section are the thread's own candidate wordings, not mine, unless attributed to me.)

- **The divide into two scopes** ("the scope of a concept" against "the scope of a claim") — fell to my same-example test: the same words fit both sides, so it was two labels on one relation. Conceded and withdrawn; what replaced it lives in file 0.
- **"Claim" as load-bearing vocabulary** — the divide was drawn at the wrong depth; my standing objection ("why we're talking about claims") ended it. The divide was restated below words.
- **"Forward" and "backward" as the words for the two requirement lists** — they invited a travel reading (I tested them as kinds of links between PP-nodes); retired for straightforward descriptions of the two lists.
- **The unification through exceptionless need, as first stated** — "having the child correlates without exception with having the parents" broke on argument links: "my pet is a mammal" can be had through the cat route, through watching the pet nurse. The restatement — absolute for concepts (a parent is a part; no route around a part), some backing route for claims — is a standing candidate, kept in file 0 with the other material on the two relations.
- **"Groundedness eliminates rival explanations" as abduction's criterion** — retracted: composed concepts are grounded (the flying pig, my catch), and "attraction" itself is not seen, so the criterion would break "matter attracts" too. My old double-standard observation still applies there; abduction's criterion stays open.
- **Reality against imagination by want alone** ("presence on want") — nightmares and intrusive images broke it; replaced by file 0's account of grounded claims (the stated context matching where the source occurred), with the want difference kept only as one way the presentations differ (also in file 0).
- **The two "come from"s as two kinds of cause** — only one is causality (the intellectual chain); reality's part is what makes the statement true, not an event that causes it. Restated as two different relations.
- **Delivery problems, for the next thread to avoid:** semantic bleach kept recurring even in accepted answers; the walks were delivered unlabeled the first time; and the replies fell into one repeated shape — the direct cause of this handoff.
