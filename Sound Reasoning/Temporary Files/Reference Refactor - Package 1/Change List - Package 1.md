## What each note became

1. **Provenance comments removed.** All "(his ruling)", "(his instruction)", "(added by him)", "pending his ok", "ratified", and the like are gone from both drafts — the reader-facing files no longer refer to the project's own process. Ratified-versus-pending tracking stays in the process files (the changes lists in Temporary Files) and in the file history. One consequence to be aware of: the old thesaurus marked some swaps "pending his ok"; the merged file lists them without markers, so a veto now happens by reading the entry and striking the line, not by scanning for a marker.
2. **Sub-bullets and indentation.** His nesting kept; sub-bullets now indent with a tab, as in his edit, in both drafts. Nested bullets are as easy for the AI as for the eye — structure like this helps both — so it goes into the formatting standards in 02 when package 2 touches that file. The near-flat single-space indent the old files used renders as barely nested at all, which is why the difference was hard to see.
3. **The two unreadable spots.**
	- The shorthand sentence became an instruction that says its own meaning: "When reporting a problem, say which layer it is in: "the thought is off" (the concept) or "the wording is off" (the language)." If that still reads as noise, deleting the line loses only the two quoted phrases.
	- "The angle is in the looking... the experiencer may be in the noticing, not in the occurring" was lifted from PP1, where its context lives; standing alone in 03 it is undecodable. The example pair was replaced with a self-contained one: "The road was icy; the bus was late." → "The road was icy, so the bus was late."
4. **The Why section rewritten.** It now opens with the actual aim — keeping the reader's cognitive load low — then treats both sources of complexity: complexity in the topic (the treatment: the right breakdown, one graspable piece at a time, and exposing the exact factor that separates clashing stances) and complexity in the telling (the existing five bullets). The closing paragraph now says the writing handles both. All new wording is his to veto or rewrite.
5. **The no-ambiguity good example replaced**: "A claim that can be compared with reality can be caught when it is wrong; a claim that cannot be compared with reality at all cannot be told apart from a made-up one." — the emphasis is now on how a claim's truth can be told, not on the claim being "everyday".
6. **The misreading rule rewritten.** The rule line kept its point; the examples became: Bad — the conversation clears the misreading up and the page stays as it was; Good — the misread sentence gets rewritten, with the experiencer line as the worked example in parentheses.
7. **The watch-list left 03 and merged with the thesaurus** into one draft file, currently titled "09 - Watch-list". 03 keeps a four-line section saying where the list lives and the short version of the handling. Structure of the merged file: how it is used → the tags (banned / conditionally banned / under watch / removed) → the patterns (alphabetical, each with its most common examples) → the words (alphabetical, one heading each: tags, pattern, scope, replacements). "How the list grew" and the other history are gone.
8. **Pattern-level entries stand above the words.** "Terms minted with a hyphen" is now a pattern entry holding its own examples, alongside the other patterns. No third list was needed: a word entry states its pattern, so the two levels cover it.
