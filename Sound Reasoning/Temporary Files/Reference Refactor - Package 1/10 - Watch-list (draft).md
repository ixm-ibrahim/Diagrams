# 10 Watch-list

The words and phrases to keep out of the project's writing, and what to say instead. Two levels: the patterns — the recurring shapes behind the individual words, each with its most common examples — and the words, one entry each, with tags, scope, and the accepted replacements. Sources: published lists of AI writing tells, and this project's own catches.

## How this file is used

- In chat, the AI swaps banned and conditionally banned words out of its own text directly — no ruling loop.
- In node text, a word tagged "banned" is swapped or deleted on sight; for a word tagged "conditionally banned", whoever writes or checks the page lists every sentence containing it, and the author rules on each sentence — AI shape or plain speech. This binds all threads.
- Where a banned word is the objection's own topic — "check" and "rule" in PP1's objection 2, "attention" in PP2's attention objection — it stays as the needed language for that objection, and the objection's framing then deserves a fresh look.
- The reference files' own prose and examples follow the same standard and are swept the same way; examples that deliberately keep flaws stay as they are.
- The best replacement depends on the context, not on the word alone — read the word's entry before proposing a fix. When a sentence stays awkward through more than one rewording, the anatomy of the harder fixes is in [[11 - Sentence Case Studies]].
- The file grows as the author adds words and verdicts; new entries follow the shapes below.

The tags:

- **banned** — swapped or deleted everywhere; no ruling needed.
- **conditionally banned** — the author rules on each use in node text; in chat, swapped directly.
- **under watch** — removed case by case; no listing loop, no standing ban.
- **removed** — was on the list and was taken off; not to be re-proposed.

A word's entry also states its pattern, where one has been identified.

## The patterns

- **Decorative triads** (banned) — listing everything in threes ("clear, concise, and compelling") whether or not three things exist.
- **Fake-weight adjectives** (banned) — "nuanced", "multifaceted", "comprehensive", "pivotal", "crucial", "robust", "compelling", "groundbreaking", "cutting-edge" — if the point has weight, the sentence shows it without the label.
- **Filler openers** (banned) — "honestly", "to be honest", "basically", "essentially", "simply put", "at its core", "fundamentally" — delete; the sentence says the same without them.
- **Grand-scenery nouns** (banned) — "landscape", "realm", "tapestry", "journey", "paradigm" — name the actual thing instead.
- **Hedge stacks** (banned) — "may potentially", "could arguably", "somewhat", "to some extent" — several maybes stacked on one claim; commit, or say plainly what is unknown.
- **Inflated verbs** (banned) — "delve", "dive into", "unpack", "navigate", "leverage", "harness", "foster", "bolster", "underscore", "streamline", "shed light on" — use the plain verb: look at, use, support, show.
- **Insider shorthand** (under watch) — words like "rule", "ground", "floor", "gate", "record", "judgment call", "real (work)", "met" — fine in their plain everyday sense ("the record shows" about an actual record); the drift is using one as a private technical term a fresh reader cannot decode ("the record" as a name for the project, "met" as a doctrine of how differences come about ("the distinction is met, not made"), "does real work" as a verdict, "grounds" as a relation the reader was never shown). Say the plain thing the word was standing for ("the difference is simply there when both occur" instead of "the difference is met"). "Rule" and "met" also have their own entries below.
- **Personification** (under watch) — reality or a statement treated as an agent: "the world was never consulted", "a claim reports our word-choices". Say what actually happens instead.
- **Placeholder objects** (conditionally banned, via "anything" / "nothing") — a bare "something", "anything", or "nothing" standing where the kind of thing should be, so the sentence has the shape of a criterion with the criterion missing ("adds nothing", "hold anything up"). Ask "what kind?" — if the sentence cannot answer, it was hiding a test with no content (case 19 in [[11 - Sentence Case Studies]]).
- **Private labels** (conditionally banned as they are caught) — a coined name the reader must decode: "the floor", "the compass", "the terminus", "the ladder", "the chain". Call the thing by its plain description.
- **Private verbs** (conditionally banned as they are caught) — a casual verb quietly installed as the term for one hidden job: "met", then "attend", then "shows up", then "arrive", then "reach", each in its turn standing for "becomes present in awareness". A plain word used once is plain; the same word doing the same hidden job across a page has become a term of art. After adopting a replacement, sweep the page for it (case 13 in [[11 - Sentence Case Studies]]).
- **Stock frames** (banned) — "it's worth noting that", "it's important to note", "serves as", "plays a crucial role", "a testament to", "not just X but Y" — say the note itself and drop the frame.
- **Sycophancy** (banned) — "great question", "you're absolutely right" — answer the question; if the person is right, the content shows it.
- **Terms minted with a hyphen** (conditionally banned — the structure itself, not any particular phrase) — words glued into an ad-hoc term; ordinary everyday compounds, like "three-sided shape", are outside the target.
	- "difference-claim" → "Saying that two things differ means nothing until what differs between them is specified." (PP2 commitment, with its restatement) and "A claim asserting that two things differ cannot be checked while no criterion is given." (PP2 Basis)
	- "the telling-apart" → "the act of telling two things apart"; "whether telling-apart is the act of a self" → "whether telling things apart is the act of a self" (PP2)
	- "a true-by-definition statement reflects our word-choices" → "a statement that is true by definition reflects only our choice of words" (PP1)
	- "being-experienced" → the sentence reworked to drop the phrase, since the passive collides with "experienced" meaning skilled: "It does not show that an occurrence must be experienced in order to occur." (PP1)
	- "The beliefs-holding-each-other alternative" → "The alternative — beliefs holding each other up —", the whole sentence reworked (PP1)
	- Kept: "The singling-out and comparing words" (PP2 Unlocks) — and with it the dictionary's "a singling-out word" entries.
- **Verdict idioms** (conditionally banned, per word) — approval standing where content should be: "does work", "earns its place", "gets right". Say what the thing contributes; if that cannot be said, the praise was hiding a gap (case 12 in [[11 - Sentence Case Studies]]).

## The words

- Entry shape: the word as a heading; a first line with its tags, its pattern where one is identified, and its scope where one is recorded; then one line per verdict — "before" → "after", with the page it came from.
- A word with no scope note is banned in all its uses until a ruling narrows it.
- Grammatical forms come along with a word ("arrive" covers "arriving" and "arrival").

### act

conditionally banned — as a noun for the project's own doings: a node's proof, the method's steps.

### anything / nothing

conditionally banned · placeholder objects — as bare placeholder objects ("adds nothing", "hold anything up"), where the sentence cannot say what kind of thing is meant. "Nothing" as the topic itself ("Nothing whatsoever is happening") is still listed, ruled case by case.

### arrive

conditionally banned · private verbs — for how things come into experience.

- "inverts the order in which things actually arrive" → "inverts the actual order" (PP2)
- "mistakes how the difference arrives" → "has it backwards: the difference... is simply there the moment both occur" (PP2)
- "too many distinctions arriving at once" → "too many distinctions at once" (PP2)
- "experience does not arrive as a list" → "does not come as a list" (PP2)

### ask

conditionally banned — the noun ("an ask"); asking a question is outside.

- "list every separate ask in it" → "list every separate request in it" (01 - Processing Queries, his edit applied to the file's other instances too)

### attend

conditionally banned · private verbs

- "only become distinct when someone attends to them" → "when someone pays attention to them" (PP2)

### met / meet

conditionally banned · private verbs

- "no language's speakers meet a world where..." → "find a world where..." (PP2)

### reach

conditionally banned · private verbs — in the sense "reach an occurrence"; had already been rejected once as a fix for "met" (case 2 in [[11 - Sentence Case Studies]]).

- "every distinction anyone has ever reached was reached in experience" → "ever noticed was noticed in experience" (PP2)
- "The objector is right that every occurrence anyone has ever reached must have come through experiencing it" → "The objector is right that no one has ever known of an occurrence except by experiencing it" (PP1)
- "You can never check on an occurrence except by being the one experiencing it." → "The only way to know of an occurrence is to be the one experiencing it." (PP1)
- "every occurrence you reach is one you are experiencing" → "every occurrence you notice is one you are experiencing" (PP1)
- The set varies on purpose — "notice" where the act itself is the point, "know of" where the finding-out is — so one replacement does not multiply into the next private verb (case 13 in [[11 - Sentence Case Studies]]).

### real

under watch — removed case by case; no standing ban.

- "a real question" → "a genuine question" (PP1, PP2), "an open question" (PP1), "a question for later" (PP2), or "worth considering" (PP2) — context picks
- "a real claim must rule something out" → "a genuine claim..." (PP1)
- "a real question that needs a real answer" → "a good question that can be investigated" (PP2)
- Kept where realness is the topic (the illusion, eliminativism, and monism objections) and as an everyday intensifier ("really wrong", "languages really do draw")

### reports / report

conditionally banned · personification

- The verb: "a true-by-definition statement reports our word-choices" → "reflects our word-choices" (PP1)
- The noun: "a report about our word-choices" → "a statement about our choice of words" (PP1); "a report of a difference is expected to say" → "someone claiming a difference is expected to say" (PP2); "is a report about what occurs" → "is a statement about what occurs" (PP2)
- Kept as plain speech: "Some people in deep meditative states report exactly that" — people reporting
- The sentence about accounts of unity was reworked whole rather than swapped word by word (PP2): "Even the person who says all distinctions dissolved describes the state through distinctions:... And such an account is itself distinct from an account of a toothache."

### rule

conditionally banned — also under insider shorthand in the patterns above; where ruling-out is the objection's own topic (PP1's objection 2), it stays as the needed language.

### run / running

conditionally banned — as the verb for how an order, a test, or reasoning operates ("the order runs one way", "the reasoning runs on"); a person running, or "this solution works"-style plain uses, are outside.

### settle / settled

conditionally banned

- "That the many occur is settled by the observations" → "The observations above already show that the many occur" (PP2)
- "is settled every time one thing is noticed" → "is confirmed every time one thing is noticed" (PP2)
- "choosing words settles nothing about the world" → "The objector is right that you cannot make something exist by defining a term for it — a definition adds a label, not a fact." The first fix ("changes nothing about the world") was turned back — true of every claim, so it separates nothing (case 20 in [[11 - Sentence Case Studies]]).
- the cluster "is settled by the world / cannot be settled the other way / nobody to settle anything" → ""Phenomena occur" is true because something does occur — the occurring is what makes it true, not our choice of words. And it cannot turn out false the other way: if nothing occurred, nobody would be there to find that out." (PP1). The first fix ("is made true by the world...") was turned back — say what makes it true concretely — and "the world" joined the list from that ruling.
