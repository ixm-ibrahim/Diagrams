## 2. The completeness argument (drafted; the author's claim, given its derivation)

The author's claim: if the tree is right, it encompasses every form of falsehood that reasoning ever passes — no counterexample.

Derivation, from settled pieces: falsehood always carries a mismatch (step 1). A mismatch is between two sides. Reality does not mismatch itself — contradictions do not occur among non-willed occurrences; only categorizations conflict. So a mismatch that reasoning fails to catch sits in one of exactly two places: **between the reasoning and reality** (unconnected — ungrounded) or **within the reasoning's own parts** (internally mismatched — inconsistent). Two sides, two placements, no third place. Any proposed counterexample must locate its mismatch somewhere, and there are only two somewheres.

Sharpening: the tree covers *justified* falsehood — falsehood passed by reasoning — which is what the primitive is about. Falsehood as such is just mismatch; the tree is about the ways reasoning goes blind to it.

## 3. "Method of reasoning" vs. "reasoning" (the author's question, answered)

"Reasoning" is enough. Reasoning reduces to a sequence of intellectual causality; intellectual causality is necessity among thoughts; necessity is pattern-level — a click is an instance of an exceptionless pattern or it is not causality. So the class ("method") re-enters through the reduction and need not be carried as a word. One guard survives the simplification, stated once: the primitive judges the patterns a run instantiates, not the knower — an honest error from incomplete inputs (time-is-infinite) is refinement's business, not disqualification. Practical consequence: the reductions owed are "ungrounded reasoning" and "inconsistent reasoning" — not "ungrounded method of reasoning."
