# Step 1 — the bottom of the inferential pipeline (settled 2026-07-08)

One question was asked and answered: **what sits at the bottom of the chain of rules, and what grounds it?**

---

## 1. What step 1 established

```
claim (its inferential proof)
  <- rules of inference                  (not yet frozen — step 3)
  <- rules of abduction                  (not yet frozen — step 2)
  <- THE TERMINUS (settled):
       plain form:    a method of reasoning that justifies falsehood
                      cannot distinguish it from truth, and is disqualified
       reduced form:  a method's verdict must be able to change when
                      reality disagrees with the claim
  <- distinction, correlation, independence, will-independence   (§3 steps 2, 5, 6, 7)
  <- phenomena occur
```

The two forms are one rule at two depths — the reduced form unpacks the plain one, the same way "truth" unpacks into "necessity within undeniable mental events."

**Why it holds — three moves, each on existing ground:**

1. **Falsehood always comes with a mismatch.** A false statement is one whose description does not correspond with what occurs — that difference *is* what falsehood means, unpacked, not a new claim. (A "falsehood" with no mismatch anywhere would not be false.) And a difference sits at §3 step 2 of the grounding chain.
2. **The mismatch stays and can be met.** Reality is independent of will (§3 step 7): the difference does not move to accommodate anyone's belief. We meet mismatches daily — every "I was wrong" click is one. Guard: the mismatch lives where description and reality differ; if that scope isn't reachable yet, it is real but unmet ("within compatible scopes").
3. **"Independent of truth" is literal.** A method whose verdict comes out the same whether or not the mismatch exists has verdicts that do not *correlate* with reality — and independence is already defined on the grounding chain as absence of correlation (§3 step 6). The painted needle is a needle that doesn't correlate with north.

**The chain-level form (the author's addition):** every link in a proof must itself be able to detect falsehood passing through it. One blind link is enough for falsehood to enter — a chain is only as discriminating as its weakest link. This is where "the structure of a claim says something about its truth" gets its derivation: you can inspect the links before touching content, and a structure with a blind link cannot establish truth no matter what it carries.

## 4. How this will be used

- **At the door of every analysis:** "speculation is neither proof nor refutation" is the terminus applied to one input-type — fiction-layer imagination, which carries no correlation with reality. In *derivation* it sits one step below the terminus; in *use* it acts first, before any comparing starts. ("Maybe you were dreaming" never enters; no energy spent.) This also blocks the move the author flagged: "I can imagine a contradiction, therefore contradictions are true" — imagining something places it in the layer where correspondence doesn't matter, and nothing there moves a verdict.
- **When any rule is challenged:** the defense must trace to the terminus. If it can't, we've found either a missing rule or a real problem — both are progress.
- **Structural inspection, before content:** check each link of a proof — could its verdict have come out the other way? A proof with a blind link is disqualified without weighing what it claims.
- *(Derivation vs. use, once: where a rule comes from is its own claim-structure — every rule is a claim; when a rule acts is its position in some other claim's analysis. Tools aren't picked up in the order they were made.)*

## 5. The terminus's own words, reduced

- **sequence** — phenomena occur one after another (§3 step 4).
- **process** — a sequence where each step depends on the one before. *(Author's own earlier node — confirm it carries over.)*
- **method** — a class of processes: a shared pattern across such sequences. *(Author's own earlier node — confirm.)*
- **reason** — a sequence of intellectual causality (§5; discovered, not decided).
- **verdict** — the final step of the process, with the deniability status it assigns.
- **justify** — a method justifies a claim when its process ends at "true" for it. So "a method that justifies falsehood" = reasoning whose final click assigns "true" to a claim that carries a mismatch.
- **inference / abduction** — reasoning's two jobs: produce a new statement; select among rivals. Reason is upstream of both, downstream of the primitives.
