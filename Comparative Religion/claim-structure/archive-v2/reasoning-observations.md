# Reasoning observations — the raw material

Step 1 of the from-scratch derivation: concrete cases of reasoning working or failing. These are the observations the rules get induced from. Add freely — everyday cases, cases from the projects, cases from arguments you've had. Each entry: what was observed, and what rule it grounds or constrains. Ordering and categorizing come later.

1. **Lucky socks.** Three wins while wearing them, offered as proof they work. The support fits "socks do nothing" equally well. → grounds the compass; shows thin samples don't reach "often follows."
2. **Horoscope and the good day.** One prediction, one match, offered as proof astrology works. Same shape as the socks. → compass; single instance is not correlation.
3. **The indoors move (observed live, ChatGPT test).** "It is raining" blocked as support for "I am wet" because *maybe the speaker is indoors* — a merely-possible alternative used to refuse a grounded first-person claim. → observed misuse; grounds "speculation is neither proof nor refutation" (L2.12); also shows the default reflex leaks on easy claims, not hard ones.
4. **"Time is infinite" from chicken-and-egg.** With limited knowledge the inference was valid — and wrong. Learning the Law produced the click that corrected it. → grounds valid-is-not-correct, refinement, knowledge-indexed verdicts.
5. **Microbial life outside Earth.** The verdict's form depended entirely on the basis chosen (statistical vs. observe-directly). → grounds basis-selection; scope fixes what kind of verdict can exist.
6. **The infinite regress.** Never observed; no grounding; nothing for a metric to weigh; loses best-fit to the terminus. → grounds "grounded rivals only" (L2.7) and best-fit (L2.1).
7. **Newtonian time.** "Time is absolute" true within its pattern of observations, wrong when exported. → grounds the scope-limit rule (L1.14).
8. **Rooster then sunrise.** An exceptionless correlation on a thin sample that is spurious. → constrains the causation rule (L1.10); shows why a discriminative step is needed beyond bare exceptionlessness.
9. **Smoking and cancer.** The rival "something that travels with smoking causes it" fits the bare correlation equally; randomized designs are what separate them. → the compass as a working procedure, not just a condition.
10. **The Quran-preservation analysis (Claude test run).** A multi-sense claim ("preserved," "unchanged") needed per-scope verdicts; manuscript uniformity flagged as fitting "stable since the archetype" exactly as well as "unchanged since revelation." → grounds scope-splitting; compass applied inside historical reasoning.
11. **Stars even / atoms even / fish prime.** Predicates that consume an exact count, aimed at referents where no exact count exists. The claims dissolve before inference starts. → grounds entry conditions: what is admissible into reasoning at all.
12. **Magnets; water and plants.** Every chain of "how" bottoms out at ignorance; no level states the intrinsic how. → grounds the intrinsic-cause terminus; a limit observation.
13. **Brains in vats.** "No one can know anything" fails when run on itself. → grounds self-application testing; structure deciding a claim before content.
14. **"Miracles are impossible."** The word "law" switched senses mid-argument — observed regularity to earn credibility, modal rule to reach "impossible." → grounds the fixed-terms condition; term-drift as a falsehood mechanism.
15. **Observed termini everywhere.** First egg from a non-chicken; people born; light from sources; the planet from dust; observations from phenomena. → grounds the origin rule (L1.11); termini are observed in the ordinary grounded sense.
16. **The false-conclusion gap.** In practice, arguments that land on falsehood from true starting points always contain a hidden jump — a premise not traced, a term drifted, a scope exported. → grounds the gapless-chain rule (L1.13) and the logical-gap thesis.
17. **The doctor and the cold** *(from 9.C, Limits of Skepticism — expand from source).* Skepticism applied without constraints rejects the diagnosis every patient accepts. → constrains where doubt is admissible; to be expanded.

18. **Gödel's statement** ("this statement is unprovable in this system"). A reflexive-scope case where self-application neither kills nor grounds the claim — within the system's own scope it is undecidable, and the verdict lives one scope up (from outside, it comes out true). → shows the self-application test (L2.13) has a third output: a stated limit, resolved by the Newtonian move — the verdict belongs to a wider scope. *(Author's case; rendering in framework terms is draft.)*
