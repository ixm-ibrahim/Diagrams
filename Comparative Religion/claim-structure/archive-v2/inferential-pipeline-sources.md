## D. Abduction rules

How to pick between rival explanations: same standards for all, steelman every known rival, enumerate all plausible alternatives, prefer the account covering the most data with the fewest unsupported assumptions, treat undifferentiated ties as a stated limit, and never let ignorance or bare possibility win.

## Gaps

Rule-families that appear thin or absent across all three source sets:

- Grounding of the inference rules themselves in observations of intellectual causality: only three extracts touch it (logic's predictive success at Inference 10, abstraction capacity at Inference 22, prior rules reused as observational premises at Inference 13) — there is no explicit statement that the rules of reasoning are themselves induced from observing reasoning succeed and fail.
- Justification of induction: the problem of induction is named and deferred (data.json line 3072) and never resolved in any extract; no rule states how many observations, or what kind, license a generalization.
- Degrees of confidence: the framework insists on per-question binary knowledge (data.json lines 2931/2937) and gestures at probabilistic significance (Inference 37), but no graded-confidence or probability-updating machinery exists; "certainty? uncertainty?" is an open TODO in the DAG notes.
- Abductive tie-breaking: "equally parsimonious explanations without decisive differentiators" is stated as a limit (3.1 node 8) with no procedure for suspension, weighting, or further discrimination.
- Weighting conflicts inside the testimony triad: dissimilarity/coherence/motive criteria are listed, and the embarrassment criterion comes with its own counterweight (fabrication for attention, 3.2 node 8), but no rule says which criterion prevails when they point in opposite directions.
- Derivation of the F-family: the testimony, corroboration, and transmission rules are asserted as requirements; no extract traces them back through a gapless chain to the axioms, as families B-C demand of every premise.
- Derivation of the diachronic claims: convergence (revisions become less fundamental) and necessary growth of explanatory power (3.1 node 9) are asserted, not derived from the correspondence definitions they depend on.
- Non-circular external validation of axioms: axioms are to be "proven by proving the system's internal and external consistency" (3.1 node 4 Limits), but no extract spells out how whole-system consistency checking avoids the circularity it is meant to escape.
