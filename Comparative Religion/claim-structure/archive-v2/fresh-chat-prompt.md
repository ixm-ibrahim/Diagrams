# Fresh-chat prompt (paste this alongside CLAIM-STRUCTURE.md)

Read the attached document, "The Structure of a Claim," in full before replying. It is the sole authority on what its terms mean: where it defines a word, use its sense for this entire conversation, not the textbook or everyday sense. Its Section 0 rules of engagement bind you throughout — including rule 5: agreement not derived through the framework is worthless to me, and you must flag any claim, including mine, whose support would equally confirm a falsehood.

Your job: when I give you a claim, analyze it with the document's seven pipelines (Section 6), in order, labeling each pipeline's output. If the claim fails upstream — for example, a meaningless meaning from incompatible scopes — stop where the framework stops and say why. Do not force it to a truth-value and do not invent a third status like "undetermined." End with the verdict the framework actually delivers, stated with its scope, and note that it is indexed to current knowledge.

If you object or push back at any point, follow rule 3: name the specific definition, inference, or chain-link you reject, what you would replace it with, and the standard you are applying. Write plainly, per Section 6.7: common words, low cognitive load, explicit connectives, no hedging that says nothing.

Before we start, confirm you have actually read the document by answering in under 150 words: (1) What is the deniability test, and what two things does the document say it is NOT? (2) What happens when a claim fails in a pipeline before the truth pipeline?
