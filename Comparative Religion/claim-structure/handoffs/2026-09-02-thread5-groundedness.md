## The record you must stay consistent with (all sealed; quoted in full)

**The two verdict words (thread 4's swap, sealed 2026-09-02):**
- truth-value (the top verdict, on the claim as made and told): true = meaningful + sound + faithful. False = some check failed — and the record always says which. When meaning passes but the sound check cannot yet be called, the verdict waits — a pending check never makes a claim false. The author's own words for the waiting rule: "if we don't know something, then we simply don't know something - not true or false yet, just speculation."
- sound / unsound (the reality check): what the claim says matches what occurs, or misses it — an unsound claim always has an "instead." The deniability machinery earns this verdict; justification is the checking work inside its set. Among meaningful claims, the content-denial of an unsound claim is sound; at the top level, false licenses nothing about the opposite.
- The speech rule (sealed): "false" never travels without the record naming which check failed; when the world is what you care about, act on the sound line.

**The definition of a claim (sealed):** "claim = a statement with a truth-value. '7 is hungry' has one — it grades false, because it fails the meaning check — so it is still a claim; 'is the kettle hot?' has none — a question takes no stand, so there is nothing to grade. 'Has a truth-value' means carries the slot, not owns a verdict: the coin under the couch has one too — a slot still waiting — and it is a claim all the same."

**The truth equation (sealed):** "true = meaningful + sound + faithful; false = any one of the three missing, and the record says which. A meaningless claim is false; an unsound claim is false no matter how well reasoned; a badly told truth is false in that telling yet sound — the same claim retold cleanly grades true. Truth is judged each time a claim is made and told — to another or to yourself. Justification is part of the sound set: sound means the claim corresponds; justified is the sound set's inner deniability level — the checking work that licenses calling it sound, not a fourth part of truth. The lucky guess: at the time of the guess the sound check is uncalled, so the truth verdict waits — the guess is just speculation; checking replaces the guess with actual sound justification, and only then does the claim grade true. The declaring at guess-time stays unforced on the act's record."

**What a claim is made of (thread 2, sealed, with thread 4's updates):** a claim's parts are the concept (singular — all its definitions and their associations together), its context ("context = the scope of observations the statement is about" — the observations anchor, the scope reaches: "it will rain tomorrow" is a claim today via the observed weather pattern), and the stand it takes (the statement says "this is so" — what separates "the kettle is hot" from "is the kettle hot?"). Making a claim is not an assessment step; the claim arrives whole, and all assessment runs on a made claim.

**Assessment (sealed):** three sets, eight pipelines, counted 3 + 4 + 1 (six workers plus two combiners; a combiner is the check of the relation between a set's workers' outputs; a one-worker set has nothing between to check). The meaningfulness set (3): concept (built from definitions, down to primitives), context (built from scopes, down to primitives), and its combiner — meaningfulness from concept and context together (both can pass while the pair fails: "seven is hungry"). The SOUND set (4), gated on meaningfulness passing: ontology, proof/signs/evidence, inferential, and its combiner — sound via deniability at its levels: deniability(known ontological conditions) & (deniability[inferential proof] <- deniability[proof/signs/evidence]) <- deniability(meaningfulness). The faithfulness set (1), gated on nothing: communication — told to another or to yourself; a claim made carries the presumption the maker holds it; a lie is the telling betraying the holding, not a second hidden claim. Failure doors, one per set: Meaningless (three ways in: no concept — an undefined word fails the concept check; no context; contexts that don't associate — "seven is hungry"), Unsound (pointed and missed, always an instead), Unfaithful (the claim is fine; the listener received a different one). False is not a fourth door — it is where every door exits.

**The sibling ruling (relocated by the swap, sealed):** meaningless is a sibling of UNSOUNDNESS, never its child — folding it into unsoundness would mint "seven is not hungry" as sound. At the top level, meaningless being a way into false is by design, safe because the top-level flip is given up.

**The grounding rule (sealed):** the meaning gate's verdicts are themselves claims — "hungry's context and seven's context don't associate" is a true, deniable claim, and its truth is what grounds the meaninglessness.

**The opposite-of-what instrument (his, thread 4):** "if it's false, its opposite is true" is oversimplified — ask "opposite of what?" Opposites can be taken at the content level ("7 is not hungry" — as meaningless as its twin, both false) or at the meaning level ("7 can't be hungry" — meaningful and TRUE; it is the grounding rule's verdict-claim in plain clothes).

**The category/value instrument (his, thread 4):** "what something 'is' is the essential things it 'has' — subtract any one of those fundamental properties (as a category, not whether its property-value is one way or another) and it no longer 'is' the same."

**The deepest rule (thread 1, sealed; post-swap reading):** "A method of reasoning that justifies falsehood can't distinguish what is true from what is not."
