The note format, one note per term (filename = id plus term, for example `A3 Qualities.md`):

- **Definition** — only ever the author's own typed words, quoted, with the date he ratified them. Until then the note is a stub (see statuses).
- **Direct parents** — links to the parent notes, one line each saying what of the parent this term narrows or uses. Parents inherit recursively from their own parents, so listing direct parents is enough — and the parent chain shows exactly what depth of vocabulary is available to this definition, which is the point: later definitions should speak in the richest available terms, not in primitives.
- **Words this term makes available** — the term itself, its inflections, and any synonym labels he ratifies.
- **Examples** — his, marked as his.
- **Open flags** — anything awaiting his word, each stated as a complete self-contained story.
- A frontmatter block (the metadata Obsidian reads): id, section, status, parents.

**Status on every note — three only:** `ratified` (wording is his typed words, dated — either typed fresh in a sitting, or verbatim-his from his notes and messages, verified against the source and cited); `in-progress` (he is working it); `stub` (not started — the note holds only the question this node must answer and the inspiration material, clearly marked "inspiration only — not live wording").

**Sections:** containers of like with like at the same phenomenological depth, as folders, each with a short map note, plus one `00 Map.md` at the root linking to everything. **He names the sections himself** (the authorship rule); his old table's section names — Phenomenological Grounding, Epistemological Grounding, Ontological Bridge, Logical Grounding, Reasoning, the Inferential-Pipeline branch — are inspiration.

**No plugins.** Plain links only, so the graph works out of the box.
