# Return — thread 4: the wording updates the three-values ruling forced (closed 2026-09-02; final form after the author's truth/soundness swap)

This thread was sent to fix two sentences that contradicted the author's ruling of 2026-08-03. In dialogue it grew: the author re-derived the definition of a claim, established the soundness verdict, and finally ruled the swap (2026-09-02): "truth/false" names the overall verdict on a claim as made and told, and "sound/unsound" names the reality check underneath. His goal, achieved: the everyday instinct "whatever is not true is false" now holds, meaningless statements included. Two design moves carry the load together: the opposite-is-true habit is given up at the top level (false licenses nothing about the opposite), and a waiting rule keeps unchecked claims out of "false."

## The two words

- **truth-value** (the top verdict, on the claim as made and told): true = meaningful + sound + faithful. False = some check failed — and the record always says which. When meaning passes but the sound check cannot yet be called, the verdict waits — a pending check never makes a claim false.
- **sound / unsound** (the reality check): what the claim says matches what occurs, or misses it — and an unsound claim always has an "instead." The deniability machinery is how this verdict is earned, and justification is the checking work inside its set. At this level the old flip survives: among meaningful claims, the content-denial of an unsound claim is sound.

## Sealed entries (final wording)

**1. The definition of a claim** (the original words restored, with the new meaning):

> claim = a statement with a truth-value. "7 is hungry" has one — it grades false, because it fails the meaning check — so it is still a claim; "is the kettle hot?" has none — a question takes no stand, so there is nothing to grade. "Has a truth-value" means carries the slot, not owns a verdict: the coin under the couch has one too — a slot still waiting — and it is a claim all the same.

**2. The truth equation:**

> true = meaningful + sound + faithful; false = any one of the three missing, and the record says which. A meaningless claim is false; an unsound claim is false no matter how well reasoned; a badly told truth is false in that telling yet sound — the same claim retold cleanly grades true. Truth is judged each time a claim is made and told — to another or to yourself. Justification is part of the sound set: sound means the claim corresponds; justified is the sound set's inner deniability level — the checking work that licenses calling it sound, not a fourth part of truth. The lucky guess (his framing, ratified 2026-09-02): at the time of the guess the sound check is uncalled, so the truth verdict waits — the guess is just speculation; checking replaces the guess with actual sound justification, and only then does the claim grade true. The declaring at guess-time stays unforced on the act's record, and the first thread's rule still convicts the method (merely matching, never knowing).

**4. The assessment structure** (thread-2 lines, swapped):

> The set formerly named the truth set renames to the sound set — same four pipelines (ontology, proof/signs/evidence, inferential, and the deniability combiner), same gate on meaningfulness, result now named sound. The top-level verdict is not any set's output: truth = meaningfulness & soundness & faithfulness, with the waiting rule; if meaning fails, false on the spot — the other sets never run. The verdict on the declaring (forced or unforced, judged by what had reached the declarer at the time) is graded beside the equation, never inside it. Failure doors: Meaningless (three ways in: no concept; no context; contexts that don't associate), Unsound (pointed and missed, always an instead), Unfaithful (the claim is fine; the listener received a different one). False is not a fourth door — it is where every door exits, and it has no entrance of its own.
> The anatomy phrase updates: a claim's third part is the stand it takes (the statement says "this is so") — the stand opens the truth-value slot; assessment fills it.
> The sibling ruling relocates to where its argument still bites: meaningless is a sibling of UNSOUNDNESS, never its child — folding it into unsoundness would mint "seven is not hungry" as sound. At the top level, meaningless being a way into false is now by design, and safe, because the top-level flip is given up.

## Method notes (his instruments from this thread)

- m1. The category/value subtraction test, his words: "what something 'is' is the essential things it 'has' — subtract any one of those fundamental properties (as a category, not whether its property-value is one way or another) and it no longer 'is' the same." The claim definition is built on it: claimhood = carrying the truth-value slot; how the slot grades never affects claimhood.
- m2. A basis belongs inside a claim when it is part of what is meant ("it will rain tomorrow" really means: given past weather patterns, rain is the reasonable expectation) — never when it is merely how the speaker came to say it.
- m3. Context clues can carry a basis without words: watching someone examine a kettle and then pronounce means the verdict is naturally taken as resulting from the examination.
- m4. Thread-2 return item 7 houses the two levels at the sound tier: the soundness-value is the slot the stand points at from birth; the sound set doesn't create the value, it forces it — asserted to forced. The truth-value is the other birth slot: the roll-up, filled by the three sets together.
- m5. "A bare assertion is as good as ungrounded" is a listener-side caution only — the claimant is judged by what he holds and what reached him, not by what he announces.
- m6. His rock analogy: an essential definition and a behavior-under-conditions definition coexist without competing.
- m7. Keep the sealed formula's top level named "deniability against known ontological conditions" — a test against what is known of reality; correspondence is what sound IS, not a test.
- m8. Sand-grains was ruled a bad specimen — do not reuse it.
- m9. His opposite-of-what instrument: "if it's false, its opposite is true" is oversimplified — ask "opposite of what?"; opposites can be taken at the content level or at the meaning level, and they grade differently.
