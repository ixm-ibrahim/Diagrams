THREAD 3 RETURN - TESTING WHETHER THE RULE IS THE FLOOR
================================================================================

THE QUESTION THIS THREAD WAS GIVEN
----------------------------------
Hunt for one real rule of reasoning, or one real preference between ways of
thinking, that does NOT ultimately depend on the rule derived in thread 1:

    "A method of reasoning that justifies falsehood can't distinguish what
     is true from what is not."

Finding one clean counterexample would mean the rule is not the floor, or a
link is missing. Finding none after a genuine hunt is evidence - never proof -
that it is the floor.

THE ANSWER
----------
No counterexample was found. Every rule of reasoning tested stands on the
rule. Evidence, not proof - a future rule could still surprise us.

HOW IT WAS TESTED
-----------------
Before judging anything, "depends on the rule" was given a concrete meaning.
A rule depends on the rule when two things hold:
  (1) Breaking it comes down to declaring or holding something true beyond
      what the evidence in hand forces.
  (2) Its real power is the rule's special contribution: one caught bad
      declaration discredits everything produced the same way, INCLUDING
      the outputs that happened to be correct (they were matching, not
      knowing). If a candidate keeps its full power without ever using
      that contribution, it does not depend on the rule.

About 45 candidates were run: the author's own recorded rules, rule-like
lines from his old website, the standard mainstream rules (statistics,
testimony, memory, proof practice, Occam's razor, and the rest), and freely
hunted candidates deliberately chosen to be the most likely to stand on
their own. The closest calls were each argued by two independent
adversaries - one defending the counterexample, one defending the rule -
and both the testing method and the draft verdict were attacked by fresh
adversaries before sealing.

WHAT STANDS ON THE RULE
-----------------------
All of it, grouped plainly:
  - Comparing against rivals before concluding (steelmanning, fair
    standards, refusing false either-ors, fewest unsupported assumptions).
  - Calibrating sources by their record (witnesses, memory, instruments -
    one caught lie burns even the liar's true reports; a lab strikes a bad
    gauge's whole log including readings known accurate, and recalibration
    is exactly "a new method with a fresh record").
  - Keeping confidence proportional to evidence.
  - Refusing to let wanting drive concluding.
  - Statistics discipline.
  - Not flip-flopping without new evidence.
  - Reopening when an equal disagrees.
  - Paying extra for unlike confirmation over ten copies of the same check.
In each one, the distinctive power is the same: one caught leap discredits
the whole way of concluding, even its lucky-correct outputs.

RULINGS THE AUTHOR ISSUED DURING THIS THREAD (NEW - CARRY AS CANON)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Old website and keep-list lines are BACKGROUND ONLY - they show how he
   used to think. Only thread-derived statements are live.
2. Lying belongs to the faithfulness set. The rule judges what a person
   concludes and holds. And before any utterance can be called false, fix
   the claim first - concept and context ("runs fine in any cold" may be
   true or false depending on what "any cold" means to that audience).
3. A meaningless claim is still a claim. A claim has three values - a
   meaning-value, a truth-value, a faithful-value - one per assessment
   set. Failing the meaning-value makes a claim INELIGIBLE for a
   truth-value ("7 is hungry"). Two wording updates follow: the old
   definition "claim = a statement with a truth-value," and thread 1's
   line saying a meaningless statement is "not a claim at all."
4. What convicts stamping TRUE on "7 is hungry" is the absent imagining:
   a hungry seven cannot be imagined - what comes instead (a person shaped
   like a 7 with a growling stomach) is a different claim.
5. The knowing-you're-wrong occurrence is not a rule. It is something that
   happens in a person, and rules are ways of producing conclusions - so
   it cannot compete for deepest rule. One bookkeeping sentence should
   travel with the floor claim: beneath every rule, this one included,
   sit the definitions and that occurrence; they are what rules are made
   of, and the rule's own derivation uses them.
6. Time-budgeting advice is not a rule of reasoning. Define its terms
   properly ("cheap" and "expensive" checks = less and more time and
   effort, comparing checks that would settle the same question) and the
   definitions contain only time, effort, and wanting - never truth,
   meaning, or claims. "Check the cheap one first" applies to reasoning
   the way it applies to shopping.

BOUNDARIES FOUND (REACH, NOT DEFECTS)
-------------------------------------
  - Someone who stays completely silent on a topic - never concludes or
    says anything about it - is untouched by the rule. Whether anything
    should ever cover that case is a later derivation, only if wanted.
  - Lying is handled by the faithfulness set, not the rule.
  - How to conduct an investigation (what to check first, where to spend
    hours) is outside, by definition.
