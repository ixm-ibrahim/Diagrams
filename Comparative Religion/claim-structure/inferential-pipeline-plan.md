## The project, in short

The inferential pipeline answers "what makes the signs actually lead to the claim?" It goes first among the seven because it carries the reasoning every other pipeline uses — it is the *why we can do any of this* pipeline.

Its structure is a tree, not a flat list, so every rule traces to its more primitive parents. The reason there must be a tree: rules of inference don't come from nowhere. Whatever makes a rule of inference correct is itself reasoning — a preference for one way of thinking over another — and that preferring is abduction. So the rules of abduction are the foundation of the rules of inference.

The claim this phase establishes or refutes: the floor of the abduction side is the author's node **"methods that justify falsehood can't distinguish it from truth"** — nothing above it but the phenomenological tree itself, every rule downstream inheriting its context. It is tested, not assumed: one known rule of inference, or one real preference between ways of thinking, that does not ultimately depend on it would refute it or expose a missing link.

His first cut at the floor's children, untested: grounding and consistency.

## Method

1. **The tree is the phenomenological tree** — each child is a scoped manifestation of its parents. The tree file tracks it.
2. **Children are found by reducing the parent to its components** — the same move that got the seven pipelines out of "claim." Never brainstormed, never sketched in ahead of the sitting that derives them.
3. **Rules are claims** — a rule's words must ground before its derivation counts. Meaning first, as everywhere else in the framework.
4. **The archived rules list is raw material, not an answer** — its rules re-enter one by one as derived nodes, or not at all.
5. **Every node gets grounded in examples** (the author's rule, 2026-07-09): each node made in the inferential pipeline carries at least one example — ideally several different kinds, chosen so they foreshadow the node's future children.

## The sittings — one layer, one question each `[DRAFT — the author corrects]`

1. **Place the floor.** Its meaning grounds first: do its words (methods, justify, falsehood, distinguish, truth) each trace to the trunk, and what is the floor itself a scoped manifestation of?
2. **Test the floor.** The counterexample hunt across established rules and the rules found in this project's files. Every rule that traces to it is evidence for; one that doesn't either refutes the floor or exposes a missing link — both are progress.
3. **Reduce the floor.** Its components give its children — where the grounding/consistency first cut gets tested against what the reduction actually yields.
4. **Descend**, one layer per sitting, until the tree carries every rule of reasoning we actually use.
5. **Write the pipeline** into inferential-pipeline.md in full, test it on readers with no prior contact with the project using claims that force abduction, then fold it back into CLAIM-STRUCTURE.md §6.5 and strike "rules of abduction" from §9's open list.
