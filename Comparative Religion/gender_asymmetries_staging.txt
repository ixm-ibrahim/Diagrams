WHAT THIS FILE IS

This file holds every staged entry across layers, in one place. Two
kinds of entries live in each layer's section:

(1) FAILED AUDIT, HELD FOR READMISSION. Items that failed at least one
    of the four filter criteria at their layer but could re-enter if a
    specific lower-layer entry or wording change is introduced. Each
    entry carries its readmission condition.

(2) PROMOTED TO NEXT-LAYER CANDIDACY. Items that passed the factual
    tests but are operating at the wrong level — they are patterns
    across entries at the current layer, not inferences drawn from
    the layer below. Held here until the next layer's file exists.

Items in this file do not carry main-list IDs. The main Layer 2,
Layer 3, Layer 4, and Layer 5 files use contiguous IDs with no gaps.
Staging IDs are separate:
  - Layer 2 failed-audit: S-*
  - Layer 2 promoted to Layer 3: L3-*
  - Layer 3 failed-audit: M-S-*
  - Layer 3 promoted to Layer 4: L4-*
  - Layer 4 failed-audit: H-S-*
  - Layer 4 promoted to Layer 5: L5-*
  - Layer 5 failed-audit: G-S-*
  - Layer 5 promoted to Layer 6: L6-*

4A. FAILED AUDIT, HELD FOR READMISSION


G-S-1. Candidate: the formal layer and the informal layer run in
       opposite directions.

       Why held: reads as a restatement of H2 with H1 and H5
       sprinkled on. H2 already says the asymmetry lives in the
       defaults rather than in the rules on paper; adding H1's
       toolkit-direction and H5's class/individual selection does
       not produce a new pattern across H-entries, only a slightly
       longer paraphrase of H2.

       Readmission condition: reframe to identify a pattern that
       the cited H-entries do not individually carry, or to name a
       mechanism across H-entries that H2 alone does not cover.


G-S-2. Candidate: the asymmetry constrains the vocabulary and the
       framings available to describe it.

       Why held: the claim is already covered by the union of H4
       (vocabulary supply and speech cost), H6 (readership
       coverage), and H7 (reception). It does not identify a
       pattern on top of that union, and it overlaps heavily with
       G1's multi-register claim.

       Readmission condition: reframe to name a specific pattern
       that is distinct from G1 and not reducible to H4 ∪ H6 ∪ H7.


G-S-3. Candidate: the framework predicts its own reception.

       Why held: restates H7 rather than identifying a pattern
       across H-entries. H7 is one of the observations L5 is built
       from, not the content of an L5 claim.

       Readmission condition: reframe as a pattern across multiple
       H-entries (for example, a pattern linking the reflexive
       shape of H7 to non-reflexive shapes in H1–H6 in a way that
       doesn't reduce to G1's multi-register claim).


G-S-4. Candidate: formal equality doctrine functions as cover — it
       leaves the asymmetry-producing defaults in place while
       appearing to address them.

       Why held: "functions as cover" is a functional-causal claim.
       The H-entries establish that formal rules are symmetric and
       defaults are not; they do not establish that the formal rule
       serves the function of keeping the default in place. The
       claim fails filter (2), smallest interpretive move.

       Readmission condition: reframe without the functional claim,
       or hold until a later layer establishes the function from
       independent evidence (e.g., cases where doctrinal shifts in
       the male-disadvantaging direction were introduced and then
       not operationalized by defaults, against cases where
       doctrinal shifts in the female-disadvantaging direction were
       operationalized).


G-S-5. Candidate: the female-direction case in each register is not
       the mirror of the male-direction case — it is the
       structurally absent case.

       Why held: sharpens H1 past what H1's cited M-entries clearly
       license. H1 says the tools get pointed in one direction and
       not the other; G-S-5 says the other direction is absent
       rather than underweighted. "Absent" is a stronger claim and
       needs evidence the H-entries don't quite carry.

       Readmission condition: demonstrate, from H-entries or from
       Layer 3 evidence, that the male-disadvantage-equivalent
       register is systematically missing — not merely
       systematically downweighted — in at least two of the
       registers G1 names.


G-S-6. Candidate: there is no neutral vocabulary in which to
       describe the asymmetry; every available register codes one
       side or the other.

       Why held: restates H4 in a stronger form without producing
       new pattern content across H-entries. H4 already covers the
       vocabulary asymmetry; G-S-6 pushes it to universality
       without the support of the other H-entries.

       Readmission condition: reframe to name a pattern across
       multiple H-entries (not just H4), or sharpen to a universal-
       vocabulary claim H4's cited evidence actually supports.


4B. PROMOTED TO LAYER 6 CANDIDACY


L6-A. Candidate: the asymmetry is self-maintaining — the same
      mechanisms that produce it also prevent its correction.

      Why held: the H-entries establish patterns, not a feedback
      loop. Moving from "the tools don't get pointed at male
      disadvantage" (H1) and "the framework takes more flak on the
      speech side" (H7) to "the tools' pointing prevents correction"
      is a dynamics claim that reaches past what L4 supports. The
      claim is plausible and may hold at a higher layer; it does
      not hold at L5 because L5 describes patterns across H-entries,
      not dynamics across time.

      Held until a Layer 6 file exists and the dynamics claim can
      be tested against a set of L5 entries and against the
      trajectory evidence the lower layers already carry (M11, I36,
      I37, I38).


L6-B. Candidate: the Layer 5 entries are not independent — G1 and
      G2 lean on mechanisms the other presupposes, and dropping
      either would weaken the other.

      Why held: this is a claim about how L5 entries couple into
      one larger pattern. It is a pattern across L5 entries rather
      than across L4 entries, which makes it L6 material by
      construction. At L5 there are only two entries; the coupling
      claim is premature to audit here.

      Held until Layer 6 and, possibly, until additional L5 entries
      are admitted that would let the coupling be tested on a
      larger set than G1 plus G2.


L6-C. Candidate: the same discourse practices that produce the
      asymmetry are also what make it hard to see.

      Why held: binds the asymmetry-producing mechanisms to the
      invisibility-producing mechanisms more tightly than H4, H6,
      and H7 do individually. "Same practices produce both" is a
      shared-function claim that reaches past the H-entries into
      causal-functional territory. Related to L6-A but narrower:
      L6-A says the asymmetry sustains itself; L6-C says the
      practices that produce it also produce its invisibility.

      Held for Layer 6. The two could turn out to be the same claim
      under a different frame, or to split cleanly; that decision
      is Layer 6's to make.
