--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRAJECTORIES OVER TIME (TS)
Concrete time-series observations: how specific sex-asymmetric
outcomes have moved over the past several decades, in which
direction, at what rate. Each entry is individually checkable
against the cited data source or an equivalent one.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TS1. Women's share of US bachelor's degrees rose from roughly 42% in
     1970 to roughly 60% by 2020, a monotonic climb across the
     entire period. The climb continued through the 1972 passage of
     Title IX and across every subsequent decade; it has not
     reversed or plateaued.

TS2. In US data, the male suicide rate rose over the past two
     decades. The female rate also rose, but by a smaller amount.
     The 3-to-4x male-to-female suicide ratio has not narrowed; in
     several recent years it has widened.

TS3. The share of US workplace fatalities that are male has
     remained roughly 92% across the past several decades. Female
     representation in safer high-status occupations has risen
     sharply over the same period; female representation in the
     most dangerous occupations (logging, fishing, roofing, mining)
     has not.

TS4. In US data, mothers' majority share of primary physical
     custody in contested cases has remained roughly stable over
     the past several decades. Several US jurisdictions have moved
     formal doctrine toward equal-custody defaults across that
     period; the actual distribution of outcomes has not converged
     to the formal doctrine.

TS5. In polling of US adults, the share of men reporting "no close
     friends" rose from roughly 3% in 1990 to roughly 15% in surveys
     conducted around 2021 (Survey Center on American Life). Women's
     reported friendship counts over the same period have been more
     stable.

TS6. US Selective Service (draft registration) has been male-only
     since its reinstatement in 1980 and remains so. Combat roles
     in the US military were formally opened to women in 2013.
     Formal sex-equality doctrine has advanced substantially over
     the same period; the male-only registration requirement has
     not changed.

TS7. The sex gap in US federal criminal sentencing has not narrowed
     meaningfully over the past several decades. The sex gap is
     larger than the black-white sentencing gap and has been
     roughly stable over the same period in which the race gap has
     been the subject of sustained reform effort.

TS8. In US Bureau of Labor Statistics data, the share of K-12
     teachers who are male declined from roughly 33% in 1980 to
     roughly 24% by 2020. The share of early childhood educators
     who are male remains in low single digits with no meaningful
     upward trend. Programs to expand female representation in
     male-dominated fields have been widespread over the same
     period; no equivalent program to expand male representation
     in teaching has been launched at comparable scale.
