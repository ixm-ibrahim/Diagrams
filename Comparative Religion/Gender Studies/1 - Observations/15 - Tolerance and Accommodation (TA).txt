--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TOLERANCE AND ACCOMMODATION (TA)
What behaviors each partner is expected to put up with, de-escalate
around, or leave over.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TA1. In relationships, men are routinely expected to tolerate a
     female partner's emotional ups and downs — stay calm, de-escalate,
     work around them. When a woman is expected to do the same for a
     male partner's emotional ups and downs, it is commonly framed as
     her enabling him, staying too long, or failing to leave.

TA2. Advice aimed at men tells them to be patient, regulate their
     emotions, and do emotional labor on behalf of their partner.
     Advice aimed at women tells them to set boundaries, advocate for
     themselves, and leave relationships that don't meet their needs.

TA3. "Walking on eggshells" is applied asymmetrically. A woman
     walking on eggshells around a man is framed as a crisis
     requiring her to get out. A man walking on eggshells around a
     woman is framed as normal relational maintenance — what a good
     partner does.

TA4. A woman raising her voice at a male partner is commonly coded
     as acceptable venting or justified anger. A man raising his
     voice at a female partner is commonly coded as verbal abuse,
     even at comparable volume and content.

TA5. When a woman gives the silent treatment, stonewalls, or
     withdraws in a relationship, it's commonly framed as
     self-protection. When a man does the same thing, it's commonly
     framed as "weaponized silence" or emotional withholding.

TA6. Women complaining about their male partners to friends — or
     consuming media content that does this on their behalf — is a
     normal, mainstream form of sociality. Men doing the same thing
     about their female partners, in the same tone, is commonly read
     as misogynistic, bitter, or a warning sign about the man.

TA7. When a woman is described as "difficult," the implicit
     expectation is that the people around her should work to
     understand and support her. When a man is described as
     "difficult," the implicit expectation is that the people around
     him should distance themselves.
