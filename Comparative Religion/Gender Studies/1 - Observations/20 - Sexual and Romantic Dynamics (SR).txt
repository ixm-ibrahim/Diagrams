--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEXUAL AND ROMANTIC DYNAMICS (SR)
Concrete patterns in dating, partner choice, and how sexual behavior
is judged.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SR1. Dating app data consistently shows that female attention
     concentrates on a minority of men (roughly the top 10–20% of
     men receive the majority of right-swipes from women), while
     male attention distributes much more evenly across women.

SR2. In younger cohorts, women now out-credential men in most
     Western countries (more bachelor's and graduate degrees) and
     out-earn men in some urban educated subsets. At the same time,
     women's stated and revealed partner preferences continue to
     favor higher-status men — men who earn more or are more
     educated than they are.

SR3. A man being involuntarily celibate past a certain age is
     socially stigmatized and treated as a mark against him. A
     woman in the same position is not stigmatized in the same way.

SR4. "Incel" — a slur for an involuntarily celibate man — is a
     mainstream pejorative with wide circulation. No equivalent
     widely-used pejorative exists for an involuntarily single
     woman.

SR5. A woman having had many sexual partners is commonly framed as
     her prerogative, her self-actualization, or her sexual freedom.
     A man having had many sexual partners is commonly framed as
     predatory, exploitative, or a warning sign about his character.
     The same behavior (extensive sexual experience) carries
     different moral weight depending on the sex of the person doing
     it.

SR6. In mainstream media and sex education, male sexual desire is
     presented as suspect by default — something to be managed,
     contained, or apologized for. Female sexual desire is presented
     as healthy by default — something to be explored, honored, or
     celebrated.
