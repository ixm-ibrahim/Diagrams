--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ONLINE DISCOURSE ECOSYSTEM (OD)
The medium most current gender discourse actually runs on. Algorithmic
sorting, harassment patterns, subculture formations, and platform
incentives that shape what readers encounter and how.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

OD1. Mainstream gender discourse — both feminist-coded and men's-
     issues-coded — increasingly takes place in algorithmically-sorted
     feed environments rather than in shared general-interest
     publications. Recommendation systems on TikTok, Instagram,
     YouTube, and X sort users into substantially different content
     streams within weeks of account creation. "What men hear about
     gender online" and "what women hear about gender online" are
     drawn from largely disjoint content sets.

OD2. Online harassment patterns are sex-asymmetric in form. Female
     targets receive a higher share of sexualized harassment (rape
     threats, sexual insults, image-based abuse). Male targets receive
     a higher share of status-attacking harassment (calling them
     losers, incels, or failures; demanding they kill themselves).
     Both patterns are real, measurable, and harmful. The female
     pattern is broadly named as harassment in mainstream coverage;
     the male pattern is less commonly named as harassment at all and
     is more often treated as ordinary online roughhousing.

OD3. Subcultures organizing around male-disadvantage observations —
     collectively called the "manosphere," including red-pill, MGTOW,
     incel, and adjacent communities — exist and contain both real
     grievance-naming and substantial misogynistic content. Mainstream
     coverage tends to flatten the distinction, treating any naming
     as continuous with the worst content (see PR1, PR2). Subcultures
     organizing around female grievances — the "4B" formation,
     certain TikTok communities, female-only forums — also exist and
     contain comparable internal variance, but mainstream coverage
     more often distinguishes their reasonable portions from their
     excesses.

OD4. The economic infrastructure of platform-monetized content
     rewards emotional intensity. Sex-specific grievance content —
     about either side — draws engagement and is therefore amplified.
     The platform incentive does not select for accurate or
     evenhanded; it selects for engaging. Claims that require both
     evidentiary sets to be held simultaneously (as this framework
     does) are at a structural disadvantage in this attention market.

OD5. Dating-app data is now the largest single body of empirical
     observation about heterosexual partner preferences and selection
     in real conditions. The data consistently confirm patterns long
     observed in survey research — concentration of female attention
     on a minority of men, women's continued preference for
     higher-status partners, and the sharply asymmetric distribution
     of right-swipes across the male population (SR1, SR2). Despite
     this, dating-app data is rarely cited in mainstream gender-
     discourse coverage; when it is, it is more often debated than
     reported.

OD6. The "tradwife" formation (women presenting traditional
     homemaking as aspirational identity) and the "passport bro"
     formation (Western men seeking partners from cultures with
     different sex-role expectations) are both responses to perceived
     defects in the current Western marriage market. They run in
     opposite directions but share a structural feature: participants
     opt out of the local mainstream gender script in favor of an
     older or foreign one.

OD7. AI companions, virtual girlfriends, and parasocial creator
     relationships are rising substitutes for human partnership on
     both sides. Male use of AI/virtual-girlfriend products is the
     more-covered side and is widely framed as a male attention or
     loneliness problem. Female use of parasocial creator
     relationships, reality TV, and serial-fiction substitutes is
     comparable in scale and less commonly framed as a substitution
     problem (cf. AB5). The cultural worry tracks the male-coded
     forms.

OD8. Platforms differ in their sex skew. Long-form podcast and
     video-essay platforms (YouTube, podcasting infrastructure) skew
     male in audience composition; short-form and feed platforms
     (TikTok, Instagram) skew female or neutral. The two halves of
     gender discourse partially live on different platforms, which
     compounds the algorithmic-sorting effect (OD1).

META OBSERVATIONS
Generalizations that surface from reading the level-1 observations
above. Each is a claim of its own; the "draws from" line names the
level-1 entries that ground it. Within a section, when a later level-1
entry reinforces a meta observation already on the page, the entry is

(OD1: "increasingly takes place in algorithmically-sorted feed
environments rather than in shared general-interest publications."
What's the venue shift?)

ODm1. Mainstream gender talk has moved from shared publications,
      where every reader could in principle see the same article,
      to algorithmic feeds, where each reader sees a custom stream.
      Draws from: OD1.

(OD1: "Recommendation systems on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and X
sort users into substantially different content streams within weeks
of account creation." How fast and how thorough is the sorting?)

ODm2. Recommendation systems pick up a new user's sex and interests
      quickly enough that the gender content they see has already
      been tailored within weeks of account creation, not months.
      Draws from: OD1.

(OD1: "'What men hear about gender online' and 'what women hear
about gender online' are drawn from largely disjoint content sets."
What's the result for the conversation?)

ODm3. The gender material men encounter online and the gender
      material women encounter online are mostly non-overlapping —
      the two sides are not reacting to the same posts, articles,
      or videos.
      Draws from: OD1, OD8.

(OD2: "Online harassment patterns are sex-asymmetric in form."
What's the rule about the shape of attacks?)

ODm4. Harassment online doesn't just target one sex more than the
      other — it uses different attack types depending on the
      target's sex.
      Draws from: OD2.

(OD2: "Female targets receive a higher share of sexualized
harassment (rape threats, sexual insults, image-based abuse)." What
is the female-side attack reaching for?)

ODm5. The attacks aimed at women cluster on the sexual axis — rape
      threats, sexual insults, image-based abuse. The vulnerability
      being targeted is sexual.
      Draws from: OD2.

(OD2: "Male targets receive a higher share of status-attacking
harassment (calling them losers, incels, or failures; demanding they
kill themselves)." What is the male-side attack reaching for?)

ODm6. The attacks aimed at men cluster on the status axis — calling
      them losers, telling them to kill themselves. The vulnerability
      being targeted is social standing and worth as a man.
      Draws from: OD2.

(OD2: "Both patterns are real, measurable, and harmful." Why does
the source observation need to assert this at all?)

ODm7. The male-targeted pattern faces a default doubt about whether
      it counts as real harm; the female-targeted pattern does not
      face the same doubt.
      Draws from: OD2.

(OD2: "The female pattern is broadly named as harassment in
mainstream coverage." What's the naming on the female side?)

ODm8. Mainstream coverage calls the female-targeted attacks
      harassment.
      Draws from: OD2.

(OD2: "the male pattern is less commonly named as harassment at all
and is more often treated as ordinary online roughhousing." What's
the naming on the male side?)

ODm9. The same coverage treats the male-targeted attacks — telling
      men to kill themselves — as ordinary online roughhousing,
      rather than as harassment.
      Draws from: OD2.

(OD2: the asymmetry across ODm8 and ODm9. Same act category, opposite

ODm10. What gets named as harassment depends on the sex of the
       target, not just on what was done.
       Draws from: OD2.

(OD3: "Subcultures organizing around male-disadvantage observations
... contain both real grievance-naming and substantial misogynistic

ODm11. The manosphere contains both legitimate naming of male-

ODm12. Mainstream coverage of the manosphere collapses any male-

(OD3: "Subcultures organizing around female grievances ... contain

ODm13. Female-grievance subcultures (4B, certain TikTok communities,

       Draws from: OD3.

(OD3: the asymmetry across ODm12 and ODm14. Same internal mix on

ODm15. Whether mainstream coverage separates a grievance
       subculture's reasonable parts from its worst content depends

       Draws from: OD3.

(OD4: "The economic infrastructure of platform-monetized content
rewards emotional intensity." What's the money rule?)

ODm16. Platforms make money from engagement, and engagement runs on
       emotional intensity — so the revenue pipe rewards heat over
       calm.
       Draws from: OD4.

(OD4: "Sex-specific grievance content — about either side — draws
engagement and is therefore amplified." What does the algorithm
amplify, and is the amplification side-neutral?)

ODm17. Grievance content draws engagement regardless of which side
       it comes from, and the algorithm amplifies it accordingly.
       The amplification picks heat, not a side.
       Draws from: OD4.

(OD4: "The platform incentive does not select for accurate or
evenhanded; it selects for engaging." What's the selection rule, in
its own right?)

ODm18. The amplification filter selects for what's engaging, not for
       what's accurate or evenhanded — these are different filters
       and produce different content.
       Draws from: OD4.

(OD4: "Claims that require both evidentiary sets to be held
simultaneously ... are at a structural disadvantage in this attention

ODm19. Claims that hold the male-side and female-side evidence at
       once lose to one-sided heated claims in this venue. The
       medium rewards picking a side.
       Draws from: OD4.

ODm23. When dating-app evidence does enter coverage, it appears as
