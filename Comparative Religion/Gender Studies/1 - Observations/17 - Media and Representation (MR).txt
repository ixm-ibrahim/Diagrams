--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEDIA AND REPRESENTATION (MR)
How men and women are depicted on screen and in commercial imagery.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MR1. Fathers in commercials and sitcoms are disproportionately shown
     as incompetent at domestic tasks (cooking, laundry, childcare)
     and needing a competent mother to step in and fix things.
     Mothers in the same media are not routinely shown as incompetent
     at those tasks.

MR2. Villains in children's media (Disney films, animated series,
     major children's franchises) are disproportionately male. Female
     villains occur but are notably rarer.

MR3. In modern action films, a small woman physically beating up
     larger men is shown as normal and requires no in-story
     explanation for how she can do it. A man physically overpowering
     a woman of similar or smaller size, when it appears on screen,
     nearly always marks that man as the villain.

MR4. "Girl power" is a mainstream slogan directly marketed to
     children on clothing, toys, and school supplies. "Boy power" is
     absent from mainstream child-directed branding.

MR5. In mixed male-female ensemble casts, the female character is
     frequently written as the smartest, most competent, or most
     morally correct member of the group. A mixed cast in which a
     man holds that role is now rare, and when it occurs, the story
     often sets him up to be humbled or exposed.

MR6. Male nudity in mainstream film is typically used for comedy
     (the embarrassed naked guy running through a scene). Female
     nudity is typically used for seduction or vulnerability.

MR7. Commercials showing men doing childcare or housework often
     show them fumbling it — wrong ingredient, diaper on wrong,
     child running riot. Commercials showing women doing the same
     tasks do not routinely show them fumbling.

MR8. A woman hitting or slapping a man in film and television is
     routinely played for laughs or framed as her righteous
     response. A man hitting or slapping a woman is consistently
     framed as assault and presented with serious weight.

MR9. Female characters who behave badly on screen — cruel,
     manipulative, explosive — are usually given surrounding context
     that explains the behavior: a trauma, a provocation, a
     situation that produced it. Male characters who behave the same
     way are usually presented as simply being that kind of person,
     with no comparable contextualizing.
