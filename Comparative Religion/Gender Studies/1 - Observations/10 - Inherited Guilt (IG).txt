INHERITED GUILT (IG)
The expectation that a living person should feel and display moral
guilt for actions taken by earlier members of their group, before any
of their own conduct has been assessed. Distinct from inherited
oppressor labels (VO11) and inherited collective responsibility
(VO12) — those are about how the label is applied; this section is
about the inner state and its performance.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

IG1. Inherited guilt is distinct from inherited responsibility (VO12).
     Responsibility is an assignment ("you are accountable for X").
     Guilt is an inner state ("you should feel bad about X").
     Mainstream gender discourse on men commonly asks for both:
     acknowledge the inherited responsibility, and display the
     corresponding emotional state. The two are conflated in everyday
     usage but are not the same ask.

IG2. The structural template is theological. Original sin — moral
     stain inherited at birth, requiring ongoing acknowledgment and
     atonement, never fully discharged — is the closest historical
     analog. Secular inherited-guilt frames inherit much of this
     structure (the flaw is born-in, atonement is ongoing, full
     release is unavailable) without the theological vocabulary.
     The structure operates whether or not the speakers identify it
     as theological.

IG3. The performance of guilt is what gets ratified. Whether the
     person actually feels guilty is uninspected. A man who publicly
     says "I see how I've benefited from patriarchy and I'm working
     on myself" is in good standing whether or not the inner state
     matches the words. The system rewards the public display, not
     the felt experience — which gives the system a hollow-performance
     dynamic and rewards the rhetorically fluent over the genuinely
     reflective.

IG4. Refusal of inherited guilt is read as evidence of the underlying
     flaw, structurally identical to the fragility move (LR11, DP10).
     Pushing back on the inherited-guilt frame becomes proof that the
     frame applies. The refusal cannot get a hearing on its merits —
     the closed loop closes around the response.

IG5. The grant of innocence is asymmetric. Women are presumptively
     innocent of inherited collective harms (VO12). Men are
     presumptively guilty until disclaimed, and disclaiming does not
     fully clear the label (VO11). The inherited-guilt regime
     therefore operates on men in a way it does not operate on women,
     even where the underlying historical record is mixed (women's
     historical participation in restrictive systems, female support
     for restrictive gender roles, female-led witch-hunt waves,
     women's lobbying for maternal-preference custody doctrines).

IG6. Inherited guilt is not discharged by individual conduct. A man
     who has personally treated women well, raised daughters,
     supported female colleagues, opposed harassment, and refrained
     from any specifically attributable wrong remains within the
     inherited-guilt frame because the frame is not indexed to his
     conduct. This separates inherited guilt from accountability:
     accountability would be discharged by addressing specific
     wrongs; inherited guilt is structurally not dischargeable.

IG7. Inherited guilt is increasingly addressed to children. Boys in
     current Western primary and secondary education are commonly
     addressed as already implicated in patriarchy, toxic
     masculinity, or men's collective record, before they have any
     conduct to be assessed by. Girls are not typically addressed as
     already implicated in any female collective record. The
     asymmetry shapes early identity formation in a way the same
     asymmetry applied to adults does not.

IG8. Inherited guilt provides ongoing moral leverage. Each generation
     can be approached as still owing something for the previous
     generation's conduct, regardless of any individual or
     generational change. The leverage does not expire, does not
     diminish with time, and does not respond to demonstrated
     change. This is unusual among moral frameworks and resembles a
     permanent debt more than a discharged offense.

IG9. The phrase "do the work" — naming a continuous, never-completed
     labor of self-examination and acknowledgment — is the
     contemporary form of the atonement structure (IG2). The work is
     unending by design; framing it as completable would dissolve
     the leverage IG8 describes.

IG10. Inherited guilt and individual moral agency are in tension.
      Holding a person responsible for their own conduct requires
      treating that conduct as theirs and as decisive. Holding the
      same person responsible for inherited collective conduct
      requires treating their group membership as decisive instead.
      Used together, the two moves let the speaker pick whichever
      framing is more damning at the moment — individual conduct when
      the conduct is bad, group inheritance when it isn't. The
      person being addressed has no available framing that defeats
      both moves.

IG11. The reciprocal move — addressing women collectively to feel
      guilt for harms historically done in women's name (witch-trial
      accusations, support for restrictive role-codes, maternal-
      preference custody lobbying, false accusations) — is not in
      mainstream cultural use. The asymmetry is not that the
      historical record on the women's side is empty; it is that the
      inherited-guilt move is licensed in one direction and not the
      other.

META OBSERVATIONS
Generalizations that surface from reading the level-1 observations
above. Each is a claim of its own; the "draws from" line names the
level-1 entries that ground it. Within a section, when a later level-1
entry reinforces a meta observation already on the page, the entry is
added to that meta observation's "draws from" rather than starting a
new one. Parentheticals show the thinking that produced each meta
observation — what was interrogated and what surfaced.

(IG1: "Inherited guilt is distinct from inherited responsibility."
What's the rule about telling them apart?)

IGm1. Asking someone to be accountable for something and asking them
      to feel a particular way about it are different kinds of
      demand. One can be made without the other.
      Draws from: IG1.

(IG1: "Mainstream gender discourse on men commonly asks for both:
acknowledge the inherited responsibility, and display the
corresponding emotional state." Why both?)

IGm2. Mainstream gender discourse on men bundles two demands
      together — accept the responsibility, and produce the matching
      feeling — and treats both as part of the same ask.
      Draws from: IG1.

(IG1: "The two are conflated in everyday usage but are not the same
ask." Why does conflation matter?)

IGm3. Conflating responsibility with guilt lets a demand for a
      particular inner state ride along with a demand for
      accountability, without the inner-state demand having to be
      argued for on its own.
      Draws from: IG1.

(IG2: "The structural template is theological. Original sin — moral
stain inherited at birth, requiring ongoing acknowledgment and
atonement, never fully discharged — is the closest historical
analog." Why call it theological?)

IGm4. The shape of inherited guilt — a stain you're born with,
      ongoing penance, no full release — comes straight out of
      religious doctrine, specifically the Christian doctrine of
      original sin.
      Draws from: IG2.

(IG2: "Secular inherited-guilt frames inherit much of this structure
... without the theological vocabulary." Why does the structure
carry over without the religious words?)

IGm5. Secular gender discourse keeps the religious shape but drops
      the religious vocabulary, so the source of the structure is
      not visible at first glance — concrete instances: the public
      acknowledgment ritual ("I see how I've benefited from
      patriarchy and I'm working on myself") and the contemporary
      atonement label ("do the work").
      Draws from: IG2, IG3, IG9.

(IG2: "The structure operates whether or not the speakers identify
it as theological." Why does it work either way?)

IGm6. A structural pattern keeps working whether or not the people
      using it can name where it came from. Knowing the source is
      not required for the structure to do its work.
      Draws from: IG2.

(IG3: "The performance of guilt is what gets ratified. Whether the
person actually feels guilty is uninspected." What's the rule about
display versus feeling?)

IGm7. What the system actually checks is the public display of
      guilt, not the inner state — even though the surface language
      is about the inner state.
      Draws from: IG3.

(IG3: "Whether the person actually feels guilty is uninspected." Why
not inspect?)

IGm8. An inner state cannot be inspected from outside, so the
      visible performance gets accepted as a stand-in. The system
      checks what it can see.
      Draws from: IG3.

(IG3: "rewards the rhetorically fluent over the genuinely
reflective." Why does fluency win?)

IGm9. Verbal fluency produces the right display on demand;
      reflection often does not. A system that grades on display
      therefore rewards fluent speakers over thoughtful ones,
      regardless of how hard each is actually thinking.
      Draws from: IG3.

(IG4: "Refusal of inherited guilt is read as evidence of the
underlying flaw." What's the rule?)

IGm10. Refusing the inherited-guilt label is treated as proof that
       the person has the very flaw the label was about.
       Draws from: IG4.

(IG4: "structurally identical to the fragility move (LR11, DP10).
Pushing back on the inherited-guilt frame becomes proof that the
frame applies." What's the structural property?)

IGm11. Inherited-guilt refusal runs as a closed loop: agreement
       counts as confirmation, and disagreement counts as
       confirmation too. This is the same shape as the "fragility"
       charge in DP10 — naming someone "fragile" if they react and
       "in denial" if they don't.
       Draws from: IG4.

(IG4: "the refusal cannot get a hearing on its merits — the closed
loop closes around the response." What does the closed loop do to
the response's chances?)

IGm12. Inside a closed-loop frame, a response cannot be evaluated
       on whether it is correct. The framing has already supplied
       the verdict before the content gets read.
       Draws from: IG4.

(IG5: "The grant of innocence is asymmetric." What's the rule about
starting points?)

IGm13. Men and women enter the inherited-guilt regime from
       different default starting points — guilty for men, innocent
       for women.
       Draws from: IG5, IG7.

(IG5: "Women are presumptively innocent of inherited collective
harms." Why does the women's side get a pass?)

IGm14. Women's historical collective record is treated as not
       generating inherited guilt for living women. The default
       move is to leave it alone rather than to bring it forward.
       Draws from: IG5, IG7, IG11.

(IG5: "Men are presumptively guilty until disclaimed." Why does
men's side start guilty?)

IGm15. Men start the inherited-guilt regime already labeled. The
       default position is one they have to actively argue their
       way out of.
       Draws from: IG5, IG7.

(IG5: "disclaiming does not fully clear the label." Why doesn't a
disclaim work?)

IGm16. Even a man who explicitly rejects the inherited-guilt label
       does not fully come out from under it. The disclaim reduces
       the label without removing it.
       Draws from: IG5.

(IG5: "even where the underlying historical record is mixed
(women's historical participation in restrictive systems, female
support for restrictive gender roles, female-led witch-hunt waves,
women's lobbying for maternal-preference custody doctrines)." Why
not the empty-record explanation?)

IGm17. The asymmetry is not because the women's-side historical
       record is empty. Concrete instances of female collective
       participation include support for restrictive gender role-
       codes, female-led witch-hunt waves, and women's lobbying for
       maternal-preference custody doctrines. The record exists;
       the inherited-guilt move just isn't run against it.
       Draws from: IG5, IG11.

(IG6: "Inherited guilt is not discharged by individual conduct ...
the frame is not indexed to his conduct." Why doesn't conduct move
the label?)

IGm18. Personal conduct doesn't move the inherited-guilt label,
       because the label was never measuring personal conduct in
       the first place. It was always about group membership.
       Draws from: IG6, IG10.

(IG6: "This separates inherited guilt from accountability:
accountability would be discharged by addressing specific wrongs;
inherited guilt is structurally not dischargeable." What's the
indexing difference?)

IGm19. Accountability is indexed to specific wrongs that can be
       identified and addressed. Inherited guilt is indexed to
       group membership, which cannot be exited. They look like
       similar moral language from outside but they discharge
       differently.
       Draws from: IG6.

(IG6: "structurally not dischargeable." Why structurally?)

IGm20. The non-dischargeability is built into the design of the
       frame. No action available to the labeled person — apology,
       reform, restitution, time — would clear it, because clearing
       it was never an option the frame allowed for.
       Draws from: IG6, IG8.

(IG7: "Inherited guilt is increasingly addressed to children." What's
the rule?)

IGm21. The inherited-guilt frame is being applied earlier in life
       than before, including to children in primary and secondary
       schools.
       Draws from: IG7.

(IG7: "Boys ... are commonly addressed as already implicated in
patriarchy, toxic masculinity, or men's collective record, before
they have any conduct to be assessed by." Why before any conduct?)

IGm22. Boys are addressed with the inherited-guilt label before any
       of their own conduct exists to be evaluated. The label is
       applied to a near-empty record.
       Draws from: IG7.

(IG7: "Girls are not typically addressed as already implicated in
any female collective record." What about the other side of the
classroom?)

IGm23. Girls in the same classrooms are not addressed as carrying
       any female collective record. There is no parallel
       curriculum module asking girls to examine female
       participation in restrictive role-codes, witch-hunt waves,
       or maternal-preference custody lobbying.
       Draws from: IG7.

(IG7: "The asymmetry shapes early identity formation in a way the
same asymmetry applied to adults does not." Why does it land harder
on kids?)

IGm24. Applied to children, the inherited-guilt frame shapes
       identity before the child has the experience or vocabulary
       to push back against the framing. Adults can refuse a frame
       they recognize; children mostly cannot recognize it as a
       frame.
       Draws from: IG7.

(IG8: "Inherited guilt provides ongoing moral leverage." What does
the leverage look like in practice?)

IGm25. Whoever speaks against the labeled group can return to make
       new moral demands at any later point, because the labeled
       group can never reach a "this is settled" state.
       Draws from: IG8.

(IG8: "Each generation can be approached as still owing something
for the previous generation's conduct." Why does the debt cross
generations?)

IGm26. The debt is renewed at each generation rather than ending
       with the people who actually did the original conduct.
       Living people are charged for acts they were not part of.
       Draws from: IG8.

(IG8: "The leverage does not expire, does not diminish with time,
and does not respond to demonstrated change. This is unusual among
moral frameworks." How is it unusual?)

IGm27. Most moral frames let an offender clear an offense — by
       changed conduct, by restitution, or by enough time. The
       inherited-guilt frame is unusual in offering none of these
       exits.
       Draws from: IG8.

(IG9: "'Do the work' — naming a continuous, never-completed labor
of self-examination and acknowledgment — is the contemporary form
of the atonement structure (IG2)." Why does this phrase exist?)

IGm28. Penance for inherited guilt has a current secular name —
       "do the work" — and a current form, even after the religious
       vocabulary has been dropped.
       Draws from: IG9.

(IG9: "The work is unending by design; framing it as completable
would dissolve the leverage IG8 describes." Why must the work stay
unending?)

IGm29. The labor is set up so it can never be finished. Allowing
       it to be finishable would end the ongoing claim on the
       labeled group, which is the part of the system that does
       the most work.
       Draws from: IG9.

(IG10: "Holding a person responsible for their own conduct requires
treating that conduct as theirs and as decisive. Holding the same
person responsible for inherited collective conduct requires
treating their group membership as decisive instead." Why are these
in tension?)

IGm30. Treating someone's own conduct as the decisive thing about
       them and treating their group membership as the decisive
       thing about them cannot both be the active rule at the same
       time. One has to displace the other.
       Draws from: IG10.

(IG10: "the two moves let the speaker pick whichever framing is
more damning at the moment — individual conduct when the conduct is
bad, group inheritance when it isn't." What does having both
available do for the speaker?)

IGm31. When two framings are both available, the speaker can
       switch between them so the verdict stays negative whichever
       way the facts run — concrete instance: bad personal conduct
       gets pinned on the individual, good personal conduct gets
       dissolved into group inheritance.
       Draws from: IG10.

(IG10: "The person being addressed has no available framing that
defeats both moves." What's the listener's position?)

IGm32. The person being addressed has no single response that
       answers both framings, since each requires a different kind
       of defense. Whichever defense they pick leaves the other
       framing untouched.
       Draws from: IG10.

(IG11: "The reciprocal move ... is not in mainstream cultural use."
What about the missing direction?)

IGm33. The mirror move — addressing women collectively to feel
       guilt for historical harms done in women's name, such as
       witch-trial accusations, support for restrictive role-codes,
       or maternal-preference custody lobbying — is not a standard
       cultural move and does not appear in mainstream curricula,
       sermons, or political speech.
       Draws from: IG11.

(IG11: "the historical record on the women's side is empty" was
already addressed by IGm17. IG11 added to IGm17's draws-from. The
new angle below is about what kind of fact the asymmetry actually
is.)

(IG11: "the inherited-guilt move is licensed in one direction and
not the other." Why one direction?)

IGm34. The inherited-guilt move is licensed only in one direction —
       applied to men but not to women — and the licensing is a
       cultural choice, not a deduction from the historical record.
       Either side's record could in principle ground the move;
       only one side's actually does.
       Draws from: IG11.
