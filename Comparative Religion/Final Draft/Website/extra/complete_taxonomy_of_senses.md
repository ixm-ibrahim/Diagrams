# Complete Taxonomy of Senses

### A systematic catalog of every modality by which phenomena become awareness

**Methodology:** Observe every distinguishable *category of qualia* (qualitative character of experience), then identify the receptor system or neural substrate responsible. Where no accepted term exists, a neologism is coined (marked with **†**) from Greek/Latin roots, with rationale given.

---

## I. EXTEROCEPTION — The External World

These senses transduce stimuli originating outside the body.

### 1. Vision (Photoreception)
- **Phenomena:** Electromagnetic radiation (~380–700 nm)
- **Organ/receptor:** Retinal photoreceptors — rods (scotopic), cones (photopic: S/M/L for color), and intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells (ipRGCs for circadian/ambient brightness)
- **Qualia:** Color, brightness, shape, depth, motion, texture-at-a-distance

### 2. Audition (Audioception)
- **Phenomena:** Pressure waves in a medium (~20 Hz–20 kHz in air)
- **Organ/receptor:** Cochlear hair cells (inner ear), organized tonotopically
- **Qualia:** Pitch, timbre, loudness, spatial direction of sound, rhythm

### 3. Olfaction (Olfactoception)
- **Phenomena:** Airborne volatile molecules
- **Organ/receptor:** Olfactory receptor neurons in the olfactory epithelium (~400 receptor types in humans)
- **Qualia:** Smell — an enormous, poorly lexicalized qualitative space

### 4. Gustation (Gustaoception)
- **Phenomena:** Dissolved chemical compounds
- **Organ/receptor:** Taste receptor cells in taste buds (tongue, palate, epiglottis)
- **Qualia:** Sweet, salty, sour, bitter, umami; possibly oleogustus (fat), kokumi (richness), starchiness, and metallic taste — each mediated by distinct receptor pathways

### 5. Tactition / Mechanoreception (Cutaneous)
- **Phenomena:** Mechanical force applied to the body surface
- **Organ/receptor:** Four principal mechanoreceptor types in glabrous skin: Meissner's corpuscles (light touch/flutter), Merkel cells (pressure/texture), Pacinian corpuscles (deep vibration), Ruffini endings (skin stretch/sustained pressure). Hair follicle receptors in hairy skin.
- **Qualia:** Pressure, texture, vibration, tickle, flutter, skin-stretch

### 6. Thermoception
- **Phenomena:** Thermal energy (temperature of surfaces, air, and radiant heat)
- **Organ/receptor:** Thermoreceptors in the skin and mucous membranes — warm-sensitive (TRPV3, TRPV4 channels) and cold-sensitive (TRPM8, TRPA1 channels) free nerve endings
- **Qualia:** Warmth, heat, coolness, cold — qualitatively distinct from pain, though extreme temperatures recruit nociceptors

### 7. Nociception
- **Phenomena:** Actual or potential tissue damage (mechanical, thermal, or chemical)
- **Organ/receptor:** Nociceptors — free nerve endings (Aδ fibers for sharp/fast pain; C fibers for dull/slow pain), distributed throughout skin, muscle, joints, viscera, and most other tissues
- **Qualia:** Sharp pain, burning pain, aching pain, throbbing pain, referred pain — distinct subtypes with distinct qualitative character

### 8. Pruriception
- **Phenomena:** Pruritogens (histamine, serotonin, proteases, bile salts, etc.)
- **Organ/receptor:** Pruriceptors — a distinct subset of C-fiber neurons (expressing MrgprA3/MrgprC11 in mice, MrgprX1 in humans), primarily in skin. Neurally and pharmacologically separable from nociception.
- **Qualia:** Itch — qualitatively distinct from pain, with its own unique compulsion (scratching vs. withdrawal)

### 9. Magnetoception (debated in humans)
- **Phenomena:** Earth's geomagnetic field
- **Organ/receptor:** Uncertain; candidates include cryptochrome proteins in the retina (radical pair mechanism) and biogenic magnetite deposits. Robust in migratory birds and sea turtles; evidence in humans is weak but non-zero (e.g., EEG responses to field rotations, Caltech 2019).
- **Qualia:** If present, likely subliminal — no known conscious quale in humans. Included for completeness as a potential contributor to spatial orientation.

## II. PROPRIOCEPTION & SPATIAL BODY SCHEMA

These senses inform awareness of the body's configuration and movement through space.

### 10. Proprioception (Kinesthesia)
- **Phenomena:** Position, tension, and movement of muscles, tendons, and joints
- **Organ/receptor:** Muscle spindles (muscle length/velocity), Golgi tendon organs (tendon tension), joint capsule receptors (joint angle)
- **Qualia:** Sense of limb position, bodily posture, and the effort/force of movement — even with eyes closed

### 11. Equilibrioception (Vestibular Sense)
- **Phenomena:** Angular acceleration (rotation) and linear acceleration (including gravity)
- **Organ/receptor:** Vestibular apparatus of the inner ear — three semicircular canals (angular acceleration via endolymph flow over hair cells) and two otolith organs (utricle and saccule, for linear acceleration and head tilt via calcium carbonate crystals on hair cells)
- **Qualia:** Balance, vertigo, dizziness, sense of which-way-is-up, sense of self-motion

### 12. Spatial Egoception **†**
- **Phenomena:** The integrated sense of where one's body is in allocentric (world-centered) space — beyond joint angles (proprioception) or vestibular tilt
- **Organ/receptor:** Not a single receptor but an integrative cortical computation — primarily hippocampal place cells, entorhinal grid cells, and head-direction cells, integrating proprioceptive, vestibular, and visual inputs
- **Qualia:** The experience of "being here," spatial orientation, knowing which direction you face and where you are in a room. Distinct from proprioception (which is body-relative) and vision (which provides input but is not the same as the resultant spatial awareness)
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *ego* (self) + Latin *ceptio* (perception) — the sense of *where the self is*. "Allocentric spatial awareness" is the descriptive phrase but lacks a single-word sense name.

## III. INTEROCEPTION — The Internal Body

These senses monitor the physiological condition of the body's interior. The primary cortical convergence zone is the **insular cortex**, sometimes called the "interoceptive cortex."

### 13. Cardioception
- **Phenomena:** Cardiac activity — heartbeat, rhythm, force
- **Organ/receptor:** Baroreceptors in the aortic arch and carotid sinus; cardiac mechanoreceptors; afferent signals via the vagus nerve. The insular cortex is the primary site of conscious heartbeat perception.
- **Qualia:** Heartbeat awareness, palpitations, cardiac anxiety. Sensitivity varies greatly between individuals (heartbeat detection tasks).

### 14. Pneumoception **†**
- **Phenomena:** Respiratory state — air hunger, breathlessness, lung inflation
- **Organ/receptor:** Pulmonary stretch receptors, peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid and aortic bodies sensing O₂/CO₂/pH), central chemoreceptors (medullary, sensing CO₂/H⁺), chest wall mechanoreceptors
- **Qualia:** Urge to breathe, air hunger, dyspnea, satisfaction of a deep breath, sense of respiratory effort
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *pneuma* (breath/air). The respiratory drive is well-studied physiologically but lacks a standard single-word "sense" name.

### 15. Gastroception **†**
- **Phenomena:** Gastric and intestinal state — distension, motility, emptiness, nausea
- **Organ/receptor:** Vagal mechanoreceptors and chemoreceptors in the gut wall; enteric nervous system afferents; serotonin (5-HT₃) receptors in gut mucosa (nausea pathway); ghrelin and GLP-1 signaling to the hypothalamus and brainstem
- **Qualia:** Hunger, satiety, fullness, stomach "emptiness," nausea, gut "butterflies," cramps
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *gaster* (stomach). "Hunger" and "nausea" are typically listed as separate interoceptive sub-modalities, but they share a receptor system and converge as awareness of the alimentary state. Grouped here as one sense with multiple qualia, analogous to how vision has color, shape, and motion.

### 16. Dipsoception **†**
- **Phenomena:** Hydration state — osmolarity, blood volume, oral/pharyngeal dryness
- **Organ/receptor:** Osmoreceptors in the organum vasculosum of the lamina terminalis (OVLT) and subfornical organ (SFO) detecting blood osmolarity; baroreceptors sensing blood volume; oral/pharyngeal dryness receptors; angiotensin II signaling
- **Qualia:** Thirst, mouth dryness, the satisfaction of drinking
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *dipsa* (thirst). Thirst is universally recognized as a distinct sensation but has no standard "-ception" name.

### 17. Visceroception
- **Phenomena:** State of the internal organs broadly — distension, inflammation, spasm, referred sensations
- **Organ/receptor:** Visceral afferents (primarily C-fibers and Aδ fibers) innervating the peritoneum, pleura, pericardium, bladder wall, uterus, and other organs. Convergence on spinal dorsal horn neurons (producing referred pain patterns).
- **Qualia:** Bladder fullness, rectal fullness, menstrual cramps, generalized visceral discomfort, urge to urinate/defecate. These are qualitatively distinct from somatic pain.

### 18. Baroception (Vascular)
- **Phenomena:** Blood pressure
- **Organ/receptor:** Baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch (stretch-sensitive mechanoreceptors in vessel walls)
- **Qualia:** Largely subliminal; can surface as lightheadedness (hypotension), head pressure (hypertension), or the feeling of "blood rushing." Baroreceptor firing also modulates pain perception and emotional reactivity, so it shapes awareness indirectly.

### 19. Thermoceptive Interoception (Core Temperature) **†**
- **Phenomena:** Core body temperature (distinct from skin thermoception, which is exteroceptive)
- **Organ/receptor:** Central thermoreceptors in the hypothalamus (preoptic area), spinal cord, and abdominal viscera
- **Qualia:** Feeling feverish, chills, the systemic sense that one is "overheating" or "freezing" — distinct from touching a hot surface. The phenomenology of fever is qualitatively different from external thermal contact.
- **Note:** Often conflated with cutaneous thermoception but constitutes a functionally and experientially distinct channel.

### 20. Chemesthesis
- **Phenomena:** Chemical irritation of mucous membranes and skin (capsaicin, menthol, ethanol, CO₂, etc.)
- **Organ/receptor:** Trigeminal nerve (CN V) free nerve endings in the nasal, oral, and ocular mucosa; TRPV1 (capsaicin/heat), TRPA1 (mustard oil/wasabi), TRPM8 (menthol/cool) channels on somatosensory neurons
- **Qualia:** Spiciness/burn (capsaicin), cooling (menthol), tingling (carbonation), nasal sting (ammonia). Chemesthesis is distinct from both taste and smell — it is a somatosensory response to chemicals, not a chemosensory one.

### 21. Fatigueception **†**
- **Phenomena:** Systemic fatigue/energy state — not local muscle soreness but the global sense of depletion or vitality
- **Organ/receptor:** Multi-signal integration: adenosine accumulation (tracked by basal forebrain), cytokine signaling (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α from immune activity), glycogen depletion signals, circadian input from the suprachiasmatic nucleus, hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis cortisol levels
- **Qualia:** Tiredness, sleepiness, exhaustion, energy/vitality, the revived feeling after rest. Phenomenologically distinct from local pain or muscle fatigue — it is a *whole-organism state*.
- **Rationale for term:** No standard term exists for the sense of systemic energy level. "Fatigue" is the closest common word.

### 22. Libidoception **†**
- **Phenomena:** Sexual arousal state — genital engorgement, hormonal arousal, desire
- **Organ/receptor:** Genital mechanoreceptors and free nerve endings (via pudendal nerve), hormonal signaling (testosterone, estrogen, dopamine), hypothalamic and limbic integration
- **Qualia:** Sexual arousal, desire, genital sensation as a distinct qualitative modality. Phenomenologically distinct from mere tactile sensation of the same anatomy — the same touch registers differently depending on arousal state.
- **Rationale for term:** From Latin *libido* (desire). Sexual arousal is universally recognized as a distinct state of awareness but is rarely classified as a "sense."

## IV. CHRONOCEPTION & TEMPORAL AWARENESS

### 23. Chronoception
- **Phenomena:** The passage of time
- **Organ/receptor:** No single receptor. Current models implicate: the suprachiasmatic nucleus (circadian scale), cortico-striatal-thalamic circuits (seconds-to-minutes scale, especially the basal ganglia and supplementary motor area), and dopaminergic modulation (dopamine speeds or slows the "internal clock"). The cerebellum contributes to sub-second timing.
- **Qualia:** Duration perception, the feeling that time is passing slowly or quickly, temporal ordering of events, rhythm perception, the "felt length" of a waiting period

## V. AFFECTIVE & HEDONIC SENSES — Felt Valence

These senses convert internal and external states into felt evaluations: good/bad, pleasant/unpleasant, attractive/aversive.

### 24. Hedonic Valence (Hedoception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** The pleasure–displeasure dimension of any experience
- **Organ/receptor:** "Hedonic hotspots" — discrete regions in the nucleus accumbens shell, ventral pallidum, parabrachial nucleus, and orbitofrontal cortex where opioid and endocannabinoid signaling converts sensory signals into felt pleasure. Displeasure involves overlapping but distinct circuits (anterior insula, anterior cingulate cortex).
- **Qualia:** Pleasure, enjoyment, suffering, aversion, comfort, discomfort. This is not a separate input channel but a *valence stamp* applied to signals from nearly every other sense. It is included as a distinct sense because *the same stimulus* (e.g., a warm bath) can be perceived with or without hedonic coloring, and the pleasure/displeasure dimension is qualitatively distinct from the sensory content itself.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *hedone* (pleasure). The hedonic dimension is well-studied in affective neuroscience but is not standardly called a "sense."

### 25. Emotion (Thymoception **†** / Affectoception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** Discrete or dimensional emotional states — fear, anger, joy, sadness, disgust, surprise, shame, guilt, awe, love, jealousy, etc.
- **Organ/receptor:** Limbic system broadly (amygdala for threat/fear, ventral striatum for reward anticipation, anterior insula for disgust and emotional awareness, anterior cingulate for emotional conflict/suffering, prefrontal cortex for appraisal and regulation). The *awareness* of emotion (as distinct from the physiological emotional response) depends critically on the **anterior insular cortex**, which integrates interoceptive, homeostatic, and affective signals into a conscious feeling.
- **Qualia:** The felt quality of each emotion — fear *feels* qualitatively different from sadness, which differs from joy. These are not merely interoceptive body states (though they include them); they carry appraisal, action-readiness, and social meaning.
- **Rationale for term:** *Thymos* (Greek: spirit, emotion, will) or *affectus* (Latin: state of mind, emotion). Preferred: **thymoception** — the sense by which one becomes aware of one's emotional state.
- **Note on interoception overlap:** The James–Lange tradition collapses emotion into interoception (we "feel" our bodily responses). Modern constructionist theories (Barrett, 2017) treat emotions as brain-constructed categories imposed on interoceptive and exteroceptive signals. Whether emotion is a separate sense or a higher-order interpretation of interoception is debated — but phenomenologically, the awareness of "being afraid" is qualitatively distinct from the awareness of "heart racing," justifying separate listing.

## VI. COGNITIVE SENSES — Awareness of Mental Operations

These are the most philosophically contentious entries. They are included because they name genuinely distinct categories of qualia — things that appear in awareness with a distinctive phenomenal character.

### 26. Nooception **†** (Thought-Awareness / Metacognition)
- **Phenomena:** The occurrence of thoughts — verbal, conceptual, abstract, or imagistic
- **Substrate:** Prefrontal cortex (especially dorsolateral PFC for executive thought), default mode network (spontaneous thought/mind-wandering), anterior prefrontal cortex (metacognitive monitoring — knowing that you are thinking)
- **Qualia:** The experience of having a thought arise, the felt difference between deliberate reasoning and spontaneous ideation, "inner speech," conceptual insight, the tip-of-the-tongue state
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *nous* (mind/intellect). Metacognition is the closest existing term, but it refers to *monitoring* of cognition, not the raw awareness-of-thought itself.

### 27. Phantasioception **†** (Mental Imagery / Imagination)
- **Phenomena:** Internally generated sensory-like representations — visual imagery, auditory imagery, motor imagery, etc.
- **Substrate:** Modality-specific sensory cortices activated top-down (visual cortex for visual imagery, auditory cortex for auditory imagery), regulated by prefrontal and parietal networks. Absence of this sense: **aphantasia** (inability to generate voluntary mental imagery).
- **Qualia:** "Seeing with the mind's eye," hearing a tune in one's head, imagining a taste, mentally rehearsing a movement. These are qualitatively similar to but distinguishable from actual percepts (less vivid, less stable, voluntarily directed). Each sensory modality has its own imagistic channel.

### 28. Mnemoception **†** (Mnemonic Awareness / Ecphory)
- **Phenomena:** The involuntary or voluntary arising of memories in awareness
- **Substrate:** Hippocampus (episodic memory encoding/retrieval), medial temporal lobe, and the re-activation of neocortical patterns from original experience; the subjective component of retrieval ("ecphory") involves prefrontal monitoring and the parietal cortex (for recollective experience vs. familiarity)
- **Qualia:** The experience of remembering — the felt pastness of a recalled event (Tulving's "autonoetic consciousness"), the warmth of nostalgia, the intrusive flash of a traumatic memory, the déjà vu experience (false familiarity signal). Distinct from imagination because it carries a phenomenal tag of *having-happened*.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *mneme* (memory). "Recall" and "retrieval" are cognitive terms; no "-ception" exists for the *experience* of a memory arising in awareness.

### 29. Familiaroception **†** (Sense of Recognition / Familiarity)
- **Phenomena:** The immediate, often pre-reflective sense that something has been encountered before
- **Substrate:** Perirhinal cortex (familiarity signals), distinguished from hippocampal recollection. Disruption produces **Capgras delusion** (recognition without familiarity) or **jamais vu** (familiarity without recognition).
- **Qualia:** "I know this," "I've seen this before," the warmth of recognition, or its pathological absence. Distinct from full episodic memory — familiarity can occur without any specific recalled episode.

### 30. Bouloception **†** (Sense of Will / Volition / Agency)
- **Phenomena:** The felt sense that one is the author of one's actions and decisions
- **Substrate:** Supplementary motor area (pre-movement "readiness potential"), anterior insular cortex, angular gyrus (disruption here produces feelings of alien control), prefrontal cortex (deliberate decision-making). The comparator model: sense of agency arises when predicted and actual sensory consequences of action match (Frith, 2005).
- **Qualia:** The experience of deciding, intending, willing, choosing, effortful self-control, the feeling "I did that." Pathological disruption: delusions of control (schizophrenia), alien hand syndrome, anarchic hand.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *boulesis* (will, volition).

### 31. Conative Effort Sense (Ponioception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** The felt sense of mental effort, cognitive strain, or ease
- **Substrate:** Anterior cingulate cortex (conflict monitoring, effort computation), dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (effortful control); dopaminergic cost-benefit circuits in the ventral striatum
- **Qualia:** The "heaviness" of a difficult math problem, the ease of a well-practiced skill, cognitive fatigue, the aversion to effortful thought, the flow state (absence of felt effort despite high performance)
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *ponos* (toil, effort). Distinct from physical fatigue (fatigueception) — this is the *mental* effort signal. Kahneman's "System 2" is largely characterized by the presence of this quale.

### 32. Epistemoception **†** (Sense of Knowing / Certainty–Uncertainty)
- **Phenomena:** The felt confidence or doubt accompanying a belief, judgment, or memory
- **Substrate:** Ventromedial prefrontal cortex (confidence signals), orbitofrontal cortex, and anterior cingulate cortex (error/conflict detection). Metacognitive calibration circuits.
- **Qualia:** Certainty, doubt, conviction, the "feeling of knowing" (the sense that you know something but can't yet retrieve it), the "tip-of-the-tongue" state's subjective certainty that the word exists, intellectual humility or overconfidence
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *episteme* (knowledge). "Metacognition" partly overlaps but is broader; this sense specifically names the *felt confidence/doubt* dimension.

## VII. SOCIAL & INTERSUBJECTIVE SENSES

### 33. Empathioception **†** (Empathic Awareness / Somatic Resonance)
- **Phenomena:** Felt awareness of another being's internal states — their pain, emotion, intention
- **Substrate:** Mirror neuron system (premotor and parietal cortex), anterior insula (shared representations of emotion — "I feel your pain" literally activates overlapping circuits), temporoparietal junction (perspective-taking), medial prefrontal cortex (mentalizing)
- **Qualia:** The visceral wince seeing someone injured, emotional contagion, the felt sense of what another person is feeling, compassionate distress. Qualitatively distinct from abstract inference about others' states — it has a *felt bodily* character.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *empatheia* (passion, feeling-in). "Empathy" is the standard term, but naming it as a sense ("-ception") emphasizes that it is a perceptual channel, not merely a cognitive inference.

### 34. Theory of Mind / Mentalizing (Noesoception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** The cognitive (not just empathic) awareness of others' beliefs, desires, intentions, and knowledge states — even when they differ from one's own
- **Substrate:** Medial prefrontal cortex, temporoparietal junction, posterior superior temporal sulcus, temporal poles. Disruption: autism spectrum (diminished spontaneous mentalizing), psychopathy (intact mentalizing, impaired empathioception).
- **Qualia:** The understanding that someone believes something false, the anticipation of how someone will react, the sense that someone is hiding something. Less visceral than empathy — more of a cognitive "modeling" of another mind.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *noesis* (understanding, cognitive awareness). Distinct from empathioception: empathioception is *feeling with* another; noesoception is *knowing about* another's mind.

### 35. Prosopic Awareness (Face/Identity Perception)
- **Phenomena:** The immediate perceptual recognition of individual faces and reading of facial expressions
- **Substrate:** Fusiform face area (FFA, identity), occipital face area (early processing), superior temporal sulcus (expression/gaze direction), amygdala (emotional evaluation of faces). Disruption: prosopagnosia.
- **Qualia:** The instantaneous "that's Sarah" experience, reading anger or joy in a glance, the uncanny valley effect. Face perception is qualitatively unlike object perception — it is holistic, automatic, and carries social meaning.
- **Note:** Could be classified under vision, but face perception is neurally and phenomenologically specialized enough to warrant distinct listing — we have dedicated cortical hardware for it, and its loss (prosopagnosia) leaves general vision intact.

## VIII. AESTHETIC, MORAL & EVALUATIVE SENSES

These are higher-order integrative senses — they don't have dedicated peripheral receptors but produce qualitatively distinct experiences that shape awareness and behavior.

### 36. Aesthetic Sense (Kaloception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** The perception of beauty, elegance, harmony, or their opposites (ugliness, dissonance, kitsch)
- **Substrate:** Default mode network and orbitofrontal cortex (beauty judgments across modalities — visual, musical, mathematical), medial prefrontal cortex, anterior insula, and reward circuits (nucleus accumbens) during peak aesthetic experience ("chills")
- **Qualia:** The experience of beauty — the shiver at a musical passage, the felt rightness of an elegant proof, the sense of visual harmony. Cross-modal: beauty in music, faces, landscapes, mathematics, and ideas all activate overlapping circuits.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *kalos* (beautiful).

### 37. Moral Sense (Ethicoception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** Moral intuitions — the immediate felt sense that something is right, wrong, fair, unjust, cruel, or virtuous
- **Substrate:** Ventromedial prefrontal cortex (moral judgment, especially personal moral dilemmas), amygdala (aversion to harm), anterior insula (disgust-based moral reactions), temporoparietal junction (assessing intent), dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (overriding intuitive judgments with reasoning)
- **Qualia:** Moral outrage, guilt, righteous anger, the felt wrongness of an injustice, moral disgust, moral elevation (the warm feeling when witnessing virtue). These are distinct from both emotion (they carry a normative/deontic dimension) and reasoning (they arrive as gut intuitions before deliberation).
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *ethikos* (moral character).

### 38. Sense of Humor (Geloception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** The perception of something as funny — incongruity resolution, absurdity, wit
- **Substrate:** Ventromedial prefrontal cortex (incongruity detection and resolution), temporal cortex (semantic processing), mesolimbic reward circuits (dopaminergic "reward" of the punchline), supplementary motor area (laughter generation)
- **Qualia:** Funniness, amusement, the involuntary compulsion to laugh, the felt absurdity of a situation. The perception of humor is a distinct quale — you cannot "reason" something into being funny.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *gelos* (laughter).

## IX. SELF-REFERENTIAL & META-AWARENESS SENSES

### 39. Autoception **†** (Sense of Self / Self-Awareness)
- **Phenomena:** The background awareness of being a continuing self — a subject distinct from the world, with a persistent identity over time
- **Substrate:** Cortical midline structures — medial prefrontal cortex (self-referential processing), posterior cingulate cortex/precuneus (self-continuity, autobiographical self), anterior insula (awareness of one's own body and emotional states as "mine"), default mode network (narrative self). Disruption: depersonalization (loss of felt selfhood), dissociative disorders.
- **Qualia:** The felt sense of "I-ness," the sense that experiences are happening *to me*, continuity of identity, the experience of "being someone." Dissolves partially in meditation, psychedelics, depersonalization, and certain neurological conditions.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *autos* (self).

### 40. Attentional Awareness (Prosoception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** The awareness of what one is attending to — and the felt shift of attention itself
- **Substrate:** Dorsal attention network (voluntary/endogenous attention — frontal eye fields, intraparietal sulcus), ventral attention network (involuntary/exogenous attention — temporoparietal junction, ventral frontal cortex), anterior cingulate (attentional conflict/selection)
- **Qualia:** The felt narrowing of focus, the "zoom-in" experience of concentration, the jarring pull of a sudden noise, the diffuse quality of open/panoramic awareness, the metacognitive awareness that your mind has wandered. Meditators specifically cultivate awareness *of* attention as an object.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *prosoche* (attention). Attention is usually considered a *mechanism*, not a sense — but one can become aware *of* attentional states, making it a genuine phenomenal category.

### 41. Oneiroreception **†** (Dream Awareness)
- **Phenomena:** The altered awareness that occurs during dreaming — including the recognition (in lucid dreaming) that one is dreaming
- **Substrate:** REM sleep circuitry (pontine brainstem, particularly the sublaterodorsal nucleus), deactivated dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (loss of critical judgment in normal dreams), reactivated DLPFC in lucid dreaming; visual cortex, limbic system (emotional content)
- **Qualia:** The immersive, often bizarre experiential world of dreams — vivid imagery without external input, emotional intensity, altered logic, the (occasional) sudden realization of dreaming. Qualitatively distinct from waking perception and from deliberate imagination.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *oneiros* (dream).

## X. LINGUISTIC & SEMANTIC SENSES

### 42. Linguistic Perception (Logoception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** The direct comprehension of language as meaningful — hearing speech or reading text and immediately experiencing meaning, not just sound or shape
- **Substrate:** Wernicke's area (posterior superior temporal gyrus — speech comprehension), Broca's area (syntactic processing and production), angular gyrus (reading/cross-modal integration), left inferior frontal gyrus, and a distributed semantic network. Disruption: aphasia (loss of the ability to extract meaning from language while hearing/vision remain intact).
- **Qualia:** The immediate felt understanding of a sentence, the felt meaningfulness of a word, the difference between hearing speech in a language you understand vs. one you don't. The latter is just sound; the former is meaning. This transition — from sound to meaning — constitutes a perceptual event.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *logos* (word, meaning, reason).

### 43. Sense of Meaning / Significance (Semainoreception **†**)
- **Phenomena:** The broader experience that something *means something* — that events, symbols, or experiences carry significance beyond their sensory content
- **Substrate:** Default mode network (narrative meaning-making), temporal poles (semantic knowledge), ventromedial prefrontal cortex (personal relevance evaluation). Disruption: existential depression (loss of felt meaning), depersonalization (loss of felt significance). Enhancement: temporal lobe epilepsy (hyperreligious/hypersignificant experiences), psychedelics (intensified sense of meaning).
- **Qualia:** The feeling that something "matters," the sense of significance in a sunset or a conversation, existential meaningfulness or meaninglessness, the "eureka" feeling of insight. Distinct from emotion — a moment can feel deeply meaningful without being happy or sad.
- **Rationale for term:** From Greek *semainein* (to signify, to mean).

### Notes on Completeness

**Why these 43 and not more?** The criterion was: *does this category of awareness have a qualitatively distinct phenomenal character that cannot be reduced to a combination of the others?* For example, "sense of direction" was not listed separately because it reduces to spatial egoception + magnetoception + visual/proprioceptive integration. "Sense of rhythm" was not listed because it reduces to audition + chronoception. However, taxonomy is always somewhat arbitrary at the borders — some listed senses (particularly in sections VII–IX) could reasonably be lumped or split further.

**Why not more interoceptive sub-senses?** Many interoceptive signals (e.g., blood glucose, blood pH, immune activation) are monitored by the body but rarely reach conscious awareness. The criterion here was *awareness*, not physiological monitoring. Signals that surface only as vague malaise are captured under visceroception or fatigueception.

**The hard problem remains.** This taxonomy catalogs the *what* and *where* of awareness modalities. It does not explain *how* or *why* any of these transductions produce subjective experience at all — that remains the hard problem of consciousness.
