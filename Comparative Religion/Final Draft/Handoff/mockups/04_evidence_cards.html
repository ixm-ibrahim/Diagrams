Section 6

Historical reliability of scripture

Source-by-source comparison across the worldview spectrum
Show
↳ Ties back to Tier 2 · Evidence
1 · Manuscript dating and earliest surviving sources
Atheism · naturalistic
No sacred manuscript posited; religious texts treated as cultural artifact.
Disqualified by exclusion — no candidate at this tier.
Polytheism · Vedic et al.
Vedas preserved orally from ~1500 BCE; Egyptian, Greek, Norse vary widely.
Strong oral preservation in one tradition; methodology varies elsewhere.
NT · P52 papyrus
Earliest fragment c. 125 CE — ~90 years after Jesus's death.
Originals lost; complete codices date to 4th century.
Quran · Birmingham ms.
Carbon-dated 568–645 CE, overlapping companions' lifetime.
Complete copies within ~20 years of Prophet's death.
Synthesis
Origin-to-manuscript gap: ~20 years (Quran), ~90 years (NT), millennia with oral preservation (Vedas). Shorter gap = stronger historical anchor; oral methodology can partially compensate when rigorous.
↳ Ties back to Tier 4 · Integrity
2 · Chain of transmission and textual variants
Atheism · n/a
No text held as preserved; no transmission claim.
Excluded — no scriptural commitment to evaluate.
Polytheism · sruti
Vedas preserved via strict mnemonic chains, mathematically verifiable.
Comparable rigor for Vedas; most others lack equivalent methodology.
NT · textual criticism
~5,800 Greek manuscripts; ~400,000 variants — more variants than NT words.
Successive copying across languages; some variants affect doctrine.
Quran · isnad + hafiz
Each verse traceable via named chain, cross-verified within first century.
Oral and written transmission preserved in parallel; Uthmanic standardization.
Synthesis
Verification mode differs: dual oral+written (Quran), manuscript volume (NT), mnemonic precision (Vedas), n/a (Atheism). Different methodologies produce different forms of confidence — but only some are auditable end-to-end.
