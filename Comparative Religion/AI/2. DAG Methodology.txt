# DAG Methodology: Critical Reading Instructions

## What This DAG Is and Isn't

This is a **phenomenological construction**, not an ontological theory. Every node describes what occurs in experience, not what exists behind experience.

### Key Principle: We Are Only Labeling

When we say "phenomena occur," we are NOT claiming:
- That someone experiences them
- That there's an experiencer
- That there's a metaphysical substrate

We ARE saying: "This happening happens" - pure occurrence without metaphysical baggage.

## How to Read the DAG Structure

### 1. Non-Linear Reading
The DAG is a **complete graph** that should be read holistically, not sequentially. 

**Correct reading**: Look up any referenced node wherever it appears in the document.
**Incorrect reading**: Assuming nodes must be understood in document order.

Example: If node T4 references IA2, go find IA2 wherever it is. The logical structure is:
- IA2 exists somewhere in the complete graph
- T4 can reference it
- No circular dependency is created

### 2. Understanding "Inherits From"
When Node X inherits from Node Y, this means:
- X uses the phenomenological observations established in Y
- X builds upon Y's labels
- The inheritance can cross categories and document sections

### 3. Phenomenological Purity Test
For each node, ask:
- "Am I adding hidden assumptions about what exists?"
- "Am I only describing what occurs?"
- "Have I smuggled in an experiencer, subject, or substrate?"

## Common Misreadings to Avoid

### Misreading 1: "But who experiences this?"
**Wrong**: "How can phenomena occur without someone experiencing them?"
**Right**: Phenomena simply occur. The question of "who" is an ontological addition, not a phenomenological observation.

### Misreading 2: "Wanting requires a wanter"
**Wrong**: "The introduction of wanting assumes someone who wants"
**Right**: "Wanting" is simply a label for a quality of directedness that occurs. No "wanter" is implied or required.

### Misreading 3: "Sensations must be pure"
**Wrong**: "SV6 is wrong because sensations can include wanting"
**Right**: SV6 provides a label for a specific phenomenological pattern (intensity without wanting). Other patterns get other labels.

### Misreading 4: "Awareness is too broad"
**Wrong**: "If awareness is just occurrence of information, then everything is aware"
**Right**: That's exactly the point. From a phenomenological perspective, occurrence IS awareness. If it didn't occur, we wouldn't be aware of it.

## The Sensations Clarification

Sensations are phenomena that:
1. Come and go independently of wanting
2. Can have wants attached to them (but the sensation itself isn't the want)
3. Example: Pain occurs (sensation) + I want it to stop (volition)

## How to Verify DAG Integrity

1. **Trace dependencies**: Start from any node and trace back through "Inherits From"
2. **Check for cycles**: If you encounter the same node twice, there's a circular dependency
3. **Verify phenomenological purity**: Each node should only describe occurrences, not explain them

## Remember: This Is Not Ontology

We're not asking:
- What sensations ARE
- WHO experiences
- WHAT causes phenomena

We're only mapping:
- What occurs
- How occurrences relate
- What patterns we observe

The bridge to ontology comes LATER, through the discovery of counter-will. Don't jump ahead.
