### Level 1 ###

**1. Phenomenological Primitives**
| ID | Node | Inherits From | Terms Introduced | Question Addressed | Intuitive Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PH1 | Phenomena occur. | --- | {occur, happen, thing, phenomenon, primitive, raw} | Starting point: this either happens, or it does not. "Thing" here means any phenomenological occurrence, and "phenomenon" is our label for anything that occurs. | Red, loud, hungry, happy, pain, want, 2, unicorn, etc. |
| PH2 | Distinct phenomena occur. | PH1 | {what, is, not, distinct, different, separate, other, uniform, one, the, a, that, each, from, than, which, multiplicity} | In what way do phenomena occur? Distinctly, not as an undifferentiated mass. | Red is different than hungry, which is different than loud |
| PH3 | Qualities occur. | PH2 | {quality, qualia, variety, flavor, kind, type, content, of, have, when, with, seem, as} | How do phenomena distinctly occur? We label the distinctions between phenomena as "qualities". | Red is a quality, hungry is a quality | 
| PH4 | Associations occur. | PH3 | {association, relation, synthesis, connection, like, with, similar, group, any, together, and, or} | How do qualities occur? With the primitive feeling that some phenomena are alike or belong together. We label these felt connections "associations". | Red is like blue, hungry is like thirsty, happiness is like sadness, pleasure is like pain |
| PH5 | Awareness is the occurrence of a quality or association. | PH4 | {given, presence, absence} | How do phenomena distinctly occur? The distinction of their occurrence is a quality. | the "there-ness" of red, loud, hungry, pain, want, 2, unicorn, etc. |

### Level 2 ###

**2. Information & Patterns**
| ID | Node | Inherits From | Terms Introduced | Question Addressed | Intuitive Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IP1 | Information is a quality or association. | PH* | {information, meaning} | How do phenomena distinctly occur? Qualities and associations are labelled as "information". | hungry, red is like blue, etc. |
| IP2 | Information belongs with a phenomenon. | IP1 | {about, regarding, belong, possess, have, source, from, to, in, by} | How does information occur? Information is "about" phenomena. | what was felt is "red" or "2" or "want" or "loud" |
| IP3 | A feature is a distinct piece of information. | IP1 | {feature} | How does information occur? Distinct pieces of information are labelled "features". | "red" is a feature of a rainbow |]
| IP4 | A pattern is a similar quality that a set of features have. | IP2,IP3 | {pattern, repetition, often} | How do features occur? A quality that a set of features share is labelled a "pattern". | red and blue have the same quality of "color" |
| IP5 | A class is a similar quality that a set of patterns have. | IP4| {class, genus, family} | How do patterns occur? A quality that a set of patterns share is labelled a "class". | color and brightness have the same quality of "vision" |
| IP6 | Sets of features that have similar patterns are analogous to each other. | IP4 | {analogy} | How do patterns occur? One set of features is labelled an "analogy" to another when they share a similar pattern. | both "loud and quiet" and "bright and dim" have the same pattern of "different intensities" |
| IP7 | Sameness is the similarity of a pattern across its features. | IP4 | {same, identical} | How do features occur? The way in which a pattern is similar across its features is labelled "sameness". | apples and strawberries (both "fruits") are the same in "red" |
| IP8 | Variation is the difference of a pattern across its features. | IP4 | {variation} | How do features occur? The way in which a pattern is different across its features is labelled a "variation". | apples and strawberries (both "fruits") are different in "taste" |

**3. Wanting & Intensity**
| ID | Node | Inherits From | Terms Introduced | Question Addressed | Intuitive Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WI1 | Wanting occurs. | PH* | {want} | How do phenomena distinctly occur? With a quality of directedness toward something, which we label "want". | want of good taste, want of "move arm" |
| WI2 | Intensity occurs. | PH* | {intensity, strength, magnitude, more, less} | How do phenomena distinctly occur? With degrees of force or strength, which we label "intensity". | low pain vs. high pain, more red vs. less red, very hungry vs. very full |
| WI3 | Direction is the wanted information. | WI1,IP1 | {direction, target, aim, purpose, toward, for} | How does striving occur? We label the information that has the "wanting" quality as "direction". | the "good taste" of a want |
| WI4 | Striving is wanting with intensity. | WI1,WI2 | {striving, desire, seek} | How does wanting and intensity occur? When they occur together, we label it "striving". | strong desire to feel pleasure, weak desire to taste vegetables |
| WI5 | Inclination is wanting without intensity. | WI1,WI2 | {inclination, predisposition} | How does wanting and intensity occur? When wanting occurs without intensity, we label it "inclination". | the "there-ness" of a want |
| WI6 | Sensation is intensity without wanting. | WI1,WI2 | {sensation, feeling} | How does wanting and intensity occur? When intensity of any quality occurs without wanting, we label it "sensation". | red, loud, 2, unicorn, hungry, happy |
| WI7 | Success is the occurrence of what is wanted. | WI3 | {success, satisfaction, achievement, accomplishment} | How does direction occur? When it occurs, we label it "success". | "arm moves" occurs with "want to move arm" |
| WI8 | Failure is the non-occurrence of what is wanted. | WI3 | {failure} | How does direction occur? When it does not occur, we label it "failure". | "fly" does not occur with "want to fly" |
| WI9 | Volition is a striving whose occurrence and intensity are inclined. | WI4,WI5 | {volition, will, intention, voluntary} | How does the occurrence of striving occur with inclination? When both its occurrence and intensity are themselves inclined (wanted without intensity), we label it "volition". | "wanting food" occurs with "wanting to want food" |
| WI10 | Compulsion is a striving whose occurrence and intensity are not inclined. | WI4,WI5 | {compulsion, involuntary, desire, urge} | How does the occurrence of striving occur with inclination? When neither its occurrence nor intensity are themselves wanted, we label it "compulsion". | "wanting cigarettes" occurs with "not wanting to want cigarettes" |
| WI11 | An outcome is a success or failure. | WI7,WI8 | {outcome, result} | How do successes and failures occur? The occurrence of either one is labelled an "outcome". | whether or not "arm moves" occurs with "want to move arm" |

**4. Temporality**
| ID | Node | Inherits From | Terms Introduced | Question Addressed | Intuitive Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Phenomena occur sequentially. | PH* | {before, after, previous, next, sequence, succession, prior, follow} | How do phenomena occur? They occur with a feeling of "before" and "after". | satisfaction comes after tasty, "wind on arm" feeling comes after "move arm" want |
| T2 | Phenomena occur presently. | PH* | {present, now, current, immediate} | How do phenomena occur? They occur with a feeling of "now". | ouch! |
| T3 | Past phenomena occur with the "before now" quality. | T1,T2 | {past, was, then} | How do phenomena with the "before" quality occur? They are labelled as the "past". | ouch "before" is not like ouch "now" |
| T4 | A memory is a present phenomenon about a past phenomenon. | T3,IP2 | {memory} | How do past phenomena occur? They are labelled as "memories". | the feeling of "ouch before" occurs right now |
| T5 | Change is the difference of qualities in a sequence. | T3 | {change, transition} | How do phenomena distinctly occur? The difference in qualities are labelled "change". | red changes to orange, pain changes to ease, silence changes to loudness |
| T6 | Recognition is when a phenomenon is like a memory. | T4 | {recognition, remembrance} | How do memories occur with present phenomena? Phenomena that are like a memory are labelled as "recognition". | "current taste" is like a memory of "strawberry taste" |
| T7 | Imagination is information from different memories. | T4 | {imagination} | How do memories occur? When different pieces of information are taken from different memories, we label that "imagination". | gray + elephant, pink + elephant, 1 + 1, flat + earth, square + circle |
| T8 | A correlation is when a phenomenon often follows another. | T4,IP4 | {correlation} | How do patterns occur with change? Phenomena that often occur after each other is labelled as a "correlation". | loud sound often comes after a bright flash of light |
| T9 | An event is the transition from "not-occurring" to "occurring". | T5 | {event, start, end, when, from, to} | How do phenomena change? They transition between absence and presence. | the occurence of pain when no pain was felt before |

### Level 3 ###

**5. Abstraction & Domain**
| ID | Node | Inherits From | Terms Introduced | Question Addressed | Intuitive Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AD1 | An abstraction is an imagined pattern. | IP*,T* | {abstraction, generalization, concept} | How do patterns occur with imagination? Imagined patterns are labelled "abstractions". | the "redness" of imagined {apple, strawberry, blood, flowers} |
| AD2 | An intension is the set of features in an abstraction. | AD1 | {intension} | How do abstractions occur? The features that an abstraction has is labelled an "intension". | the intension of "apple" is {fruit, crisp, has a core}, the intension of "fruit" is {grows out of a flower, has seeds} |
| AD3 | A domain is the set of imaginations that have the same abstraction. | AD1 | {domain, scope, category} | How do abstractions occur with imaginations? Imaginations with the same abstractions is labelled a "domain". | "redness" has {apple, strawberry, blood}, "number" has {one, two, three} |
| AD4 | An intersection is when a feature from one intension is the same as another. | AD2 | {intersection, overlap} | How do intensions occur? The same feature in different intensions is labelled an "intersection". | "red" is an intersection betweeen the apple:{red, crisp, has seeds} and rainbow:{red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple} intensions |
| AD5 | A match is when a phenomenon is in a domain.| AD3 | {match, fit, place, satisfy, in} | How do domains occur? A phenomenon in a domain is labelled a "match". | apple is in the "red" domain |
| AD6 | A domain contains another when its intension is a subset of the other domain's intension. | AD4,AD5 | {contain, within, in, out} | How do domains occur? A domain is labelled to "contain" another when the contained domain has the same intension with more features. | "fruit" contains "apple" because fruit has {grows from flower, has seeds} while apple has {grows from flower, has seeds, red, sweet, crisp} |
| AD7 | An conjunction is when a phenomenon in one domain matches with another. | AD5 | {conjunction} | How do matches occur with domains? A phenomenon that matches with multiple domains is labelled a "conjunction". | "apple" conjuncts with the "red" and "round" domains |
| AD8 | A hierarchy is a set of domains and their associations. | AD6,AD7 | {hierarchy, taxonomy, classification, tree, map} | How do domains occur? The way in which domains are related to each other is labelled a "hierarchy". | the "food" hierarchy is where "food" contains "fruits" and "vegetables", "fruits" contains "apples" and "bananas", "vegetables" contains "carrots" and "peas" |
| AD9 | A phenomenon is meaningful when it is placed within a hierarchy. | AD8 | {meaning, context} | How do hierarchies occur? The placement of a phenomenon in a hierarchy is labelled "meaningful". | "apple" is meaningful when it is placed in the "food" hierarchy, but is not meaningful in the "numbers" hierarchy |

**6. Ontological Bridge***
| ID | Node | Inherits From | Terms Introduced | Question Addressed | Intuitive Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OB1 | Independence is when variations in one pattern do not correlate with variations in another pattern. | IP*,T* | {independence} | How do features occur? The way in which a pattern is different across its features is labelled a "variation". | the taste fruits are independent from the sound of voices |
| OB2 | Wants do not correlate with sensations. | WI6,OB1 | {} | How do wants and sensations occur? Wants occur independently from sensations. | "want to fly" does not correlate with "occurrence of flight" |
| OB3 | Outcomes do not correlate with want. | WI11,OB1 | {} | How do outcomes and sensations occur? Wants occur independently from outcomes. | "want to fly" does not correlate with whether "flight" occurs or not |
| OB4 | A limit is what is independent from want. | OB2,OB3 | {limit, constraint, boundary, against} | How do wants occur with outcomes and sensations? What occurs independently from want is labelled a "limit". | "flight" is a limit to want, as well as all "sensations" and "outcomes" |
| OB5 | Nature is the set of properties for a limit. | OB4 | {nature} | How do limits occur? A limit's properties is labelled its "nature". | "apple" has {"round", "red", "sweet"}, "arm movement" has {"wind feeling on arm", "proprioceptive awareness", "visuals of an arm changing"} |
| OB6 | A structure is a set of limits. | OB4 | {structure, system, composition, network, build, topology} | How do limits occur? A set of them is labelled a "structure". | a "maze" is a structure built by "walls" of {hard, rough} and "passageways" of {breezy, ease} |
| OB7 | An object is a structure with nature. | OB5,OB6 | {object} | How do structures and natures occur? A structure with nature is labelled an "object". | a "maze" is {confusing}, a "hand" {moves, feels} |

**7. Recognition & Possibility**
| ID | Node | Inherits From | Terms Introduced | Question Addressed | Intuitive Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RP1 | A phenomenon is new when it occurs without recognition. | T* | {new, novel} | How do phenomena occur with memories? Phenomena that don't exist in memory are labelled "new". | tasting a new food, being intimate for the first time, seeing a new landmark |
| RP2 | A thought is a set of imaginations and their associations. | T* | {thought, concept, idea, analysis} | How does imagination occur? Connected imaginations are labelled "thoughts". | {gray, elephant} with {big, cool}, {pink, elephant} with {fake, possible with pink paint}, {1, 1} with {2}  |
| RP3 | Visualization is the sensation of an imagination. | T*,WI* | {visualization, picturing, quasi-sensual} | How do phenomena distinctly occur? Distinct phenomena occur as "imagination". | the "color" of an imagined "elephant", the "number" of an imagined "quantity", the "truth" of an imagined "logical statement" |
| RP4 | Imagination and thoughts are independent from occurrence. | T7,OB1 | {} | How does imagination occur? What occurs is imagined occurs independently from what . | thinking of a new recipe, trying a new chess strategy, playing devil's advocate for a new concept |
| RP5 | Possibility is a thought constrained by imagined limits. | RP2,OB4 | {possibility, potential, could} | How do imagination and limits occur? Thoughts constrained by imagined limits are labelled "possibilities". | {coin flip} has possibilities of {heads, tails}, {unicorn} has possibilities of {rainbow farts} |
