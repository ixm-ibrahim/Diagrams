# Book of Mormon — Reading Plan with Project-Aligned Notes

*Five batches, roughly equidistant by page count (1981 LDS edition, ~531 pp of body text), broken at natural narrative seams. Notes flag passages relevant to the LDS Addendum's framework — anthropomorphism, polytheism/Godhead, atonement & the Fall, pre-mortal existence, KJV-language parallels, anachronisms, witnesses, doctrinal evolution, Moroni's promise as feeling-based epistemology, and the Adam/fortunate-fall reading.*

---

## Batch 1 — 1 Nephi 1 → 2 Nephi 24 (~117 pp, 46 chapters)

**Narrative**: Lehi's family leaves Jerusalem (~600 BCE), obtains the brass plates from Laban, journeys to the promised land. Nephi inherits leadership. Lehi's deathbed blessings. Heavy Isaiah block begins.

**What should pop out:**

- **1 Nephi 13–14** — the "great and abominable church" frames mainstream Christianity as having removed "many plain and precious things" from the Bible. This is the *structural justification* for LDS scripture's existence. Note that it parallels Islam's claim of corrupted prior scripture — but Islam locates the corruption in transmission (a process), while this locates it in deliberate suppression by a named institution. Different claim, different evidentiary burden.

- **1 Nephi 20–22** — **first major KJV-Isaiah block** (Isaiah 48–49 in 17th-century English on plates allegedly written ~600 BCE in Reformed Egyptian). Watch the language: "thou," "ye," "shouldst," KJV italics preserved in places. This is the start of the textual fingerprint the project flags under "KJV-language parallels" (Phase 4 / Objection-set on resistance to fabrication).

- **2 Nephi 2:25** — **"Adam fell that men might be; and men are, that they might have joy."** This is the locus classicus of the **"fortunate fall"** reading. Mark it. This is exactly the soteriological inversion your Phase 7 / Section 7.3 work is built around — fall as required upward step rather than rupture to be repaired. Compare with Islam's Adam narrative (Q 2:35–37, 7:23): Adam errs, repents, is forgiven, the relationship is restored without ontological elevation through transgression.

- **2 Nephi 3** — Joseph (in Egypt) prophesies of a future "choice seer" named Joseph, son of Joseph. This is a self-referential prophecy of Joseph Smith embedded in scripture allegedly translated by Joseph Smith. Worth noting as a structural feature.

- **2 Nephi 5:21** — the **dark-skin curse on the Lamanites** ("a skin of blackness... that they might not be enticing"). This is the textual seed of the race-and-priesthood theology that wasn't formally repudiated even after the 1978 lifting. Cross-reference your Handoff doc's note on pre-mortal-choices-shape-earthly-station as moral problem.

- **2 Nephi 9 (Jacob's atonement sermon)** — strongly Trinitarian-sounding language; flag the tension with later LDS Godhead doctrine where Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are three separate beings.

- **2 Nephi 12–24** — **Isaiah 2–14 reproduced almost verbatim from the KJV**, including translator italics. This is one of the cleanest empirical handles in the entire book: a translation made in the 1820s of plates allegedly inscribed ~550 BCE reproduces a 1611 English translation of a Hebrew text, italics-and-all. Note the magnitude — it's not a paraphrase, it's a copy with minor edits.

## Batch 2 — 2 Nephi 25 → Mosiah 16 (~108 pp)

**Narrative**: Nephi's commentary closes 2 Nephi. Jacob, Enos, Jarom, Omni, Words of Mormon — the "small plates" finish and Mormon's editorial bridge appears. Mosiah opens: King Benjamin's address, then Zeniff's record, Abinadi confronts King Noah.

**What should pop out:**

- **2 Nephi 25–30** — Nephi's exposition: explicit framing of "the Jews" as having stumbled and the gentiles receiving the fullness. **2 Ne 29:3** — the "Bible, Bible, we have got a Bible" speech is the polemical core of the open-canon claim. Worth steelmanning before critique: it's a coherent argument *if* you've already accepted the great-and-abominable corruption frame.

- **2 Nephi 31–32** — baptism, gift of the Holy Ghost. Mark for Phase 6 (similarities/differences on Salvation): the BoM teaches infant baptism is "solemn mockery" (later, in Moroni 8), but is otherwise close to Protestant evangelical patterns.

- **Jacob 2:23–30** — **polygamy condemned**. Note the exact language: "this people begin to wax in iniquity... they understand not the scriptures, for they seek to excuse themselves in committing whoredoms, because of the things which were written concerning David and Solomon his son. Behold, David and Solomon truly had many wives and concubines, which thing was abominable before me." Compare directly with D&C 132 (1843, post-BoM) which commands polygamy and cites David and Solomon as positive precedents. This is the cleanest internal contradiction in the LDS canon and a centerpiece for your Phase 4 "doctrinal evolution between editions" analysis.

- **Jacob 5** — **Allegory of the Olive Tree** (Zenos). Massive chapter. The structural argument LDS apologists make is that it's too coherent to be fabricated by a 23-year-old. Steelman: it *is* a sustained extended allegory. Critique: extended allegories of national/ecclesiastical history are common in 19th-century revivalist preaching (compare Bunyan, Whitefield, restorationist tracts available in Smith's environment). Bushman (LDS source) documents the religious print environment.

- **Enos through Words of Mormon** — these short books are easy to skim. Pay attention to Omni's mention of the **Mulekites** (Mosiah 25 recaps): a second migration from Jerusalem ~589 BCE arriving with no record and forgetting their language. Archaeological footprint: zero.

- **Mosiah 3:19** — **"the natural man is an enemy to God"** — King Benjamin's anthropology. This is *strongly* Protestant/Calvinist in flavor and sits awkwardly with the 2 Nephi 2:25 "joy of the fall" frame. Watch how these two anthropologies coexist without being reconciled.

- **Mosiah 3 broadly** — explicit pre-existence language and atonement theology in the mouth of an angel speaking to a pre-Christian Nephite king. This is one of the strongest internal markers for the project's "anachronistic theology" pattern: post-NT Christian categories articulated centuries before they existed.

- **Mosiah 13–16 (Abinadi)** — **Mosiah 15:1–5**: "God himself shall come down... the Father, because he was conceived by the power of God; and the Son, because of the flesh; thus becoming the Father and Son — and they are one God." This is **modalism** — Father and Son as roles of one being — which contradicts the later LDS doctrine of separate embodied persons. Mark heavily. This is direct evidence of doctrinal evolution *within* LDS scripture itself.

## Batch 3 — Mosiah 17 → Alma 30 (~110 pp)

**Narrative**: Abinadi's martyrdom, Alma the Elder's conversion and establishment of the church at the Waters of Mormon. Alma the Younger's dramatic conversion (Saul/Paul parallel). Transition from kingship to judges. Alma and the sons of Mosiah become missionaries. Nehor, Amlici, Korihor — three antagonists.

**What should pop out:**

- **Mosiah 18** — baptism instituted by Alma the Elder *before* Christ, with explicit Christian baptismal formulas. Anachronism flag.

- **Mosiah 27 (Alma the Younger's conversion)** — structural parallel to Acts 9 (Paul on the road to Damascus): persecutor struck down by angelic visitation, repents, becomes chief missionary. Note that this is the *second* Saul/Paul-shaped conversion narrative in the BoM after Enos. Pattern to track.

- **Mosiah 29** — King Mosiah's case against monarchy and institution of the reign of the judges. This is a strikingly 18th-century American political theology placed in the 1st century BCE. The vocabulary of "voice of the people" sits naturally in 1828 Jacksonian America; it does not sit naturally in any attested pre-Columbian polity. Track for the "resistance to fabrication" tier.

- **Alma 7:10** — Jesus "shall be born of Mary, at Jerusalem which is the land of our forefathers." **Bethlehem, not Jerusalem**, in every other Christian source. LDS apologetics treat "land of Jerusalem" as a regional designation; the textual reading is what it says.

- **Alma 11:26–40** — the **Zeezrom dialogue**. Amulek explicitly affirms "there is no more than one God" — strongly monotheistic Trinitarian language that contradicts the later D&C / King Follett doctrine of multiple gods and exaltation. Another internal evolution marker.

- **Alma 13** — **Melchizedek priesthood passage**, including pre-mortal foreordination ("being called and prepared from the foundation of the world"). This is the BoM's clearest pre-mortal-existence passage. For your Phase 4 work, note: the priesthood-restoration narrative (John the Baptist conferring the Aaronic, Peter-James-John conferring the Melchizedek) is *absent* from the 1830 BoM and the 1833 Book of Commandments — it appears in 1834–35 retellings. The priesthood concept exists here; the restoration chain doesn't.

- **Alma 16, 22, 50** and scattered — **anachronism cluster**: "steel" swords, horses, chariots, "silk and fine-twined linen," wheat and barley, elephants (Ether). None of these have archaeological footprint in pre-Columbian Mesoamerica at any time, let alone in the BCE/early-CE periods claimed. Track these as you encounter them — they accumulate to the Phase 3 "alignment with historical record" critique.

- **Alma 30 (Korihor, the anti-Christ)** — Korihor demands a sign, is struck dumb, confesses he was deceived by an angel-disguised devil, dies trampled. Steelman: it's a literary set piece against rationalist skepticism. Critique: the rhetorical move is to frame any demand for evidence as satanic deception — this is *exactly* the epistemic move your project's Rules of Reasoning are written against ("faith without verification or skepticism without evidence — both bypass reasoning"). A book that anathematizes the demand for evidence cannot then appeal to evidence for its own authority without contradiction.

## Batch 4 — Alma 31 → 3 Nephi 7 (~128 pp)

**Narrative**: Alma's missionary work among the Zoramites, the great sermon on faith (32), Alma's counsel to his sons (36–42), the long war chapters (43–63), Helaman's stripling warriors, the secret combinations of the Gadianton robbers, signs of Christ's birth, decline into tribalism, government collapses before the cataclysm.

**What should pop out:**

- **Alma 32** — the **faith-as-seed sermon**. Mark this carefully. The epistemology here — "if a seed swelleth, and sprouteth, and beginneth to grow, then ye must needs know that the seed is good" (32:30) — is **the canonical statement of LDS truth-testing via felt experience**. This is the precursor to Moroni 10:4. For your project, this is the central methodological collision: the LDS framework treats subjective swelling-in-the-bosom as confirmation of objective truth claims. Your File 0 Rules of Reasoning are written against precisely this move. Steelman it before critiquing: William James's *Varieties* treats this seriously; the appeal isn't naïve. Critique: it is symmetrically available to every religious tradition with a contemplative practice — it cannot select between them, and therefore cannot be evidence for *this* tradition specifically.

- **Alma 36** — Alma the Younger's conversion retold by him in the **most-cited chiastic structure** in the BoM. LDS apologists argue chiasmus is evidence of Hebraic literary origin and beyond Smith's compositional ability. Steelman: the structure is real and the chapter is well-crafted. Critique: (1) chiasmus is also found in English-language texts Smith would have known (KJV, hymns, oral preaching); (2) the human eye finds chiastic patterns readily even where authors didn't intend them; (3) the strength of the chiasmus claim has been quantified and challenged in non-LDS scholarship (see John W. Welch's original 1969 paper vs. later statistical critiques).

- **Alma 39–42** — **the atonement theology core**. Alma 42 in particular: the mercy-vs-justice argument, the necessity of a Mediator. Read this *carefully* and slowly. This is the chapter that does the work for your Phase 7 (LDS Soteriology) write-up. Note the categories: justice as an impersonal cosmic principle that even God cannot violate ("the work of justice could not be destroyed... God would cease to be God") — this is a startling claim about divine sovereignty. Compare with Islamic theology where Allah is not subject to any external law (the Ash'ari position; cf. your Euthyphro appendix 9.F).

- **Alma 43–63 (war chapters)** — these are tedious to read but matter because (1) they ground the BoM as a war chronicle with named places that should be archaeologically locatable and aren't; (2) Captain Moroni's title-of-liberty episode (Alma 46) reads as 18th-century American patriotic theology projected backward; (3) the **stripling warriors** of Helaman (Alma 53, 56–58) — 2,000 young men, none die — note the literary shape rather than the historical plausibility.

- **Helaman 5** — **Nephi and Lehi in prison surrounded by fire, not consumed, ministering angels**. Direct structural parallel to Daniel 3 (Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego) and Acts 12 (Peter freed by angel). Note the literary recombination of biblical motifs.

- **Helaman 13–15 (Samuel the Lamanite)** — prophesies the signs of Christ's birth and death with five years' precision. Pay attention because the *fulfillment* in 3 Nephi 1 and 3 Nephi 8 is what you'll read next.

- **3 Nephi 1–7** — the signs are fulfilled (day-night-day, new star), then the Nephite government fragments into tribes. Set-up for the cataclysm.

## Batch 5 — 3 Nephi 8 → Moroni 10 (~134 pp)

**Narrative**: The great destruction at Christ's death (3 Nephi 8–10). Christ's three-day appearance to the Nephites (11–28). The 200-year golden age (4 Nephi). Mormon narrates the destruction of his people (Mormon 1–9). Moroni interpolates the Jaredite record (Ether), then closes with his own final exhortations (Moroni).

**What should pop out:**

- **3 Nephi 8–10** — the cataclysm: cities sunk, mountains raised, three days of darkness. Note the scale: this would be the largest geological event in human history and should leave an archaeological signature across Mesoamerica. None exists at ~33 CE.

- **3 Nephi 11–14** — **Christ's appearance and his sermon, which reproduces the Sermon on the Mount almost verbatim from the KJV** including translator italics. This is the second great KJV-parallel block in the BoM (the first was the Isaiah block in Batch 1). Together they bracket the book. The structural problem is the same: a sermon Jesus gave in Aramaic in Galilee ~30 CE is reproduced in 1611 English in the Americas, italics and all, in a translation completed in 1829. Mark every match against Matthew 5–7.

- **3 Nephi 11** specifically — **the baptismal/Godhead formula** ("Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are one"). Strongly compatible with the modalism of Mosiah 15, awkward with later embodied-separate-persons doctrine. Add to your doctrinal-evolution dossier.

- **3 Nephi 15:21–24** — Christ identifies Lehi's people as the "other sheep" of John 10:16. The literal-flock interpretation is an LDS distinctive. Note that Islam can absorb this verse differently (other communities receiving prophets) without requiring transoceanic migration.

- **3 Nephi 17–19** — Christ's deeply moving ministry to the children, prayer, sacrament institution. The emotional/literary power of these chapters is real — steelman it. Many converts cite these chapters specifically. The project's coherence-axis posture: emotional resonance is not evidence of historical occurrence.

- **4 Nephi** — 200 years of peace in one chapter. Note what's missing: any cultural, technological, or political specificity. A 200-year golden age leaves no detail in the record. Contrast with how the war chapters had granular detail; the period that should be most worth preserving is summarized in five pages.

- **Mormon 1–9** — Mormon's first-person narrative of the Nephite destruction (~385 CE) at the hill Cumorah. **Cumorah specifically**: the LDS-traditional location (upstate New York) cannot reconcile with the Mesoamerican geography demanded by the war chapters; the apologetic "two-Cumorahs" theory is a post-hoc rescue. Track this as a representative case of geography-doesn't-match-text.

- **Mormon 9:32–34** — **Reformed Egyptian** mentioned by name. "We have written this record... in the characters which are called among us the reformed Egyptian... none other people knoweth our language." This is the language nobody else has ever attested. For Phase 3 (chains of transmission) and Phase 4 (resistance to fabrication), Reformed Egyptian is a pure linguistic singularity: a script and language family otherwise unattested in the historical or epigraphic record.

- **Ether (Jaredite record)** — the second civilization, from the Tower of Babel (~2200 BCE), traveling to the Americas in **sealed barges with glowing stones for light** (Ether 2–3). Read Ether 3 carefully: the Brother of Jared sees the finger and then the body of the pre-mortal Christ. **This is one of the BoM's most explicit anthropomorphic passages** — directly relevant to the Phase 1 / File 5 alignment-with-reality work on God's nature, and to the broader Quranic anthropomorphism dialogue (Handoff Q2.5 / Q3.5). Note: Christ here has a body of spirit, which the text describes as having form and members like a body of flesh. This is *not* the *bilā kayf* posture of "affirm without modality"; it is a positive metaphysical claim about divine embodiment.

- **Ether 15** — final Jaredite battle, two million dead, sole survivor Coriantumr. No archaeological footprint for a battle of this scale.

- **Moroni 7** — **faith, hope, charity sermon — close paraphrase of 1 Corinthians 13** (Pauline language ~50 years before Paul writes it, in a different hemisphere). Another anachronism cluster.

- **Moroni 8** — infant baptism is "solemn mockery." Compare with the rest of historical Christianity for context.

- **Moroni 10:3–5** — **the promise**. "I would exhort you that ye would ask God... if these things are not true; and if ye shall ask with a sincere heart, with real intent, having faith in Christ, he will manifest the truth of it unto you, by the power of the Holy Ghost." This is the **canonical capstone of the felt-truth epistemology** introduced in Alma 32. It is the book's own offered method of verification. Your entire File 0 / Rules of Reasoning framework is in direct collision with it. Read it carefully and slowly. The steelman: the verse adds qualifiers ("sincere heart, real intent, faith in Christ") that LDS apologetics use to insulate it from falsification — every negative result can be attributed to insufficient sincerity. The critique: this insulation is precisely the structure that makes the test unfalsifiable, and an unfalsifiable test cannot select between truth and falsehood. The Handoff doc's Q3.8 response — *refuse the bait of community/feeling as evidence, symmetrically* — applies here as the final methodological move.

## Closing note

Two textual blocks bracket the book — the Isaiah block in Batch 1 and the Sermon on the Mount block in Batch 5. Both are KJV-language with italics preserved. These are the cleanest empirical handles in the entire text and deserve careful comparison with a KJV opened side-by-side.

Two epistemological pillars also bracket the book — Alma 32 (faith-as-seed) in Batch 4 and Moroni 10:4 (the promise) in Batch 5. Both belong on the coherence-vs-comfort axis discussion in your File 0 / Rules of Reasoning material.

Two anthropological passages — 2 Nephi 2:25 ("Adam fell that men might be") in Batch 1 and Ether 3 (pre-mortal Christ's body) in Batch 5 — are the textual core for your Phase 7 (Adam narrative / fortunate fall) and Phase 1/4 (God's nature / anthropomorphism) work respectively.

If you can mark only those six passages while reading, the project-relevant skeleton of the book is captured.
