1. What are the apparent similarities and differences between Islam and Christianity?
	- Both religions differ in salvific mechanism but achieve the same result: God is One, just, merciful, loving, holds us accountable to our own actions, merits righteousness and condemns sin, and requires faith to be eligible for salvation.
2. What is the relationship between God's mercy/love, justice, and forgiveness (soteriology)?
	- Christianity: Sin incurs a real debt (ie. death, loss of sanctifying grace) that we can't pay on our own, so God personally satisfies that burden for us (out of His love and to preserve perfect justice, such that we can be forgiven while also not leaving the debt unsettled)
	- Islam: God holds us accountable only to our own actions, rewarding righteousness and condemning sin (such that the path to forgiveness is opened before the path to punishment, and good deeds can erase and transform bad deeds - both out of His mercy)
3. What is the source of the fundamental, theological difference in soteriology?
	- Christianity: Adam earned the debt of original sin, which God (in the Person of Jesus) fulfilled its settlement and defeated death on our behalf (as finite mankind can't recreate a divine grace when lost, which can only be supplied by God)
	- Islam: Adam slipped in his obedience to God, immediately repented, and was forgiven by God: an experience meant to teach him - and all mankind - of what his relationship with God is, what his purpose in life is, and who his enemy is
4. Which salvific narrative better fits reality?
	- Christianity: Justice is fundamentally about settlement (sin creates a spiritual debt that must be satisfied - such that it can be paid for by an eligible substitute [in Christ])
	- Islam: Justice is fundamentally about accountability (every soul bears only its own burden, such that both the debt of sin and reward of righteousness are borne only by the guilty)
	- Results: Islam accomplishes everything the Christian Jesus does, but with a more perfect form of accountability, consistency, and better fitting the mold of a "loving father"
5. Which salvific narrative looks more like a corrupted version of the truth?
	- Both religions affirm God's perfect justice and the need for God's grace and mercy - but they are redirected differently in both narratives
	- Relative to Christianity, Islam sharpens God's oneness and rationality, magnifies mercy and accountability, and diminishes the emphasis on Satan's power
	- Relative to Islam, Christianity increases God's expectations, emphasizes Satan's power, reduces God's oneness and original mercy, and diminishes the salvific function of our good deeds, our spiritual status, and rationality
	- Results: Christianity looks more like a corrupted version of Islam, while Islam looks more like a corrected version of Christianity
