Note that the following sections only compare trinitarianism (sourced by the New Testament) and monotheism (sourced by the Quran). Even though Atheism's rebuttals fit the natural data, it neither explains why reality exists, nor does it actually refute (or reduce confidence in) the alignment of a transcendent creator with reality. And Polytheism is set aside because a plurality of ultimate powers doesn’t naturally yield the unified, law-governed order we actually observe - and multiplying ultimate agents introduces unexplained conflict/coordination that the world’s tight regularities don’t display.

1. Textual/Historical Claim
 - What is the claim?
     - New Testament
         - The Bible possesses a textual and historical preservation that is superior to all alternatives, which are not authorized by God (ie. deceptive or demonic in origin).
         - Specifically: if the resurrection of Jesus is shown to be decisively true (without any reasonable alternative), then it incapacitates any argument that Jesus was not divine.
     - Quran
         - The Quran possesses a preservation and inimitability that is superior to all alternatives, which are not authorized by God (ie. deceptive or demonic in origin).
         - Specifically: if the Quran is shown to be perfectly preserved and cannot have been produced by a human being, then it incapacitates any argument that Jesus was divine.
 
 - Is it ontologically possible and epistemologically attainable?
     - New Testament: Yes
         - Texts and historical events can be preserved over time, and their preservation can be analyzed by eliminating alternatives for fabrication and falsehood .
     - Quran: Yes
         - Texts and historical events can be preserved over time, and their preservation can be analyzed by eliminating alternatives for fabrication and falsehood.
         - Additionally, humans have a hard limit to what they are capable of doing, so anything that falls beyond that limit would verify inimitability.
 
 - What are the criteria for success and failure?
     - New Testament
         - Truth is defined/identified by consistency and reality, and falsehood by inconsistency and contradiction with reality.
         - If the Bible is authorized by God, then it must be the epitome of consistency and correspondence with reality. As a result, if all falsehoods necessarily contradict with reality, then the Quran cannot possess the same level of consistency and coherence as the Bible.
         - Therefore, success is showing that the Bible has a better form of preservation than the Quran and is more consistent and coherent. Failure is if the Quran's preservation and inimitability are stronger than the Bible's, since no manmade effort can ever surpass God's power.
     - Quran
         - Truth is defined/identified by consistency and reality, and falsehood by inconsistency and contradiction with reality.
         - If the Quran is authorized by God, then it must be the epitome of consistency and correspondence with reality. As a result, if all falsehoods necessarily contradict with reality, then the Bible cannot possess the same level of consistency and coherence as the Quran.
         - Therefore, success is showing that the Quran is truly inimitable, has a better form of preservation than the Bible, and is more consistent and coherent. Failure is if the Quran could have been manmade, or if Bible's preservation and historical reliability are stronger than the Quran's - since no manmade effort can ever surpass God's power.



2. Oral, Written, and Archaeological Presence
 - Who were the eyewitnesses, and what did they bear witness to?
     - New Testament
         - Three significant events: Jesus' 3-year earthly ministry, 40 days of Jesus' resurrection, and the post-resurrection vision that appeared to Paul.
         - Thousands of people witnessed Jesus's ministry (about 3 years long), while over 500 witnessed his 40-day bodily resurrection (between 30-33 AD). Finally, Paul witnessed a spiritual and personal vision of Jesus (between 33-36 AD).
     - Quran
         - Two significant events: the Quran's revelation to the Prophet Muhammad over 23 years, and his character, personality, and ministry in that time period.
         - Tens of thousands of people witnessed the Quran's revelation to the Prophet Muhammad (which were unique, context-specific events that occurred from 610-632 AD), and even more witnessed his ministry in that time.

 - How reliable was the cultural context for preserving oral tradition accurately?
     - New Testament
         - Core concepts preserved without verbatim transmission.
         - The Jewish culture at the time had a strong tradition of oral preservation - and their preserved texts meant that verbatim oral transmission was unnecessary.
         - The Bible largely reflects this: at least 35-80 years had passed after Jesus' ministry before the Gospels were written, in which cultural and linguistic barriers were crossed (transitioning from Jewish and Aramaic to Greek and Roman). Given the oral tradition before the texts were written and their standardization in a foreign language, this supports the claim that core concepts were preserved.
         - This makes verbatim preservation unlikely, but this is in line with classical views, as the tradition is seen as "living" where the core is preserved without significant corruption.
     - Quran
         - Verbatim preservation with the Quran, semi-verbatim preservation with the Prophet's life and character.
         - The 7th century Arabian culture already had an exceptionally strong tradition of oral preservation, as they lacked any written and textual presence before the Quran - which was the first completed written work of Arabic literature ever written. This meant that their oral literature before (and during) the Quran's revelation relied heavily on verbatim memorization in order to be preserved.
         - The Quran largely reflects this: it was memorized verbatim by all witnesses, with daily, audible, and communal prayers serving as a living verification by the community, in a language that continues to exist today and is geographically widespread. It was also preserved only in the Arabic that it was revealed in - with its rasm (consonantal skeleton) serving as its preservation in written form (and all translations only being considered as exegeses of the Arabic recitation) - thus eliminating possibilities of mythologization and gradual shifts in translation.
         - As for the Prophet's life and character, its oral tradition lasted for 100-200 years before being written and codified. As such, what is recorded isn't necessarily a verbatim transmission of what the original narrators said (with more room for fabrication and error) - the core narrative being reliably preserved, but requiring specialized scholarly effort to authenticate.

 - When was the text written and standardized, and by whom?
     - New Testament
         - Written by multiple authors (not Jesus) sometime 10-70 years after Jesus' ministry, while the canon was standardized in 382 AD by the Council of Rome.
         - The earliest New Testament texts were written 7-12 years after Jesus' ministry (in the earliest estimates), and were likely Galatians, Thessalonians (both by Paul), and possibly even James (although this is not a majority view). However (excluding James), Paul was not a witness to Jesus' earthly ministry nor his resurrection - and since the Gospels (eyewitness accounts of Jesus' ministry) were written 10-70 years after Jesus (considering both early and late dating), this puts them being written at a later time than the non-eyewitness accounts. This also means that none of the texts are authored by Jesus himself.
         - Meanwhile, heresies were prevalent from Paul's ministry and continued into 400 AD, with many heretical/apocryphal works being written. This resulted in a gradual process of standardization that culminated in the Council of Rome in 382 AD, establishing the New Testament canon (which was first proposed by Irenaeus in 180 AD).
     - Quran
         - Three significant events: the writing and ordering of the Quran during the Prophet Muhammad's life, the first complete written Quran being codified 2 years after his death, and the written standardization of the dialect/accent 20 years after his death.
         -  While the Quran was primarily memorized verbatim by the community, it was also written by the Prophet's scribes - namely, Zaid b. Thabit and his team - shortly after the revelation occurred. After his death, Abu Bakr (the first caliph after the Prophet) compiled the first written text of the entire Quran, as it was textually fragmented so far (because the Quran was revealed in context-specific situations). Finally, as Islam began to spread to the non-Arabian world, the dialect and diacritics were standardized 20 years after the Prophet's death by the fourth caliph, Uthman.
         - As for the Prophet's life and character, this started to be written down within 100 years after the Prophet's death, while the most rigorous works (the 6 Sahih Collections) were written 200 years after his death (or 300-500 years for the Shia collections).

 - What corroborating archaeological finds are there?
     - New Testament
         - Strong historical and cultural corroboration, with earliest manuscripts of the New Testament dating to 125 AD.
         - Some non-Christian writers (namely, Josephus and Tacitus, around 100 AD) reference Jesus in their historical works - although, none of them mention his resurrection or divinity (one passage in Josephus is debated). Moreover, the locations of cities, cultural practices of the Jews and Romans, and inscriptions of historical people all corroborate what is mentioned in the New Testament. Meanwhile, the earliest manuscript we have dates to 125 AD, although that is the exception: the vast majority of them are between 300-400 AD.
         - Therefore, as each of the New Testament books are claimed to have been written long before 125 AD, the primary means of dating when they were written is a purely logical endeavor, without corroborating archaeological manuscripts to verify.
     - Quran
         - Overwhelming historical and cultural corroboration, with the earliest manuscripts dating to the Prophet's life.
         - The Islamic Golden Age was a direct result of the Prophet's legacy, leaving us with overwhelming evidence of Islam's influence. Moreover, every single geographical location relevant to the Prophet's life is well known and corroborated, as well as extensive knowledge into the Prophet's life and the practices of the early Muslims. Finally, the earliest Quranic manuscript we have dates to the Prophet's own lifetime (which is verbatim of what we have now), although the vast majority of them are between 100-200 years after the Prophet's death.
         - Therefore, not only is the Quran reliably preserved verbatim by communal memorization, it is also soundly verifiable through archaeological means.



3. Assessment of the Chains of Transmission
 - How many independent streams of transmission are there, leading to the text's standardization? (ie. According to accepted tradition, who were the custodians of the message until its standardization?)
     - New Testament
         - 3 main streams (the synoptic, johannine, and pauline chains) that merge with Irenaeus, leading directly to the Council of Rome.
         - The synoptic stream goes from: Jesus → Peter (and the other Apostles) → Mark → Matthew & Luke → Papias → Polycarp of Smyrna → Irenaeus of Lyons.
         - The johannine stream goes from: Jesus → John the Apostle → Polycarp of Smyrna → Irenaeus of Lyons.
         - The pauline stream goes from: Jesus (post-resurrection vision) → Paul → Clement of Rome → Ignatius of Antioch & Polycarp of Smyrna → Irenaeus of Lyons.
         - From Irenaeus (from all three streams), we get the patristic chain: Irenaeus → Tertullian → Origen of Alexandria → Eusebius → Athanasius of Alexandria → the Council of Rome (led by Pope Damascus I).
         - Notes: Irenaeus was the earliest to list the 4 gospels and Paul's letters as canon, while Athanasius made the first known list of the mainstream New Testament canon. Also, even though Matthew was an eyewitness, he uses Mark's work (and other unknowns) as a reference. Luke uses Mark as well.
     - Quran
         - 2 main streams: the Quran and Hadith.
         - The Quranic stream goes from: Muhammad → scribes (led by Zaid b. Thabit) and memorizers (thousands, with hundreds memorizing the whole Quran) → Abu Bakr → Hafsa → Uthman.
         - The Hadith stream goes from: Muhammad → first generation Muslims (who met the Prophet) → second generation Muslims (and their texts) → third generation Muslims (and their texts) → the 6 standardized compilations (by Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Al-Trimidhi, Al-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah).
         - Notes: the Quran is claimed to be authored by God (ie. infallible), while the hadith are the works of men (ie. fallible). The Quranic scribes were led by Zaid b. Thabit (who would write almost immediately after each revelation), and that there were thousands of memorizers (with hundreds who memorized the entire Quran). Abu Bakr (with Zaid b. Thabit) was the first to standardize a full, written copy of the Quran (verified by those who directly witnessed the Quran's revelation), with Hafsah (one of the Prophet's wives) being its guardian. Lastly, Uthman was the one to textually standardize the Quran's accent/diacritics.

 - Are there competing claims, and if so, what are their chains of transmission?
     - New Testament
         - Yes - the Jewish Christians and Gnostics.
         - The Jewish Christians believed in Jesus' virgin birth and that he was the Messiah, but rejected Paul as a heretic and practiced Mosaic Law. As we don't have their text, it is also debated whether or not they believed that Jesus was divine.
             - Their chain goes from: Jesus → James (with Peter and the other Apostles) → oral transmission → written texts (now lost).
         - The Gnostics believed that Jesus was purely divine, and the god of the Old Testament was a flawed creator, lesser than the true, transcendent God.
             - Their stream goes from: Jesus → secret knowledge to a chose disciple → private line of enlightened teachers → Gnostic gospels.
         -Notes: all Jewish Christian writings are lost, and what is known about them only comes from hostile sources, making much of it debated - including whether the "human Jesus" belief was early or later. The Ebionites were a noteworthy sect within the Jewish Christians, only differing by rejecting the virgin birth. After the Bar Kokhba revolt, they (along with the Jews in Rome) were expelled and dispersed throughout Africa, the Mediterranean, and parts of the Arab world. As a result, very little is known about them with a strong certainty, with the only other possible references originating in later 7th-century Arab history.
     - Quran
         - No with the Quran, yes with the Hadith (Shia vs. Sunni).
         - There are no competing claims for the reliability or content of the Quran.
         - The closest comparison is the qira'at (diffferent accentual/dialectical recitations) that the Prophet himself is reported to have used, but they are all accepted as authentic and even continue to be openly recited today.
         - The only other comparison is the report of the Satanic verses, mentioned by Muslim historians 100–250 years after the Prophet's death. When surah al-Najm was revealed (which ends with the command to prostrate to God) and the Prophet recited it publicly, the Makkan pagans are reported to have prostrated in awe of the Quran, their hearts touched by its power. Out of embarrassment, they later claimed they had heard 1-2 pagan-affirming verses and prostrated for that reason, alleging that Muhammad must have been tricked by Satan without realizing. However, this is a weak biographical report with no supporting transmission, no parallel incident in the historical record, and a motive (pagan embarrassment) that aligns more closely with their behavior outside of this incident. As such, it is not considered a successful challenge to the Quran's reliability or authority.
         - There was also the Shia/Sunni split, but they differed over the nature of Ali b. Abi Talib, which is who the Shia assert was the rightful caliph after the Prophet's death. They believed that those closest to the Prophet corrupted the religion after he died, fabricating reports in his name to gain political power. As such, they have their own set of hadith compilations, in which the Prophet entrusted the true interpretation of the religion with Ali and his descendants (including Fatima, the Prophet's daughter and Ali's wife).
         - The Shia Hadith stream goes from: Muhammad → Ali b. Abi Talib → Hasan & Husain b. Ali → Ali Zayn al-Abidin → Muhammad al-Baqir → Jafar al-Sadiq → (the remaining Twelver imams) → Al-Kulayni (compiler of Shia hadith).

 - What are the strongest, independent chains of custodianship for each stream of transmission? (ie. How do we actually know what happened?)
     - New Testament
         - Custodianship converges with Irenaeus, Eusebius, and Paul.
         - Synoptic Chain (Mark)
             - Eusebius → Papias (now lost) →  unnamed "Elder" → Mark → Gospel of Mark → Peter → Jesus
             - Irenaeus → unknown oral transmission (likely Polycarp → Papias) → Mark → Gospel of Mark → Peter → Jesus
         - Synoptic Chain (Matthew)
             - Eusebius → Papias (now lost) →  unknown oral tradition → Matthew → Q/Logia (now lost, written in Hebrew or Aramaic) → Jesus
             - Irenaeus → unknown oral transmission (likely Polycarp → Papias) → Matthew → Gospel of Matthew (written in Greek) → Jesus
             - Unknown author → the Didache → almost identical Lord's Prayer as in Matthew → Jesus 
         - Synoptic Chain (Luke)
             - Irenaeus → unknown oral transmission → Luke → Gospel of Luke → unknown eyewitnesses → Jesus
         - Johannine Chain
             - Gospel of John → unknown disciple whom Jesus loved (self attested) → Jesus's crucifixion
             - Irenaeus → Polycarp (oral, not written) → John → Gospel of John → Jesus
         - Pauline Chain
             - Paul's letters →  Paul (self-attested) → Jesus (post-resurrection vision)
             - Clement of Rome →  Paul →  Paul's letters
             - Ignatius of Antioch →  unknown Antioch oral tradition →  Paul →  Paul's letters
         - Jewish Christian/Nazarene Chain
             - Clement of Rome →  Paul's letters →  Judaizers → Jesus
             - Eusebius → unknown Jewish Christian oral tradition → unknown author of Hebrew/Nazarene Gospel (now lost - possibly Q/Logia) → Jesus
             - Eusebius → Papias (now lost) →  unknown oral tradition (likely via → Matthew → Q/Logia [now lost]) → Jesus
             - Justin Martyr → unknown oral tradition of tolerant Jewish Christians → Jesus
             - Justin Martyr → unknown oral tradition of separatist Jewish Christians → Jesus
         - Ebionite Chain
             - Epiphanus → Ebionite gospel (now lost) → unknown Ebionite tradition → James and/or Peter → Jesus
             - Irenaeus → unknown Ebionite tradition → unknown author of Ebionite gospel → Jesus
             - Justin Martyr → unknown oral tradition of Jewish Christians who believed Jesus was a "man of men (ie. naturally born) → Jesus 
         - Gnostic Chain
             - Irenaeus → Gnostic tradition → secret (unknown) oral tradition → secret (unknown) apostle → Jesus
         - Notes: each chain begins with the earliest preserved source in that transmission. John is the only Gospel writer reported to have witnessed the crucifixion directly, while Paul's letters were written earlier than the Gospels. It is not unusual that the Gospel authors did not identify themselves within the text, since their audiences already knew the authors, with manuscripts being tagged with their names - much like authored books today. The Christian claim is that the Holy Spirit is the primary custodian of truth, ensuring its preservation regardless of historical circumstances.
     - Quran
         - Quranic custodianship from Uthman, Hafsa, Abu Bakr, and those who memorized the Quran, while Hadith custodianship converges with the textual compilations.
         - Quranic Chain
             - 1,000s of named individuals (and 10,000s of unknown individuals) who memorized the Quran → Muhammad
             - Uthman b. Affan → Hafsah b. Umar →  Abu Bakr → Zaid b. Thabit → original scribes and memorizers → Muhammad
         - Hadith Chain (Sunni)
             - 6 standardized compilations (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Al-Trimidhi, Al-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah) and more (Muwatta of Imam Malik, Musnad Ibn Hanbal, etc.) → line of known individuals, each with an authenticity grading → Muhammad
                 - If a link between narrators is unknown, the hadith is given a very weak grading.
         - Hadith Chain (Shia)
             - 4 standardized compilations (al-Kulayni, Ibn Babawayh, al-Tusi) and more (ex. Bihar al-Anwar, Nahj al-Balagha, etc.) → students and companions of an infallible imam → line of infallible, divinely guided Imams → Muhammad
         - Notes: the Islamic claim is that the angel Gabriel - also known as the Holy Spirit and responsible for conveying revelation to all Prophets - is the custodian of the Quran between God and Muhammad. The Quran further asserts that God Himself guarantees its preservation, reinforcing the belief that truth remains distinguishable from falsehood. Additionally, a core difference between Sunni and Shia theology is that Shia Muslims hold the lineage of Ali b. Abi Talib to be infallible through their imams for twelve generations.

 - What are the weakest links in the strongest chains of custodianship?
     - New Testament
         - All chains have gaps (either relying on unknown transmission or lost works) except for the Pauline chain.
         - Synoptic Chains
             - Both chains for Mark support the claim that he learned from Peter, but rely on lost work (Papias), an unnamed transmitter, and an unspecified oral tradition.
             - The chains for Matthew and Luke both depend on unknown transmitters, and Luke additionally relies on unknown source material.
                 - Note that Matthew, Mark, and Luke state that only the female disciples witnessed the crucifixion (the most important theological event) - meaning that neither Peter (Mark's source) nor Matthew (another disciple of Jesus) witnessed it either.
         - Johannine Chain
             - The chain for John depends on Irenaeus, whose claim for John's authorship relies on Polycarp, who only orally testified this to Irenaeus without writing it in his work.
                 - John is the only Gospel author who states that he witnessed the crucifixion.
         - Pauline Chain
             - Paul's letters are self-attesting and corroborated by Clement of Rome.
             - Ignatius of Antioch also corroborates Paul, but his chain relies on unknown oral transmission.
         - Jewish Christian/Nazarene, Ebionite, and Gnostic Chains
             - All orthodox-competing chains rely on lost work and unknown oral traditions.
             - The Gnostic chain is the exception in that their gospels are preserved, but their chain appears intentionally hidden (according to Irenaeus).
         - Notes: all chains about the earthly Jesus contain varying degrees of historical uncertainty, with each relying on some unknown or uncorroborated transmission.
     - Quran
         - The potential weaknesses of the oral and textual chains are mitigated by the other.
         - Quranic Chain
             - The standardized written text safeguarded by Hafsah is corroborated by mass memorization, while mass memorization is protected from alteration by the standardized text.
         - Hadith Chain (Sunni)
             - The more narrators in a chain, the greater the risk of weakness - especially with single-narrator reports (known as 'ahad' hadith).
             - The science of authenticating hadith and examining narrators was established alongside the compilers, allowing hadith narrations to be classified based on their authenticity - and putting more pressure on the compilers for being reliable.
         - Hadith Chain (Shia)
             - Reliability is tied to the infallibility of the narrators, which is unfalsifiable and supported by fewer corroborating chains than in Sunni transmission - leaving greater room for weakness.
         - Notes: the Quran appears to be reliably preserved in history (verified at a later step), while Hadith reliability is dependent on the strength of its compilers and methods.



4. Alignment with Historical Context
 - What was the historical context throughout the chain of custodianship?
     - New Testament
         - Three significant time periods: the crucifixion of Jesus, the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple, and the Bar Kokhba revolt.
         - From Jesus' ministry (30–33 AD) until the destruction of the Temple in 70 AD, the earliest Christian community existed as a small, multilingual Jewish sect within the Roman Empire. Oral reports about Jesus circulated both publicly and privately and were centered around the Church of Jerusalem.
         - After 70 AD, Christianity spread more rapidly across the Roman Empire, shifting from a primarily Jewish and Aramaic context to a predominantly Gentile and Greek-speaking one - even while facing persecution. Written accounts multiplied and circulated during this phase, including traditions that were later deemed heretical.
         - After the failed Bar Kokhba revolt (132–136 AD), the split between Christianity and Rabbinic Judaism became permanent. Christianity developed a more formal institutional structure, and Church Fathers began defending and consolidating apostolic tradition against rival claims. After Constantine's conversion in 312 AD, Rome emerged as the new capital of Christianity.
     - Quran
         - Three significant time periods: the time of Quranic revelation, the time of Quranic textual standardization, and the time of Hadith compilation.
         - The Quran was revealed to Prophet Muhammad over 23 years (610-632 AD, 13 in Makkah and 10 in Madinah) uniting warring, independent tribes into a single nation and extending its message into northern Africa and southern Europe.
         - After the Prophet's death in 632 AD, the next 4 caliphs standardized the Quranic text to combat the threat of memorizers falling in battle, as well to ensure its meaning would be consistent among the Arab tribes with different dialects.
         - After Uthman standardized the Quranic dialect (between 560-656 AD), Islam expanded beyond Arabia, making it harder to verify which reports about the Prophet were authentic. From 684-915 AD, hadith compilations were written and a formal science of authentication emerged. This period also marked the beginning of the Islamic Golden Age, during which many branches of knowledge - including, but not limited to, Hadith - were developed and systematized.

 - What were the standards of testimony and custodianship in this time?
     - New Testament
         - Eyewitness testimony, gradually shifting towards scholarly textual standardization.
         - In the 1st-century Jewish context, valid testimony required at least two credible witnesses (free, adult males without conflict of interest), based on Deuteronomy 19:15 and Mishnaic traditions. Roman standards similarly valued reputable eyewitnesses (non-criminals) and oral testimony in court, with writing reserved for limited formal records.
         - After 70 AD, as Christianity spread under Roman persecution, standards shifted toward Greco-Roman historiography and Jewish diaspora oral practices, resulting in semi-verbatim preservation and the growth of written accounts that defended core narratives against competing versions. From the 2nd-4th centuries, Roman legal writing expanded, while Christian custodianship formalized around three criteria: apostolic origin, catholicity (universal acceptance), and non-contradiction with established faith.
         - Thus, the Bible's guiding standard was credible eyewitness testimony with faithful transmission of meaning and message, not verbatim memorization. This is reflected in the numerous early textual variants, which arose from scribes attempting to better preserve and transmit the intended truth than the original.
     - Quran
         - Communally verbatim oral transmission.
         - Pre-Islamic Arabia was a predominantly oral culture that lacked literary and textual presence. As a result, they preserved history through memory, oral performance, and poetic competition rather than writing - which was a skill that was seen as a source of immense cultural pride and honor. Eloquence, genealogy, and precise memorization were central to cultural identity, and written materials - though scarce - were secondary aids to the superior discipline of trained memory.
         - This same system continued during the Quran's revelation and after, producing mass, communal, verbatim memorization, later mirrored in Hadith sciences as well. Daily prayers, being communal and audible, required Quranic recitation - meaning any mistake could be corrected publicly, reinforcing collective preservation. As a result, the oral Quran functioned as a primary, living authority, while also anchoring its textual standardization and safeguarding its exact wording.

 - Is there anything anachronistic (historically or logically out of place) about the claim or its custodianship?
     - New Testament
         - Many examples against Jewish expectations.
         - Social and Cultural
             - Female testimony was not considered credible, yet all 4 Gospels report that the first witnesses of Jesus' resurrection were women.
             - Jews and Samaritans were hostile toward one another, yet Jesus is depicted engaging a Samaritan positively.
             - Despite purity laws and social boundaries, Jesus is shown dining with, healing, and ministering to those considered "unclean", including tax collectors, lepers, prostitutes, and other outcasts.
             - In a culture where superiors would not submit to inferiors, Jesus is baptized by John in an act of symbolic cleansing - despite John being lower in status.
         - Theological
             - Although Jewish monotheism rejected the idea that God could be a man, Jesus is depicted as divine - God Himself. However, relative to Greco-Roman beliefs, this claim would not have seemed anachronistic.
             - Although Jewish tradition emphasized God's open and jealous self-disclosure, Jesus' divine identity is portrayed as implied or veiled rather than explicit.
             - Although the expected Messiah would triumph, Jesus is portrayed as crucified by Rome - a shameful death reserved for criminals. The resurrection resolves this issue, but the very concept of a dying-rising figure was not unusual in Greco-Roman contexts.
             - Despite the expectation that authentic messages would be clear and consistent from the start (and orally transmitted as such), the Gospels appear to reflect a rising Christology, with Jesus's divinity becoming more easily identifiable in later-written texts.
         - Notes: Jesus also reinterpreted Jewish understanding of the law (such as being more lenient with stoning, yet more strict with divorce), but these are non-fundamental. Also, regarding the rising Christology: some argue that this sign of changing what Jesus himself claimed, while others defend by defining it as progressive revelation, where different authors highlight different aspects of the complete, unified truth.
     - Quran
         - Many examples against the 7th century Arabian context.
         - Social and Cultural
             - Females were seen as inferior to males, yet the Quran states that men and women are equal under the sight of God. When women weren't able to own property, inherit wealth, and consent to marriage, now they can. Where daughters could be buried alive due to her birth dishonoring her family, now that was considered a crime.
             - Despite deeply embedded tribalism (where an individual's identity, protection, nobility, and honor were entirely dependent on their tribe), the Quran states that the most noble in the sight of God are those who are most righteous.
             - Despite Arabian expertise and history of established poetry and eloquence, the Quran's structure does not fall in any prior literary or oral genres.
             - Despite the Quran being considered the height of eloquence (even by its opponents), it is attributed to Muhammad - a man known to be illiterate. Yet there are many instances where the verses are immediately revealed in response to live, unpredictable situations - without later revision, proofreading, correction, or alteration to the original words.
         - Theological
             - In a context in which religious scriptures are written by men (and are defended as divinely guided), the Quran claims to be authored by God Himself, where it explicitly states that He will be the one to preserve it.
             - Despite being geographically surrounded by polytheists (denying the hereafter), Jews (rejecting an Arab prophet), and Christians (upholding Trinitarian or heterodox views), the Quran proclaims a pure monotheism distinct from all three - though closest in form to Jewish belief.
         - Notes: The Quran also reinterpreted Jewish understanding of the law (such as explaining why the laws were so strict) and Jesus' identity as pure human being, but these are non-fundamental. Also, some argue that the Quran's historical and natural claims are uniquely and strikingly consistent with established history and science, but that is also non-fundamental.



5. Resistance to Fabrication
 - What possibilities are there for fabrication and error in the claim?
     - New Testament
         - Liar, lunatic, or demonic influence.
         - Because Jesus left no written texts, the claim of his divinity depends entirely on the accuracy of the Gospel accounts. If the Gospels are assumed to be fully accurate, then error could only originate from Jesus himself - either by deliberately deceiving his followers in being authorized by God, by sincerely speaking under delusion, or by being influenced by an unseen/demonic source (knowingly or unknowingly).
     - Quran
         - Liar, lunatic, or demonic influence.
         - The Quran (spoken through Muhammad) claims to be a verbatim revelation from God. Therefore, all potential fabrication or error must originate from Muhammad himself - either by deliberately deceiving his followers in being authorized by God, by sincerely speaking under delusion, or by being influenced by an unseen/demonic source (knowingly or unknowingly).

 - How strongly does the claim resist fabrication and error?
     - Both
         - To be able to distinguish between truth and falsehood, there must be some identifiable property between competing claims that would render one true and the other false.
         - What is the epistemological difference between an honest man and a liar?
             - People lie to gain something (ex. money, status, revenge) they can't get by being honest. But lies fundamentally contradict reality, and so any attempt to mix truth with lies inevitably snowballs and exposes those contradictions over time into unresolvable inconsistencies.
         - What is the epistemological difference between an intelligent man and an insane one?
             - Both might appear complicated and deep on the surface, yet intelligence leads to consistent and hierarchal complexity, while insanity leads to chaos, ineffectiveness, and inconsistency.
         - What is the epistemological difference between divine inspiration and demonic inspiration?
             - God, angels, and demons all act in mysterious ways, making it initially appear impossible to distinguish between them. Both are beyond our capacity to sensually witness and verify, yet if God is fundamentally True while demons preach falsehood, then a demonic influence will appeal to arguments that inhibit proper reasoning, thus disabling one's ability to distinguish truth from falsehood.
     - New Testament

                     - Internal consistency: no internal evidence of layered composition, stylistic plurality, editorial seams, or revision-patterns that would indicate distributed authorship, literary construction, or developmental redaction
                 - Only when these corruption windows are positively ruled out - not merely unrecorded - does the preservation claim move from probable to historically secure.
             - Note that each requirement ultimately reduces to the elimination of plausible alternatives - in the absence of documented alternatives. This reflects the distinction between reasonable uncertainty and speculative doubt (or, unreasonable uncertainty): the mere absence of rival narratives does not by itself prove exclusivity (since "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence"), but if a text originated publicly, was preserved from the outset, and faced no viable conditions for alternative versions to develop, then the absence of rivals is exactly what we would expect. Once a claim's environment leaves no plausible pathway for an alternative, demanding imaginary alternatives becomes speculation, not skepticism (since "speculation is not refutation").
             - This guards against both retroactive assumption (accepting a claim merely because no rival is recorded) and hyperskepticism (rejecting a claim by inventing scenarios without evidence). Instead, we commit to evidence-based constraints applied to known history, ensuring that our conclusions rest on what is possible under the documented environment, not on blind trust or imaginative doubt.

             - What is being incrementally added: progressive revelation gradually reveals a new part of the whole Truth, while gradual development reveals a new part of an evolving falsehood. As such, answering this question requires first independently establishing the truth.

             - Access to original events: primary sources are direct witnesses to events, while secondary sources reconstruct events from intermediaries. Because reconstruction requires inference, a primary source inherently has access to information that no secondary reconstruction can fully recover, verify, or correct.
         - What is the epistemological difference between known and unknown authorship?
             - Accountability vs. speculation: known authorship allows a claim to be historically tested (the author's life, motives, competence, access, and context can be evaluated), while unknown authorship forces reliance on speculation about who the author was, what they knew, and why they wrote. Therefore, known authorship allows evidence-based confidence, while unknown authorship invites open-ended doubt that cannot be resolved by historical method.
         - What is the epistemological difference between known and unknown preservation?
             - Traceable vs. untraceable transmission: known preservation provides an identifiable chain of custody that can be checked, challenged, or corroborated at each stage, while unknown preservation lacks verifiable checkpoints and collapses into trust or conjecture. Therefore, known preservation allows us to distinguish between continuity and corruption, while unknown preservation prevents any meaningful judgment about either.

            - Quran: 7th century context preserved verbatim with the Quran, semi-verbatim preservation with the Prophet's life and character.
        - When was the text written and standardized, and by whom?
            - New Testament: The Gospels and Epistles were written by multiple authors (not Jesus) sometime 10-70 years after Jesus' ministry, while the canon was standardized in 382 AD by the Council of Rome.
            - Quran: Three significant events: the writing and ordering of the Quran during the Prophet Muhammad's life, the first complete written Quran being codified 2 years after his death, and the written standardization of the dialect/accent 20 years after his death.
        - What corroborating archaeological finds are there?
            - New Testament: Archaeology shows strong historical and cultural corroboration, with earliest manuscripts of the New Testament dating to 125 AD.
            - Quran: Archaeology shows overwhelming historical and cultural corroboration, with the earliest manuscripts dating to the Prophet's life.

    C. Assessment of the Chains of Transmission
        - How many independent streams of transmission are there, leading to the text's standardization?
            - There are 3 main streams of transmission (the synoptic, johannine, and pauline chains) that merge with Irenaeus, starting the patristic stream and leading directly to its standardization in the Council of Rome.
            - There are 2 main streams of transmission: the Quran (through its eyewitnesses, both orally and textually) and Hadith (through chains of eyewitness testimony, until its textual standardization).
        - Are there competing claims, and if so, what are their chains of transmission?
            - New Testament: Yes - the Jewish Christians and Gnostics are competing claims to Jesus' message.
            - Quran: No competing claims to the Quran's transmission, while there are competing claims for the transmission of Hadith (Shia vs. Sunni).
        - What are the strongest, independent chains of custodianship for each stream of transmission?
            - New Testament: Custodianship converges with Irenaeus, Eusebius, and Paul.
            - Quran: Quranic custodianship rests on Uthman, Hafsa, Abu Bakr, and those who memorized it verbatim, while Hadith custodianship converges with the textual compilations.
        - What are the weakest links in the strongest chains of custodianship?
            - New Testament: All chains have gaps (either relying on unknown transmission or lost works) - except for the Pauline chain.
            - Quran: The potential weaknesses of the oral and textual chains are mitigated by the other.

    D. Alignment with Historical Context
        - What was the historical context throughout the chain of custodianship?
            - New Testament: Three significant time periods: the crucifixion of Jesus, the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple, and the Bar Kokhba revolt.
            - Quran: Three significant time periods: the time of Quranic revelation, the time of Quranic textual standardization, and the time of Hadith compilation.
        - What were the standards of testimony and custodianship in this time?
            - New Testament: Custodianship started with eyewitness testimony, gradually shifting towards scholarly textual standardization over time.
            - Quran: Standard custodianship was communally verbatim oral transmission.
        - Is there anything anachronistic (historically or logically out of place) about the claim or its custodianship?
            - New Testament: Many anachronistic examples against Jewish expectations.
            - Quran: Many anachronistic examples against the 7th century Arabian context.

    E. Resistance to Fabrication
        - What possibilities are there for fabrication and error in the claim?
            - New Testament: Jesus could theoretically be a liar, lunatic, or be under demonic influence.
            - Quran: Muhammad could theoretically be a liar, lunatic, or be under demonic influence.
        - How strongly does the claim resist fabrication and error?
            - New Testament: Jesus' character assessment strongly resists being a liar, lunatic, or being under demonic influence, but the trinitarian doctrine is weak by logical consistency - which is resolved by faithful historical and textual preservation by its custodians.
            - Quran: Muhammad's character assessment strongly resists being a liar, lunatic, or being under demonic influence.
        - What possibilities are there for fabrication and error with the claim's custodianship?
            - New Testament: The custodianship of Jesus' message could have been with bad faith, revisionism, and gradual Christological development.
            - Quran: The custodianship of the Quran could have been with preservation errors and contextual reinterpretation.
        - How strongly does the claim's custodianship resist fabrication and error? (Requirements for Trusting Defense)
            - New Testament: To verify strong custodianship of Jesus' message, Paul's reliability and alignment with what the earthly Jesus said needs to be independently established (before claiming truth).
            - Quran: To verify strong custodianship of the Quran, all plausible natural pathways for fabrication or alteration need to be eliminated.
        - How strongly does the claim's custodianship resist fabrication and error? (Texts)
            - New Testament: Gospel custodianship, defending claims, and fabrication/error claims all fundamentally rely on speculation.
            - Quran: Historical constraints only allow Muhammad as the Quran's origin, leaving no viable alternative authorship, transmission, or fabrication with its custodianship.
        - How strongly does the claim's custodianship resist fabrication and error? (Trustworthiness)
            - New Testament: Deception and/or demonic inspiration are both plausible explanations for Paul's work, with all defenses requiring retroactive assumptions of truth.
            - Quran: All requirements for Muhammad fabricating the Quran are historically unsupported.

    F. Comparative Summary

            - Quran: publicly verifiable and correctable by a lay person

7. Conclusion
 - How strong is the claim and its custodianship?
     - New Testament
         - Weak claim, moderate custodianship, all defenses requiring retroactive assumptions of truth
         - Although the Gospels portray a Jesus whose character strongly resists the charge of fabrication, the reliability of his message solely depends on the integrity of its transmission. Because Jesus left no verified writings and authorized no surviving text during his lifetime, all information about him reached us through later custodians. This means that if fabrication or error occurred, it must have entered through the channels that preserved his memory - not through Jesus himself. The establishment of the truth, therefore, shifts to them.
         - In the earliest period, those channels were not unified. Multiple teachers, communities, and gospels circulated in competition with one another. Among these, the stream that prevailed and became mainstream Christianity was the Pauline stream. Paul's letters are the earliest surviving Christian writings, and his theology most closely resembles later Trinitarian orthodoxy. However, the New Testament itself records that Paul faced persistent accusations from other early Christians, and several of his practices - most notably his stance on the Law - sharply diverged from the Jewish followers of Jesus. When these criticisms are read alongside Jesus' own warnings about false teachers, the result may not be proof that Paul was unreliable, but it shows that this possibility can no longer be eliminated. To defend Paul as the truthful, accurate, authoritative transmitter of Jesus' message, one must either retroactively assume that his version was divinely protected, or appeal to the strength of the early Church's custodianship to dismiss the possibility of early theological corruption.
         - Yet when examining the Gospel tradition, the same pattern appears. All 4 Gospels were written after Paul's letters. Only 2 are attributed to eyewitnesses, and only John is claimed to have witnessed the crucifixion itself - the single most important event in Christian theology. Yet John is also the latest Gospel and reflects the highest Christology, written after decades of possible doctrinal development and corresponding at a time when Pauline Christianity had already gained traction. Meanwhile, the chains of transmission that lead to these texts pass through a long period of lost sources, anonymous editors, and unknown oral transmitters. The first figure to clearly name the four Gospels as a unified canon is Irenaeus, writing roughly 150 years after Jesus. His testimony is also self-referential: we possess no independent corroboration for his claims (that don't appeal to unknown transmission), nor for his descriptions of the rival Christian groups whose writings have vanished. Thus, all surviving transmission streams - including the winning one - rest on broken links, lost documents, and insiders attesting to themselves.
         - As a result, the reliability of the New Testament cannot be established without speculation or retroactive assumption. It's a double-edged sword: dating the Gospels earlier shortens the writing gap but lengthens the period of untraceable oral transmission; dating them later shortens the oral period but places composition farther from the events. Either way, unknown transmission is unavoidable. Because “all information requires a source” and claims must be externally verifiable to distinguish them from fabrication, a system whose core doctrines rely on untestable links and self-attestation appears to fundamentally act just like a false claim - producing a fatal circular dependency: the doctrine is needed to trust the text, but the text is needed to justify the doctrine. It is an infinite regress of trustworthiness: both depend on the other's reliability, yet neither can function as the independent foundation for the other - thus contradicting "all information has a source". A truth claim that cannot stand without first being assumed collapses under its own epistemology - it can't be called “trustworthy” if the defense relies on assumptions that themselves still require proof.
         - This leads to the same result reached in the previous section: Trinitarianism cannot ground its core belief - God's nature - in either a self-consistent logical model, or a historically verifiable transmission. The failure of one magnifies the weakness of the other. If the theology is false, the historical weakness is expected; if the theology is true, its preservation should be clear, consistent, and resistant to fabrication from the start. This is not what the Biblical historical record reflects.
     - Quran
         - Strong claim (pending logical verification), strong custodianship, alternatives require historically unsupported speculation
         - The Quran claims to be the direct speech of God, revealed in Arabic through Muhammad over 23 years, recited publicly in specific historical moments, memorized verbatim by many contemporaries, and written down by named scribes while he was alive. Within about two years of his death it was compiled as a complete written corpus, then pronunciation/diacritics were standardized within two decades (to prevent drift as Islam expanded to non-Arabic regions). We still have very early manuscript evidence and a continuous oral tradition consistent with that corpus, and no rival claims to the Quran's authorship is ever recorded as a sustained, competing lineage. The result is an unusually redundant chain (oral plus written, public plus named individuals) that sharply limits the space for silent post-Muhammad alteration.
         - As a result, because that custodianship is so constrained, any serious challenge has to be aimed at Muhammad himself. The options are: (1) he knowingly fabricated it, (2) he was deluded, (3) he was influenced by a deceptive supernatural source, or (4) he absorbed and repackaged outside material (from Jews, Christians, poets, storytellers) using extraordinary native brilliance.
         - The first option (conscious fabrication) sits badly with cost and timing. Muhammad delivered material that attacked his society's religious authority and economics while he and his followers were weak, targeted, and in danger. He refused offers of wealth and status to stop. He also recited verses that restricted him personally, publicly corrected him, or created short-term exposure in return for long-term outcomes that, at the time, did not look realistic to expect (for example, intertribal unification and moral reform in a region defined by tribal rivalry, or rebuking him for dismissing a blind man while cutting himself off from the support he was trying to achieve). This is not how a self-serving fabricator normally behaves, because it trades immediate safety for futures that were not obviously achievable when spoken.
         - The second option (delusion/instability) is hard to reconcile with the historical result: over those same 23 years, he is credited with unifying rival tribes under a shared moral-legal order, institutionalizing daily communal recitation of the text, and leaving behind a movement that heavily tied itself to both his personality and the text itself (leading to a golden age at a time of global darkness). That pattern looks like disciplined social and intellectual structuring, not incoherence.
         - The third option (demonic influence) runs into self-sabotage. The Quran repeatedly teaches listeners to seek refuge in God from satanic manipulation, gives criteria for detecting deception, and portrays demonic influence as an enemy to be unmasked and resisted. That is not how a deceiver typically sustains control; it arms the audience against the deceiver's own alleged method.
         - The fourth option says: Muhammad was simply astonishing - a kind of untrained prodigy who absorbed Jewish, Christian, and regional ideas, reorganized them into a single totalizing theology, expressed them in highly structured, high-register Arabic that impressed expert audiences, and then delivered that material live, on demand, across decades, without visible drafting, revision, or collapse of internal stance - all while running a community, negotiating tribal politics, surviving persecution, and leading in war. This explanation requires assuming an unprecedented combination of flawless memory, literary invention, theological synthesis, political foresight, and timing luck - all appearing in a man with no prior record of producing anything like this before prophethood and under severe, hostile scrutiny once it began. That level of "coincidental brilliance plus perfect execution" is not historically nor logically defensible, as invoking it is itself a speculative assumption that goes beyond the observable record.
         - What remains after those routes is this: the main exposure point is Muhammad himself, not later editors. The transmission after him is so tightly constrained and publicly checkable that there is no parallel Quranic lineage to appeal to. The next question, then, is not further speculation in "was the Quran altered?" but a testable "is the Quran what it says it is?" The Quran claims to be the direct speech of God. On its own terms, that means it must be internally self-consistent, free of contradiction, and aligned with sound reasoning and observable reality, because it ties God to perfect truth and non-contradiction. This is explored in the following analysis, which tests to verify that claim of internal and external coherence.
     - Both
         - If truth is defined by consistency, and God is the Truth, and reality itself is sustained by His consistent and unwavering will, then the true religion (by definition) must reflect that same, maximal consistency in both its message and its preservation. As a result, a belief that must be presupposed or speculated about in order to be defended can't be verified or be distinguishable from falsehood - and a system that cannot distinguish itself from a well-crafted falsehood cannot serve as a reliable guide to ultimate truth. And when we apply that to the New Testament and Quran, we find that the direction of speculation flips: the preservation of the New Testament requires speculation to be true, while the preservation of the Quran requires speculation to be false.
         - For the New Testament, the central claim (that Jesus is divine in the Trinitarian sense) passes through transmission lines with anonymous or lost intermediaries, early competing claims (to which their works and arguments are lost), and later standardization. In order to believe that this theology is true (ie. is the original), we have to first assume that the winning stream preserved the authentic truth, that rising Christology is "progressive revelation" and not doctrinal evolution, and that rivals were mistaken. But this is self-referential: the doctrine is used to validate the text, and the text is used to validate the doctrine: the claim needs external speculation to prove that it is true.
         - For the Quran, the core claim (that it is the direct speech of God) is tied to established historical constraints: Muhammad publicly recited it in real time, eyewitnesses mass-memorized the text verbatim, known scribes wrote while he was alive, it was compiled within two years of his death, and its pronunciation was stabilized across the community within two decades. Early manuscript evidence and established 7th century Arab standards corroborate these constraints, and no rival claims to the Quran's transmission exist (neither before nor after Muhammad's death). That means the usual path for post-founder corruption (quiet or hidden revision, doctrinal editing generations later) is largely blocked. To reject authenticity, you can't point to a vague gap in transmission; you'd have to argue that Muhammad himself fabricated the Quran or was otherwise the single point of failure - but this also conflicts with the historical constraints. In other words, here the speculation runs the other way: the Quran needs speculation to be false.
         - In comparison with the New Testament, the Quran enters history with a different pattern of preservation - public, continuous, and empirically accessible - making fabrication difficult from the start. If we judged the Quran by the Bible's standards, it would exceed what Muslims even claim for it; and if we judged the Bible by the Quran's standards, it would be considered one of the weakest narrations. The Quran's defenses are also stronger manifestations of the same categories: a “living tradition” is where the entire community safeguards the text, with any normal person being able to correct an authority (not the expectation of differing accounts); "progressive revelation" is where it was revealed over time while the Messenger is still alive and held accountable in public (not where fundamental narratives are written decades after his death); and "God preserved the scripture" is where preservation is obvious to a normal while incapacitating the plausibility of alternatives (not where it requires speculation to do the same). This also makes verification accessible: it is communal and open, not dependent on specialists. By contrast, defending the New Testament's highest claims requires expert historical reconstruction to decide which sources are "authentic", why rivals are invalid, and why one theological line alone goes back to Jesus without modification. This raises a basic question: if revelation is meant to guide both the uneducated and the elite, why would salvation depend on arguments and knowledge that ordinary people can't personally verify?
         - All of this lands us in the same place: a belief that must be presupposed in order to be defended cannot be independently verified or meaningfully distinguished from a well-crafted falsehood. And a system whose historical record is objectively weaker than that of a rival claim cannot be the final revelation of the God who authored reality, because the true religion cannot be less "true" - less consistent, less constrained against fabrication, less available to honest scrutiny - than a false one.
         - From here, the question shifts. The Quran does not only claim preservation; it claims to be the direct speech of God and the epitome of consistency - even inviting readers to test its claims. As such, the next section compares the "fruits" of Christianity with Islam's - verifying its inimitable internal coherence, moral structure, and correspondence with reality.
