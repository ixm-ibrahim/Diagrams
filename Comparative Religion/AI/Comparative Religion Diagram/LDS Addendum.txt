###############################################################
          LDS Addendum to the Comparative Religion Diagram
###############################################################

This file holds the LDS-side additions to be merged back into the
main project. Each phase mirrors the structure of the original
file it extends, so any block here can be lifted into the
corresponding section of the original document.

LDS canonical sources used:
 - The Standard Works: King James Bible, Book of Mormon, Doctrine
   and Covenants, Pearl of Great Price (Moses, Abraham, Joseph
   Smith—History, Articles of Faith).
 - Foundational doctrinal teachings treated as canonical/binding by
   the LDS Church: King Follett Discourse, the Lectures on Faith
   (when explicitly retained as doctrine), and statements of
   modern prophets reaffirmed in General Conference.

Steelmanning rule (from project rules): the LDS position is
presented in its strongest internally consistent form before
critique; commitments adopted in any one section are applied
uniformly across all sections.



###############################################################
 PHASE 1 — Section 5: Alignment with Reality (LDS addendum)
###############################################################

----------------------------------------------------------------
 Systems to Compare — add LDS as its own category
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS (Latter-day Saint theology): there is a plurality of
   transcendent, embodied divine Beings ("Gods") progressing
   under a set of eternal, uncreated laws; one of these Beings
   (Heavenly Father) is the God of this world and presides over
   the Godhead (Father, Son, and Holy Ghost — three distinct
   Personages unified in purpose, not in substance). Matter,
   intelligences, and eternal laws are co-eternal with God and
   were not created ex nihilo; God organized rather than
   originated reality. Humans are literal spirit children of
   Heavenly Father who pre-existed mortal life and may, by
   covenant and ordinance, progress toward exaltation (godhood).

   (Categorically: this is a form of polytheism — the only god
   we worship is the Father, but other gods exist and we
   ourselves may become gods. It is listed as a separate
   category here because LDS doctrine differs from classical
   polytheism in important structural ways: a strict moral
   hierarchy headed by the Father, the existence of a Godhead
   unified in purpose, and the framing of the divine plurality
   as a single chain of eternal progression rather than a
   pantheon of competing wills.)


----------------------------------------------------------------
 Alignment with Inferences from Reality — LDS rows
   (each block below is to be inserted under the matching
   existing inference in the main file)
----------------------------------------------------------------

2. Things are defined (or known) by their consistent properties.
 - LDS: Partial
     - Within the Godhead, LDS theology cleanly avoids the
       Trinitarian tension: the Father, the Son, and the Holy
       Ghost are three separate Personages, each with consistent
       identifying properties, unified in purpose rather than in
       essence. So the divine Persons themselves are
       distinguishable.
     - However, the broader category of "God" loses its
       defining consistency. "God" no longer denotes a unique
       ontological kind distinct from creation: the Father was
       once a mortal man on another world ("As man now is, God
       once was; as God now is, man may become"), and exalted
       humans may themselves become gods. The Creator/creature
       boundary therefore reduces to a difference of stage of
       progression rather than essential category.
     - As such, LDS preserves distinguishability of Persons
       within the Godhead but dissolves it between God-as-kind
       and creature-as-kind — which is functionally an
       inconsistency at the categorical level.


4. All information comes from a source.
 - LDS: No
     - This universe was organized (not created ex nihilo) by
       the Father, but matter, intelligences (the eternal
       elements of the human soul), and eternal laws are
       uncreated and co-eternal with God. None of these have a
       source.
     - Moreover, the Father Himself is not the terminal source:
       He once progressed to godhood from a prior mortal state,
       implying a prior God who exalted Him, and so on. This
       generates an infinite regress of progenitor Gods — the
       same structural problem identified earlier with Atheism's
       chain-without-end.
     - LDS thus replaces a single uncaused source with multiple
       uncaused categories (gods, intelligences, matter, laws),
       no one of which terminates the chain. By this project's
       standard, that is indistinguishable from contradiction or
       false belief unless rescued by additional revealed
       authority (which itself must then be verified).


9. Speculation is neither proof nor refutation.
+
11. Every inference has a limit.
 - LDS: Maybe
     - Reality and observation alone do not yield: (a) an
       embodied, formerly mortal Father, (b) co-eternal
       uncreated intelligences and matter, (c) a chain of
       progenitor Gods, (d) the existence of other inhabited
       worlds presided over by other gods, or (e) eternal laws
       prior to and independent of God. Each of these is
       reachable only by extending past the natural inferential
       limit on the strength of additional revelation through
       Joseph Smith and his successors.
     - This is internally consistent only if that additional
       revelation has historical and textual authority strictly
       superior to all alternatives (and superior to the
       deductions of section 4 of this project). Otherwise,
       LDS is using revealed claims to override what the
       natural inferences from reality would conclude.
     - Conditional verdict: structurally similar to the
       Trinitarian dependence on textual authority, but
       heavier, because LDS must overturn more of section 4's
       deductions (uncaused source, transcendence of time and
       space, God's will as the fundamental material of
       reality) — not merely supplement them.


13. Every thing that has a beginning has a preceding cause.
+
14. All things are contingent on their causes (that are not
    the thing).
 - LDS: No
     - The Father is the cause of this world's organization, but
       He Himself once had a beginning as a mortal — His
       progression to godhood is therefore a transition He
       underwent rather than His unconditioned nature. By
       symmetry, a prior God must have presided over His
       progression, and a prior God over that one, ad infinitum.
     - Intelligences (the eternal core of the human soul) and
       matter are explicitly uncreated and beginningless in LDS
       doctrine.
     - Either the chain of progressing Gods terminates in a
       genuinely uncaused First, in which case the project's
       deduction in section 4 (transcendent, atemporal,
       uncaused God) is reaffirmed and the rest of LDS's
       progressing-Gods chain is unnecessary; or the chain is
       beginningless, which is the infinite-regress problem
       this project already flagged as indistinguishable from
       contradiction.


16. Reality has a universally applicable set of fundamental
    laws.
 - LDS: Partial
     - LDS affirms that reality is governed by universal,
       intelligible laws. So far, fit with the inference.
     - However, in LDS theology these eternal laws are above
       God Himself: He is bound by them, and His godhood
       depends on perfect adherence to them. The laws are not
       "made of God's will" (as the project's deduction in
       section 4 concludes); they are independent of and prior
       to Him.
     - This forces the question: if the laws are not from God,
       where do they come from? LDS does not answer; they
       simply are. This is the same structural answer Atheism
       gives ("the laws just exist"), and it leaves the laws
       themselves unexplained.


21. Consciousness can't be reduced to purely physical
    components.
 - LDS: Yes (with a wrinkle)
     - LDS affirms an enduring spiritual component of the
       person: each individual existed as a spirit in a
       pre-mortal life and possesses an eternal "intelligence"
       prior even to that spirit birth. So phenomenal
       consciousness is not reduced to gross matter.
     - The wrinkle: LDS doctrine teaches that "all spirit is
       matter, but it is more fine or pure" (D&C 131:7–8) — so
       LDS technically denies a hard immaterial dualism.
       Consciousness is grounded in a refined form of matter
       rather than in something categorically non-physical.
     - In functional terms, this aligns with the inference
       (consciousness is not reducible to ordinary physical
       components), though for different metaphysical reasons.


23. Intelligence correlates with success and
    purposeful/conscious complexity.
 - LDS: Yes
     - The universe's order reflects the organizing intelligence
       of the Father; eternal progression itself is a
       monotonic increase in intelligence, glory, and capacity.
       Strong fit.


25. We have a disposition for having a purpose and feeling
    wanted.
 - LDS: Yes
     - Each human is the literal spirit child of Heavenly
       Father (and, in standard LDS teaching, of Heavenly
       Mother), with a divine pedigree and a designed
       trajectory toward exaltation. Our innate sense of
       purpose reflects this premortal identity. Strong fit.


27. We have the capacity to make choices, based on our
    knowledge and desires.
 - LDS: Yes
     - Moral agency is structurally central to LDS theology —
       the pre-mortal "War in Heaven" turned on agency itself,
       and the entire Plan of Salvation depends on mortal
       beings exercising real choice under conditions of
       opposition. Strong fit.


28. We have a disposition towards concepts of morality.
+
29. We have a disposition towards concepts of justice.
 - LDS: Yes (with a Euthyphro wrinkle)
     - As children of God, we share His moral nature, and
       eternal laws make morality and justice intelligible and
       universal. Strong fit at the level of disposition.
     - The wrinkle: because God Himself is bound by eternal
       laws He did not author, LDS effectively answers
       Euthyphro's dilemma by the second horn — God wills
       what is good because it is good, with goodness defined
       outside Him. Goodness is therefore prior to and
       independent of God, which is incompatible with the
       project's deduction that God's will is the fundamental
       material of reality.


33. There is a seen reality, and an unseen reality.
 - LDS: Yes
     - LDS posits a richly structured unseen reality: a
       pre-mortal existence, the spirit world after death,
       three degrees of post-resurrection glory (celestial,
       terrestrial, telestial), outer darkness, angelic
       messengers, and continued revelation. Strong fit.


36. Falsehood is easier and more common, while good is harder
    and more valued.
 - LDS: Yes
     - The Fall introduced opposition as a necessary condition
       of agency ("it must needs be, that there is an
       opposition in all things" — 2 Nephi 2:11). Exaltation
       requires sustained righteousness, covenants, and
       ordinances; mere belief is insufficient. Good is hard,
       valued, and rewarded. Strong fit at the descriptive
       level.


37. Reality could have been different.
 - LDS: Partial
     - The particular Plan of Salvation that governs this
       world was selected (Christ's plan was accepted over
       Lucifer's). So in the contingent details, reality could
       have been different.
     - But the bounds within which any plan could exist are
       set by eternal laws that God did not author and cannot
       override. So at the deepest level, the fundamental
       structure of reality is necessary, not contingent on
       God's will. This is unlike Trinitarianism and
       Monotheism (where God could have willed any framework
       at all).


38. We fear death, yet we will die.
 - LDS: Yes
     - Death is the passage into the spirit world, followed by
       resurrection, judgment, and assignment to one of the
       three kingdoms of glory (or outer darkness for sons of
       perdition). Our choices here echo eternally, which
       makes the natural fear of death well-calibrated to its
       weight.



----------------------------------------------------------------
 Section 5 Conclusion — LDS entry
   (to be inserted into the "Conclusion: does the belief system
   align with reality?" block at the end of the original file)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS: No (introduces infinite regress, multiplies uncaused
   categories, and is more dependent on textual authority than
   Trinitarianism)
     - LDS keeps several strengths shared with Monotheism and
       Trinitarianism: the unseen reality has rich structure,
       consciousness is not reduced to gross matter, moral
       agency and human purpose are intrinsic to the design,
       and intelligence governs the universe's order.
     - However, LDS makes commitments that conflict with the
       deductions of section 4:
         - It posits a chain of progressing Gods with no
           identified terminal source (infinite regress).
         - It posits uncreated, co-eternal matter, intelligences,
           and laws — distributing "uncausedness" across
           multiple categories instead of grounding it in a
           single uncaused source.
         - It places eternal laws above God, so God's will is
           not the fundamental material of reality (contra
           section 4); justice and goodness exist independent
           of God's authorship, with God merely conforming to
           them perfectly.
         - It dissolves the categorical distinction between
           Creator and creature: God was once a man, and men
           may become gods. "God" no longer denotes an
           ontological kind, only a stage of progression.
     - Trinitarianism required us to infer that "inferring about
       reality doesn't lead to spiritual truth" so that one of
       its core doctrines (the Trinity) could be exempted from
       the rule that "things are defined by their consistent
       properties". LDS goes further: it requires that
       inference plus the further claim that revealed
       metaphysics can override the deductions of section 4
       directly (uncaused source, atemporality, transcendence,
       will-as-fundamental-material). The cost is heavier.
     - As such, like Trinitarianism, LDS only remains viable if
       its scriptural and historical preservation can
       demonstrate authority strictly superior to all
       alternatives — superior not only to the Quran's
       Monotheism, but also to the New Testament's
       Trinitarianism (since LDS positions itself as restoring
       what those texts allegedly lost). This will be tested
       in the historical comparison.

 PHASE 2 — Section 6.1–6.2: LDS Textual Claim + Presence
###############################################################

Preface (to be appended to the existing note at the top of
File 6): the comparison widens to a third branch — LDS scripture
(the Standard Works). Trinitarianism remains in the comparison
via the New Testament; pure Monotheism remains via the Quran;
LDS enters because its section-5 verdict was contingent on its
revealed authority being strictly superior to both alternatives.
The relevant LDS canon comprises: the King James Bible (treated
as canon "as far as it is translated correctly"), the Book of
Mormon, the Doctrine and Covenants, and the Pearl of Great Price
(Moses, Abraham, Joseph Smith—History, Articles of Faith).
Because LDS treats the Bible's correctness as conditional on
its restored canon, the load-bearing LDS evidence is the
non-biblical scripture — primarily the Book of Mormon (BoM),
secondarily the D&C and Pearl of Great Price (PoGP).


----------------------------------------------------------------
 1. Textual/Historical Claim — LDS rows
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - What is the claim?
     - LDS
         - The Standard Works (with the BoM, D&C, and PoGP as
           the load-bearing additions to the Bible) possess a
           divinely restored truthfulness superior to all
           alternatives — including the Bible and Quran — which
           are either incomplete, partially corrupted, or
           unauthorized restorations of the original Gospel.
         - Specifically: if the Book of Mormon is shown to be
           of divine origin (genuinely ancient, accurately
           translated from the golden plates), then it
           incapacitates any argument that Joseph Smith was not
           God's prophet — which in turn validates the D&C and
           PoGP as authoritative continuing revelation, and
           establishes the LDS Church as the divinely restored
           Church.

 - Is it ontologically possible and epistemologically
   attainable?
     - LDS: Yes, with one important asymmetry
         - Texts and historical events can be preserved, and a
           prophetic restoration via revelation is internally
           coherent.
         - The asymmetry: in the NT and Quran cases, the
           textual and physical artifacts that ground the claim
           (manuscripts, gathered communities, geographies,
           prior scriptures used) are still accessible to
           examination. In the LDS case, the primary physical
           artifact (the golden plates) is by claim no longer
           accessible — Joseph Smith reports that he returned
           them to the angel Moroni. The translation method
           (seer stone in a hat, with the plates often covered
           or in another room) is also not reproducible.
         - Verification therefore necessarily reduces to (a)
           the witnesses' testimony, (b) internal textual
           features of the BoM, (c) doctrinal and editorial
           stability over time, and (d) archaeological,
           historical, and linguistic alignment of the BoM's
           claims with the actual ancient New World.

 - What are the criteria for success and failure?
     - LDS
         - Truth is defined/identified by consistency with
           reality; falsehood by inconsistency and contradiction
           with reality.
         - If the LDS canon is authorized by God, then it must
           be the epitome of consistency and correspondence
           with reality. As a result, if all falsehoods
           necessarily contradict with reality, then neither
           the Bible nor the Quran can possess the same level
           of consistency and coherence as the Standard Works.
         - Therefore, success is showing: (a) the BoM's
           historical claims (ancient American civilizations
           with specified peoples, languages, technologies,
           flora, and fauna) are archaeologically, linguis-
           tically, and genetically corroborated; (b) the
           text's doctrines and wording are stable across
           editions and revelations; (c) the chain from Joseph
           Smith → manuscripts → modern canon is unbroken and
           tamper-resistant; (d) the text is beyond what any
           19th-century human author could plausibly produce
           under known conditions; and (e) LDS scripture's
           preservation strictly exceeds that of the Bible and
           the Quran. Failure is if the BoM's historical
           claims are contradicted by the historical, archaeo-
           logical, or genetic record, if doctrines or
           wordings shift substantively across editions, if a
           plausible naturalistic account of the text's
           production exists, or if either the Bible or the
           Quran exhibits stronger preservation than the LDS
           canon.


----------------------------------------------------------------
 2. Oral, Written, and Archaeological Presence — LDS rows
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - Who were the eyewitnesses, and what did they bear witness to?
     - LDS
         - Four significant event-clusters, all within roughly
           ten years (1820–1830) in upstate New York and
           northern Pennsylvania.
         - (1) The First Vision (1820): Joseph Smith, alone,
           reports seeing God the Father and Jesus Christ in a
           grove of trees. No corroborating witnesses. Multiple
           accounts (1832, 1835, 1838, 1842) differ on the
           number and identity of the personages (one being,
           "the Lord", an angel, two Persons), Smith's age,
           and his motivation for praying.
         - (2) Visits of the angel Moroni and the recovery of
           the golden plates (1823–1827): Joseph Smith, alone,
           directs and receives. Family members report Smith's
           statements about the plates but did not see them.
         - (3) Translation of the Book of Mormon (1827–1829):
           Joseph Smith dictates to scribes (Oliver Cowdery,
           Martin Harris, Emma Smith, others). The scribes did
           not see the plates during translation; Smith
           dictated while looking into a hat containing a seer
           stone (the same kind of stone Smith had previously
           used for treasure-seeking, a documented part of his
           pre-prophetic career).
         - (4) The witness statements (1829): the Three
           Witnesses (Oliver Cowdery, David Whitmer, Martin
           Harris) report seeing the plates and the angel
           Moroni "by the power of God" — a visionary
           experience, not handling the physical plates. The
           Eight Witnesses (mostly Smith and Whitmer family
           members) report having "hefted" the plates with
           their hands. Both statements are preserved in the
           front matter of every BoM edition.
         - (5) Continuing revelations to Joseph Smith and
           successors (1823–present, recorded primarily in the
           D&C): the bulk are private revelations to Smith
           alone or to small councils; some have multiple
           reported witnesses (e.g., Sidney Rigdon co-witnesses
           the vision in D&C 76).
         - In total: the BoM's foundational events depend
           heavily on Smith himself, with a small cluster of
           additional witnesses for the plates' existence (most
           of whom were close associates or family members,
           and several of whom later left the Church — though
           none, by available reports, formally retracted
           their witness statements).

 - How reliable was the cultural context for preserving oral
   tradition accurately?
     - LDS
         - The relevant question is not classical oral tradition
           but manuscript and editorial fidelity, because the
           gap between event and printing was months, not
           decades or centuries.
         - 1820s rural New York was literate, had immediate
           access to printing, and produced the Book of Mormon
           in print (5,000 copies, E.B. Grandin press) in March
           1830 — within months of the dictation's completion.
           So the textual base was fixed in print very quickly.
         - That fast loop is a strength on its own. The
           weakness is the surrounding context: Smith's region
           and decade (the "burned-over district" during the
           Second Great Awakening) was saturated with revival
           prophecy, visionary claims, and folk treasure-magic
           with seer stones (in which Smith was an active and
           documented participant prior to receiving the
           plates — see, e.g., his 1826 trial for "glasslooking"
           in Bainbridge). Multiple other new prophetic
           movements arose in the same decade and region
           (Shakers, Millerites, Oneida, Strangites, etc.).
         - This means: the form of preservation (rapid printing)
           is strong; but the context provides multiple ready
           naturalistic templates (visionary claims, seer-stone
           treasure-finding, popular speculation about the
           Hebrew origins of Native Americans, e.g. Ethan
           Smith's *View of the Hebrews*, 1823, and James
           Adair's *History of the American Indians*, 1775)
           that a 19th-century author would have had ready
           access to and could draw from.

 - When was the text written and standardized, and by whom?
     - LDS
         - Three components, each with its own timeline.
         - **Book of Mormon**: dictated 1828–1829 by Joseph
           Smith to multiple scribes; printed March 1830 (1st
           edition, 5,000 copies). Subsequent major editions:
           1837 (Smith-supervised revisions), 1840
           (Smith-supervised), 1879 (versification and chapter
           reorganization by Orson Pratt), 1920 (further
           revisions), 1981 and 2013 (modern critical editions
           by the LDS Church). Approximately 3,900 differences
           between the 1830 and current editions, most
           orthographic but with substantive doctrinal changes
           — e.g., 1 Nephi 11:21 changed from "the Lamb of
           God, yea, even the Eternal Father" (1830) to "the
           Lamb of God, yea, even the Son of the Eternal
           Father" (post-1837), reflecting doctrinal evolution
           from a modalist Christology toward the modern
           Godhead.
         - **Doctrine and Covenants**: Smith's revelations
           recorded from 1823 onward, first published as the
           Book of Commandments (1833, partial), then expanded
           as the Doctrine and Covenants (1835). The 1835
           edition introduced revisions to earlier revelations
           — adding text, modifying wording, in some cases
           changing the doctrinal content (e.g., references to
           plural marriage, priesthood structure). The canon
           has both grown (later revelations added through 1981
           and 1978, including Official Declaration 2 ending
           the priesthood ban on Black men) and shrunk (the
           Lectures on Faith were canon 1835–1921, then
           removed). Standardization is therefore ongoing
           rather than fixed.
         - **Pearl of Great Price**: compiled 1851 by Franklin
           D. Richards; canonized 1880. Contains the Book of
           Moses (revealed 1830–1831), the Book of Abraham
           (translated 1835 from Egyptian papyri Smith
           purchased from Michael Chandler), Joseph Smith—
           History (drawn from Smith's 1838 account), and the
           Articles of Faith (1842). Notable: the Book of
           Abraham papyri were thought lost after Smith's
           death; portions were rediscovered at the
           Metropolitan Museum of Art in 1966 and returned to
           the LDS Church.

 - What corroborating archaeological finds are there?
     - LDS
         - Substantial corroboration for the 19th-century
           events (well-documented contemporaries, printing
           records, newspaper accounts, court records); very
           limited to negative corroboration for the ancient
           claims.
         - **Book of Mormon's ancient civilizations**: the BoM
           describes large, literate, iron-age civilizations
           (Nephites, Lamanites, Jaredites) inhabiting the
           Americas between c. 2200 BC and 421 AD, possessing
           steel swords and breastplates, chariots, wheat,
           barley, silk, domesticated horses, elephants,
           cattle, sheep, oxen, and a Hebrew/Egyptian-derived
           writing system ("Reformed Egyptian"). None of these
           — civilizations, technologies, animals, crops, or
           script — have been corroborated by mainstream
           archaeology for that time and place. The Smithsonian
           Institution (1996, 1998) and the National Geographic
           Society (1998) issued formal statements indicating
           the Book of Mormon is not used by their
           archaeologists as a guide to New World archaeology
           because nothing in it has been independently
           confirmed. No city named in the BoM (Zarahemla,
           Bountiful, Nephihah, Cumorah, Jershon, etc.) has
           been identified at any site.
         - **Population genetics**: Native American populations
           are overwhelmingly of Asian (Beringian) descent, not
           Hebrew or Middle Eastern. The 2014 LDS Church Gospel
           Topics essay "Book of Mormon and DNA Studies"
           acknowledges this and shifts to a "limited
           geography" reading in which a small Nephite/Lamanite
           population's genetic signature could have been
           swamped — a shift that departs from earlier
           prophetic statements (e.g., Joseph F. Smith,
           Spencer W. Kimball) treating all American natives
           as "Lamanites".
         - **Book of Abraham papyri**: the recovered papyri
           (Joseph Smith Papyri I, X, XI) have been examined by
           Egyptologists from outside and within the LDS Church
           and identified as fragments of the Egyptian Book of
           Breathings and the Book of the Dead — common
           funerary texts from c. 50 BC, completely unrelated
           to Abraham. Smith's "translation" bears no
           resemblance to the actual text of the papyri, and
           the facsimile interpretations (still printed in the
           PoGP) do not match standard Egyptological readings
           of the figures. The 2014 LDS Gospel Topics essay
           acknowledges this and proposes a "catalyst" theory
           — that the papyri inspired but did not directly
           source the revelation.
         - **D&C and 19th-century events**: extensively
           corroborated by historical records — Smith's life,
           the Nauvoo period, the migration to Utah, etc., are
           among the best-documented religious movements of
           the 19th century. This is a strength specifically
           for the modern events but does not transfer to the
           ancient claims of the BoM or Book of Abraham.
         - **Earliest manuscripts**: the "Original Manuscript"
           of the BoM (Cowdery and others writing as Smith
           dictated) is partially extant (~28% recovered); the
           "Printer's Manuscript" (Cowdery's copy used for the
           1830 printing) is fully extant. These date to
           1829–1830 and are securely tied to Smith's lifetime
           — comparable to the Quranic situation in immediacy
           of attestation, but unlike the Quran the
           manuscripts attest to a 19th-century production
           event, not an ancient one.


----------------------------------------------------------------
 Comparative Summary — Subsections 1–2
   (to be appended to or merged with whatever section-level
   summary the original file uses at this point — currently
   the original file goes directly into subsection 3, with no
   intermediate summary. I'm including a brief integrated
   reading below so the three-way comparison stays explicit
   from the start. Drop it under subsection 2 if you want a
   visible synthesis; otherwise hold for the section's overall
   summary later.)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - All three traditions assert preservation/inimitability
   exceeding the alternatives.
 - On immediacy of text-to-event:
     - NT: gap of 10–70 years for the Gospels; canon
       standardized centuries later.
     - Quran: written within Muhammad's lifetime; fully
       compiled within 2 years of his death; standardized 20
       years later.
     - LDS: BoM dictated and printed within months;
       D&C/PoGP standardized through the 19th century. So on
       immediacy alone, the LDS textual base is strong, but
       what it attests is the 19th-century production
       event — not the ancient events the BoM claims.
 - On archaeological corroboration of the *content's claims*
   (i.e., the events the text claims to record):
     - NT: strong cultural/geographic corroboration for the
       1st-century setting; manuscripts attest the text by
       125 AD.
     - Quran: overwhelming corroboration of every named
       location, person, and event in 7th-century Arabia,
       with Quranic manuscripts within Muhammad's lifetime.
     - LDS: extensive corroboration of the 19th-century
       events and people surrounding Joseph Smith; effectively
       none for the ancient civilizations the BoM claims
       (no archaeology, no genetics, no script, no
       independently corroborated geography). The Book of
       Abraham case is stronger as a negative: the source
       artifacts have been recovered and identified as
       unrelated to Abraham.
 - On editorial stability:
     - NT: stable canon from the late 4th century, with minor
       textual variants well documented in the
       manuscript record.
     - Quran: verbatim stability from the 7th century; rasm
       fixed, recitations within accepted qira'at variants.
     - LDS: ~3,900 textual differences between the 1830 BoM
       and current editions (most minor, some doctrinally
       substantive); D&C revisions and removals across
       editions; canon both expanded and contracted over time.
       This is more editorial flux than either alternative.

 - Provisional reading of subsections 1–2 (not yet a final
   conclusion — that comes after subsections 3–6):
     - LDS scripture is *very well attested as a 19th-century
       production*. As a record of ancient events, it is
       poorly attested — the surviving evidence either does
       not support or actively contradicts the claims it
       makes about pre-Columbian America and ancient Egypt.
     - The Quran's preservation is verbatim from contemporary
       eyewitnesses with the named geography all attested.
     - The NT's preservation is strong for cultural setting
       but weaker on immediacy and verbatim accuracy.
     - On the metric most diagnostic of authenticity —
       correspondence between the text's claims and the
       physical/historical record — the ordering at this
       stage is: Quran > New Testament > LDS canon.

 Phase 2 — Notes for downstream phases
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - The "catalyst" theory for the Book of Abraham introduces a
   concept LDS apologetics increasingly relies on: a revealed
   text whose content is doctrinally true regardless of any
   relationship to its claimed physical source. If accepted,
   this commitment must be applied consistently — including
   to the Book of Mormon (whose ancient source is no longer
   available) and to any future criticism. Phase 4 (resistance
   to fabrication) will test what that consistency costs.
 - Steelmanned LDS responses to register for Phases 3–5:
     - The limited-geography model for the BoM (Mesoamerican
       setting) is the strongest internally coherent reply to
       archaeological/genetic non-attestation.
     - The witnesses' testimonies were never formally
       retracted, even by witnesses who left the Church.
     - The Book of Mormon's internal complexity, including
       chiasmus, has been argued to exceed Smith's plausible
       capacity as a 24-year-old farm boy with limited
       formal education.
     - Textual revisions across editions are argued to be
       corrections of printer's errors and grammar, not
       doctrinal changes — a claim to be examined in detail
       in Phase 4.

----------------------------------------------------------------
 3. Assessment of the Chains of Transmission — LDS rows
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - How many independent streams of transmission are there,
   leading to the text's standardization?
     - LDS
         - 3 main streams (BoM, D&C, PoGP) that all originate
           from a single human source — Joseph Smith — and
           converge through the early LDS Church's printing
           and editorial decisions.
         - The BoM stream goes from: Joseph Smith (dictating
           while looking into a hat containing a seer stone)
           → scribes (Oliver Cowdery primarily, with Martin
           Harris, Emma Smith, John Whitmer, others) → the
           Original Manuscript (1828–1829, ~28% extant) →
           the Printer's Manuscript (Cowdery's copy, fully
           extant) → E.B. Grandin's 1830 first edition →
           subsequent editions (1837, 1840, 1879, 1920,
           1981, 2013).
         - The D&C stream goes from: Joseph Smith (private
           revelations, almost entirely to him alone; a few
           with small-council witnesses) → manuscript
           revelations → the Book of Commandments (1833,
           partial; most of the print run destroyed by a mob
           in Independence, MO) → the Doctrine and Covenants
           (1835, with substantive revisions to earlier
           revelations) → subsequent editions (1844, 1876,
           1921, 1981) → continuing additions (Official
           Declarations 1 [1890] and 2 [1978]).
         - The PoGP stream goes from: Joseph Smith (Book of
           Moses, revealed 1830–1831 as a new translation
           of Genesis; Book of Abraham, translated 1835 from
           Egyptian papyri he purchased from Michael Chandler;
           Joseph Smith—History, drawn from his 1838 account)
           → manuscripts → Franklin D. Richards's 1851
           compilation → canonized 1880 by general conference
           vote.
         - Notes: the LDS claim is that the Holy Ghost is the
           living custodian of truth, and that the
           "priesthood keys" of continuing revelation passed
           through Joseph Smith to subsequent presidents of
           the Church. All three streams therefore depend on
           Smith's prophetic authority being authentic. Unlike
           the NT (3 independent eyewitness/apostolic streams
           that merge later) or the Quran (one prophet with
           thousands of independent memorizers and scribes),
           the LDS canon converges at the source rather than
           after.

 - Are there competing claims, and if so, what are their chains
   of transmission?
     - LDS
         - Yes — multiple, primarily through the 1844
           succession crisis.
         - At Smith's death (June 27, 1844, Carthage, IL), there
           was no undisputed successor. The principal claimants
           and their later trajectories:
             - **Brigham Young / Quorum of the Twelve** →
               became the LDS Church (Salt Lake City).
               Mainstream LDS asserts Young inherited the
               keys at the August 8, 1844, meeting (where
               many witnesses reported Young's appearance
               transfigured to look and sound like Smith).
             - **Sidney Rigdon** → claimed to be Smith's
               appointed guardian; led a brief faction
               that died out.
             - **James Strang** → produced a letter from
               Smith claiming his appointment; led the
               Strangite branch (still extant, small).
               Strang also produced "translated" plates
               (the Voree Plates) — paralleling Smith's
               method.
             - **Joseph Smith III** (Smith's son, supported by
               Emma Smith) → led what became the
               Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of
               Latter-Day Saints (RLDS, now the Community
               of Christ). Doctrinally diverged from the
               Utah LDS Church: rejected polygamy from
               the start, ordains women, currently
               theologically progressive.
         - Each branch traces back to Joseph Smith but
           disputes the others' line of authority. Notably,
           the RLDS/Community of Christ retained custody of
           several key foundational documents — most of the
           Printer's Manuscript of the BoM, the Joseph Smith
           Translation of the Bible (Smith's revision of the
           KJV), and other early Smith papers — and was
           physically descended through Smith's own wife and
           son.
         - The closest internal "qira'at-equivalent" is the
           textual revisions across BoM editions (3,900+
           differences between 1830 and current). Unlike the
           qira'at, these revisions were neither authorized
           by Smith in his lifetime nor publicly disclosed to
           readers between editions.

 - What are the strongest, independent chains of custodianship
   for each stream of transmission?
     - LDS
         - Custodianship converges with Joseph Smith himself,
           then bifurcates after 1844 between the LDS Church
           and the Community of Christ.
         - **Book of Mormon Chain**
             - 1830 first edition (Grandin) → Printer's
               Manuscript (Cowdery, fully extant) → Original
               Manuscript (~28% extant) → Smith (dictation
               with seer stone in a hat) → golden plates (by
               claim, returned to angel Moroni) → Moroni and
               other Nephite prophets (by claim) → original
               Nephite, Lamanite, and Jaredite authors (by
               claim, c. 2200 BC – 421 AD).
             - Three Witnesses (Cowdery, Whitmer, Harris) →
               angelic vision of the plates (1829) → Smith.
             - Eight Witnesses (Smith and Whitmer family
               members) → physical handling of plates by
               claim (1829) → Smith.
         - **Doctrine and Covenants Chain**
             - 1835 D&C → Book of Commandments (1833,
               partial) → manuscript revelations →
               Joseph Smith (private revelation, with the
               Holy Ghost as claimed custodian) → God.
             - Multi-witness exceptions: D&C 76 (the Vision
               of the Three Degrees, witnessed by Smith and
               Sidney Rigdon together); D&C 110 (Kirtland
               Temple vision, Smith and Cowdery); the
               Three Witnesses statement; the 1836 Kirtland
               Temple events (multiple witnesses to spiritual
               manifestations).
         - **Pearl of Great Price Chain**
             - 1851 PoGP (Richards) → 1842 Times and Seasons
               publication of the Book of Abraham → 1835
               translation manuscripts → Joseph Smith
               (translation by revelation) → Egyptian papyri
               (purchased 1835, portions recovered 1966) →
               Abraham (by claim, c. 2000 BC).
             - For the Book of Moses: 1851 PoGP → 1830–1831
               revelations to Smith → Moses (by claim).
             - For Joseph Smith—History: 1851 PoGP → 1842
               publication → 1838 account → Joseph Smith.
         - Notes: every chain begins and effectively ends with
           Joseph Smith. The "post-Smith" portion is well
           documented (this is not a Papias-style oral chain
           through unknown transmitters), but the "pre-Smith"
           portion of each ancient claim is wholly
           inaccessible to independent verification — the
           plates are gone (by claim), the papyri have been
           recovered and disagree with the translation, and
           the revelations are by their nature private.

 - What are the weakest links in the strongest chains of
   custodianship?
     - LDS
         - All chains have a single human bottleneck and lack
           the means for independent verification.
         - **BoM**
             - The Original Manuscript is only ~28% extant
               (the rest deteriorated after Smith placed it in
               the Nauvoo House cornerstone in 1841).
             - The translation method (seer stone in a hat,
               plates often covered or in another room) is
               identical to Smith's documented pre-prophetic
               practice of seer-stone treasure-hunting — for
               which Smith was tried in court in 1826
               (Bainbridge, NY, "glasslooking" charge). This
               creates a strong naturalistic prior on the
               same method.
             - The plates themselves were never available for
               examination by anyone outside Smith's
               immediate circle.
             - The Three Witnesses report seeing the plates
               "by the power of God" — i.e., in a visionary
               experience, not normal handling. David Whitmer
               later clarified the experience was perceived
               with "the eye of faith". Martin Harris,
               separately questioned, gave similar visionary
               accounts.
             - The Eight Witnesses, who claimed physical
               handling, were drawn from two families: four
               Whitmers, one Whitmer relative (Hiram Page),
               and three of Smith's own family (his father
               and two brothers).
             - Witness trajectories: Cowdery, Whitmer, and
               Harris were all excommunicated or otherwise
               departed from the LDS Church in the late
               1830s. Cowdery and Harris later returned;
               Whitmer never did and founded his own (now
               extinct) Church of Christ. None formally
               retracted their printed testimony, but at
               various points each publicly accused Smith of
               being a fallen prophet.
         - **D&C**
             - Most revelations have no contemporary witness
               outside Smith himself. The chain "Smith → God"
               is uncorroborated for the bulk of the canon.
             - Revisions between the Book of Commandments
               (1833) and the D&C (1835) include substantive
               additions — for example, the priesthood
               hierarchy in D&C 27 is more developed than its
               1833 counterpart; references to Melchizedek
               priesthood ordination did not appear in the
               earliest manuscripts of D&C 18 but were added
               later. So early revelations were modified to
               reflect later doctrinal positions.
             - Canonicity itself fluctuated: the Lectures on
               Faith were canon 1835–1921, then removed; the
               1890 Manifesto and 1978 priesthood revelation
               were added; some sections were renumbered or
               reorganized across editions.
         - **PoGP**
             - The Book of Abraham source papyri have been
               recovered (Joseph Smith Papyri I, X, XI) and
               identified by Egyptologists — including
               LDS-affiliated Egyptologists working from the
               actual fragments — as portions of the standard
               Egyptian Book of Breathings and Book of the
               Dead, c. 50 BC. Smith's "translation" bears no
               relationship to the actual text of the
               recovered papyri, and the facsimile
               interpretations conflict with established
               Egyptological readings. This is the single
               strongest direct refutation of a claimed
               translation in any of the canon under
               consideration: the source document is
               available, and it does not say what the
               "translation" says.
             - The "catalyst theory" (now the LDS Church's
               published position) — that the papyri inspired
               but did not directly source the revelation —
               is unfalsifiable: any future archaeological
               result, however unrelated to Abraham, can be
               accommodated under it. By this project's
               standard, an unfalsifiable defense is
               indistinguishable from special pleading.
             - First Vision: multiple incompatible accounts
               (1832, 1835, 1838, 1842) on basic facts —
               number and identity of the personages (one
               being, "the Lord", an angel, two distinct
               Persons), Smith's age (14 or 15 in different
               accounts), and his motivation (forgiveness of
               personal sins vs. confusion about which church
               to join). The 1838 account is the version
               canonized in the PoGP; earlier accounts were
               not published until the 20th century.
         - Notes: all LDS chains converge on one individual
           (Smith) who had a documented prior career in
           treasure-divination using the same instrument
           (seer stone) and the same method (in a hat). This
           is a sharper convergence than the NT (where Paul
           was at least one degree removed from Jesus and
           initially opposed) or the Quran (where Muhammad
           was the source of the revelation, but the
           preservation chain immediately multiplied across
           tens of thousands of independent memorizers,
           making the chain non-singular even though the
           source was one person).


----------------------------------------------------------------
 4. Alignment with Historical Context — LDS rows
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - What was the historical context throughout the chain of
   custodianship?
     - LDS
         - Three significant time periods: the Restoration era,
           the succession and Utah migration, and the modern
           accommodation/global era.
         - **The Restoration era (1820–1844)**: from Smith's
           First Vision (claimed 1820, written 1832+) through
           his death at Carthage Jail. Covers the BoM
           publication (1830), the founding of the Church
           (1830), the Kirtland and Missouri periods
           (1831–1838) including the failed Kirtland Bank
           (1837) and the Missouri "extermination order"
           (1838), the Nauvoo period (1839–1844) including
           the private practice of plural marriage (publicly
           denied during Smith's life), Smith's candidacy for
           U.S. president, the destruction of the *Nauvoo
           Expositor* press, and Smith's killing by a mob.
         - **The succession and Utah migration (1844–1890)**:
           the disputed succession (Young vs. Rigdon vs.
           Strang vs. Joseph III), the Mormon Trail
           (1846–1847), settlement of the Salt Lake Valley,
           open practice of polygamy (publicly announced
           1852), the Mountain Meadows Massacre (1857), the
           Utah War (1857–1858), federal anti-polygamy
           legislation (Edmunds Act 1882, Edmunds-Tucker Act
           1887), and the 1890 Manifesto officially ending
           polygamy as a condition of Utah statehood.
         - **The accommodation and global era (1890–present)**:
           assimilation into mainstream American religion,
           1978 priesthood revelation (allowing Black men to
           hold the priesthood), global missionary expansion,
           and — beginning in the 2010s — the LDS Church's
           Gospel Topics essays, which formally acknowledge
           historical issues previously denied or downplayed
           (polyandry, the seer-stone method of translation,
           the Book of Abraham–papyri mismatch, the Mountain
           Meadows Massacre, race and the priesthood).
         - Cultural context: the entire foundational period
           sits inside the Second Great Awakening (1790–1840),
           specifically the "burned-over district" of upstate
           New York — saturated with revivalist new
           religions, visionary claims, treasure-digging folk
           magic, anti-Masonic political fervor (following
           the 1826 William Morgan affair), and popular
           speculation about Native Americans as descendants
           of the Lost Tribes of Israel (Ethan Smith's *View
           of the Hebrews*, 1823, published in Smith's
           region; James Adair's *History of the American
           Indians*, 1775).

 - What were the standards of testimony and custodianship in
   this time?
     - LDS
         - Literate print culture with active journalism, legal
           records, and sworn affidavits.
         - 1820s–1840s America had:
             - High Protestant literacy (King James Bible was
               the cultural standard reading text).
             - Active local and regional newspapers with
               rapid news circulation.
             - Common-law standards of testimony (developed
               from English law): credible witnesses,
               affidavits under oath, court records.
             - Active publication of religious tracts,
               revivalist sermons, and competing prophetic
               claims, all of which generated public response
               and counter-tracts.
         - As a result, the historical record contains
           extensive independent testimony from Smith's
           contemporaries — both supportive and hostile —
           that can be cross-checked. Key examples:
             - The 1826 Bainbridge, NY court record on
               Smith's "glasslooking" charge (treasure-digging
               by seer stone).
             - The Hurlbut/Howe affidavits (1833–1834,
               published in *Mormonism Unvailed*, 1834):
               sworn statements from dozens of Smith's
               neighbors in Palmyra and Manchester describing
               the Smith family as treasure-diggers and
               visionaries, and rebutting Smith's prophetic
               claims.
             - Contemporary newspaper coverage from across
               New York, Ohio, Missouri, and Illinois.
             - Sworn legal documents from the Nauvoo period,
               including affidavits from women involved in
               polygamy.
             - The Nauvoo Expositor (1844), in which Smith's
               former close associates publicly disclosed
               doctrines and practices Smith was concealing
               from the wider Church.
         - In short: the standard of testimony in 1820s–1840s
           America was strong enough that Smith's claims
           *could* have been independently corroborated if
           true, and the existing independent testimony
           largely cuts the other way.

 - Is there anything anachronistic (historically or logically
   out of place) about the claim or its custodianship?
     - LDS
         - Many examples — and unlike the NT and Quran, most
           cut *against* the LDS claim (i.e., they suggest
           19th-century origin rather than ancient origin)
           rather than against the cultural expectations of
           Smith's audience.
         - **In the Book of Mormon, against its claimed
           ancient American setting (anachronisms)**:
             - **Domesticated animals**: horses (1 Nephi 18:25,
               Alma 18:9), elephants (Ether 9:19), cattle,
               oxen, sheep, swine, asses, goats (Ether 9:18,
               1 Nephi 18:25) — none documented in
               pre-Columbian America at the times the BoM
               describes.
             - **Crops**: wheat, barley, silk (Ether 9:17,
               Alma 1:29) — none documented for that period.
             - **Metallurgy**: iron, steel, swords (Jarom 1:8,
               Ether 7:9), brass plates, gold and silver
               currency systems (Alma 11) — no archaeological
               evidence in the relevant timeframe.
             - **Technology**: chariots (Alma 18:9–10) — no
               pre-Columbian wheel-and-axle technology for
               transport.
             - **Script**: "Reformed Egyptian" (Mormon 9:32)
               — no such script identified by any non-LDS
               scholar.
             - **King James English**: ~1,400 instances of
               "and it came to pass"; verbatim KJV-1611
               renderings of long Isaiah passages, including
               italicized words the KJV translators added
               (which would not exist in any pre-1611
               source); reproduction of KJV translation
               errors. A 6th-century-BC Nephite working from
               brass plates predating Christ should not
               produce English text dependent on a 1611
               translation of the Masoretic Text.
             - **Christian theology in pre-Christian text**:
               2 Nephi (written ostensibly c. 559 BC by
               Nephi) discusses Christ by name, baptism by
               immersion in the name of the Trinity,
               churches, bishops, and explicit Christology —
               doctrines that postdate the BoM's claimed
               authors by centuries.
             - **Anti-Masonic vocabulary**: "secret
               combinations" used by the Gadianton robbers
               (Helaman 6, 3 Nephi 7) closely mirrors the
               anti-Masonic rhetoric in upstate New York
               after the 1826 William Morgan affair.
         - **In the production of LDS scripture, the
           narrative's coherence with Smith's documented
           contemporary life**:
             - Smith used the same seer stone, with the same
               method (placed in a hat to exclude light),
               for both his pre-prophetic treasure-digging
               career (1822–1826, court-documented) and the
               BoM translation (1828–1829).
             - The First Vision, claimed for 1820, is not
               recorded in any document until 1832, and only
               privately mentioned even then. The first
               public account is 1842. The canonized version
               (1838) contradicts the 1832 version on basic
               details. A foundational vision that important
               should have been preached publicly from the
               start.
             - Plural marriage was practiced privately from
               1833 (Fanny Alger) onward but publicly denied
               by Smith and the Church until after the
               westward migration. The 1843 revelation
               canonizing plural marriage (D&C 132) was
               concealed from Smith's wife Emma and from the
               broader Church for years.
             - The Book of Abraham translation (1835) of
               common Egyptian funerary papyri as a record
               of Abraham fits the pre-Champollion American
               cultural expectation that Egyptian
               hieroglyphics were mystical and unreadable —
               but cannot survive Champollion's decipherment
               (which by the 1960s and the recovery of the
               papyri became publicly verifiable).
         - **Notable contrast with the NT and Quran cases**:
             - The NT contains many details that *cut
               against* what an inventing community would
               want (women as first resurrection witnesses,
               disciples' cowardice, crucifixion shame) —
               the criterion of embarrassment.
             - The Quran contains many details that *cut
               against* what a 7th-century Arabian author
               would want or could fabricate (gender
               equality, anti-tribalism, immediate
               revelation in front of hostile witnesses,
               grammar contrary to Arabian pride).
             - The BoM, by contrast, fits the cultural
               expectations of 1820s upstate New York
               remarkably well: a new American scripture
               (revivalist demand), Native Americans as
               Hebrews (popular speculation), anti-Masonic
               themes (current political concern), dispen-
               sational theology (Second Great Awakening
               theme), Christian doctrine projected back into
               pre-Christian times (typological reading,
               typical of revivalist preaching). There is no
               systematic feature of the BoM that cuts
               *against* what its first audience expected
               or wanted — which is precisely the inverse of
               the criterion of embarrassment.

 Comparative Summary — Subsections 3–4
   (insert at end of subsection 4 if a synthesis is wanted; the
   original file goes straight into subsection 5, so this is
   provided as a bridge.)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - On stream independence:
     - NT: 3 partly independent streams (synoptic, johannine,
       pauline) that merge later through patristic chains.
     - Quran: 2 streams (Quran, Hadith), with the Quranic
       chain multiplying instantly into thousands of
       independent memorizers.
     - LDS: 3 streams (BoM, D&C, PoGP) that all *converge at
       the source* — Joseph Smith — and only branch
       (post-1844) into LDS Church vs. Community of Christ
       vs. smaller successor groups. The source-convergence
       means the LDS streams are not meaningfully
       independent; verifying them reduces to verifying one
       man.
 - On testimony standards in context:
     - NT: 1st-century Jewish/Roman standards; eyewitness
       primary, written secondary.
     - Quran: 7th-century Arabian oral mastery; verbatim
       memorization in mass; public daily recitation as
       error-correction.
     - LDS: 1820s American print culture with newspapers,
       courts, and sworn affidavits — standards strong enough
       that we *can* cross-check, and the cross-check
       evidence largely conflicts with Smith's account
       (Bainbridge trial, Hurlbut affidavits, contradictory
       First Vision accounts, public denial of polygamy).
 - On anachronism direction:
     - NT: anachronisms cut against the *cultural
       expectations of the audience* — supporting criterion
       of embarrassment.
     - Quran: anachronisms cut against the *cultural
       expectations of the audience* — same direction.
     - LDS: anachronisms cut against the *ancient setting the
       text claims* (horses, steel, KJV English, pre-Christian
       Christology) and *fit* the cultural expectations of
       the audience (Hebrew Native Americans, anti-Masonic
       themes, revivalist theology). This is the inverse
       direction — exactly what we would expect of a
       19th-century production rather than an ancient one.

 - Provisional reading of subsections 3–4: LDS's streams
   collapse to a single, documented individual with a prior
   career using the same divinatory instrument; the
   surrounding 19th-century record contains substantial
   contrary testimony from contemporaries; and the
   anachronisms point uniformly toward 19th-century origin
   rather than ancient authorship. Combined with subsection
   2's finding (rich documentation of the 19th-century
   production, essentially none of the ancient claims), the
   ordering on chain-and-context fidelity at this stage
   continues to be: Quran > New Testament > LDS canon.

###############################################################
 PHASE 4 — Section 6.5: LDS Resistance to Fabrication
###############################################################

This is the heaviest filter in File 6. Because the LDS chains
converge at a single human source (Joseph Smith), the claim's
resistance to fabrication reduces almost entirely to (a) Smith's
character and competence, and (b) the architecture of the
material around him. The eight question-blocks of subsection 5
are answered below in the same order as the original file.


----------------------------------------------------------------
 1. What possibilities are there for fabrication and error in
    the claim?
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - Liar, lunatic, demonic influence — or any combination of
       the three, plus a fourth category not in play for the NT
       or Quran: a documented prior career using the same
       instrument.
     - The LDS canon is wholly mediated through Joseph Smith
       (the Holy Ghost is the claimed *spiritual* custodian,
       but every concrete revelation reaches the community
       through Smith and, after 1844, through the line of
       successor presidents). Therefore all potential
       fabrication or error must originate in Smith himself —
       either by deliberately deceiving his followers in being
       authorized by God, by sincerely speaking under delusion,
       by being influenced by an unseen/demonic source, or by
       continuing a pre-existing folk-magic practice (the seer
       stone in a hat) into an increasingly elaborate
       revelatory frame.
     - This is structurally similar to the Muhammad case
       (single human source) but with one critical asymmetry:
       Muhammad has no documented prior practice of producing
       revelatory content, while Smith does — court-documented
       seer-stone treasure-divination for hire (1822–1826),
       culminating in the 1826 Bainbridge, NY trial.


----------------------------------------------------------------
 2. How strongly does the claim resist fabrication and error?

 3. What possibilities are there for fabrication and error with
    the claim's custodianship?
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - Pre-Smith inaccessibility, intra-Smith revision, and
       institutional editorial control.
     - If any significant error or fabrication occurred,
       mainstream LDS would differ from the original truth of
       the claimed Nephite, Egyptian, and Mosaic sources.
       Evaluating custodianship therefore requires exposing
       the link between (a) those ancient sources and Smith,
       and (b) Smith and modern LDS. This exposes the
       following potential sources of fabrication and error:
     - **Pre-Smith Inaccessibility**
         - The golden plates were, by claim, returned to the
           angel Moroni after the translation — no plates
           are available for examination, no second
           independent translation is possible, and the
           "Reformed Egyptian" script is not attested by any
           non-LDS source.
         - The Book of Abraham papyri were recovered in 1966
           (Joseph Smith Papyri I, X, XI) and have been
           identified by Egyptologists — including those
           working from inside the LDS academic community —
           as portions of the standard Egyptian *Book of
           Breathings* and *Book of the Dead*, c. 50 BC,
           unrelated to Abraham. Smith's "translation" does
           not match the actual text. The Church's "catalyst"
           theory (the papyri inspired but did not source the
           revelation) is unfalsifiable.
         - The Book of Moses is presented as a revelatory
           expansion of Genesis 1–7 with no claimed physical
           source at all.
         - Every LDS ancient claim is therefore either
           (a) inaccessible by stipulation (plates), (b)
           accessible and disconfirmed (papyri), or (c) not
           tied to any artifact (Book of Moses).
     - **Intra-Smith Revision**
         - The BoM has ~3,900 textual differences between the
           1830 first edition and the modern critical text.
           Most are minor; some are doctrinally substantive
           (e.g., 1 Nephi 11:21 — "the Eternal Father" in
           1830, "the Son of the Eternal Father" post-1837 —
           a shift away from modalism toward the modern
           Godhead).
         - The D&C revisions between the Book of Commandments
           (1833) and the D&C (1835) include substantive
           additions: priesthood hierarchies developed beyond
           what the earliest revelations contained,
           references to Melchizedek priesthood ordinations
           added retroactively to earlier sections.
         - The canon itself has fluctuated: the Lectures on
           Faith were canon 1835–1921 (with content
           incompatible with the modern Godhead doctrine
           — a single God Personage with the Son as His
           glory), then removed. Sections were added (1890
           Manifesto, 1978 priesthood revelation) and
           reorganized.
         - These edits were not publicly disclosed to readers
           as the editions changed; the existence of
           substantive doctrinal revision is now mainstream
           among scholars and (partially) acknowledged by the
           Gospel Topics essays.
     - **Institutional Editorial Control**
         - Until the 21st century, the LDS Church controlled
           access to Smith's foundational documents (the
           Original Manuscript fragments, the Book of
           Commandments revision history, Smith's diaries,
           the early First Vision accounts). The Joseph Smith
           Papers Project (2008–present) is the first
           comprehensive open publication.
         - For most of LDS history, the Church catechized
           the 1838 First Vision account and the canonized
           D&C wording as fixed and original — without
           disclosing earlier variants. This is a long window
           in which faithful members had no access to the
           evidence that would let them check the official
           narrative.
         - The 2010s Gospel Topics essays acknowledge many
           previously denied or downplayed issues (polyandry,
           seer-stone translation, Book of Abraham–papyri
           mismatch, multiple First Vision accounts, race
           and the priesthood, Mountain Meadows Massacre) —
           but the acknowledgment came after the evidence
           had been pushed into public view by external
           scholarship, not as proactive disclosure by the
           institution.

 4. How strongly does the claim's custodianship resist
    fabrication and error? (Observed Patterns)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - Single source, documented prior practice of the
       translation method, dependent revision history, and
       known ready-template environment.
     - **Single human source — same individual for all three
       streams**
         - BoM, D&C, and PoGP all originate with Smith. The
           closest "second source" is Sidney Rigdon (D&C 76)
           and Oliver Cowdery (D&C 110 — Kirtland Temple
           vision). Both are intra-Smith-circle witnesses,
           not independent streams.
         - Post-Smith authority depends on the doctrine that
           prophetic keys pass through the line of LDS
           presidents — a chain whose authority is itself
           established by Smith's authority.
     - **Identical method to documented prior folk-magic
       practice**
         - The translation method (seer stone in a hat) is
           identical in instrument and procedure to Smith's
           1822–1826 treasure-digging career, for which he
           was charged in court in 1826. The Bainbridge case
           was not a one-off — multiple neighbors testified
           in 1833 (the Hurlbut affidavits) to a years-long
           pattern of seer-stone-based treasure operations
           involving Smith and his father.
         - This means the *naturalistic prior probability*
           that the method produces fabricated revelation is
           directly attested in Smith's own life, before the
           BoM was produced.
     - **Documented intra-canon doctrinal evolution**
         - Earliest BoM (1830): Christology and Godhead more
           modalistic — "the Father" and "the Son" presented
           in ways that can be read as one Person in two
           roles. Many BoM passages explicitly identify Jesus
           as "the Eternal Father".
         - Mid-Smith (1832–1835): D&C and Lectures on Faith
           describe Godhead as the Father (a personage of
           spirit) and the Son (a personage of tabernacle/
           body) — a binitarian framework.
         - Late Smith (1843–1844): King Follett Discourse and
           D&C 130, 131, 132 articulate the fully developed
           plurality-of-Gods, embodied-Father, eternal-
           progression doctrine.
         - This is the clearest in-record doctrinal evolution
           in any canon under comparison — and it occurs
           within the lifetime of the founder, not centuries
           later.
     - **Ready-template environment**
         - Ethan Smith's *View of the Hebrews* (1823, second
           edition with expanded Native-Americans-as-Hebrews
           thesis) was published in Poultney, Vermont — about
           75 miles from where Oliver Cowdery (the BoM's
           principal scribe) was raised. Ethan Smith was the
           pastor of Cowdery's family's church. The book lays
           out: Native Americans as descendants of the Lost
           Tribes of Israel; ancient pre-Columbian
           civilizations destroyed by warfare between
           righteous and apostate factions; a sealed record
           uncovered in a hill; the destiny of the surviving
           remnant. These are the structural beats of the
           Book of Mormon.
         - Anti-Masonic rhetoric was at peak intensity in
           upstate New York between 1826 and 1830 (the
           William Morgan affair, the rise of the Anti-Masonic
           political party). The BoM's "secret combinations"
           of the Gadianton robbers mirrors anti-Masonic
           tropes precisely.
         - The "burned-over district" produced multiple other
           prophets in the same period (Shakers, Strangites,
           Millerites, Oneida) — the prophetic role itself
           was a culturally available template.
     - **Pattern when summarized**: a single source with prior
       deceptive practice using the identical method, in a
       cultural context that supplied the template, producing
       a text that evolved doctrinally inside his lifetime,
       under institutional editorial control for over a
       century afterward.

 5. How strongly does the claim's custodianship resist
    fabrication and error? (Steelmanned Defense)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - The text is too complex for an uneducated farm boy; the
       witnesses never recanted; the Spirit testifies; and
       the doctrinal evolution is progressive revelation, not
       development.
     - **Smith's Capacity vs. the Text's Complexity**
         - The BoM is ~270,000 words, with hundreds of named
           people and places, internal chronological
           consistency over a 1,000-year arc, and Hebrew
           literary structures (chiasmus, parallelism) that
           Smith would not have studied formally. Producing
           this in roughly 65 working days of dictation,
           without notes or revisions, without education in
           Hebrew literature, exceeds plausible human
           capacity.
         - Therefore, divine origin is the most economical
           explanation.
     - **The Witnesses Never Formally Retracted**
         - The Three and Eight Witnesses' statements are
           printed at the front of every BoM edition. Even
           after Cowdery, Whitmer, and Harris were excom-
           municated or otherwise left the Church (and at
           various points publicly accused Smith of being a
           fallen prophet), none of them formally retracted
           their printed witness to the plates.
         - This is presented as resistance to fabrication: if
           the experience had been staged, motivated
           ex-members would have been positioned to expose
           it.
     - **Spiritual Confirmation (the Moroni Promise)**
         - Moroni 10:3–5 invites readers to pray about the
           BoM and receive a witness from the Holy Ghost of
           its truth. This experiential confirmation is
           presented as the primary epistemic basis for LDS
           belief, with historical evidence as supporting.
     - **Progressive Revelation, Not Development**
         - Each Christological and theological refinement
           through Smith's career is framed as additional
           light and knowledge progressively revealed to a
           living prophet, not as a doctrine in flux. The
           pattern of "line upon line, precept upon precept"
           (Isaiah 28:10, repeatedly cited in LDS
           hermeneutics) frames the evolution as the unfolding
           of one truth rather than the migration of opinion.
     - **The Plates Were Returned, Not Lost**
         - The unavailability of the plates is not a
           concealment but a stipulated condition of the
           commission: Moroni took them back. The witnesses'
           statements satisfy the biblical requirement that
           a matter be established by two or three witnesses
           (Deuteronomy 19:15, 2 Corinthians 13:1).
     - **Limited-Geography Model**
         - The lack of archaeological corroboration for the
           BoM's named cities reflects misidentification of
           the geography by 19th- and 20th-century LDS
           readers, not absence of the events. Under a
           Mesoamerican setting, the named civilizations
           overlap regions where archaeological evidence is
           more difficult to fix precisely; absence of named
           cities is therefore not falsification.

 6. How strongly does the claim's custodianship resist
    fabrication and error? (Requirements for Trusting Defense)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - Each LDS defense requires conditions that the available
       evidence does not supply — and several reduce to
       circular or unfalsifiable structures.
     - **Smith's Capacity vs. the Text's Complexity**
         - The argument compares the BoM's structural features
           against an idealized "uneducated farm boy" Smith.
           But this idealization is partly a product of
           later LDS hagiography. Smith was literate, raised
           in a household with bibles and other religious
           texts, exposed to revivalist preaching, and active
           in religious debate from his teens. Cowdery (the
           principal scribe and Smith's cousin) was a trained
           schoolteacher. Rigdon (joining late 1830, but a
           candidate for collaboration on subsequent works)
           was an experienced minister and rhetorician.
         - Chiasmus exists in the KJV Bible (which Smith
           knew thoroughly) and in 19th-century English
           prose; its presence in the BoM is not by itself
           a marker of Hebrew authorship.
         - The 65-day dictation timeline is itself part of
           the LDS narrative; independent corroboration is
           limited.
         - As such, the "capacity" argument requires
           accepting a thin biographical profile of Smith
           that the broader evidence contradicts. Without
           establishing the profile independently, this
           defense reduces to retroactive assumption.
     - **The Witnesses Never Formally Retracted**
         - The witnesses' refusal to retract a printed
           testimony is consistent with several
           explanations besides truth:
             - Continued sincere belief in a visionary
               experience that did not require physical
               plates (Whitmer's "eye of faith" qualification).
             - Personal investment in a public statement that
               retraction would humiliate.
             - Continued belief in some divine action without
               continued belief in Smith's specific
               authority — which is what Cowdery, Whitmer,
               and Harris's later religious paths actually
               look like.
         - Moreover, the criterion of "did not formally
           retract" admits both genuine witness and committed
           visionary error. To distinguish, we would need
           independent access to whether the plates actually
           existed and were translated — which is exactly
           what the LDS chain forbids by stipulation.
     - **Spiritual Confirmation (the Moroni Promise)**
         - The Moroni 10 promise functions as a
           method-of-verification that can equally confirm
           truth or falsehood. Many sincere people pray about
           competing scriptures (the Quran, the Bhagavad
           Gita, the Book of Mormon, the Doctrine and
           Covenants, etc.) and report receiving inward
           confirmation. If the Moroni promise is valid,
           every such inward confirmation is also valid,
           rendering the criterion unable to distinguish
           truth from falsehood.
         - By the project's standard (section 3): any method
           that can prove falsehood cannot prove truth. The
           Moroni promise as a standalone epistemic test
           fails this filter.
     - **Progressive Revelation vs. Gradual Development**
         - As with the NT's rising Christology, "progressive
           revelation" and "gradual development" appeal to
           the same data: an evolving doctrine across a
           prophet's career. Distinguishing them requires
           first establishing what is true — i.e., is the
           late doctrine the truth being progressively
           uncovered, or the falsehood being progressively
           constructed? Asserting the former without
           independent grounds is circular.
         - The LDS case is in fact harder than the NT case
           here: the NT's rising Christology unfolds across
           multiple authors and decades; the LDS doctrinal
           evolution unfolds inside the lifetime of a single
           prophet, with the late doctrine (King Follett
           Discourse) contradicting earlier teachings (BoM
           Christology, Lectures on Faith Godhead). For
           "progressive revelation" to apply, Smith must have
           been receiving an evolving truth from God across
           14 years — which is internally coherent only if
           we already grant Smith's prophetic authority.
     - **The Plates Were Returned, Not Lost**
         - The "returned to Moroni" stipulation is by design
           a stipulation that closes off the most direct
           verification path. This is the same epistemic
           structure as the Catalyst Theory for the Book of
           Abraham: any future evidence that could refute
           the claim is excluded in advance by the claim's
           framing.
         - This is the inverse of the Quran's situation,
           where the artifacts of preservation (manuscripts,
           memorizers, geographies, named figures) are all
           still accessible to verification. The closer a
           claim is to such accessibility, the more its
           preservation is subject to evidence-based
           constraint; the further it is, the more it
           depends on retroactive assumption.
     - **Limited-Geography Model**
         - The limited-geography model is internally coherent
           but unfalsifiable: any specific Mesoamerican
           region proposed (Mesoamerica generally, Yucatan,
           the Olmec heartland, etc.) does not match the
           BoM's named cities or specific events; when one
           proposal fails, another is offered. This is the
           structure of speculation-as-defense the project
           rejects: a defense that cannot fail does not
           support truth.
         - Moreover, the limited-geography model contradicts
           early prophetic statements (Joseph F. Smith,
           Spencer W. Kimball, Bruce R. McConkie) treating
           all Native Americans as Lamanites. To accept the
           limited-geography model, the LDS reader must also
           accept that early Church leaders were mistaken on
           a foundational point — which weakens (rather than
           supports) the continuing-prophetic-authority
           premise.

 7. How strongly does the claim's custodianship resist
    fabrication and error? (Results — Texts)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - LDS custodianship has fewer unknowns than the NT, but
       the *known* constraints actively disconfirm rather than
       reliably confirm.
     - **Unknowns and Their Constraints**
         - On the 19th-century side, the chain is essentially
           known: Smith's life and movements, the scribes,
           the printing, the editions are all documented in
           detail through the Joseph Smith Papers Project,
           contemporary newspapers, and court records. So,
           unlike the NT case (whose links depend on lost
           works and unnamed transmitters), the LDS modern
           chain is well-constrained.
         - On the pre-Smith side, the situation is the
           opposite of well-constrained: the constraints are
           known and they cut against the claim.
             - The golden plates are by stipulation
               unavailable — no constraint can be set
               because no artifact exists to examine.
             - The Egyptian papyri have been recovered and
               positively identified as common funerary
               texts. The constraint is known and
               disconfirms the translation.
             - The BoM's described pre-Columbian American
               civilizations (horses, steel, wheat, chariots,
               Hebrew/Egyptian script, large cities) are
               archaeologically unattested. The constraint is
               known and disconfirms the claim within the
               regions and timeframes the BoM specifies.
         - So the LDS chain inverts the NT problem: the NT
           has unreliable *unknown* constraints (we don't
           know what the intermediate transmitters did with
           the message); the LDS canon has reliable *known*
           constraints (we know what the papyri actually
           say, we know what pre-Columbian archaeology shows)
           that contradict the claim.
     - **Power-Based vs. Truth-Based Suppression**
         - The LDS institution exercised significant
           editorial control over its foundational documents
           for over 150 years. The 2010s Gospel Topics essays
           are the first comprehensive institutional
           acknowledgment of several issues that were not
           merely undisclosed but actively denied (e.g.,
           polyandry, multiple First Vision accounts,
           seer-stone translation method).
         - For "the institution suppressed false claims" to
           function as a defense (the NT-style argument), the
           institution must first be shown to be true. The
           LDS suppression history runs in the opposite
           direction the defense requires: it is a record of
           an institution withholding evidence against
           itself, which is the failure mode this project
           flagged for retroactive assumption in section 3.
     - **Criterion of Embarrassment**
         - The BoM and D&C contain very few details that cut
           against the institution's interests in the way the
           NT's "women as first resurrection witnesses" or
           the Quran's "Prophet rebuked in public" do.
         - The candidates that are sometimes proposed
           (Smith's failed Kirtland Bank, the Missouri
           failures, the Mountain Meadows Massacre) are not
           internal-text details — they are historical
           events that the Church initially denied,
           minimized, or relocated blame for, and
           subsequently acknowledged only after external
           pressure. This pattern is the inverse of the
           embarrassment criterion: it is institutional
           reluctance to publicize unflattering material,
           not preservation of it inside the canon.
     - **Progressive Revelation vs. Gradual Development**
         - The LDS doctrinal arc is the clearest in-record
           case of evolution across the project's
           candidates: BoM Christology (1830) → Lectures on
           Faith Godhead (1835) → King Follett Discourse
           (1844) is a documented progression toward the
           modern doctrine. Defenders cite progressive
           revelation; critics cite developmental drift; the
           data fit either reading. Without independently
           establishing Smith's prophetic authority, the
           defense is circular.

 8. How strongly does the claim's custodianship resist
    fabrication and error? (Results — Trustworthiness)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - Liar, lunatic-as-confabulator, demonic, and prior-
       practice continuation are all live possibilities; the
       defenses available cannot rule them out without
       retroactive assumption of the very claim under
       evaluation.
     - **Deception and Dishonesty**
         - Smith's documented gains (church leadership,
           political offices, ~30+ plural marriages including
           polyandry and teenagers, control of the Kirtland
           Safety Society and then the Nauvoo economy,
           candidacy for U.S. President) provide a
           substantial worldly payoff profile.
         - Smith's documented concealments (plural marriage
           publicly denied, multiple First Vision versions
           not disclosed, the *Nauvoo Expositor* destroyed
           after exposing private practice) establish a
           pattern of strategic information control over
           inconvenient truths.
         - Smith's prior career (seer-stone treasure-digging,
           court-documented in 1826) shows that the
           method of producing revelatory content predates
           the prophetic claim, and the same instrument
           continued to be used.
         - The "Smith would have given up if he had been
           lying" argument fails the same way the parallel
           Paul argument was shown to fail in the original
           file: people sustain a lie when they continue to
           benefit from it, even at high cost, because
           their alternative (admitting fabrication) is
           worse. Smith's death at Carthage occurred in a
           jail cell while he was attempting to defend his
           religious and political position — not as a
           voluntary martyrdom for a chance to recant.
         - The honest-vs-liar analysis without retroactive
           assumption strongly favors the liar / capable-
           confabulator end of the spectrum.
     - **Insanity and Simplicity**
         - Smith was clearly intelligent and organized; the
           pure-insanity model fails.
         - The capable-confabulator model fits the evidence
           cleanly: an intelligent participant in a
           visionary culture who began with sincere folk-
           magic belief, escalated into more elaborate
           revelations as his community formed, and managed
           internal contradictions through continuing-
           revelation framing and editorial control.
     - **Demonic Inspiration**
         - LDS doctrine moves the conception of God in the
           directions the project's section 4 derived as
           failure modes: plurality of gods, embodied
           Father, eternal matter prior to God, infinite
           regress of progenitors, God bound by external
           law, creature-Creator continuity. These are
           precisely the doctrinal directions that erode
           "things are defined by their consistent
           properties" and "God's will is the fundamental
           material of reality".
         - LDS also introduces esoteric, reserved ritual
           (the temple endowment, with covenants and tokens
           that must not be disclosed publicly) — contrary
           to the publicness Jesus and Muhammad both
           demanded of their messages, and aligned with the
           secrecy-and-tokens pattern that the project's
           own deductions about deception flag (cf. the
           Gnostic chain in the NT discussion: "their chain
           appears intentionally hidden").
         - As with the NT case, demonic inspiration cannot
           be definitively proven, but it cannot be ruled
           out either, and the doctrinal direction is the
           direction the project's standard flags as
           characteristic.
     - **Overall** — without retroactive assumption that the
       LDS claim is true, every available defense reduces to
       either circular reasoning (progressive revelation,
       witnesses' non-retraction) or to unfalsifiable
       stipulation (catalyst theory, returned plates,
       limited-geography model). The character profile of
       Smith, the documented prior practice using the same
       method, the doctrinal evolution inside his lifetime,
       and the inverse-direction anachronisms together
       provide multiple converging natural pathways for
       fabrication that the LDS canon's custodianship does
       not close off.

 7. Conclusion — LDS row + integrated three-way conclusion
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - How strong is the claim and its custodianship?
     - LDS
         - Weak claim, mixed custodianship (strong on the
           19th-century side, decisively failing on the
           ancient side), all defenses requiring retroactive
           assumption or unfalsifiable stipulation.
         - Unlike the NT and Quran candidates — both of
           whom present a prophet whose character is the
           load-bearing element of resistance to fabrication
           — the LDS canon presents a prophet whose
           documented life supplies multiple natural
           pathways for fabrication directly. Smith was
           tried in 1826 for using the *same* seer stone, in
           the *same* method (placed in a hat), to produce
           supernatural content for paying clients. Two
           years later, the same instrument and method were
           used to produce the Book of Mormon. The
           naturalistic prior on the production method is
           therefore not a speculative external imposition;
           it is documented in Smith's own life before the
           prophetic claim.
         - On the textual side, the LDS canon presents the
           reverse of the structural problem the NT faces.
           The NT problem is unreliable *unknown* constraints
           — we don't know what intermediate transmitters
           did with the message, so we can't rule out
           fabrication. The LDS problem is unreliable *known*
           constraints — we *do* know what the recovered
           Book of Abraham papyri say (common funerary
           texts, unrelated to Abraham); we *do* know what
           pre-Columbian archaeology shows (no horses,
           steel, wheat, chariots, Hebrew/Egyptian script in
           the relevant period); we *do* know what the early
           BoM editions read (a more modalist Christology
           than the modern editions); we *do* know what the
           early First Vision accounts say (incompatible
           details with the canonized version). What is
           known disconfirms the claim within the regions
           and timeframes the claim itself specifies.
         - On the chain side, every LDS stream converges at
           one man and only branches after his death into
           rival successor lines. The Three Witnesses saw
           the plates in a visionary experience ("the eye
           of faith"); the Eight Witnesses were drawn from
           two families (Smiths and Whitmers); witnesses'
           later trajectories include disaffection,
           excommunication, and at various points public
           accusations that Smith was a fallen prophet —
           consistent with continuing belief in a visionary
           experience but not establishing the physical
           reality of the plates.
         - On the doctrinal side, LDS introduces commitments
           that move conception of God away from what File
           4 derived from reality (plurality of gods,
           embodied Father, eternal uncreated matter and
           intelligences, infinite regress of progenitor
           gods, God bound by external law, Creator/creature
           continuity), and introduces esoteric, reserved
           rituals (the temple endowment) that pattern-match
           the project's own deductions about deception
           (hidden chains, public victory mistaken for
           spiritual truth).
         - For LDS to be defended, the reader must accept:
           (a) that the witnesses' visionary experience
           establishes physical plates that no one else
           ever examined; (b) that the recovered papyri
           somehow inspired but did not source a translation
           that contradicts them ("catalyst theory" —
           unfalsifiable); (c) that the absence of
           archaeological corroboration for any BoM
           civilization is explained by a "limited
           geography" model that no one can specify and
           every proposal of which has failed (also
           unfalsifiable); (d) that intra-lifetime doctrinal
           evolution is "progressive revelation" rather than
           developmental drift (circular without first
           granting Smith's authority); and (e) that the
           Moroni promise of spiritual confirmation
           establishes truth — a criterion that, applied
           consistently, also confirms competing scriptures
           and therefore cannot distinguish truth from
           falsehood.
         - As such, the reliability of the LDS canon cannot
           be established without retroactive assumption,
           and unlike the NT case, the available *known*
           evidence actively works against the assumption.
           This is the failure mode the project identifies
           in section 3 (Building Confidence): a method that
           can be used to justify falsehood cannot
           distinguish truth from falsehood. The LDS canon's
           defenses are unable to fail; the LDS canon's
           ancient claims, where falsification *is*
           possible, have been falsified.

 - All Three
     - Restating and extending the NT/Quran summary at the
       end of File 6 to absorb LDS:
     - If truth is defined by consistency, and God is the
       Truth, and reality itself is sustained by His
       consistent and unwavering will, then the true
       religion (by definition) must reflect that same,
       maximal consistency in both its message and its
       preservation. A belief that must be presupposed or
       speculated about in order to be defended cannot be
       independently verified or distinguished from
       falsehood — and a system that cannot distinguish
       itself from a well-crafted falsehood cannot serve as
       a reliable guide to ultimate truth.
     - When this standard is applied:
         - **The New Testament's preservation requires
           speculation to be *true***: we have to grant
           Paul's reliability and alignment with the
           earthly Jesus, treat the rising Christology as
           progressive revelation rather than gradual
           development, and trust unknown transmitters and
           lost works. Each of these can be granted, but
           only by retroactive assumption.
         - **The Quran's preservation requires speculation
           to be *false***: every alternative naturalistic
           pathway (hidden composition, hidden education,
           extraordinary native talent) requires
           historically unsupported coordination, secrecy,
           or genius beyond what Muhammad's documented life
           supports. The constraints positively rule out
           the alternatives.
         - **The LDS canon's preservation requires the
           *known* evidence to be wrong or irrelevant**:
           the recovered Book of Abraham papyri must be
           inspired-but-not-sourced; the archaeological
           and genetic record must be either misread or
           limited to a small Mesoamerican region no one
           can specify; the intra-lifetime doctrinal
           evolution must be progressive revelation; the
           multiple incompatible First Vision accounts must
           be different facets of the same vision; and the
           1826 Bainbridge seer-stone trial must be
           coincidence. Each of these is logically
           possible. But each requires the available
           constraints to lose their force — which is
           the structure of *unfalsifiability*, not
           verification.
     - Comparing the three:
         - The Quran enters history with a pattern of
           preservation — public, continuous, empirically
           accessible — that makes fabrication difficult
           from the start. Its defenses are stronger
           manifestations of the same categories that the
           NT and LDS appeal to (a living tradition,
           progressive revelation, divine preservation), but
           in forms that incapacitate rather than require
           speculation.
         - The New Testament enters history with strong
           cultural corroboration of its setting and a
           prophet whose character profile resists
           fabrication, but with chains of transmission
           that pass through anonymous editors and lost
           sources and a doctrine whose logical model
           cannot be coherently stated. Its defense
           requires speculation in one direction.
         - The LDS canon enters history with a strong
           19th-century paper trail and a prophet whose
           documented life supplies natural pathways for
           fabrication directly, with chains that converge
           at one source, and with a doctrine whose
           deviations from File 4's deductions are the
           directions the project's standard flags as
           characteristic of distortion. Its defense
           requires the *known* available evidence to be
           wrong or irrelevant — a heavier ask than the NT
           and an inverse of the Quran.
     - Therefore the ordering, after the historical
       comparison filter, is:
         - **Quran**: survives this filter.
         - **New Testament**: does not survive — the claim
           of textual superiority to the Quran cannot be
           established without retroactive assumption.
         - **LDS canon**: does not survive — the known
           constraints positively support fabrication, and
           the defenses available are unable to fail by
           their own structure.
     - With both NT and LDS eliminated at the historical
       filter (NT through speculation-required-to-be-true,
       LDS through known-evidence-disconfirms), the
       remaining candidate is the pure Monotheism sourced by
       the Quran. The non-fundamental comparison that follows
       (File 7) does not re-open the question of which canon
       is authentic — that has been settled — but uses the
       theological coherence of the surviving candidate
       against the eliminated candidates as a verification
       check: a True religion that aligns with reality and
       has the strongest historical authority should also
       remain maximally coherent under theological scrutiny.
       LDS is carried forward into that comparison alongside
       Christianity not because its historical claim
       survived, but so that its theology can be checked for
       internal coherence and for whether its corruption
       pattern (relative to either remaining alternative)
       matches what the project's standard for deception
       would predict.

     - LDS

           that is righteous is favored of God. But behold,
           this people had rejected every word of God, and
           they were ripe in iniquity." (1 Nephi 17:35)
         - "Behold, doth he cry unto any, saying: Depart
           from me? Behold, I say unto you, Nay; but he
           saith: Come unto me all ye ends of the earth, buy
           milk and honey, without money and without
           price." (2 Nephi 26:25)
         - "Behold, he who has repented of his sins, the same
           is forgiven, and I, the Lord, remember them no
           more." (D&C 58:42)
         - "For behold, this is my work and my glory — to
           bring to pass the immortality and eternal life of
           man." (Moses 1:39, PoGP)
         - "God so loved the world, that he gave his only
           begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him
           should not perish, but have everlasting life."
           (KJV John 3:16, also LDS canon)
     - Differences
         - Christianity
             - God's love is His very nature, climaxing in
               the atonement.
         - Islam
             - God inscribed mercy upon his nature (note that
               "mercy" in Arabic is a form of one-sided love
               — the stronger two-sided love is reserved for
               those who love God).
         - LDS
             - God's love motivates not only our salvation
               from sin but our *exaltation* — His glory and
               work is to bring His children to the same
               state He occupies. Love is therefore directed
               toward elevating us to His category, not just
               restoring us to a creature-relationship with
               Him.
             - However: God is also bound by eternal laws of
               justice that constrain the application of
               mercy. Mercy operates within justice ("mercy
               cannot rob justice" — Alma 42:25), and the
               atonement of Christ is the mechanism by which
               mercy's claim does not violate justice's
               claim.

 - iii. God is perfectly just and will judge.
     - LDS
         - "Mercy cannot rob justice." (Alma 42:25)
         - "What, do ye suppose that mercy can rob justice?
           I say unto you, Nay; not one whit. If so, God
           would cease to be God." (Alma 42:25)
         - "For behold, justice exerciseth all his demands,
           and also mercy claimeth all which is her own; and
           thus, none but the truly penitent are saved."
           (Alma 42:24)
         - "And our God is a God of truth, and cannot lie."
           (Ether 3:12)
         - "And he commanded them that they should preach
           nothing save it were repentance and faith on the
           Lord, who had redeemed his people." (Mosiah 18:20)
         - "The Son of Man cometh in the glory of his Father,
           and then he shall reward every man according to
           his works." (D&C 6:33)
         - Three Degrees of Glory (D&C 76, Vision of the
           Three Degrees): after judgment, humans are
           assigned to one of three kingdoms of glory —
           celestial, terrestrial, telestial — or, for the
           sons of perdition, to outer darkness.
     - Differences
         - Christianity
             - God's justice is fully satisfied in Christ's
               vicarious substitution.
         - Islam
             - God's justice is expressed without vicarious
               substitution.
         - LDS
             - God's justice is satisfied by Christ's
               atonement *and* by the individual's repentance
               and obedience to ordinances. Christ's atonement
               makes the path possible; the individual's
               works are required to walk it.
             - Judgment is granular: each person is assigned
               to one of three kingdoms of glory based on
               the laws they were willing to abide and the
               ordinances they received. Universal
               resurrection ("salvation" in the broad LDS
               sense) is granted to nearly all by Christ's
               grace; "exaltation" (celestial kingdom,
               godhood, eternal families) requires temple
               ordinances and faithful endurance.
             - Note: the BoM statement that "God would cease
               to be God" if justice were not maintained
               implies God is subject to a law of justice
               external to and prior to Him — consistent with
               File 5/Phase 1's finding that LDS places
               eternal laws above God.

 B. Morality & Salvation
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - i. Humans are morally responsible agents.
     - LDS
         - "Wherefore, men are free according to the flesh;
           and all things are given them which are expedient
           unto man. And they are free to choose liberty and
           eternal life, through the great Mediator of all
           men, or to choose captivity and death, according
           to the captivity and power of the devil; for he
           seeketh that all men might be miserable like unto
           himself." (2 Nephi 2:27)
         - "Adam fell that men might be; and men are, that
           they might have joy." (2 Nephi 2:25)
         - "For behold, this life is the time for men to
           prepare to meet God; yea, behold the day of this
           life is the day for men to perform their labors."
           (Alma 34:32)
         - "We believe that men will be punished for their
           own sins, and not for Adam's transgression."
           (Articles of Faith 1:2)
         - "Therefore, cheer up your hearts, and remember
           that ye are free to act for yourselves — to choose
           the way of everlasting death or the way of eternal
           life." (2 Nephi 10:23)
     - Differences
         - Christianity
             - Salvation is achieved through Jesus' atonement,
               not by our own righteousness — by God's grace.
         - Islam
             - Salvation is achieved by personal righteousness,
               made possible by God's mercy — by God's grace.
         - LDS
             - Salvation (broad: universal resurrection) is by
               Christ's grace alone, freely given.
             - *Exaltation* (the full LDS soteriological goal:
               eternal life in the celestial kingdom and the
               path to godhood) requires the combination of
               grace and works — "it is by grace that we are
               saved, after all we can do" (2 Nephi 25:23).
               Both faith in Christ and obedience to specific
               ordinances (baptism, confirmation, endowment,
               temple sealing) are required.
             - Moral agency is rooted in a pre-mortal
               existence: we chose to come to mortality and
               accept the Plan of Salvation in the pre-mortal
               Council in Heaven. Agency is essential and
               cannot be overridden — God cannot save a
               person against their will.

 - ii. Humans sin and need God's forgiveness.
     - LDS
         - "For the natural man is an enemy to God, and has
           been from the fall of Adam, and will be, forever
           and ever, unless he yields to the enticings of the
           Holy Spirit, and putteth off the natural man and
           becometh a saint through the atonement of Christ
           the Lord, and becometh as a child, submissive,
           meek, humble, patient, full of love, willing to
           submit to all things which the Lord seeth fit to
           inflict upon him, even as a child doth submit to
           his father." (Mosiah 3:19)
         - "Behold, he who has repented of his sins, the same
           is forgiven, and I, the Lord, remember them no
           more." (D&C 58:42)
         - "By this ye may know if a man repenteth of his
           sins — behold, he will confess them and forsake
           them." (D&C 58:43)
         - "We believe that men will be punished for their
           own sins, and not for Adam's transgression."
           (Articles of Faith 1:2)
         - "We believe that through the Atonement of Christ,
           all mankind may be saved, by obedience to the
           laws and ordinances of the Gospel." (Articles of
           Faith 1:3)
     - Differences
         - Christianity
             - The mechanism for God's forgiveness is linked
               to Christ's atonement.
         - Islam
             - The mechanism for God's forgiveness is grounded
               as a direct interaction with God.
         - LDS
             - The mechanism for God's forgiveness is
               linked to Christ's atonement plus the
               individual's repentance, confession, and
               obedience to gospel ordinances (baptism for
               the remission of sins, the sacrament for its
               renewal, etc.). Christ's atonement is the
               necessary mechanism; the individual's
               repentance and ordinances are the necessary
               conditions for receiving it.
             - The atonement covers the consequences of the
               Fall (physical and spiritual death, separation
               from God) — but not Adam's sin as personally
               imputed; humans are punished only for their
               own sins.

 - iii. God merits righteousness, but no one "earns"
       Paradise/Heaven by human merit alone (i.e., without
       God's grace).
     - LDS
         - "For we know that it is by grace that we are
           saved, after all we can do." (2 Nephi 25:23)
         - "Yea, come unto Christ, and be perfected in him,
           and deny yourselves of all ungodliness; and if ye
           shall deny yourselves of all ungodliness, and love
           God with all your might, mind and strength, then
           is his grace sufficient for you, that by his grace
           ye may be perfect in Christ." (Moroni 10:32)
         - "Wherefore, dearly beloved brethren, let us
           cheerfully do all things that lie in our power;
           and then may we stand still, with the utmost
           assurance, to see the salvation of God." (D&C
           123:17)
         - "He that is faithful and endureth shall overcome
           the world." (D&C 63:47)
     - Differences
         - Christianity
             - Salvation is fundamentally grace through
               faith, where works are only its fruit/evidence.
         - Islam
             - Salvation is fundamentally by God's mercy,
               which manifests through God's love of
               righteousness and weighing of our deeds.
         - LDS
             - For universal resurrection / general
               salvation: by Christ's grace alone, with no
               human merit required.
             - For exaltation (the LDS distinctive goal —
               eternal life in the celestial kingdom, eternal
               families, becoming as God is): by Christ's
               grace *plus* faithful endurance, obedience to
               commandments, and the receipt of specific
               temple ordinances. "After all we can do"
               (2 Nephi 25:23) is the canonical LDS framing
               — grace is sufficient, but only after the
               candidate has done all that lies in their
               power.
             - This produces a two-tier soteriology unique
               among the three candidates: salvation by
               grace alone (broad), exaltation by grace plus
               works and ordinances (narrow).

               traditions place the atonement primarily or
               exclusively at the cross.

 Notable LDS-specific theological additions not present in

 - D. Pre-mortal Existence and the Plan of Salvation
     - LDS doctrine includes a pre-mortal life in which all
       humans existed as spirit children of Heavenly Father.
       In the pre-mortal Council in Heaven, Heavenly Father
       presented the Plan of Salvation; Jesus volunteered to
       fulfill its requirements while honoring agency;
       Lucifer proposed an alternative that would force
       salvation and demanded the glory for himself.
       Lucifer's plan was rejected, and he and a third of
       the spirits rebelled and were cast out. The remainder
       chose to come to mortality (Abraham 3, Moses 4).
     - Neither mainstream Christianity nor Islam holds a
       pre-mortal existence for individual human spirits.

 - E. Continuing Revelation and Living Prophets
     - LDS doctrine holds that the heavens are open and that
       God continues to reveal His will through living
       prophets (the President of the Church). New scripture
       can be added to the canon; existing doctrine can be
       refined or extended.
     - Articles of Faith 1:9: "We believe all that God has
       revealed, all that He does now reveal, and we believe
       that He will yet reveal many great and important
       things pertaining to the Kingdom of God."
     - Mainstream Christianity holds the canon closed;
       Islam holds the canon closed with Muhammad as the
       final messenger.

 - F. Temple Ordinances and Exaltation
     - LDS doctrine teaches that certain ordinances —
       endowment, sealing of marriages and families for
       eternity, baptism for the dead — are required for the
       highest degree of celestial glory (exaltation).
       These ordinances are performed in temples and are
       partially reserved from public view (covenants and
       tokens are not disclosed outside the temple).
     - Neither mainstream Christianity nor Islam holds a
       comparable system of reserved ritual as soteriologic-
       ally required.

----------------------------------------------------------------
 2. What is the relationship between God's mercy/love, justice,
    and forgiveness (soteriology)? — LDS row
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - Out of God's love for us, He opens the path to both
       forgiveness and exaltation through Christ's atonement
       (which satisfies the demands of eternal justice),
       combined with our faith, repentance, and obedience to
       gospel ordinances — making it possible not only to be
       saved from sin but to progress toward becoming like
       Him.
     - **Justice**
         - Giving everyone what they deserve, in the
           categorical sense: justice is bound by eternal,
           uncreated laws that even God Himself must
           respect. ("If so, God would cease to be God." —
           Alma 42:25)
         - There are two distinct justice claims to be
           satisfied:
             - The consequences of Adam's Fall (physical and
               spiritual death) entered human history as a
               result of the transgression and apply to all
               descendants — but not as personal guilt
               ("We believe that men will be punished for
               their own sins, and not for Adam's
               transgression" — Articles of Faith 1:2).
               These consequences are satisfied universally
               by Christ's atonement; resurrection is
               granted to all by grace.
             - Personal sins, by contrast, require personal
               accounting. Each sin must be either repented
               of and covered by Christ's atonement, or
               punished individually ("I, God, have suffered
               these things for all, that they might not
               suffer if they would repent; But if they
               would not repent they must suffer even as I"
               — D&C 19:16–17).
         - Justice is both two-sided (righteousness is
           rewarded, sin is punished) and granular: the
           final judgment assigns each person to one of
           three kingdoms of glory based on the laws they
           were willing to abide and the ordinances they
           received.
     - **Forgiveness**
         - When the punishment for a sin is removed by
           virtue of Christ's atonement being applied.
         - The atonement is the universal mechanism, but
           its application to the individual requires
           specific conditions: faith in Christ, repentance,
           baptism by proper authority (priesthood), the
           gift of the Holy Ghost, and endurance to the
           end. For exaltation specifically, additional
           temple ordinances (endowment, sealing) are
           required.
         - "Behold, he who has repented of his sins, the
           same is forgiven, and I, the Lord, remember them
           no more." (D&C 58:42)
         - As a result, God does not "simply" forgive in
           the sense of waiving punishment unilaterally —
           justice still has its demands — but He has
           established a mechanism (Christ's atonement plus
           the individual's response) through which
           forgiveness can occur without violating justice.
     - **Love/Mercy**
         - God's love is fundamentally directed toward our
           exaltation, not merely our forgiveness: "For
           behold, this is my work and my glory — to bring
           to pass the immortality and eternal life of man."
           (Moses 1:39)
         - Mercy operates within and through the atonement,
           and is conditioned on the individual's acceptance
           and obedience. Where mercy alone could not
           satisfy justice, Christ's atonement supplies the
           bridge.
         - "Mercy cannot rob justice." (Alma 42:25) — mercy
           and justice are co-eternal demands; the atonement
           is the mechanism that lets both be satisfied
           together.
 - Examples
     - LDS
         - The full doctrine of justice, mercy, and atonement
           in Alma 42 (BoM): "If so, God would cease to be
           God. ... And thus God bringeth about his great
           and eternal purposes, which were prepared from
           the foundation of the world. And thus cometh
           about the salvation and the redemption of men,
           and also their destruction and misery."
         - "For behold, I, God, have suffered these things
           for all, that they might not suffer if they would
           repent; But if they would not repent they must
           suffer even as I; Which suffering caused myself,
           even God, the greatest of all, to tremble because
           of pain, and to bleed at every pore, and to
           suffer both body and spirit." (D&C 19:16–18)
         - "For we know that it is by grace that we are
           saved, after all we can do." (2 Nephi 25:23)
         - "We believe that through the Atonement of Christ,
           all mankind may be saved, by obedience to the
           laws and ordinances of the Gospel." (Articles of
           Faith 1:3)
         - "Wherefore, redemption cometh in and through the
           Holy Messiah; for he is full of grace and truth.
           Behold, he offereth himself a sacrifice for sin,
           to answer the ends of the law, unto all those
           who have a broken heart and a contrite spirit."
           (2 Nephi 2:6–7)
         - "For behold, this is my work and my glory — to
           bring to pass the immortality and eternal life of
           man." (Moses 1:39)


----------------------------------------------------------------
 3. What is the source of the fundamental, theological
    difference in soteriology? — LDS row (Adam narrative)
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - LDS
     - Adam fell as part of God's intended plan — necessary
       for humanity, agency, growth, and joy — and Christ's
       atonement, prepared before the foundation of the
       world, was provided to undo the Fall's consequences
       and open the path to exaltation.
 - **Adam's Necessary Fall**
     - In LDS doctrine, the Fall is not a tragic
       interruption of God's original plan; it is the
       intended mechanism of the Plan of Salvation.
       Pre-mortally, in the Council in Heaven, the Plan was
       presented: spirit children of Heavenly Father would
       come to a mortal world, fall, exercise agency under
       conditions of opposition, and progress (with
       Christ's atonement) toward exaltation. Lucifer
       proposed an alternative that would coerce salvation
       and demanded the glory; Christ volunteered to fulfill
       the Plan while honoring agency. Lucifer's plan was
       rejected.
     - God created Adam in a state of innocence (not "perfect
       holiness" in the Christian sense) and placed him in
       Eden — a transitional, paradisiacal state from which
       he was to descend into mortality.
         - Adam and Eve were given two commandments that
           could not both be kept in Eden's pre-Fall state:
           "be fruitful and multiply, and replenish the
           earth" (Genesis 1:28, Moses 2:28) and "of the
           tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou
           shalt not eat of it" (Genesis 2:17, Moses 3:17).
           In Eden, Adam and Eve were as innocent children;
           they could not have children themselves without
           taking on a fallen, mortal nature.
         - The Fall, by partaking of the tree of the
           knowledge of good and evil, supplied the
           knowledge, mortality, and capacity for
           procreation needed to fulfill the broader
           commandment.
     - Eve, in LDS teaching, is regarded with honor for
       partaking first — she is presented as discerning the
       necessity of the Fall, and Adam followed in
       solidarity. Both are treated as agents acting in
       (eventual) alignment with God's plan, not as moral
       failures who blamed each other.
         - "Were it not for our transgression we never
           should have had seed, and never should have known
           good and evil, and the joy of our redemption, and
           the eternal life which God giveth unto all the
           obedient." (Moses 5:11, PoGP — Eve's response
           after the Fall)
     - The Fall is termed a "transgression" rather than a
       "sin" in the same sense — a deliberate breaking of a
       lesser command in order to fulfill a greater purpose,
       not a rebellious offense against the infinite God.
       Mainstream LDS theology distinguishes Adam's
       transgression from ordinary sin, and Adam himself is
       regarded as one of the great prophets (the "Ancient
       of Days", Michael the archangel in pre-mortal life).
 - **Consequences of the Fall**
     - The Fall introduced two kinds of death:
         - Physical death (mortality entered the world for
           Adam and his descendants).
         - Spiritual death (separation from God, expulsion
           from His direct presence).
     - Both consequences apply universally to Adam's
       descendants, but not as personal guilt — humanity
       inherits the *condition*, not the *culpability*.
       Each person is born innocent, accountable only at
       the age of eight (the LDS age of accountability,
       D&C 68:25–27), with the universal capacity for
       agency.
     - The Fall also brought knowledge of good and evil,
       the ability to procreate, and the opportunity for
       moral growth under opposition — all of which are
       *positive* outcomes from the LDS standpoint, since
       they are prerequisites for the Plan of Salvation.
     - "And the Messiah cometh in the fulness of time, that
       he may redeem the children of men from the fall. And
       because that they are redeemed from the fall they
       have become free forever, knowing good from evil; to
       act for themselves and not to be acted upon, save it
       be by the punishment of the law at the great and
       last day." (2 Nephi 2:26)
     - Christ's atonement, prepared from before the
       foundation of the world, was provided as the
       mechanism to undo the Fall's universal consequences
       (resurrection, return to God's presence for judgment)
       and the individual consequences of personal sin
       (forgiveness and exaltation for those who accept
       Christ and his ordinances).
         - LDS doctrine emphasizes that the atonement
           happened *in* Gethsemane (where Christ bled at
           every pore from the weight of human sin and
           suffering — D&C 19:18) and was *completed* on
           the cross. Most other Christian traditions
           place the atonement primarily or exclusively at
           the cross.
 - Examples (LDS)
     - "Adam fell that men might be; and men are, that they
       might have joy." (2 Nephi 2:25)
     - "And now, behold, if Adam had not transgressed he
       would not have fallen, but he would have remained
       in the garden of Eden. And all things which were
       created must have remained in the same state in
       which they were after they were created; and they
       must have remained forever, and had no end. And they
       would have had no children; wherefore they would
       have remained in a state of innocence, having no
       joy, for they knew no misery; doing no good, for they
       knew no sin." (2 Nephi 2:22–23)
     - "Were it not for our transgression we never should
       have had seed, and never should have known good and
       evil, and the joy of our redemption, and the eternal
       life which God giveth unto all the obedient." (Moses
       5:11)
     - "And Adam blessed God and was filled, and began to
       prophesy concerning all the families of the earth,
       saying: Blessed be the name of God, for because of
       my transgression my eyes are opened, and in this
       life I shall have joy, and again in the flesh I
       shall see God." (Moses 5:10)

 - The LDS Adam narrative is structurally a hybrid of the
   Christian and Islamic positions:
     - With Christianity: a Fall with universal consequences,
       requiring Christ's atonement for redemption.
     - With Islam: Adam not personally guilty for
       descendants' sins; agency intact; the Fall as
       part of God's plan rather than its disruption.

       mechanism* of the Plan of Salvation, not merely
       permitted but planned and prepared for.
 - This hybrid status will be critical in Phase 8 (the
   4.1–4.12 walk on which salvific narrative better fits
   reality), where the LDS position will sometimes align
   with the Islamic answer (personal accountability,
   children born innocent, agency-centered framing) and

 - The key tension to test in Phase 8: if Christ's
   atonement was prepared "from before the foundation of
   the world" specifically to satisfy a law of justice that

   God merely conforming to them.
 - Phase 9 (corruption comparison) will test whether the

   pattern relative to either Christianity or Islam — in
   particular, whether the "necessary Fall" framing has
   the structure of a coherent restoration or of a
   reconciliation-of-tensions invented after the fact to
   absorb both halves of the older debate.



###############################################################
 PHASE 8 — Section 7.4: LDS Salvific Narrative vs Reality
###############################################################

Each block below is an LDS row to be inserted under the existing
Christianity/Islam blocks for sub-questions 4.1–4.12. Where the
original has a "Fundamental Difference" two-way line, an LDS
row is added; where 4.11 and 4.12 have lettered sub-points, the
LDS row is added under each letter.


 - 4.1. What status was Adam created in? Or, why was Adam
        created to begin with?
     - LDS
         - In a state of innocence as a pre-mortal spirit
           given a physical body, placed in Eden as a
           transitional state before mortal probation —
           created so that he and all humanity could descend
           into mortality, exercise agency under opposition,
           and progress toward exaltation.
         - Adam pre-existed as a spirit child of Heavenly
           Father (named Michael in pre-mortal life — the
           "Ancient of Days"). In the Council in Heaven, he
           accepted the Plan of Salvation; mortality was his
           opportunity to be embodied, fall, and progress.
           Eden was the prepared launchpad for that
           descent, not a permanent home.
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Perfect holiness and communion with God, made to
           be in Heaven" (Christianity) vs. "Honor and
           natural inclination towards God, made to be tested
           on Earth" (Islam) vs. "Innocent embodied
           pre-mortal spirit, placed in Eden as the gateway
           to mortal probation and ultimate exaltation"
           (LDS)


 - 4.2. What did God provide Adam with?
     - LDS
         - A physical body (a divine gift in itself, since
           embodiment is a prerequisite for eventual
           exaltation), Eve as an essential companion, two
           specific commandments (multiply / don't eat), a
           prepared Plan including Christ's atonement,
           knowledge of God, and the prepared adversary
           (Satan) whose opposition would activate agency.
         - Embodiment is uniquely important in LDS doctrine
           — Satan and his followers, having rejected the
           Plan, cannot be embodied; this is a permanent
           disadvantage that exalted humans will have over
           them.
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Dominion, friendship with God, blissful
           responsibility, infused knowledge, immortality,
           and rightly ordered conscience" (Christianity)
           vs. "Bliss, elevated honor, taught knowledge of
           reality, and warning of Satan" (Islam) vs.
           "Embodiment, companion, two intentionally
           contradictory commandments, the prepared
           atonement of Christ, and the prepared opposition"
           (LDS)


 - 4.3. What was Eden's purpose? Why did it come with a
        prohibition?
     - LDS
         - Eden was a transitional, preparatory state — not
           Adam's permanent home, but the launchpad for
           mortal probation. The prohibition (don't eat from
           the tree of knowledge of good and evil) was
           paired with another commandment (be fruitful and
           multiply) that could not be fulfilled in Eden's
           pre-Fall state. The two commandments were
           intentionally given to force a choice between
           innocence and progression — a choice that, by
           design, required Adam and Eve to transgress in
           order to fulfill the broader purpose.
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Permanent home of perfected communion, with the
           prohibition as a test/indicator of trust"
           (Christianity) vs. "Temporary home as a study
           guide for the Earthly test, with the prohibition
           as a lesson" (Islam) vs. "Transitional launchpad,
           with the prohibition intentionally paired with a
           contradicting commandment to force the Fall that
           the Plan required" (LDS)


 - 4.4. What changed about Adam's state when he sinned?
     - LDS
         - Gained mortality, spiritual separation from God's
           direct presence, knowledge of good and evil,
           capacity to procreate, and activation of agency
           under opposition. None of these are lost goods —
           they are *prerequisites* for the Plan that Adam
           accepted pre-mortally. The "Fall" is a
           descent-by-design from innocence into the fuller,
           more capable, but harder state required for
           exaltation.
         - Note that LDS calls this a "transgression" rather
           than a "sin" in the ordinary sense: Adam knowingly
           broke a lesser command in order to fulfill a
           greater one.
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Loss of innocence and sanctifying grace;
           ontological wounding of human nature"
           (Christianity) vs. "Loss of innocence and
           blissful ignorance; epistemological discovery of
           sin's consequences and need for God's mercy"
           (Islam) vs. "Transition from innocence into the
           fuller state required by the Plan: mortality,
           knowledge, procreation, and agency under
           opposition gained as positive outcomes" (LDS)


 - 4.5. What was God's immediate response to Adam's sin?
     - LDS
         - God explains the consequences (mortality,
           expulsion from Eden), but the expulsion is framed
           as the next step in the Plan rather than as
           judicial punishment. He provides clothing, reveals
           the gospel to Adam (including Christ's coming
           atonement), teaches Adam to offer animal
           sacrifices in similitude of Christ, and accepts
           Adam back into a worshipful relationship —
           ultimately calling Adam a prophet.
         - "And in that day the Holy Ghost fell upon Adam,
           which beareth record of the Father and the Son,
           saying: I am the Only Begotten of the Father from
           the beginning, henceforth and forever, that as
           thou hast fallen thou mayest be redeemed, and all
           mankind, even as many as will." (Moses 5:9)
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Judicial curses, clothing out of mercy, and
           expulsion from Eden" (Christianity) vs. "Reminder
           of Satan's enmity, teaching of repentance,
           forgiveness out of mercy, and descent from Eden"
           (Islam) vs. "Explanation of consequences as
           planned, descent framed as next step in the Plan,
           immediate gospel revelation including the coming
           atonement, and Adam restored to prophetic
           communion" (LDS)


 - 4.6. Why wasn't Adam placed back into Eden? Or — was Adam
        actually forgiven in that moment, or otherwise saved
        or pardoned from his sin's consequences?
     - LDS
         - Yes, Adam was forgiven, and he was never meant to
           return to Eden — Eden was a transitional state,
           and Earth is the planned probationary state. The
           "not returning" is not punishment but planned
           progression.
         - The temporal consequences of the Fall (mortality,
           separation from God's direct presence, knowledge
           and opposition) were retained because they are
           the *features* of mortal probation, not its
           penalties. Christ's atonement covers their
           eventual reversal (resurrection and return to
           God's presence) for those who accept the gospel.
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Adam wasn't forgiven from the temporal
           consequences, but saved from eternal, spiritual
           death (because of Jesus)" (Christianity) vs.
           "Adam was fully forgiven and was placed on Earth
           after learning the requisite knowledge to be
           successful" (Islam) vs. "Adam was forgiven, with
           the temporal consequences retained as planned
           features of mortal probation rather than as
           penalties" (LDS)


 - 4.7. What state are Adam's descendants born into?
     - LDS
         - Born innocent and pure, with full moral agency,
           and NOT inheriting Adam's guilt — but inheriting
           mortality and being born into a fallen world
           where opposition operates.
         - "We believe that men will be punished for their
           own sins, and not for Adam's transgression."
           (Articles of Faith 1:2)
         - "And little children also have eternal life."
           (D&C 137:10)
         - Each person is accountable beginning at age 8
           (D&C 68:25–27); children who die before that age
           are saved automatically.
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Just like Adam: inheriting original/ancestral
           sin" (Christianity) vs. "Just like Adam: born
           pure, innocent, and being naturally inclined
           towards God" (Islam) vs. "Born innocent and pure
           (like Islam), with no inherited guilt — but
           inheriting mortality and a fallen environment
           (like Christianity's universal consequence
           framework)" (LDS)


 - 4.8. On what basis are Adam and his descendants ultimately
        saved from their sins?
     - LDS
         - Two-tier basis.
         - **Universal resurrection / general salvation**:
           on the basis of Christ's atonement alone, freely
           given to all humanity regardless of personal
           merit. All will be resurrected and stand before
           God's judgment.
         - **Exaltation** (the LDS soteriological goal —
           celestial kingdom, eternal life with God, eternal
           families, godhood): on the basis of Christ's
           atonement *plus* the individual's faith,
           repentance, baptism by proper authority, gift of
           the Holy Ghost, endurance to the end, and receipt
           of temple ordinances (endowment, sealing).
         - "We know that it is by grace that we are saved,
           after all we can do." (2 Nephi 25:23)
         - "We believe that through the Atonement of Christ,
           all mankind may be saved, by obedience to the
           laws and ordinances of the Gospel." (Articles of
           Faith 1:3)
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Belief in Christ as our Savior from our sins"
           (Christianity) vs. "Belief in God and His
           all-encompassing mercy" (Islam) vs. "Christ's
           atonement is necessary for both tiers; faith
           alone for resurrection, faith plus works plus
           ordinances for exaltation" (LDS)


 - 4.9. Under God's justice, which is greater/heavier: sin or
        righteousness/repentance?
     - LDS
         - For general salvation: Christ's atonement overcomes
           sin universally — neither sin nor human
           righteousness is the decisive factor, because the
           atonement is the mechanism.
         - For exaltation: righteousness/repentance > sin,
           but through Christ's atonement as the necessary
           condition. Sincere repentance erases personal sin
           ("Behold, he who has repented of his sins, the
           same is forgiven, and I, the Lord, remember them
           no more" — D&C 58:42), but exaltation also
           requires positive righteousness in the form of
           ordinances and endurance, not just absence of
           sin.
         - Practically: LDS aligns with the Islamic
           "righteousness/repentance > sin" framework when
           it comes to personal accountability, but inserts
           Christ's atonement as the necessary mechanism
           through which that weighting can occur.
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Sin > righteousness/repentance" (Christianity)
           vs. "Righteousness/repentance > sin" (Islam) vs.
           "Christ's atonement makes righteousness >
           sin possible; sin is erased by sincere
           repentance through Christ; exaltation requires
           righteousness positively, not just sin's
           absence" (LDS)


 - 4.10. How is God's mercy revealed within His justice?
     - LDS
         - Mercy is revealed through a pre-planned
           mechanism: Christ's atonement, prepared from
           before the foundation of the world specifically
           to satisfy the demands of an eternal justice
           that even God Himself must respect.
         - In Gethsemane (and on the cross), Christ — a
           separate Personage from the Father, the
           pre-mortal firstborn spirit of Heavenly Father,
           the literal son in the flesh — bore the
           suffering for the sins of all humanity who
           would accept him. This satisfies justice's
           claim for those who repent, while leaving
           justice's claim active for those who do not
           ("I, God, have suffered these things for all,
           that they might not suffer if they would repent;
           But if they would not repent they must suffer
           even as I" — D&C 19:16–17).
         - Mercy did not "rob justice" (Alma 42:25) —
           justice was met by the atonement, and mercy
           operates within that frame to extend forgiveness
           and exaltation to those who respond.
         - Critically, in LDS doctrine the law of justice
           itself is *not* something God authored; it is an
           eternal, uncreated principle that even God
           respects. The atonement is the mechanism by
           which God works within that law to extend mercy.
     - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
         - "Sins are punished; forgiveness occurs after/by
           means of Christ willingly bearing the punishment
           on our behalf" (Christianity) vs.
           "Righteousness is rewarded while sins are
           punished; forgiveness occurs before/instead of
           punishment" (Islam) vs. "Christ (separate
           Personage from the Father) bore punishment for
           those who repent, satisfying an eternal law of
           justice external to God; mercy then operates
           within that satisfied justice to extend
           forgiveness and exaltation to those who accept
           Christ and his ordinances" (LDS)


 - 4.11. What do the differences reveal about God and His
         justice and mercy?

     - A. Both require God's mercy, but:
         - LDS
             - Justice requires Christ's atonement plus the
               individual's faith, repentance, and ordinances
               before forgiveness can occur.
         - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
             - "Punishment always occurs" (Christianity) vs.
               "Forgiveness waives punishment" (Islam) vs.
               "Punishment occurs (borne by Christ) for those
               who accept him; punishment occurs (borne by
               self) for those who don't — and additional
               ordinances are required for exaltation
               specifically" (LDS)

     - B. As a result of God's justice:
         - LDS
             - Both sin's debt and the merit of righteousness
               and repentance are recorded; Christ's atonement
               provides the necessary mechanism, but the
               individual's response (faith, repentance,
               ordinances) is still required and weighed.
         - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
             - "Mercy is not embedded in the law"
               (Christianity) vs. "Mercy is embedded in the
               law" (Islam) vs. "Mercy is embedded in the
               atonement, which mediates between an eternal
               law of justice and the individual's response"
               (LDS)

     - C. As a result of God's mercy:
         - LDS
             - Christ — a separate Personage from the Father,
               the firstborn spirit, the literal son in the
               flesh — willingly bore sin's debt in
               Gethsemane and on the cross for those who
               accept him.
         - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
             - "Jesus Christ — God in the human Person —
               willingly bore sin's debt for us" (Christianity)
               vs. "God delays punishment to give us
               abundant opportunities to be forgiven first"
               (Islam) vs. "Christ (a separate divine
               Personage, not God-in-the-human-Person) bore
               sin's debt for those who accept him through
               faith and ordinances" (LDS)

     - D. As a result of what God holds us accountable to:
         - LDS
             - Universal resurrection is unconditional by
               Christ's grace. Exaltation requires the
               combination of faith, righteousness,
               repentance, and ordinances. Each person is
               judged proportionally and assigned to one of
               three kingdoms of glory based on the laws
               they were willing to abide and the ordinances
               they received.
         - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
             - "Salvation is grounded in Christ's
               righteousness, not our own" (Christianity)
               vs. "Salvation is grounded in God's mercy by
               weighing our own faith, righteousness, and
               repentance against our sins" (Islam) vs.
               "Resurrection is grounded in Christ's grace
               alone; exaltation is grounded in Christ's
               atonement *plus* our faith, righteousness,
               repentance, and ordinances — graded by our
               willingness to obey" (LDS)


 - 4.12. Which salvific narrative better fits reality?

     - A. "Our actions are a sign of our moral values."
         - LDS
             - Actions are required as the external
               expression of inner submission to Christ;
               faith without works is dead. This is
               especially clear for exaltation, which is
               explicitly conditioned on faithful endurance
               and the receipt of ordinances. Strong fit at
               this level.
         - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
             - "Righteousness is a sign of inner faith
               (strong fit)" (Christianity) vs.
               "Righteousness is a sign of inner faith
               (strong fit)" (Islam) vs. "Righteousness is
               a sign of inner faith AND a positive
               requirement for exaltation (strong fit)"
               (LDS)

     - B. "We have a disposition towards concepts of
          justice."
         - LDS
             - Hybrid fit. On one side: each person is
               accountable for their own sins, not Adam's
               — strong fit (matches Islam). On the other
               side: Christ vicariously bears the penalty
               of those who repent, an innocent bearing
               the burden of the guilty — weak fit
               (matches Christianity). Additionally,
               Adam's Fall consequences (mortality,
               separation) transfer universally to
               descendants even though guilt does not —
               which is itself a partial transfer of an
               innocent person's burden.
             - Moreover, justice is grounded in eternal
               laws that even God Himself must respect, so
               the source-of-justice question reduces to
               unauthored law rather than to God's nature
               — which is not a feature of either
               Christianity or Islam.
         - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
             - "Justice is about debt/settlement (weak
               fit)" (Christianity) vs. "Justice is about
               credit/accountability (strong fit)" (Islam)
               vs. "Hybrid: personal guilt is
               non-transferable AND consequences transfer
               AND Christ vicariously satisfies penalty,
               under a law external to God (mixed fit
               with new architectural cost)" (LDS)

     - C. "Falsehood is easier and more common, while good
          is harder and more valued."
         - LDS
             - Strong fit at the descriptive level.
               Exaltation specifically requires sustained
               effort: ordinances, covenants, endurance,
               and overcoming the natural man. Evil is
               easier; good is harder and more valued.
             - But the LDS framing also says that exaltation
               is the achieving of the same category God
               occupies — which means the "value" of
               righteousness is partly the value of
               eventually becoming a god, not only of
               pleasing God. This introduces a different
               kind of motivation than either Christianity
               or Islam: righteousness is the path to
               one's own deification, not only to
               relationship with God.
         - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
             - "The effort for righteousness is not a basis
               for its value, out of God's mercy (partial
               fit)" (Christianity) vs. "The effort for
               righteousness is a basis for its value, out
               of God's mercy (strong fit)" (Islam) vs.
               "Effort for righteousness is heavily valued
               AND is the path toward one's own
               exaltation/godhood (strong fit at the
               descriptive level, with a distinctive
               motivational framing)" (LDS)

     - D. Conclusion
         - LDS positions itself as preserving the best of
           both Christianity (Christ's atonement is the
           necessary mechanism) and Islam (personal
           accountability, innocent birth, agency-centered
           framing) — and on several diagnostic questions
           it does land closer to the Islamic answer than
           Christianity does. In particular: personal
           accountability (4.7, 4.11.D), heavy valuation of
           righteous effort (4.12.A, 4.12.C), and the
           "Fall as forgiven" (4.6) all align with Islam
           over Christianity.
         - However, LDS introduces new architectural
           commitments that don't appear in either
           candidate and that the project's File 5 / Phase 1
           already flagged as failure modes:
             - **Justice is grounded outside God** in
               eternal laws He did not author and must
               respect ("God would cease to be God" — Alma
               42:25). This means goodness and justice are
               prior to and independent of God — exactly
               the Euthyphro second horn the project's
               File 5 / Phase 1 flagged as an inconsistency
               with "God's will is the fundamental material
               of reality".
             - **Mercy operates only through a legal
               mechanism** (Christ's atonement) which
               itself exists to satisfy a law God did not
               author. Mercy is therefore not freely
               extended on God's authority alone — it
               requires the prior satisfaction of an
               external constraint. This is closer to the
               Christian justice-debt framing than to the
               Islamic mercy-embedded-in-law framing, even
               though LDS aligns with Islam on personal
               accountability.
             - **Two-tier soteriology with reserved
               ordinances**: exaltation requires temple
               covenants and signs that are not publicly
               disclosed. This introduces a structural
               element of esoteric requirement that neither
               Christianity nor Islam holds, and that
               pattern-matches the project's own
               deductions about deception (cf. Phase 4:
               hidden chains, esoteric reserved ritual as
               characteristic of distortion).
             - **Christ is a separate Personage, not God in
               the human Person**: this avoids the
               Trinitarian "no consistent identifying
               property" problem but introduces a different
               one — the substitutionary atonement is now
               being borne by a different divine being from
               the Father, whose justice-claim Christ is
               satisfying. The "love manifested in God
               bearing the cost Himself" framing
               (Christianity's strongest theological move)
               is weakened, because the cost is borne by a
               separate divine person on the Father's
               behalf.
             - **The Fall as planned mechanism** resolves
               the Christian "tragic Fall → happy outcome
               anyway" tension and the Islamic "Adam was
               forgiven" framing into "the Fall was the
               plan all along". But this requires accepting
               that God planned a transgression as the
               means to a greater good — which raises a
               coherence question the other candidates do
               not face: why give two intentionally
               contradictory commandments, when an
               unambiguous commandment to descend into
               mortality would have served the same
               purpose without requiring transgression as
               the mechanism?
         - Therefore on this filter alone, the ordering is:
           **Islam > LDS > Christianity** — LDS aligns
           closer to Islam than Christianity does on the
           diagnostic accountability and effort questions
           (4.7, 4.11.D, 4.12.A, 4.12.C), but introduces
           new architectural costs (eternal law above God,
           reserved esoteric ordinances, separate-Personage
           atonement, planned-transgression mechanism)
           that Islam does not carry. Where Christianity's
           narrative fails by being internally complex in
           ways that don't fit personal accountability,
           LDS's narrative absorbs some of that failure by
           hybridizing — but the hybrid itself introduces
           new failures.
         - And critically: LDS's improvements over
           Christianity on this filter do not undo the
           File 5 / Phase 1 finding that LDS fails on the
           fundamental alignment with reality (infinite
           regress of progenitor Gods, uncreated matter and
           intelligences, eternal laws above God, Creator/
           creature continuity). Nor do they undo the File
           6 / Phases 2–5 finding that LDS does not survive
           the historical filter (known evidence
           disconfirms; defenses reduce to circular or
           unfalsifiable). The theological gains LDS makes
           over Christianity on this filter are real, but
           they're built on top of two prior filters that
           already eliminated it.
         - Fundamental Difference (three-way)
             - "Partial accountability (weak fit)"
               (Christianity) vs. "Perfect accountability
               (strong fit)" (Islam) vs. "Improved
               accountability over Christianity (closer to
               Islam) at the cost of new architectural
               commitments (eternal law above God, reserved
               ordinances, separate-Personage atonement)
               (mixed fit, with the gains offset by the
               new costs)" (LDS)

 Phase 8 — Notes for downstream phases
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - Phase 9 (corruption comparison) will test whether the
   LDS Fall/atonement narrative looks more like a corrected
   restoration or like a deceptive reconciliation of the
   Christian and Islamic narratives — i.e., a "hybrid that
   absorbs both sides" pattern is precisely what a
   sophisticated deception would look like to a 19th-century
   American audience already exposed to both traditions.
 - The "necessary Fall" framing creates a useful test for
   Phase 9: did the LDS narrative emerge by adding *new*
   information (which a true restoration would do) or by
   harmonizing tensions already present in the existing
   traditions (which a fabrication would do)? Joseph Smith's
   own background (KJV Bible literacy, revivalist
   exposure, Ethan Smith's *View of the Hebrews* in the
   neighborhood) is consistent with someone working from
   the existing tensions rather than receiving truly new
   information.
 - The "Christ as separate Personage from the Father"
   commitment plus the "eternal law above God" commitment
   plus the "exaltation through reserved ordinances"
   commitment together form a single coherent pattern: God
   is *not* the ultimate source, and *we* (the prepared,
   ordained, endowed) are on the same ladder He is. This
   pattern is the one direction of distortion File 4 most
   sharply derives against. Phase 9 will examine whether
   this pattern matches what we'd expect from a deceptive
   corruption of either of the surviving candidates.

 PHASE 9 — Section 7.5: LDS as Corruption Comparison
###############################################################

The original subsection 5 asks: if Christianity is true, what
deceptive changes would Islam introduce? — and the reverse. For
the LDS case, we ask the same question in both directions:

 A. How were God's expectations changed?
----------------------------------------------------------------

       with a finite set of obligations PLUS specific

     - Effective Changes

     - Goal of Deception

 B. How was God's justice changed?
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - Christianity → LDS
     - Justice is the settlement of sin's debt ➡ Justice

     - Goal of Deception

           justice from God's nature to an unauthored

 - Fundamental Difference (vs. Islam → LDS)

 D. How was God changed?
----------------------------------------------------------------

This is where the LDS corruption pattern is most diagnostic.
Both Christianity and Islam present God's nature as One in
some sense; LDS presents a plurality of gods in a literal
sense, plus embodied divine Persons, plus eternal progression
on which God and creature occupy different stages of the same
ladder.

 - Christianity → LDS
     - God is Triune (one essence, three Persons) ➡ God is
       three separate divine Personages plus a Heavenly
       Mother, with other gods existing over other worlds,
       and humans potentially becoming gods themselves
     - Original Truth (assumed)
         - God is one in essence and three in Persons.
         - The Persons are not distinct in essence nor in
           mode, but in eternal relations.
     - False Corruption
         - LDS changes the Trinitarian "one essence, three
           Persons" into "three separate beings, unified
           only in purpose" — formally resolving the
           Trinitarian incoherence by collapsing it into
           polytheism.
         - The Father is given a glorified physical body
           ("the Father has a body of flesh and bones as
           tangible as man's" — D&C 130:22).
         - A Heavenly Mother is added.
         - Other gods are asserted to exist over other
           worlds.
         - The Father is said to have once been a mortal
           who progressed to godhood (King Follett
           Discourse), creating an infinite regress of
           progenitor gods.
         - Humans are said to be on the same ladder, and
           may themselves become gods through exaltation.
     - Effective Changes
         - Plurality is escalated beyond Trinitarianism's
           "one-in-three" to outright multiplicity.
         - The Father is embodied (a direct contradiction
           of immateriality).
         - Creator/creature continuity is asserted (a
           direct contradiction of the categorical
           difference both Christianity and Islam hold).
         - The source of God's godhood is displaced into
           an infinite-regress chain.
     - Goal of Deception
         - To make the Trinity "make sense" by reading it
           polytheistically while keeping the divine
           Persons.
         - To flatter human ego with the possibility of
           godhood ("ye are gods" — applied literally,
           rather than as Christ's typological reading in
           John 10:34).
         - To make God comprehensible by making Him
           anthropomorphic (an embodied Father with a
           wife and family) — which lowers the bar of
           transcendence in exchange for emotional
           accessibility.

 - Islam → LDS
     - God is One (indivisible, transcendent, in a
       category above all creation) ➡ God is multiple
       embodied progressing Beings on an infinite ladder,
       with humans on the same ladder
     - Original Truth (assumed)
         - God is absolutely One, indivisible, with no
           internal or external plurality, in a
           categorical class above all creation.
     - False Corruption
         - LDS proposes: a Godhead of three separate
           Personages; a Heavenly Father and Heavenly
           Mother; other gods over other worlds; a
           Father who once was a man and progressed to
           godhood; humans who may themselves become
           gods.
         - This is the *maximum* possible deviation from
           Islamic monotheism within the framework of
           the project: it is a more thorough rejection
           of pure oneness than Christianity's Trinity
           is, because the Trinity at least preserves
           "one essence" while LDS does not.
     - Effective Changes
         - Internal plurality (multiple separate divine
           Persons) is added.
         - External plurality (other gods over other
           worlds) is added.
         - Embodiment is attributed to God.
         - Creator/creature continuity is asserted —
           humans may become gods, God was once a man.
         - The ontological category "God" no longer
           denotes a unique class, only a stage of
           progression.
     - Goal of Deception
         - To make God maximally relatable (embodied,
           family, formerly mortal) at the cost of
           transcendence.
         - To make humans maximally divine (potential
           gods, eternal intelligences) at the cost of
           creatureliness.
         - To collapse the categorical Creator/creature
           distinction entirely.
         - This is the directional pattern Satan's
           original temptation in Eden took ("ye shall
           be as gods" — Genesis 3:5). Both Christianity
           and Islam treat that promise as the
           paradigmatic lie. LDS treats it as a
           prophetic statement of what humans actually
           are destined for.

 - Fundamental Difference (vs. Christianity → LDS)
     - "⬇ God's oneness (further from Trinity into
       polytheism), ⬇ God's transcendence (embodied
       Father), ⬇ Creator/creature distinction (humans
       become gods)"
 - Fundamental Difference (vs. Islam → LDS)
     - "⬇⬇ God's oneness (multiple separate divine
       Persons plus multiple gods), ⬇⬇ God's
       transcendence (embodied, formerly mortal,
       progressing), ⬇⬇ Creator/creature distinction
       (same ladder)"

 G. Results
----------------------------------------------------------------

 - **LDS looks more like a corrupted version of both
   Christianity and Islam (a second-order corruption) than
   either looks like a corrupted version of LDS.**

 - In the original NT-vs-Quran analysis, the result was:
   "Christianity looks more like a corrupted version of
   Islam, while Islam looks more like a corrected version
   of Christianity" — the drift from Islam to Christianity
   matches the deceptive-corruption pattern; the drift from
   Christianity to Islam matches the corrective pattern.

 - When LDS is added, the directionality extends: the drift
   from Christianity to LDS is in the *same direction*
   Christianity drifted from Islam, but further — more
   plurality, less transcendence, more mediation, less
   creature-Creator distinction. From Islam's frame, LDS is
   a second iteration of the same deceptive direction
   Christianity is the first iteration of. From
   Christianity's frame, LDS is a further drift away from
   the partially preserved monotheism Christianity still
   maintains.

 - LDS does *partly* correct Christianity on some
   diagnostic axes (personal accountability, innocent
   birth, repentance erasing sin) — the same axes Islam
   already corrects. But these are not novel LDS
   contributions; they are reuses of corrections Islam
   makes more cleanly.

 - The novel LDS contributions are uniformly in the
   direction the project flags as deceptive: embodiment of
   God, plurality of gods, deification of humans, esoteric
   reserved ritual, continuing-revelation framework that
   absorbs any future doctrinal pressure, and the elevation
   of Satan to literal spirit brother of Christ.

 - Critically: the LDS direction matches the *very first*
   deceptive promise in the Genesis narrative both
   Christianity and Islam preserve: "ye shall be as gods,
   knowing good and evil" (Genesis 3:5). Both Christianity
   and Islam treat that promise as the paradigmatic
   lie — the original deception of humanity. LDS treats
   that promise as a true prophecy of human destiny
   (exaltation = becoming gods). This is the single most
   diagnostic doctrinal alignment with the deceptive
   pattern the project identifies.

 - The drift to LDS also satisfies the project's
   vulnerability-exploitation criterion at multiple
   points: it exploits ego (humans can become gods), the
   desire for superiority (over those who don't have
   temple ordinances), misplaced identity (we are spirit
   children, with family on both sides of the veil),
   love of ease (continuing revelation can update any
   inconvenient doctrine), and worldly attachment
   (Heavenly Mother, embodied family, eternal families,
   priesthood structure mapping onto familiar social
   hierarchy).

 - Therefore: from the standpoint of either Christianity
   or Islam being assumed true, LDS's pattern of changes
   matches what a deceptive corruption would look like —
   more so than either Christianity or Islam does relative
   to the other.

 - The previous result holds:
     - Islam appears more like the correction of Christianity.
     - Christianity appears more like a corruption of Islam.
     - LDS appears more like a corruption of both —
       extending the same drift Christianity makes
       relative to Islam, and adding new architectural
       commitments uniquely in the deceptive direction.

 - On this filter alone, the ordering is:
   **Islam > Christianity > LDS** — with the gap between
   Christianity and LDS larger than the gap between Islam
   and Christianity, because LDS's drift is further from
   either candidate's center than they are from each
   other.

Now we have all three assumed-true directions tested. The

     - Christianity as corruption: drift toward Trinity,

       elevation of Satan. Pattern: deceptive direction.
     - LDS as corruption: same direction as Christianity's,
       but further (more plurality, more anthropomorphism,

       amplified.

 - **LDS-assumed-true vs. Christianity vs. Islam**
     - Christianity as corruption: drift toward simpler
       Godhead (Nicene Trinity), removal of priesthood and
       temple, loss of eternal progression. Pattern:
       subtractive — strips the richness LDS sees as the
       original gospel.
     - Islam as corruption: same direction further — total
       loss of Christological mediation, total
       monotheization, total loss of Heavenly Mother and
       pre-mortal existence. Pattern: subtractive further.

 - **Evaluating which direction better matches the
   deception pattern**:

 - Both the LDS-as-corruption framing (Phase 9 original) and
   the LDS-true framing (this supplement) are internally
   coherent narratives. The question is which one survives
   external testing against the project's prior filters.

 - **The LDS-true frame's structural problem**: it requires
   prior commitments that the project's prior filters have
   already eliminated:
     - It requires the embodied-Heavenly-Father, multiple-
       gods, eternal-progression doctrines to be the
       *original* gospel — but File 5 / Phase 1 showed
       these doctrines fail alignment with reality
       (infinite regress, eternal law above God,
       Creator/creature continuity).
     - It requires the Book of Mormon and Joseph Smith's
       revelations to be authoritative restorations — but
       File 6 / Phases 2–5 showed the historical filter
       eliminates them (known evidence disconfirms; the
       Book of Abraham papyri actively contradict the

       defenses reduce to circular or unfalsifiable).
     - It requires the "Great Apostasy" narrative —
       but no contemporary or near-contemporary source
       attests an apostasy of the kind the LDS narrative
       requires, and the early Christian writings that
       *do* survive (Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, the
       Didache, etc.) show continuity with the apostolic
       teaching, not a discontinuity of the magnitude
       LDS requires.

     - They do not require the LDS-distinctive doctrines to
       be the original — instead, they treat LDS as the
       addition of new doctrines to an existing tradition.

       merely returning to) the existing Christian
       tradition.
     - The "additive" reading is simpler than the
       "subtractive" reading: it requires fewer
       speculative claims (no Great Apostasy needs to
       be posited; no plain and precious truths need to
       have been removed without trace) and aligns with
       the documentary record (Joseph Smith's biography,

 - **Diagnostic test for which direction matches deception**:
     - The project's criteria (subsection 5 preface):

 - A. Scripture
     - i. Language
         - LDS
             - Messenger spoke American English (specifically

               speech.

               in modern English; the LDS canon has been
               translated into 100+ languages, but the
               English text is treated as definitive.
             - Distinctive pattern: scripture in the
               *messenger's* spoken language (like the
               Quran) but in a *register the messenger did
               not natively use* (unlike the Quran, where
               the Arabic is the actual Arabic Muhammad
               spoke, and unlike the NT, where the Greek
               is a translation from the Aramaic Jesus
               spoke). This third pattern — pseudo-archaic
               vocabulary applied to a contemporary
               messenger's everyday language — has no
               parallel in either Christianity or Islam.
     - ii. Structure and Timelessness
         - LDS
             - The scripture is multi-component: the BoM
               is structured chronologically as a Nephite
               history (with editorial framing by Mormon
               and Moroni); the D&C is structured
               chronologically by revelation date; the
               PoGP is a compilation of separate works
               (Moses, Abraham, JS—History, Articles of
               Faith).
             - God reveals progressively through history
               AND continuously through living prophets in
               the present.
             - Timelessness is asserted through the
               doctrine of continuing revelation: any
               future doctrinal need can be addressed by
               a new revelation from the current
               President. This is unique among the three
               candidates — neither Christianity (closed
               canon) nor Islam (Muhammad as seal of the
               prophets) has an open canon.
             - The price of timelessness-through-update is
               that any past doctrine, including
               canonical text, can be modified by later
               revelation: D&C sections have been added,
               revised, and removed; the BoM has been
               edited ~3,900 times across editions; the
               Lectures on Faith were canon 1835–1921 and
               then removed. Stability is therefore
               traded for adaptability.
     - iii. Faith and Reason
         - LDS
             - Faith is described as "things which are
               hoped for and not seen" (Alma 32:21), and
               is grown through experiment ("If ye will
               give place, that a seed may be planted in
               your heart" — Alma 32:28).
             - The primary epistemic criterion for
               confirming the truth of LDS scripture and
               doctrine is the Holy Ghost's witness — the
               "burning in the bosom" (Moroni 10:3–5,
               D&C 9:8–9): pray about it sincerely, and
               God will manifest the truth to you by the
               Holy Ghost.
             - Reason is valued but ultimately subordinate
               to the Spirit and to the living prophets:
               "When the Prophet speaks, the thinking is
               done" (a 1945 *Improvement Era* statement
               since formally walked back by the Church
               but reflective of the practical attitude).
             - Distinctive: the "milk before meat"
               doctrine — some teachings (notably temple
               covenants and tokens) are reserved for the
               spiritually prepared, and challenging
               doctrine is treated as a spiritual failing
               rather than a rational engagement. This
               pattern has no parallel in Islam (which
               makes its core doctrine publicly available
               and verifiable) and only a partial parallel
               in Christianity (which traditionally values
               the Magisterium but does not reserve the
               core gospel from public view).

           against an infinite God that finite humans
           cannot pay; God in Christ pays it on our
           behalf; forgiveness flows from accepting
           Christ.

           for its own actions, rewarding righteousness
           and condemning sin, opening the path to
           forgiveness before punishment; good deeds
           erase and transform sin.
         - LDS: Christ's atonement is the necessary
           legal mechanism by which the demands of an
           eternal law of justice (above God) are
           satisfied; mercy then operates within that
           satisfied justice. General salvation
           (resurrection) by Christ's grace alone;
           exaltation by Christ's grace plus faith,
           repentance, ordinances, and endurance.

           and was forgiven — modeling the relationship
           God intends for all humanity.
         - LDS: Adam's "Fall" was a planned and
           necessary transgression — without it, no
           humanity, no agency, no joy. Adam was
           forgiven; descendants inherit the conditions
           (mortality, opposition) but not the guilt.
           Christ's atonement, prepared from before the
           foundation of the world, was the predetermined
           mechanism.

           — strong fit.

 The Sieve of Truth — LDS-aware version
----------------------------------------------------------------

This project began with a simple question: How can we
distinguish between truth and falsehood?

Here is the sieve we built (with LDS integrated):

    0. The Question: in a world where some things are true
       and others are false, how can we know which is
       which?
    1. Pursuit of Truth: truth is external to me and
       anchored in reality, not my preference. If I am
       unwilling to be corrected, then I'm not pursuing
       truth but my own affirmation.
    2. Discovering Reality: we observe stable patterns in
       reality — with limitations. Truth is neither
       identical to nor blocked by perception: it is
       found by iterative, consistent inference.
    3. Building Confidence: no claim can justify itself by
       assuming itself: any method that can "prove"
       falsehood can't be used to verify truth.
    4. Defining God: if the source of reality is defined
       as "God", and our purpose defined by "religion",
       then we can deduce:
           - God possesses ultimate power, transcendence,
             intelligence, awareness, and will.
           - The True religion corresponds to God's will,
             distinguishes mysteries from contradictions,
             and requires faith founded on reason.
    5. Alignment with Reality: comparing various
       worldviews to these inferences and deductions
       results in:
           - Rejection of Atheism and Polytheism.
           - Possibility of Trinitarianism — only if it
             has superior historicity to all other
             candidates, and only when also inferring
             that "inferring about reality doesn't lead
             to spiritual truth".
           - **Rejection (or contingent retention) of
             LDS** — LDS introduces infinite regress of
             progenitor Gods, multiplies uncaused
             categories (matter, intelligences, eternal
             laws), and places eternal law above God.
             Its viability requires textual authority
             *more* than Trinitarianism's does, since
             LDS must overturn (not merely supplement)
             section 4's deductions.
    6. Historical Comparison: comparing the historicity
       of the New Testament (source of Trinitarianism),
       the Quran (source of pure Monotheism), **and the
       LDS Standard Works** (the load-bearing LDS canon)
       results in:
           - New Testament's historicity requires
             speculation to be strong.
           - Quran's historicity requires speculation to
             be weak.
           - **LDS canon's historicity requires the
             known evidence to be wrong or irrelevant.**
             The Book of Abraham papyri have been
             recovered and contradict the translation;
             pre-Columbian archaeology does not support
             the BoM's civilizations; intra-lifetime
             doctrinal evolution is documented; and the
             defenses available (catalyst theory,
             limited geography, Moroni promise) are
             unfalsifiable or circular by the project's
             own standards.
    7. Non-Fundamental Comparison: comparing the theology
       of Christianity (NT religion), Islam (Quran
       religion), **and LDS** (Standard Works religion)
       results in:
           - Christianity appears more like a corruption
             of Islam.
           - Islam appears more like a correction of
             Christianity.
           - **LDS appears more like a further corruption
             of both** — extending Christianity's drift
             from Islam and adding new architectural
             commitments uniquely in the deceptive
             direction.

As the end of this process, the conclusion is the same as
the original — if anything, *strengthened* by the LDS branch:
if the goal is to distinguish truth from falsehood, and if we
refuse methods that make truth indistinguishable from
falsehood, and if we refuse methods that make fabrication
indistinguishable from truth, then the remaining candidate
must be the one that maximizes correspondence with reality,
coherence, non-circular confidence, and historically
constrained authority. Based on the evidence (shown previously
in each section of the project), the only candidate that
satisfies all of this is Islam. The LDS branch's addition does
not present a new candidate that survives the sieve; it
presents a further illustration of the *direction* the sieve
was built to detect as deceptive.

The conclusion of the original project stands without
modification, but with one important *strengthening*: the
addition of the LDS branch as a third candidate, tested under

distinguish truth from falsehood, then you must reject any
approach that makes falsehood indistinguishable from truth.

answer" that can be used equally well by truth and falsehood.

 - It introduces, at section 5 (alignment with reality),
   an infinite regress of progenitor gods, uncaused matter
   and intelligences, and eternal laws above God — failure
   modes section 4 derives against.

   "the evidence is irrelevant" rather than "the evidence
   is wrong" — i.e., unfalsifiability rather than

that fails on independent filters in the same direction is
showing a coherent pattern, not isolated weaknesses. And the
direction LDS fails in is the direction the project's
hierarchical logic identifies as characteristic of distortion.

falsehood by correspondence with reality, coherence, non-
circular justification, and historically constrained authority

more starkly than Christianity does. If the project's
methodology has any descriptive power at all, the LDS branch's

       archaeological refutation), identify why you
