1. God possesses power and intelligence that encompasses all of reality.
Observations
    Required Inferences
         - Speculation isn't refutation.
         - Reality is ordered, operating on consistent and intelligible laws.
         - Reality tends to progress from order to disorder.
         - Reality has a universally applicable set of fundamental laws.
         - Reality (and life) is a hierarchy of deeply interconnected systems.
         - Life comes from other life, yet originated from non-life.
         - Intelligence correlates with success and purposeful/conscious complexity.
         - Everything that has a beginning has a preceding cause.
         - Every inference has a limit.
 
    Specific Observations
         - The universe exists at a scale physically beyond human comprehension...
             - ... With a granularity/precision that exists at a scale beyond human comprehension.
             - ... With interconnected components that require epistemological probability to understand (from our inability to model all the variables).
             - ... Such that all things obey fundamental, intelligible, and discoverable laws...
                 - ... With those laws so finely tuned that any tiny variation would cause a universal collapse...
                     - ... Which are most evident in the formation of life, which must have required incredibly specific conditions to form.
                     - ... Such that reality constantly maintains that tuning with an unwavering precision at an incomprehensibly massive scale of time.
             - ... In a world where careful, intelligence, and conscious maintenance is required to counteract the tendency to disorder.
             - ... In a world where consistent complexity is a sign of conscious intelligence, while inherent disorder is a sign of the lack thereof.
         - The universe is observed to have a fundamental beginning, which requires a trigger to cause its existence (as something can't come from nothing).
             - It would also require the trigger to be something that is fundamentally not the universe.
         - Whatever the fundamental source of the universe is, it is defined with the label of "God".

The Deduction (and why)
 - God possesses power and intelligence that encompasses all of reality.
     - The vastness, tuning, and consistency of the universe requires power, intelligence, and maintenance on an ultimate scale, as the tiniest of changes would cause a universal collapse.
         - The magnitude of this precision compounds over time exponentially, as every moment could be the one with reality collapses, yet it still persists.
     - Bringing the entire cosmos, its universal laws, and life itself into being from a state of non-being is the ultimate expression of power, as it demonstrates a power that is not limited by space nor time.
         - This also demonstrates supreme conscious intelligence, as a creation of such consistently interconnected complexity requires knowledge of how to avoid the infinitely other alternatives that would cause the universe to crash and burn.

Addressing Objections and Alternatives
 - These objections generally rely on the speculation that "valid possibilities exist because we don't know otherwise", but that's an argument from ignorance: it doesn't disprove the inductive inferences from what we observe in reality.
 - Without clear counterexamples that invalidate the patterns (and to iteratively converge upon the truth, such that it is distinguishable from falsehood), we favor the best-fit explanation: a transcendent God as the source, which explains the data with fewer assumptions than multiplying unverified options.
 
1. The universe is just the result of random chance, meaning the universe is an unconscious "accident".
     - This claim solely depends on the assumption that there is no basis to believe in something that can't be seen when there are natural alternatives to consider, as "God" can't be seen and so it is irrational to consider Him as the universe's cause over possible theoretically natural explanations.
         - The issue with this assumption is that it is equally metaphysical/unseen as the concept of God, yet it is so much less supported by reality and the useful inferences we can make.
         - This claim can't be supported by reality, as the inherent, intelligible, and discoverable order within the universe indicates otherwise.
         - Moreover, the speed at which random events statistically progress (ex. like with the theory of human evolution) is much slower than what reality presents (ex. for humans to have evolved from single-cell organisms, it must have taken 2-3 billion years - which still relies on staggering improbabilities that would have otherwise took much, much longer), which further supports the inference of conscious intelligence over unconscious randomness.
             - And this is all occurring within the staggering improbability of abiogenesis itself (how life began), within the staggering improbabilities of the Earth being hospitable and the even more staggering chance of the universe's fundamental constants being tuned as they are.
     - As such, this objection is just speculation without counterexamples: saying "random chance could explain it" doesn't disprove the inductive pattern of order requiring intelligence, as we observe no unguided examples of such complexity emerging.

2. We can explain our fine-tuned universe with multiverse theory (as it would be just like the Earth is with other planets) as a rare universe among infinite universes in which life is possible.
     - This doesn't explain anything, as it just redirects the discussion of the source of the universe back one layer: how then did the multiverse form?
     - Moreover, a "multiverse" is a theoretical, speculative, and unobserved concept, which we can't derive valid inferences from - as we can with reality.
     - Finally, even if we assume that this multiverse is somehow "uncreated" (as with God), it still is a less powerful explanation than that of God, as multiverse theory still doesn't explain phenomenal consciousness.
         - And we can't really conceive of an "uncreated" universe anyways, as something can't be caused by nothing - so a multiverse just introduces more "things" that need an explanation for how they came to be.
     - As such, this is an argument from ignorance - claiming "infinite universes could exist" treats lack of knowledge as refutation, but without counterexamples from reality, it doesn't invalidate the best-fit explanation of a single, intentional source.

3. If everything has a cause, then God must have a cause as well.
     - This is more thoroughly addressed in future deductions, but in short: this is a category error, as this inference requires the flow of time to occur, and so if God is atemporal, then it would be irrational to apply the same requirements of temporality to something atemporal.
     - As such, demanding a cause (ie. a temporal property) for an atemporal being is speculation: no counterexample exists to show that atemporal "things" need causes, so the inductive rule holds only for temporal reality.

4. There could be limits to God's power.
     - We are limited by time and space, yet the origin of the universe can't be limited by time nor space (as it transcends them) - meaning God is not limited by time nor space.
         - We are unable to conceive of a reality beyond time and space, so it is impossible for us to conceive of limits to God's power - especially with the vastness, tuning, and intelligence required to create the universe indicates that He could have made it any way He wanted.
         - It is impossible for us to rationally define a limit to the power of a being that is atemporal and non-physical; from our vantage point, this power is functionally infinite.
     - In short: speculating on limits without evidence doesn't refute the inference.

2. God transcends time and space.
Observations
    Required Inferences
         - "God possesses power and intelligence that encompasses all of reality".
         - Speculation isn't refutation.
         - Every inference has a limit.
         - All information comes from a source.
         - All things are contingent on their causes (that are not the thing).
         - Time, as we experience it, is linear and progresses asymmetrically.
         - There is a seen reality, and an unseen reality.
         - Time, as we experience it, is linear and progresses asymmetrically.
         - Every thing that has a beginning has a preceding cause.
         - Consciousness can't be reduced to purely physical components.

    Specific Observations
         - The universe is observed to have a fundamental beginning, which requires a trigger to cause its existence (as something can't come from nothing).
             - It would also require the trigger to be something that is fundamentally not the universe.
         - All things are contingent, originating from something else that is not the thing:
             - Chickens and eggs come from each other - except for the first egg, which came from a non-chicken.
                 - Animals come from other animals - except for the first animal, which came from a non-animal.
                     - Life comes from other life - except for the first living thing, which came from a non-living thing.
                         - Temporal things are made from other temporal things - except for the first temporal thing, which came from a non-temporal thing.
                             - Our laws of causality are inferred from events within time, so they can't be applied to a reality that may exist outside of time.
                             - In other words, all things are contingent on something else that is also contingent, except for the universe itself, which is contingent on something that isn't contingent itself.
         - Unseen "things" like laws of physics, mathematics, and consciousness operate independently of physical constraints yet govern or emerge within them.
             - The universe's order and laws apply universally, suggesting a source not limited by the spatial or temporal boundaries it creates.

The Deduction (and why)
 - God transcends time and space.
     - If the universe is temporal and spatial, and the source of the universe must fundamentally be a non-universe, then the source of the universe must be atemporal and aspatial.
     - If we define "God" as the source of the universe, and the source of the universe is atemporal and aspatial, then God must be atemporal and aspatial.
     - This has a number of implications:
         - God is eternal, experiencing no past, present, or future: He simply is (but not like how we imagine someone frozen in time, where they can't do anything).
         - God is immaterial, as he is not composed, caused, or governed by physical processes.
         - God must be uncaused, as causation does not apply to atemporal beings.
             - Not to mention that God would be the one to invent the reality of causation to begin with.
         - God is limitless/infinite, as any limit we can conceive of is structured within time and space - which He inherently transcends.
             - As such, no temporal terms/concepts/language can be applied to God without introducing inconsistencies.

Addressing Objections and Alternatives
1. There is no such thing as "atemporality" and "aspatiality".
     - If you've inferred that all created things fundamentally come from something different (taken from "all inferences have limits"), as well as the fact that reality only operates under a specific set of finely-tuned parameters, then the deduction that the universe must be caused by something that isn't the universe necessarily follows.
        - The universe is fundamentally composed of a spatial dimension and a temporal dimension - all space for all time. 
        - Therefore, it must have originated from an atemporal and aspatial source, to be consistent with your inferences.
        - To claim something like "atemporality doesn't really exist because we can't experience it" is like saying "gravity doesn't really exist because we can't experience it": in both cases, we infer that the effects must have come from some unseen fundamental cause, to which we're simply identifying/putting a name to it.

2. God could be part of time and space, perhaps as an advanced being within a larger cosmos.
     - This claim makes God akin to an advanced alien, who could potentially have the technological power to simulate a reality that we all experience.
         - As such, this would make God contingent on prior causes.
         - This being would not then be identified as God to begin with, as this alternative explanation doesn't resolve the fundamental question of the universe's fundamental cause.

3. The concept of existing "outside time and space" is meaningless, as we can't comprehend what that means.
     - Firstly, it is a false equivalence to equate the limits of our understanding to the limits of reality - if you've previously inferred that "perception, memory, and reasoning are imperfect and subject to error".
     - Secondly, transcendence is not meaningless because we can still define it by what it's not - if you've previously inferred that "things are identified (or known) by distinguishing their properties from others".
         - If you're inferred that "all things are contingent on their causes (that are not the thing)", then you must infer that the origin of anything you observe must have an origin that is fundamentally not the thing.
         - If the universe is fundamental temporal and spatial, then its source must be atemporal and aspatial.
         - Note that this understanding doesn't require complete knowledge of how transcendence works in totality, it merely identifies fundamental properties that are useful for us to understand.
             - This is like our current understanding of quantum mechanics: we don't currently know what causes the collapse of the wave function, nor do we have a means to know if it's deterministic or indeterministic, yet we can still develop cutting-edge technology based on the little that we do know.

4. If God is outside of time, how can He cause an event in time, like the universe's beginning?
     - A more encompassing and useful question would be, how could God create the universe without prior "materials"?
         - God is transcendent, so we can't project our notions of causality onto Him.
         - As such, the "how" is incomprehensible to us - we would first need the capacity to explore atemporality in the same way an atemporal being could.
             - This is akin to knowing about "infinity": we may be able to rationalize and define "infinity", but we can never truly wrap our minds around comprehending it to its full extent.
             - We can only visualize infinity by its relationship to the numbers we can conceive of (ie. numbers can be increased without end, so "infinity" is that limit - if we could do so).
         - As a result, this is a paradox: we are incapable of answering this question given our incapacity to test atemporal phenomena, yet we know it must have occurred given that the universe must have had a beginning (that isn't the universe).

3. God wanted the universe to be created exactly as it is.
Observations
    Required Inferences
         - "God possesses power and intelligence that encompasses all of reality".
         - Speculation isn't refutation.
         - All things are contingent on their causes (that are not the thing).
         - Reality tends to progress from order to disorder.
         - Biological structures are goal-oriented.
         - Intelligence correlates with success and purposeful/conscious complexity.
         - There is a seen reality, and an unseen reality.
         - We fear death, yet we will all die.
         - Reality could have been different.

    Specific Observations
         - If the universe depends on precise tuning to exist, and the tuned values could have been different, then the universe could have been different.
             - Destruction is an easier task than creation and maintaining order, so these tunings exist despite that pattern.
         - All things are contingent, originating from something else that is not the thing.
             - The universe is unique in that, while everything else must be contingent on another contingent thing, the universe itself must be contingent on something that is non-contingent itself.
         - All things are intelligible under a teleogical (goal-oriented) framework, including biological structures (like our eyes and organs), physical structures (like the Earth and stars), and logical structures (like epistemology and fallacies).
             - Even metaphysical concepts can be seen teleologically, such as our own consciousness and our innate drive to search for our purpose.

The Deduction (and why)
 - God wanted the universe to be created exactly as it is.
     - If the universe could have been different, and God consciously created the universe, then God could have wanted the universe to be different.
         - In other words, the universe is a product/result of God's will: He must have willfully/intentionally chosen this universe to exist as it does.
         - Additionally, if reality tends towards disorder and requires intelligent care to maintain its order, then the universe's maintenance must also be reduced to God's will.
     - This means that God intentionally created us, and with the desire to know our purpose (ie. to know what is wanted of us).

Addressing Objections and Alternatives
1. There is no governing will: it's all just random development.
     - To use the concept of "randomness" is to remove "purpose", "will", and "determinism" from the system, regardless if they are there or not.
         - For example, when we say that a coin toss is "random", we're saying that "we are ignoring the determining factors and looking at trends" of the coin toss.
     - As such, the universe could both be random development and guided by a governing will, without contradiction, as the governing will could be choosing to create phenomena that we would classify as "random" (from our own limits).
         - Even if we assume that there is a contradiction, then this claim is less supported by our inferences than the claim that there is a will, especially "intelligence correlates with success and purposeful/conscious complexity" in light of "reality tends to progress from order to disorder" and "all things are contingent on their causes (that are not the thing)".

2. God could have wanted a different universe, with this one being an accident/error or some subconscious action - or, He only created the initial conditions without knowing how they evolve over time.
     - If God has such transcendent power to create the vast universe without prior "materials", and that destruction is an easier task than creation, then if God had so willed, He could have more easily destroyed this universe than to have created it.
         - If God is beyond time (ie. He sees the entirety of the universe's timeline all at once), then we can't conclude that God can somehow be surprised by what He creates (ie. He creates something He didn't know would happen in the future) - which this claim requires in order to be true.
             - Furthermore, if the universe was created subconsciously, not only would this mean that God is somehow unaware that the universe exists, but it would also require some "higher" or "parallel" force that God is not aware of (akin to how we are consciously unaware of what our brains do).
             - This means that this "God" we were defining was actually this "passive atemporal brain" all along - which reduces this claim to "the universe was created by some unconscious process, not a conscious creator".
             - As such, there is no basis for this claim - see "God possesses power and intelligence that encompasses all of reality" for the more complete explanation.
         - If God has the knowledge and intelligence to know how His creations function, while also having the power to make it function any way He wants, then there is no basis to conclude that God can be surprised by anything He creates.

3. This is just the Anthropic Principle: we only observe a fine-tuned universe because we couldn't exist to observe it otherwise.
     - While this theory certainly addresses an observational bias in why we observe our specific conditions, it's still limited by being unable to explain the fundamental question of causality.
         - It's like saying we won some cosmic lottery: just because we're here to see it doesn't explain how or why the lottery was set up at all.
         - As such, the Anthropic principle is true within a temporal context, but does not address the source of that temporal context.
     - This means that God's will is a more encompassing explanation (as God would have authored the Anthropic Principle to begin with) - addressing both the source of our phenomenal consciousness and why the universe exists as it does to begin with.

4. If reality is contingent, then it must operate according to its cause - meaning it couldn't have been different.
     - Our "could be different" observation comes from two sources:
         - Applying hypothesis and reasoning with the fine-tuning of the universe, allowing us to visualize what could occur according to the inferences and laws we know, if they were different.
         - Recognizing that what the universe is contingent on is fundamentally atemporal, meaning we can't project our understanding of cause and effect into that transcendent scope.
     - As such, this statement is misunderstanding where the "possible difference" is actually located, as the source of the difference must be transcendent (ie. what caused the universe to occur) rather than within the universe.
         - A more precise statement would be, "if reality is contingent on a transcendent cause, then it can't operate any differently than what that transcendent cause requires".

5. If God wanted the universe "exactly as it is", why did He choose to do it?
     - This requires a different approach - a different source of information to answer - which is addressed in future nodes.
         - When we ask "why" of anything, we're really asking , "what is its purpose?", or, "for what reason is it wanted?"
             - This is different than asking "how" of something, as we're really asking, "what caused its existence?", or, "what are the physical processes that explain how it came into existence?"
         - As such, if we want to know the universe's purpose, we would need to identify what God wants with it.
             - This question seems directly linked to our drive to define our own purpose in life.

4. God is consistent.
Observations
    Required Inferences
         - "God transcends time and space".
         - All information comes from a source.
         - Things are defined (or known) by their consistent properties.
         - Things are identified (or known) by distinguishing their properties from others.
         - Reality is ordered, operating on consistent and intelligible laws.
         - Reality has a universally applicable set of fundamental laws.
         - Proper reasoning corresponds with reality.
         - Time, as we experience it, is linear and progresses asymmetrically.
 
    Specific Observations
         - The universe operates under unchanging, discoverable laws that apply universally without exception or contradiction.
             - These laws remain consistent over time, allowing predictions and scientific progress.
         - The concept of "change" is a temporal concept, as it requires a difference between "before" and "after".
             - "Consistency" is the opposite of "change", as something without a difference between "before" and "after" remains unchanging.

The Deduction (and why)
 - God is consistent.
     - If "change" requires time to occur, and God is beyond time, then God is beyond change.
         - This also means that God doesn't "change His mind", as we do - when we are surprised or learn about a better approach to something.
     - Reality's laws are observed to be consistent, which can only reflect the consistency of their creator (as a fundamentally changing/chaotic agent would not be capable of creating long-term consistency).

Addressing Objections and Alternatives
1. Consistency is just a human projection onto reality: we can't identify God's properties by it.
     - If consistency was limited to perception, everyone would perceive different things.
         - Observations from independent observers serve to challenge biases in perception: if everyone observes the same thing, then a bias in perception can't be concluded.
         - Note that this is not claiming that "mass perception = 100% understanding", as it is possible for us to possess limits in our perception that limit our understanding.
             - Rather, this is claiming that, because the inferences you've made, biases in perception can be resolved with further study and observation until consistency is observed.
     - For us to be able to infer God's properties from reality, we need to ask, "what properties would a universe-creator need to create our universe?"
         - This question comes with the observation that a creation's properties reflects the knowledge and abilities of its creator, as we've already inferred that "intelligence correlates with success and purposeful/conscious complexity".
             - The strength of a house reflects the architectural knowledge of its creator; the realism of a painting reflects the creator's eye for detail, etc.
         - As such, if the universe is fundamentally consistent, then its creator must have the capacity to create consistency - otherwise, if its creator was fundamentally inconsistent, then he wouldn't have the focus necessary to produce consistent creations.

2. If God is consistent, why does reality include apparent paradoxes, like quantum randomness?
     - Firstly, paradoxes occur at the limits of our knowledge because we observe behaviors that our inferred laws don't fully explain.
         - This requires further study and guided observation to resolve - as opposed to a "contradiction", in which it can never be resolved.
         - As such, paradoxes should be interpreted as a sign of our own ignorance, not as a sign of a fundamental contradiction in reality
             - This is supported by almost every inference we've made so far, the most fundamental being "truth is defined by reality".
             - According to this inference, it is inconceivable for reality to possess a contradiction.
     - Secondly, to address quantum behavior: even though we can't predict the outcome of a single quantum event, the statistical probabilities that model quantum systems are themselves consistent (ie. the rules of randomness are not random themselves).
         - This objection conflates unpredictability with inconsistency: while a single quantum event may be unpredictable to us, the statistical laws that govern quantum mechanics are incredibly stable and consistent.
         - Furthermore, this inability to predict quantum behavior fundamentally stems from ignorance on why the quantum wave function collapses, which means that, as of now, quantum randomness is a paradox, not a contradiction.

3. God's consistency implies predictability, limiting omnipotence.
     - This is misapplying what we mean by "consistent":
         - If God is atemporal, then He is beyond time - not that He is completely frozen within time, such that He never changes.
         - Rather, properties that are fundamental to Him are consistent - like His power is consistent, His presence is consistent, etc.
         - As such, "consistency" here means that God cannot be self-contradictory, not that it means God's actions are monotonously predictable.
     - "Consistency" here doesn't mean that there is no diversity in how His properties manifest - actually, this is very much not true.
         - We initially started by making observations of reality, and reducing the complexities to consistent rules that we noticed.
         - This consistency that we noticed is observed to be fundamental/structural - some abstract law that independent systems seem to follow, even though those systems are incredibly complex and diverse.
      - As such, God's consistency doesn't contradict His power - especially since we're observing His consistency by means of how He chooses to use His power!
         - A good analogy is the game of chess: its rules are perfectly consistent and never change, but these unchanging rules allow for a near-infinite number of unique and creative games.
         - Similarly, God's consistent nature is the foundation from which a vast diversity of actions and creations can originate - meaning His consistency is the source of His reliable power, not a limit upon it.

4. Wouldn't a miracle be an act of inconsistency?
     - Assuming "miracle" here is in the context of religion, then it depends: is there an underlying, fundamental rule that encompass both "miracles" and "non-miracles"?
         - If so, then both of these types of phenomena would fall under that inference, avoiding the challenge of inconsistency.
         - The "God's will is the fundamental material of reality" node would be that inference - however, since this question is most likely referring to miracles in the context of religion, future nodes down the line will address that element.

5. God's will is the fundamental material of reality.
Observations
    Required Inferences
         - "God transcends time and space".
         - "God wanted the universe to be created exactly as it is".
         - Proper reasoning corresponds with reality.
         - There is a seen reality, and an unseen reality.
        - All things are contingent on their causes.
         - Everything that has a beginning has a preceding cause.
         - Reality has a universally applicable set of fundamental laws.
         - Reality tends to progress from order to disorder.

    Specific Observations
         - If the universe had a beginning and came from a transcendent source, then it must have been created without pre-existing materials by some transcendent means.
         - Reality is comprised of more than just physical objects, as it also includes:
             - Unseen forces
             - Natural and inferred laws, both local and fundamental
             - Phenomenal experience.
         - The universe's existence is not a one-time event but a continuous process.
             - This continuous process depends on precise, unchanging laws that could have been otherwise, yet persist without fail.
                 - These laws govern everything from the subatomic to the cosmic scale, requiring constant "upkeep" against the natural tendency toward disorder.
             - The fact that reality persists moment to moment, despite the natural tendency toward disorder, suggests an active, ongoing "force" is required to sustain its existence and order.
                 - Without this sustained order, reality would collapse into chaos, as even minor deviations in fundamental constants would prevent the formation of matter, stars, or life.
                 - This "force" would be unseen and manifest at the smallest/deepest level of reality, comprising all things and the unseen structure between them.

The Deduction (and why)
 - God's will is the fundamental material of reality.
     - If the universe was created from a state of non-existence with no pre-existing matter, and God is the transcendent source, then the "substance" of creation cannot be purely temporal or physical: it must derive from something atemporal and non-material.
         - And, as there is nothing fundamentally else except Him, this "substance" can only be God's will.
     - Therefore, we can interpret "reality = God's will" at a fundamental level.
         - This will is not a physical material like atoms or energy but the foundational "stuff" from which reality emerges: an atemporal force that imposes order, laws, and purpose onto what would otherwise be chaos or non-existence.
         - This also means that the purpose of our phenomenal senses is to translate God's will into our awareness.
     - Consequently, the consistent laws of nature we observe are a direct reflection of God's unchanging will, while the ongoing persistence of reality against disorder demonstrates His sustained intentionality.
         - This means that reality is not self-sustaining or accidental, but rather a direct expression of God's consistent will and completely dependent on it.
         - This is also the reason that allows us to infer God's necessary properties from the structure of reality.

Addressing Objections and Alternatives
1. Science explains reality through fundamental laws and matter, without needing a "will" as material: this is just a God-of-the-gaps argument.
     - Firstly, science reduces to making inferences (using math, logic, etc.) that are useful enough that allow us to make predictions that come true, and you don't need to know everything in order for a science to be useful.
         - Quantum mechanics is a perfect example of this, as we don't know why the quantum wave function fundamentally collapses, yet we can still observe statistical/probabilistic trends that allow us to create cutting-edge technology.
         - This is universally applicable, as you don't need to know how something fundamentally works: you don't need to have a PHD in math in order to make a circle or a wheel, or to have a PHD in logic to know that "apples are not rocks", etc.
         - As such, to make this statement more accurate: "science is useful in our lives without knowing how reality fundamentally operates".
     - Secondly, a quick aside into the God-of-the-gaps argument (that unexplained phenomena are due to divine intervention, rather than scientific understanding):
         - This argument presupposes its premise (that reality is explained by science without needing God), as it challenges the claim that whatever we know occurs because of science, while whatever we don't know must occur because of God (ie. the more we know about reality, the less God is necessary to explain it).
             - The popular example of this is when Isaac Newton was discovering his laws of gravity, he would notice minor variations that his math could not explain, so he attributed those variations to God Himself - yet we can now explain those variations with Einstein's theories of relativity.
         - However, this claim has no basis: if reality truly is shaped by God, then both what we know and what we don't know are governed by His will.
             - As such, it would be wrong to assume that unexplained phenomena are due to divine intervention rather than scientific understanding, but not because all things can be explained without God, but rather because all phenomena can be explained with divine intervention, including what is known.
     - To conclude: just because we can benefit from science without knowing the full picture doesn't mean the full picture doesn't exist.
         - Science is the study of reality, while God's will is the explanation for why science functions the way it does: there is no contradiction between the two.

2. This sounds like pantheism or panentheism, where God is identical to or infused in the universe in some way.
     - Not necessarily, as God is still transcendent: if the universe didn't exist, that doesn't necessitate that God doesn't exist - only that we won't exist to discover Him.
         - There is no basis for this inference, as we don't observe creators fundamentally becoming what they create.
         - This would be like saying an author is the book itself because the story is entirely composed of his creativity, or that he needs to physically become the ink and paper in order to compose a story.

3. If God's will is the fundamental material of reality, does this mean our wills are fully determined by God, undermining our inferred capacity for free choice?
     - So far, this node focuses on the relationship between reality and God's will as a whole, without addressing the specifics of our wills yet or what free agency means - this is discussed further in a future node.
         - For now, this raises an open question that must be carefully framed to avoid premature conclusions. The dilemma reduces to:
             - If God's will determines everything, then our wills are actually determined by God - meaning that our wills aren't actually "free" (from determinism).
             - But if we have "free" will, then it would need to be "free" from being determined by a prior cause - seemingly conflicting with God being the cause of all things.
          -  This becomes significant because, if we don't have "free" will, then that appears to conflict with our prior inferences about morality, justice, and responsibility - how can our choices be considered "good" or "bad" if we don't have a choice in our actions? How can we be held accountable to them if none of us can choose otherwise?
     - As such, the question fundamentally reduces to: can God determine/cause/create something that is indeterministic or atemporal?
         - In other words, can our wills be dependent on God while also being "free" from reality's cause-and-effect?
         - If this is possible, then it would require that our wills must exist in a unique "layer" of reality - one that is still created by God (and thus distinct from Him), but not part of the physical, deterministic universe (and thus distinct from it).
