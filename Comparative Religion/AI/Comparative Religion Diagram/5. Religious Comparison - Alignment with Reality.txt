########################################
          Systems to Compare
########################################

 - Atheism: there is no transcendent, willful, or intelligent source of reality: all phenomena are the result of unguided material processes and laws.
 - Polytheism: reality originated from multiple, transcendent creators (often competing with one another), without a higher, all-encompassing creator above them.
 - Trinitarianism: there is a single, transcendent, willful, and intelligent source of reality, who exist as 3 Persons - distinct neither in essence/substance nor form/mask/body.
 - Monotheism: there is a single, transcendent, willful, and intelligent source of reality - without divisibility, partners, and equals.

Conclusion: does the belief system align with reality?

 - Atheism: No (internally contradictory)
     - Every argument essentially reduces to "any explanation with a supernatural god can be refuted with the possibility of a natural explanation". As such, to make Atheism align with reality, we are required to assume that "speculation is proof/refutation" - thus equally weighting knowledge with ignorance.
     - While this does allow us to dismiss arguments for God's existence in favor of unobserved possibilities that fit the data, it also doesn't actually refute the opposing claim (that God is real), nor does it prevent the opposition from making the same kind of argument in return (that God's existence explains those possibilities and could have created those "natural processes" as well).
     - As such, both a claim and its opposite can be valid under this argument, making truth fundamentally indistinguishable from falsehood and invalidating Atheism's authority to make claims about reality.

 - Polytheism: No (not the best fit)
     - Reality doesn't support inferring a Polytheistic source of reality. Reality can be observed to operate under fundamental, universally applicable behaviors, and we were created with the capacity for logic that seeks and recognizes a single, fundamental cause for all things.
     - Furthermore, the possibility for the gods' wills to contradict each other doesn't align with reality either, as no contradictions in reality have ever been observed.
     - As such, while the existence of multiple atemporal gods seems speculatively valid, it can't be the best explanation for reality's source.

 - Trinitarianism: Yes
     - This methodology reveals that properly inferring about reality necessarily leads to the discovery of a single, transcendent source of the universe (of course, assuming the inferences are true without omitting any that are significant).
     - As for the concept of Personhood (which originates from scripture), it requires us to infer that "inferring about reality doesn't lead to spiritual truth", as there is no rationally coherent, identifiable distinction between the 3 Persons. This extra inference allows the Trinity to become an exception to "things are identified (or known) by distinguishing their properties from others".
     - As such, because truth still needs to be distinguishable from falsehood, its truth now becomes entirely contingent upon proving that its historical and textual authority is sound and superior to all alternatives.

 - Monotheism: Yes
     - This methodology shows that properly inferring about reality necessarily leads to the discovery of a single, transcendent source of the universe.
     - It also seems to be initially superior to Trinitarianism, as Monotheism can more clearly assert that truth is fundamentally distinguishable from falsehood. However, this will be verified (or refuted) in the next step, as the properties of the textual evidence will need to be compared. Because God is necessarily consistent, and this is reflected in the coherence and consistency of reality, we can expect that the True religion will reflect the same coherence and consistency.
