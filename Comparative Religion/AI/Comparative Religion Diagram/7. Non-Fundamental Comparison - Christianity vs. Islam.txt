1. What is the relationship between God's mercy/love, justice, and forgiveness (soteriology)?
 - Christianity
     - Out of God's love for us, He opens the path to forgiveness by personally satisfying the debt of our sins, saving us from a burden we can't repay without sacrificing justice.
     - Justice
         - Giving everyone what they deserve (ie. what they have a right to experience, based on how well they fulfilled their responsibilities).
         - More specifically: the debt of sin must be satisfied with a punishment - proportional to the sin's weight. And considering that every sin is against an infinite God, the weight of each sin is fundamentally beyond our limited capacity to pay.
     - Forgiveness
         - When the punishment for a sin is removed.
         - This can only occur after the debt is satisfied (ie. the punishment is experienced) - either when the offender experiences it himself, or if someone else who's qualified willingly intercedes and experiences it on their behalf (ie. vicarious satisfaction).
         - As a result, God can't "simply" forgive (ie. waiving punishment) - that would be unjust, as the cost from our sins is still not being paid for.
     - Love/Mercy
         - God is love itself: the very essence of affectionately desiring our salvation and happiness.
         - This is ultimately manifested in the Atonement, in which Jesus (the filiation - Person of the Son) personally satisfied humanity's debt through his resurrection (by defeating death - the ultimate result of Adam and Eve's original sin) - changing the fundamental salvific requirement from "impossibly perfect righteousness and repentance" to "faith in Jesus Christ" (ie. belief and trust in his divinity).
 - Islam
     - Out of God’s mercy, He opens the path to forgiveness through sincere repentance and good deeds - each soul answering only for its own actions - so justice is preserved without the loss of any righteous act
     - Justice
         - Giving everyone what they deserve (ie. what they have a right to experience, based on how well they fulfilled their responsibilities).
         - More specifically: we only sin against ourselves, and we are only righteous to our own benefit in the same way. 
             - We are the only ones affected by our own actions, as God is neither benefited nor harmed by our deeds.
             - No soul bears the burden of another: we are held accountable only for our own actions.
             - All actions are accounted for, no matter how small.
             - Justice is two-sided and proportional: righteousness is rewarded, while sin is punished.
         - Forgiveness
         - When the punishment for a sin is removed.
         - This can only occur when sincere repentance and restitution has occurred (to the best of ability), or when the weight of a superfluous righteous deed is heavier than the weight (or lack thereof) of the sin.
         - Since God is the Just, and He is the only one who truly knows the weight of our own deeds, then He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills - without dismissing or causing our righteous actions to go to waste.
      - Love/Mercy
         - God is the all-merciful, the ever-merciful: His mercy (a one-sided form of love) encompasses all things, opening the path to forgiveness (ie. delaying punishment) before opening the path to punishment. He also reserves a special, two-sided love for those who enter Paradise.
         - The path to forgiveness is open for as long as we're alive: good deeds are multiplied, punishment is delayed, and even the smallest of good deeds have the potential to outweigh bad deeds - all out of God's mercy.

2. What is the source of the fundamental, theological difference in soteriology?
 - Christianity
     - Adam earned the debt of original sin against an infinite god - making it impossible for a finite mankind to redeem themselves - which is why God gifted us with Jesus Christ (the perfect, divine man) as our Redeemer
     - Adam's Fall from Grace
         - God created Adam and Eve in original perfection, holiness, and justice - in perfect obedience and friendship with God, and in perfect harmony with God's creation.
             - They enjoyed perfect bliss, with Adam assigning names to everything - including his wife.
         - The were given one commandment - not to eat from the Tree of the Knowledge Good and Evil - yet they succumbed to the serpent's temptation (with Eve tempting Adam) and ate from it, freely choosing to disobey God.
             - And when they were caught, they instantly started blaming each other.
         - The fundamental sin is that they preferred self-rule over God's rule - offending against the infinite God - thus earning them an infinite debt.
             - In that act, they earned "original sin": the loss of their original sanctifying grace (the life of God in the soul) and rupturing the friendship with God that sustained their original state of perfect holiness.
         - This choice has long-term consequences, affecting the state of all of their descendants (ie. they will be born in this broken state, lacking what Adam lost).
             - The Fall is also considered a "happy fault" (felix culpa), due to the gifts God continually bestows upon us - despite our broken nature - ultimately culminating in sending Jesus Christ as our Redeemer.
     - Consequences of the Fall
         - God swiftly and justly judges them for their disobedience, expelling them from Eden to Earth and punishing them with suffering, toil, ignorance, and concupiscence (the inclination to sin, even after baptism).
             - The main consequence is the earning of "death": the spiritual separation from God, with bodily death also entering human history (as well as the risk of permanent "death" in the Hereafter).
         - This is why we can't "save" ourselves: there is no act of righteousness nor act of repentance that we can do to fully redeem ourselves from our sins.
             - Finite, limited creatures can't fully satisfy an offense against an infinite God.
         - This is why God gifted us with Jesus Christ - a redemption we didn't deserve - a "second Adam" who perfectly fulfilled God's covenants (unlike the first Adam) and defeated death (the consequence of Adam's sin) by means of his resurrection.
 - Islam
     - Adam slipped in his obedience to God, immediately repented, and was forgiven by God; an experience meant to teach him - and all mankind - of what his relationship with God is, what his purpose in life is, and who his enemy is
     - Adam's Slip and Forgiveness
         - God intends for mankind to be placed on Earth as vicegerents (ie. caretakers, representatives of God).
             - The angels question this, mentioning that they already worship God perfectly while mankind will only cause corruption - to which God replies that he knows something they don't know.
         - God then created Adam with honor, innocence, and a natural inclination to God (ie. the fitrah), teaching him the names of all things and placing him in a blissful garden.
             - All of creation was subjugated to them, including the angels - except for Satan, who was arrogant and racially envious of how God honored Adam.
         - They were given one commandment - to not eat from a particular tree - and a warning that Satan was their enemy, yet they slipped in their obedience to God by Satan's temptation and willingly ate from the tree.
             - Satan lied to them, marketing the tree as the tree of life and an eternal kingdom.
         - They were remorseful, and God taught them how to seek repentance - to which they sincerely repented to God.
     - Consequences of the Slip
         - God accepted their repentance and forgave them - for God is the Oft-Forgiving, the All-Merciful.
             - Their mistake is described as a "slip" for this reason: they were in perfect obedience to God for some time, momentarily broke that obedience, and upon realizing, sought repentance and God's forgiveness.
             - This is what the angels neglected to see - mankind's positive attributes and willful inclination towards God.
         - God then informs them of His plan: for them to live temporarily on Earth - where it was already prepared for them, rich with blessings from a bountiful Lord - in which they will be tested in their actions for their ultimate fate.
             - Those who resist Satan's temptation, love and worship God alone, and pursue truth and righteousness will enter a blissful Paradise by His mercy and earning His two-sided love; and those who disbelieve and succumb to his temptation will be admitted into Hell - a wicked destination.
         - This is why every Prophet and Messenger came with the same message: to worship God alone, pursue truth and righteousness, and to abstain from the corruptions that were prevalent at their times.
             - This is what "Islam" and "Muslim" mean: to willingly submit to God's will against one's own desires - to resist worldly temptation and to have faith in God and the hereafter and to do good works.
 - Examples
     - Christianity
         - The creation of Adam and Eve (Genesis 1:26-31; 2:7-25).
         - God's command to Adam, the serpent's temptation, and their disobedience (Genesis 2:16-17; 3:1-6).
         - Adam and Eve's excuses, God's judgement, and their expulsion from the garden (Genesis 3:7-25).
         - "For there is no one who does not sin." (1 Kings 8:46, 2 Chronicles 6:36)
         - "Surely I was sinful at birth / sinful from the time my mother conceived me." (Psalm 51:5)
         - "Know that a person is not justified by the works of the law, but by faith in Jesus Christ." (Galatians 2:16)
     - Islam
         - God's purpose of mankind on Earth, the angels' question, and teaching Adam the names (Quran 2:30-33).
         - God honoring mankind and subjugating the angels (Quran 2:34, 17:70, 20:116, 49:13).
         - Satan's arrogance, temptation, and God's warning (Quran 2:34-36, 7:12-18,20-22, 18:50, 20:117,120).
         - Adam's slip, repentance, and God's response in teaching & forgiveness (Quran 2:36-69, 7:22-27, 20:117-124).
         - "We have indeed made whatever is on earth as an adornment ... ˹He is the One˺ Who created death and life in order to test which of you is best in deeds. And He is the Almighty, All-Forgiving." (Quran 18:7, 67:2)



3. Which salvific narrative better fits reality?

 - 3.1. What status was Adam created in? Or, why was Adam created to begin with?
     - Christianity
         - In a state of original perfection and holiness, being made in the image of God.
         - Adam was formed from the ground, made in the image and likeness of God and given the breath of life, and was placed in Eden, being tasked with its till/keep, and given the dominion over all living creatures. This is interpreted as being created in a state of original, perfect holiness and justice (elevated friendship and communion with God).
     - Islam
         - In a state of honor and naturally inclined towards God, whose purpose is to be vicegerents on Earth.
         - Adam was formed from the Earth and God's spirit in an honored state, being instilled with the fitrah (natural inclination towards God) and created for him and his progeny to be vicegerents (someone representing and exercising delegated power on behalf of a sovereign/ruler) on Earth. God initially places Adam in Eden to enjoy a time of bliss.
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Perfect holiness and communion with God, made to be in Heaven" vs. "Honor and natural inclination towards God, made to be tested on Earth"

 - 3.2. What did God provide Adam with?
     - Christianity
         - Preternatural gifts, dominion, a companion, and responsibility.
         - Adam was given a garden with an abundance of accessible food and dominion over all living creatures, having the authority to name the animals himself. He was also given Eve as a companion, and the responsibility to till and keep the garden (a task that lacked toil and pain). This is interpreted as God giving Adam sanctifying grace (friendship with God), infused knowledge with preternatural gifts (ie. rightly ordered passions, freedom from suffering, immortality), and the inscription of the natural law (a rightly ordered conscience) on his heart.
     - Islam
         - Knowledge, elevated honor above the angels, a companion, a blissful life in Eden, and a warning them of Satan and how they can be expelled.
         - Before placing Adam in Eden, God created Eve as a spouse, instilled the fitrah in him, and taught him the names of all things - knowledge the angels didn't have - and elevated him over them by commanding the angels to prostrate to him. Then, God places him in Eden, promising that he would experience bliss without hunger, thirst, scorching heat, or feeling naked or otherwise vulnerable - and with a clear warning about Satan's enmity and his desire to expel them from the garden.
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Dominion, friendship with God, blissful responsibility, infused knowledge, immortality, and rightly ordered conscience" vs. "Bliss, elevated honor, taught knowledge of reality, and warning of Satan"

 - 3.3. What was Eden's purpose? Why did it come with a prohibition?
     - Christianity
         - A place of perfect communion, where the prohibition serves as a test of filial trust.
         - Eden was a dwelling place for man to till and keep (as a priestly responsibility) that is pleasant to the sight and good for food. God's only prohibition was from them eating from the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and  Evil, for doing so will cause them to die. This is interpreted as Eden being a holy place of perfect communion with God, where Adam is given free will and a moral test of filial trust (ie. assurance in God's love) and obedience. The choice to autonomously disobey God would then result in the loss of that sanctifying grace.
     - Islam
         - A blissful dwelling place, where the prohibition serves as a study guide for their purpose on Earth.
         - Eden was a dwelling place for Adam and Eve to experience bliss, ease, and to eat from whatever they wished - neither experiencing hunger, thirst, scorching heat, nor feeling naked or otherwise vulnerable. They were only prohibited from eating from a certain tree (its textual significance only in God's selection of it, in which Satan deceives them into thinking that it grants immortality and an everlasting kingdom), as well as being warned of Satan's enmity. This is interpreted as a training ground for Adam to learn what his relationship with God is, what his purpose in life is, and who his enemy is.
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Permanent home of perfected communion, with the prohibition as a test/indicator of trust" vs. "Temporary home as a study guide for the Earthly test, with the prohibition as a lesson"

 - 3.4. What changed about Adam's state when he sinned?
     - Christianity
         - Loss of innocence, sanctifying grace, and preternatural gifts.
         - Adam's eyes were opened to his nakedness, and he covered himself out of shame. His sin resulted in his expulsion from Eden, which  is interpreted as a rupture of communion: Adam effectively loses sanctifying grace, original justice/holiness, and the preternatural gifts. Human nature also becomes wounded as Adam's sin affects his progeny, where there is now a liability to suffering and death beyond Eden.
     - Islam
         - Loss of ignorance, and gaining of knowledge of his need for God's mercy.
         - Adam's nakedness became apparent, and he covered himself out of shame. His sin (a slip within the greater context of persistent obedience) brought him out of his blissfully ignorant state - now fully understanding the consequences of his sins and his need for God's mercy, forgiveness, and grace.
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Loss of innocence and sanctifying grace; ontological wounding of human nature" vs. "Loss of innocence and blissful ignorance; epistemological discovery of sin's consequences and need for God's mercy"

 - 3.5. What was God's immediate response to Adam's sin?
     - Christianity
         - God gives him fair, judicial curses, clothes him out of his mercy, and expels him from Eden.
         - God questions Adam - causing his confession. Then, He pronounces judicial curses upon Adam, Eve, and the serpent that deceived them, covers them in proper clothing, and promptly expels them from Eden - barring it with a flaming sword to prevent them from returning (to return to immortality). The clothing is interpreted as a mercy within the judgement, and the judgements are interpreted to be a fair and just response, in order to uphold the moral order by protecting humanity from perpetuating a disordered state.
     - Islam
         - God reminds Adam of his enemy, teaches him how to repent, and accepts his repentance.
         - God questions Adam - causing his confession. Then, God reminds Adam of Satan's enmity, tenderly teaches him how to repent for his sins, and turns towards him - accepting his repentance and forgiving him. He then informs Adam, Eve, and Satan of their temporary stay on Earth (where their livelihood was specifically prepared for them out of God's mercy) where their true test of faith will begin - with Satan as their enemy, and God as their ever-near merciful guidance, presence, and comforter.
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Judicial curses, clothing out of mercy, and expulsion from Eden" vs. "Reminder of Satan's enmity, teaching of repentance, forgiveness out of mercy, and descent from Eden"

 - 3.6. Why wasn't Adam placed back into Eden? Or - was Adam actually forgiven in that moment, or otherwise saved or pardoned from his sin's consequences?
     - Christianity
         - No, or not explicitly - forgiveness comes later as a result of Jesus.
         - God's warning to Adam was that, if the tree was eaten from, then he would surely die - which is precisely the world we live in today, where the end of each person is death. This means that Adam was not forgiven, since the consequences of his sin were not removed. However, the real risk is spiritual death (in which we die without proper faith in God), but we are saved from this out of God's mercy in Jesus fulfilling that burden.
     - Islam
         - Yes - the descent was not a punishment, but the next step in God's plan.
         - God's warned Adam that Satan would try to expel them from Eden, which did occur - but this was a part of God's plan from the beginning (once Adam was taught who his enemy was and how to repent), and the text explicitly states that Adam was forgiven immediately after repenting to God anyway. Satan was similarly banished from Eden, but God cursed him instead (as instead of repenting, he persisted in arrogance).
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Adam wasn't forgiven from the temporal consequences, but saved from eternal, spiritual death (because of Jesus)" vs. "Adam was fully forgiven and was placed on Earth after learning the requisite knowledge to be successful"

 - 3.7. What state are Adam's descendants born into?
     - Christianity
         - Same as Adam after the Fall: in a state of original/ancestral sin.
         - Because Adam's Fall marks all humanity, we're all born in the same post-Fall condition that he was in: being placed on Earth (outside of Eden) and under the same judicial curses (mortality, cursed ground, pain and suffering, a world marked by sin and death). This is interpreted as us being born into a state of "original sin" or "ancestral sin" - inheriting the same consequences given to Adam.
     - Islam
         - Same as Adam after the Fall: in a state of original forgiveness.
         - We're all born in the same post-Fall condition that Adam was in: being placed on Earth (outside of Eden) and under the same test that was intended for all humanity. No soul bears the burden of another, so each person (including Adam) begins life with the fitrah - a pure, natural disposition towards God - only inheriting what was learned because of the descent (our needed relationship with God, with Satan as our enemy).
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Just like Adam: inheriting original/ancestral sin" vs. "Just like Adam: born pure, innocent, and being naturally inclined towards God"

 - 3.8. On what basis are Adam and his descendants ultimately saved from their sins?
     - Christianity
         - God's grace through the atoning death and resurrection of Christ - who reconciled humanity's penalty of sin and allowing our forgiveness - and our faith in that.
         - Our forgiveness and salvation from ultimately come on the basis of God's grace through Jesus Christ, who died for our sins and defeated death (ie. the penalty of sin for humanity) through his resurrection. This act applies even to all who lived before Jesus (including Adam), and those who live after receive that grace only through faith and the fulfilment of the sacraments. 
     - Islam
         - God's mercy through each person's faith, righteousness, and repentance.
         - Our forgiveness and salvation ultimately comes on the basis of God's mercy and promise: for any one of us who repents, believes in God, and does good, God promises to forgive and pardon their bad deeds - for He is the Oft-Forgiving, Ever-Merciful. This applies to everyone who's ever lived, from Adam until now.
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Belief in Christ as our Savior from our sins" vs. "Belief in God and His all-encompassing mercy"

 - 3.9. Under God's justice, which is greater/heavier: sin or righteousness/repentance?
     - Christianity
         - Sin > righteousness/repentance.
         - God is perfectly just: the wages of sin is death, and there is no forgiveness of sins without shedding blood (ie. some punishment or sacrifice). Because sanctifying grace is a participation in God's own life, it cannot be recreated or restored by our finite efforts in righteousness and repentance; only a perfect being can restore it (which occurs through Christ's perfect righteousness and vicarious atonement, whose resurrection defeated the penalty of death on our behalf). This is why no amount of righteousness or repentance can undo, revert, or otherwise pardon the guilt and penalty of even a single sin. This is also why Adam's sin (and ours) wasn't temporally forgiven - for its natural consequence is ongoing earthly toil, suffering, and separation from perfect communion with God - and how we're all able to be saved due to Christ's redeeming grace.
     - Islam
         - Righteousness/repentance > sin.
         - God is the Just: every action, no matter how big or small, will be taken into account, and no bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another. At the same time God is also the Oft-Forgiving, Most-Loving: we're held accountable only to our knowledge and intentions, and the path to forgiveness is opened before the path to punishment. As a result, God values righteousness and repentance over mistakes: good deeds are multiplied out of His mercy (while bad deeds are recorded as they are), sincere repentance erases previous sins (and can even turn them into good deeds), and lasting deeds of righteousness outweigh isolated slips. This is why Adam's sin was only a slip in God's eyes - a momentary lapse of judgement between consistent obedience and sincere repentance (all willfully done) - and why he was forgiven once he turned back to God (which is the same for us as well).
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Sin > righteousness/repentance" vs. "Righteousness/repentance > sin"

 - 3.10. How is God's mercy revealed within His justice?
     - Christianity
         - Sin must be punished, but God willingly took its burden for our sake.
         - God expects perfect obedience to His will, which is why any sin earns death and separation from God - and because God's justice is perfect, the debt of every sin must be satisfied. But since every sin is against an infinite God, its weight is far beyond our finite capacity to repay by our own righteousness or repentance.
         - Yet, out of His love and desire for our salvation (and despite us being unworthy of it), God reveals His mercy by sending Christ - who lived sinlessly, willingly bore the penalty for our sins on our behalf (ie. death), and defeated it through his resurrection. In this way, both perfect justice and mercy are expressed: sin is truly punished, but its punishment is willingly borne by Christ instead of falling on us - allowing forgiveness to occur without compromising justice. As a result, our righteousness and repentance do not pay for sin, but follow as participation in Christ's perfection - with the eligibility for that mercy starting with our faith and trust in what he has done for us.
     - Islam
         - Righteousness is rewarded while sin is punished, but God forgives before punishing.
         - God expects willing worship and obedience expressed through faith, repentance, and righteousness within one's real capacity (which is known and  determined by God for each person). As such, every person is held fully accountable only to their own deeds, with all actions personally owned and proportionally weighed under God's Justice: righteousness is rewarded and sin is punished.
         - Yet, out of His love and mercy, God opens the path to forgiveness before punishment by delaying penalty, multiplying good deeds, recording sins only as they are, and erasing (even transforming) sins through sincere repentance and restitution to the best of one’s ability. In this way, both perfect justice and mercy are expressed: each person receives exactly what they are accountable for, while God does not dismiss or waste the sincere righteous actions of believers, and sincere repentance is accepted first and remains a direct doorway to forgiveness.
     - Fundamental Difference
         - "Sins are punished; forgiveness occurs after/by means of Christ willingly bearing the punishment on our behalf" vs. "Righteousness is rewarded while sins are punished; forgiveness occurs before/instead of punishment"

 - 3.11. What do the differences reveal about God and His justice and mercy?
     - A. Both require God's mercy, but:
         - Christianity
             - Justice requires sin to be satisfied by punishment or sacrifice before forgiveness can occur.
         - Islam
             - Justice requires surplus righteousness or sincere repentance before forgiveness can occur.
         - Fundamental Difference
             - "Punishment always occurs" vs. "Forgiveness waives punishment"
     - B. As a result of God's justice:
         - Christianity
             - Sin incurs a debt greater than the merit of any righteousness and repentance a person can do (for any sin is against the infinite God).
         - Islam
             - Sincere righteousness and repentance is rewarded and multiplied (by God's mercy) - unlike sins, which are recorded as is.
         - Fundamental Difference
             - "Mercy is not embedded in the law" vs. "Mercy is embedded in the law"
     - C. As a result of God's mercy:
         - Christianity
             - Jesus Christ - God in the human Person - willingly bore sin's debt for us (out of his love).
         - Islam
             - God delays punishment to give us abundant opportunities to be forgiven first - with sincere repentance able to erase and transform sins.
         - Fundamental Difference
             - "God bore sin's penalty for us" vs. "We bear both our righteousness (including repentance) and sins"
     - D. As a result of what God holds us accountable to:
         - Christianity
             - We can't satisfy the debt of sin ourselves, so our salvation ultimately depends on God's grace in Christ's atonement and our faith in him to be justified/eligible for that grace.
             - Once faith in Christ is present, righteousness (with confession) is still required to participate in God's grace - but it is not why we are saved.
         - Islam
             - Our actions are personally and proportionally weighed, where our salvation depends on our faith, righteousness, and repentance - within the capacity God decreed for each of us.
             - With faith in God, He accepts our sincere repentance, erases our sins, and multiplies our good deeds - thus waiving the punishment.
         - Fundamental Difference
             - "Salvation is grounded in Christ's righteousness, not our own" vs. "Salvation is grounded in the weighing of our own righteousness (including faith) and repentance against our sins"

 - 3.12. Which salvific narrative better fits reality?
     A. "Our actions are a sign of our moral values."
         - This ties moral evaluation to what we do, not what we claim (or feel) - the basis for accountability and justice.
         - Christianity
             - Actions are a participation in God's grace and perfection.
             - Faith by itself, if it has no works, is dead - even demons believe in God. As such, righteousness is the necessary fruit and evidence of faith: if it is missing, then it reveals a necessary deficiency in faith. This makes it a strong fit with this inference.
         - Islam
             - Actions are the external expression of one's internal submission to God.
             - The requirement for God's forgiveness is faith and righteousness, such that the first people condemned by God are the hypocrites who claim to believe in Him but don't do so in their actions. This makes it a strong fit with this inference.
         - Fundamental Difference
             - "Righteousness is a sign of inner faith (strong fit)" vs. "Righteousness is a sign of inner faith (strong fit)"
     B. "We have a disposition towards concepts of justice."
         - This expects people to be held accountable to their choices, where the guilty bear a proportional cost - and the innocent are not punished.
         - Christianity
             - Justice is debt-centric: sin creates a spiritual debt that must be satisfied - such that it can be paid for by an eligible substitute.
             - Sin causes debt, but an eligible innocent can pay it instead of the guilty. This means that the essence of justice is settlement (with gratitude & love), not accountability - a weak fit with this inference.
         - Islam
             - Justice is accountability-centric: the debt of sin (and reward of righteousness) is borne only by the guilty.
             - Every soul bears its own burden - accountable only to their choices: those guilty of sin will receive its due, and those guilty of righteousness will receive its due - a strong fit with this inference.
         - Fundamental Difference
             - "Justice is about debt (weak fit)" vs. "Justice is about credit (strong fit)"

     C. "Falsehood is easier and more common, while good is harder and more valued."
         - This comes from the observation that righteousness (like order) requires personal maintenance - while sin (like disorder) requires personal neglect.
         - Christianity
             - Human righteousness is harder, but it cannot outweigh human sin.
             - Sin causes our ineligibility for salvation, while good can't restore it - but Christ's perfection can. This means that the essence of good's value is restricted to God's goodness, not the effort it requires - making it a partial fit with this inference.
         - Islam
             - Human righteousness is harder, therefore it outweighs human sin.
             - Evil is easier and more common, but good is weighted more strongly in judgement - precisely because God embeds His mercy in the judgement and values more the hard path and sincere return. This makes it a strong fit with this inference.
         - Fundamental Difference
             - "The effort for righteousness is not a basis for its value, out of God's mercy (partial fit)" vs. "The effort for righteousness is a basis for its value, out of God's mercy (strong fit)"
     D. 
         - Conclusion
             - Islam: God accomplishes everything the Christian Jesus is claimed to accomplish, but starting from the time of Adam and with a more perfect form of accountability - allowing a stronger case for coherence to be built.
             - At its root, the fundamental difference between the two narratives is how God’s justice is oriented (debt vs. credit) - and the basis for salvation follows from that.
             - For Christianity’s justice, all sins must be paid for. The core "debt of sin" (loss of sanctifying grace) sits in a higher tier than any act of human righteousness: no amount of good deeds can recreate that grace - only participating in it (if it is restored by God). Out of His mercy, God Himself satisfies this debt on our behalf through Christ - and while this certainly exposes God's love for us, it also leads to concepts such as an innocent bearing the burden of the guilty (both in Adam’s descendants sharing the debt of his act and in Christ satisfying the debt for us), sin’s debt being valued in a higher category than righteousness’ credit, and God’s treatment of Adam after his sin being different than Christ's (who is God in the human Person) treatment of sincere believers after their sins.
             - Meanwhile, Islam’s justice holds each person fully accountable for their own actions - crediting both sins and good deeds - while God, out of His mercy, values sincere righteousness and repentance more than our sins. God's relationship with Adam is the same as His relationship with us today: Adam sins, repents, and is forgiven, and is placed on earth fully forgiven and pure while being judged only for his own choices. As a result, this narrative does not accept concepts such as an innocent bearing the burden of the guilty (which would be unjust) or sin being valued more than righteousness (which would contradict God’s mercy and align with Satan’s desire for us to despair of it).
             - Therefore: even though both narratives affirm faith, righteousness, and God's justice and mercy/love, Islam's narrative cleanly fits our natural disposition for personal accountability while Christianity's narrative leads to concepts that don't cleanly fit. Moreover, Islam's narrative is more consistent: God treats Adam the same way He treats us - with original forgiveness and mercy. As a result, Islam better fits reality.
         - Fundamental Difference
             - "Partial accountability (weak fit)" vs. "Perfect accountability (strong fit)"
