1. What is the relationship between God's mercy/love, justice, and forgiveness (soteriology)?
 - Christianity
     - Out of God's love for us, He opens the path to forgiveness by personally satisfying the debt of our sins, saving us from a burden we can't repay without sacrificing justice.
     - Justice
         - Giving everyone what they deserve (ie. what they have a right to experience, based on how well they fulfilled their responsibilities).
         - More specifically: the debt of sin must be satisfied with a punishment - proportional to the sin's weight. And considering that every sin is against an infinite God, the weight of each sin is fundamentally beyond our limited capacity to pay.
     - Forgiveness
         - When the punishment for a sin is removed.
         - This can only occur after the debt is satisfied (ie. the punishment is experienced) - either when the offender experiences it himself, or if someone else who's qualified willingly intercedes and experiences it on their behalf (ie. vicarious satisfaction).
         - As a result, God can't "simply" forgive (ie. waiving punishment) - that would be unjust, as the cost from our sins is still not being paid for.
     - Love/Mercy
         - God is love itself: the very essence of affectionately desiring our salvation and happiness.
         - This is ultimately manifested in the Atonement, in which Jesus (the filiation - Person of the Son) personally satisfied humanity's debt through his resurrection (by defeating death - the ultimate result of Adam and Eve's original sin) - changing the fundamental salvific requirement from "impossibly perfect righteousness and repentance" to "faith in Jesus Christ" (ie. belief and trust in his divinity).
 - Islam
     - Out of God’s mercy, He opens the path to forgiveness through sincere repentance and good deeds - each soul answering only for its own actions - so justice is preserved without the loss of any righteous act
     - Justice
         - Giving everyone what they deserve (ie. what they have a right to experience, based on how well they fulfilled their responsibilities).
         - More specifically: we only sin against ourselves, and we are only righteous to our own benefit in the same way. 
             - We are the only ones affected by our own actions, as God is neither benefited nor harmed by our deeds.
             - No soul bears the burden of another: we are only held accountable for our own actions.
             - All actions are accounted for, no matter how small.
             - Justice is two-sided and proportional: righteousness is rewarded, while sin is punished.
         - Forgiveness
         - When the punishment for a sin is removed.
         - This can only occur when sincere repentance and restitution has occurred (to the best of ability), or when the weight of a superfluous righteous deed is heavier than the weight (or lack thereof) of the sin.
         - Since God is the Just, and He is the only one who truly knows the weight of our own deeds, then He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills - without dismissing or causing our righteous actions to go to waste.
      - Love/Mercy
         - God is the all-merciful, the ever-merciful: His mercy (a one-sided form of love) encompasses all things, opening the path to forgiveness (ie. delaying punishment) before opening the path to punishment. He also reserves a special, two-sided love for those who enter Paradise.
         - The path to forgiveness is open for as long as we're alive: good deeds are multiplied, punishment is delayed, and even the smallest of good deeds have the potential to outweigh bad deeds - all out of God's mercy,

2. What is the source of the fundamental, theological difference in soteriology?
 - Christianity
     - Adam earned the debt of original sin against an infinite god - making it impossible for a finite mankind to redeem themselves - which is why God gifted us with Jesus Christ (the perfect, divine man) as our Redeemer
     - Adam's Fall from Grace
         - God created Adam and Eve in original perfection, holiness, and justice - in perfect obedience and friendship with God, and in perfect harmony with God's creation.
             - They enjoyed perfect bliss, with Adam assigning names to everything - including his wife.
         - The were given one commandment - not to eat from the Tree of the Knowledge Good and Evil - yet they succumbed to the serpent's temptation (with Eve tempting Adam) and ate from it, freely choosing to disobey God.
             - And when they were caught, they instantly started blaming each other.
         - The fundamental sin is that they preferred self-rule over God's rule - offending against the infinite God - thus earning them an infinite debt.
             - In that act, they earned "original sin": the loss of their original sanctifying grace (the life of God in the soul) and rupturing the friendship with God that sustained their original state of perfect holiness.
         - This choice has long-term consequences, affecting the state of all of their descendants (ie. they will be born in this broken state, lacking what Adam lost).
             - The Fall is also considered a "happy fault" (felix culpa), due to the gifts God continually bestows upon us - despite our broken nature - ultimately culminating in sending Jesus Christ as our Redeemer.
     - Consequences of the Fall
         - God swiftly and justly judges them for their disobedience, expelling them from Eden to Earth and punishing them with suffering, toil, ignorance, and concupiscence (the inclination to sin, even after baptism).
             - The main consequence is the earning of "death": the spiritual separation from God, with bodily death also entering human history (as well as the risk of permanent "death" in the Hereafter).
         - This is why we can't "save" ourselves: there is no act of righteousness nor act of repentance that we can do to fully redeem ourselves from our sins.
             - Finite creatures can't make full satisfaction for an offense against an infinite God.
         - This is why God gifted us with Jesus Christ - a redemption we didn't deserve - a "second Adam" who perfectly fulfilled God's covenants (unlike the first Adam) and defeated death (the consequence of Adam's sin) by means of his resurrection.
 - Islam
     - Adam slipped in his obedience to God, immediately repented, and was forgiven by God; an experience meant to teach him - and all mankind - of what his relationship with God is, what his purpose in life is, and who his enemy is
     - Adam's Slip and Forgiveness
         - God intends for mankind to be placed on Earth as vicegerents (ie. caretakers, representatives of God).
             - The angels question this, mentioning that they already worship God perfectly while mankind will only cause corruption - to which God replies that he knows something they don't know.
         - God then created Adam with honor, innocence, and a natural inclination to God (ie. the fitrah), teaching him the names of all things and placing him in a blissful garden.
             - All of creation was subjugated to them, including the angels - except for Satan, who was arrogant and racially envious of how God honored Adam.
         - They were given one commandment - to not eat from a particular tree - and a warning that Satan was their enemy, yet they slipped in their obedience to God by Satan's temptation and willingly ate from the tree.
             - Satan lied to them, marketing the tree as the tree of life and an eternal kingdom.
         - They were remorseful, and God taught them how to seek repentance - to which they sincerely repented to God.
     - Consequences of the Slip
         - God accepted their repentance and forgave them - for God is the Oft-Forgiving, the All-Merciful.
             - Their mistake is described as a "slip" for this reason: they were in perfect obedience to God for some time, momentarily broke that obedience, and upon realizing, sought repentance and God's forgiveness.
             - This is what the angels neglected to see - mankind's positive attributes and willful inclination towards God.
         - God then informs them of His plan: for them to live temporarily on Earth - where it was already prepared for them - in which they will be tested in their actions for their ultimate fate.
             - Those who resist Satan's temptation, love and worship God alone, and pursue truth and righteousness will enter a blissful Paradise by His mercy and earning His two-sided love; and those who disbelieve and succumb to his temptation will be admitted into Hell - a wicked destination.
         - This is why every Prophet and Messenger came with the same message: to worship God alone, pursue truth and righteousness, and to abstain from the corruptions that were prevalent at their times.
             - This is what "Islam" and "Muslim" mean: to willingly submit to God's will against our own desires - to resist worldly temptation and to have faith in God and the hereafter and to do good works.
