# LDS Addition — Development Plan

## What I read in the project

The project is a hierarchical "sieve of truth" with eight numbered files (0–8) plus appendices. After the **God-definition tier** (File 4, "Defining God"), the comparative work happens across four downstream files:

- **File 5** — *Religious Comparison: Alignment with Reality.* Compares four worldview families (Atheism, Polytheism, Trinitarianism, Monotheism) against ~13 inferences from reality, each line answered Yes / Maybe / No with justification, then a per-worldview conclusion.
- **File 6** — *Historical Comparison: New Testament vs. Quran.* Seven subsections: (1) textual/historical claim, (2) oral/written/archaeological presence, (3) chains of transmission, (4) alignment with historical context, (5) resistance to fabrication, (6) summary, (7) conclusion.
- **File 7** — *Non-Fundamental Comparison: Christianity vs. Islam.* Seven subsections: (1) apparent similarities/differences across God / Morality & Salvation / Jesus, (2) soteriology, (3) source of the soteriological difference (Adam narratives), (4) which salvific narrative fits reality (twelve sub-questions, 4.1–4.12), (5) which looks like a corrupted version, (6) standouts, (7) conclusion.
- **File 8** — *Results and Conclusion.* Sieve of Truth, Cost of Disagreement, final synthesis.

The shape of an "entry" for a given faith therefore touches all four downstream files. The plan below adds LDS as a new entry, picking up *after* the God-definition for LDS is already in place, and going phase-by-phase to the project's conclusion. Each phase is sized to fit in one prompt's worth of work.

## Architectural notes (decide as we go, don't decide now)

- **Where LDS slots into File 5.** LDS theology is neither classical Trinitarianism nor strict Monotheism — the Godhead is three separate beings, the Father has a body, multiple gods exist, exaltation makes humans into gods. It might be added as its own fifth column, or argued to belong inside Polytheism (or a "Henotheism" subcategory). We can land on this in Phase 1.
- **File 6 was scoped to NT vs Quran.** Adding LDS means either widening File 6 to a three-way comparison or branching a new comparison once Trinitarianism is eliminated (since LDS positions itself as restoring lost Christian truth via additional revelation). We can pick the cleaner option once we see how LDS lands in Phase 1.
- **Which LDS scripture to assess.** Book of Mormon, Doctrine & Covenants, and Pearl of Great Price each have different historical claims. Phase 2 will scope this.

## The phases

**Phase 1 — Section 5: LDS Alignment with Reality**
Add LDS to the "Systems to Compare" list, then walk through each of the ~13 inference rows already in File 5 (inferences 2, 4, 9+11, 13+14, 16, 21, 23, 25, 27, 28+29, 33, 36, 37, 38) and add an LDS answer with justification. Close with an LDS entry in the per-worldview conclusion.

**Phase 2 — Section 6.1–6.2: LDS Textual Claim + Presence**
Define the LDS scriptural claim (BoM, D&C, PoGP), check ontological/epistemological feasibility, and set success/failure criteria. Then cover eyewitnesses (Joseph Smith, Three Witnesses, Eight Witnesses), the cultural context for transmission, when texts were written and standardized, and archaeological corroboration.

**Phase 3 — Section 6.3–6.4: LDS Chains + Historical Context**
Map LDS chains of transmission (Smith → witnesses → scribes → modern canon, plus revisions across editions), and assess how the scriptures' historical claims line up with the historical record (ancient American civilizations, Reformed Egyptian, the Book of Abraham papyri, archaeology of named sites).

**Phase 4 — Section 6.5: LDS Resistance to Fabrication**
The heaviest subsection. Test alternative naturalistic explanations, the witnesses' credibility and later trajectories, KJV-language parallels, doctrinal evolution between editions, and the patterns we'd expect a fabrication to display vs. those we'd expect divine revelation to display.

**Phase 5 — Section 6.6–6.7: LDS Historical Summary + Conclusion**
Summarize and write the conclusion for whether LDS scripture clears the historical filter.

**Phase 6 — Section 7.1: LDS Apparent Similarities/Differences**
Add LDS to subsection 1's three blocks: A. God (i. One, ii. Merciful & loving, iii. Just/Judge), B. Morality & Salvation (i. Moral agency, ii. Sin/forgiveness, iii. No earning by merit alone), C. Jesus.

**Phase 7 — Section 7.2–7.3: LDS Soteriology + Adam Narrative**
Lay out the LDS view of mercy/justice/forgiveness/atonement (including exaltation and eternal progression), and the LDS Adam narrative — particularly the "fortunate fall" reading ("Adam fell that men might be").

**Phase 8 — Section 7.4: LDS Salvific Narrative vs Reality**
Walk through 4.1–4.12 with the LDS view (status Adam was created in, what God provided, Eden's purpose, what changed at the Fall, God's response, return to Eden, state of descendants, basis of salvation, sin vs righteousness weighting, mercy within justice, what differences reveal, which fits reality).

**Phase 9 — Section 7.5: LDS as Corruption Comparison**
Test LDS against the corruption pattern: assuming mainstream Christianity (or Islam) is true, what changes does LDS introduce, and do they match the deceptive-corruption profile (mystery-as-contradiction, comfort-as-truth, etc.)?

**Phase 10 — Section 7.6–7.7: LDS Standouts + Theology Conclusion**
Note what stands out about LDS scripture and organization (priesthood structure, temples, living prophets, exaltation, eternal families), then write the theological-comparison conclusion.

**Phase 11 — Section 8: Update Results & Conclusion**
Integrate LDS into the Results summary, the Sieve of Truth, the Cost of Disagreement, and the final Conclusion to reflect where LDS lands in the sieve.
