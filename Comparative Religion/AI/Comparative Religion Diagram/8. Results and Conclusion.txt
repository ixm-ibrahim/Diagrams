-------
Results
-------

This project began with a simple question: How can we distinguish between truth and falsehood?
 
Not "what do I prefer" or "what comforts me" or even "what affirms my beliefs", but identifying what is actually true - by the same standards we use to distinguish truth anywhere else. In practical terms, we want to figure out: "given the abundance of information (where much of it false and only some of it true), what conditions are required to reliably and confidently filter falsehood from truth?"
 
This project functions as that progressive filter, where each section adds a set of necessary conditions for truth to remain distinguishable from fabrication. When any of these necessary constraints are rejected, the original question collapses - because what remains can be used to justify both truth and falsehood, making them indistinguishable (thus allowing us to validly defend false beliefs, without realizing). What follows is the path that allows us to derive the natural conclusions from narrowing these hierarchical constraints.


1. Pursuit of [Any] Truth
 - Results
     - The pursuit of truth starts with me: if I'm not willing to be corrected or recognize that my beliefs could be wrong, then I'm not really pursuing truth - I am only pursuing affirmation (comfort, loyalty, fear-management, aesthetics, tribe, etc.).
     - Truth is external to me, defined by reality (rather than preference).
 - What this Eliminates
     - Selecting (or sticking to a belief) [solely] from affirmation, whether from comfort, fear, aesthetics, cultural inheritance, tribe, or familiarity.
 - Why this Opens the Next Section
     - Once you are committed to being corrected by reality (ie. a neutral, external standard [to your preferences]), we can now define reality itself.

2. Discovering Reality
 - Results
     - We have phenomenological awareness (senses, memory, imagination, reasoning) and limitations in perception (bias, error, ignorance, forgetfulness).
     - Reality (ie. what is phenomenologically observed) presents stable patterns, allowing us to infer about reality's structure.
 - What this Eliminates
     - Separating truth/reality from awareness (ie. associating speculation with truth), allowing falsehood to be justified.
     - "Perception = truth" (given our limitations) as well as "perception = inability to arrive at truth" (given our ability to detect patterns).
 - Why this Opens the Next Section
     - Given our limitations in perception, we need to define an approach that builds confidence in the patterns we do notice - without granting falsehood the same privileges as truth.

3. Building Confidence [in Logical and Historical Claims]
 - Results
     - Claims (ie. information that are true or false) can't be justified by assuming itself - that is assertion, not justification or proof.
     - Any approach that can be used to "prove" falsehood makes truth and falsehood indistinguishable.
     - Logical confidence requires axioms grounded in reality, premises grounded in axioms, internal and external consistency, and absence of valid objections and alternative explanations.
     - Historical confidence requires eyewitnesses and archaeology, independent corroboration, traceable and unbroken chains, and strong resistance to fabrication.
 - What this Eliminates
     - Circular (ie. baseless) logic and authentication - allowing liars and fabricators to justify any false belief.
     - Confidence based on speculation (ie. ignorance) and untrustworthy (ie. unreliable) sources.
 - Why this Opens the Next Section
     - Using this approach, we can now build confidence in defining reality's source (ie. God), as well as our purpose in it (ie. religion).

4. Defining God
 - Results
     - God possesses ultimate power and intelligence, transcends time and space, and built realty by His will.
     - The True religion corresponds to God's will, distinguishes mysteries from contradictions, and requires faith supported by reason.
 - What this Eliminates
     - Any worldview that lacks this description of God, and any religion that lacks these requirements.
     - Faith without reason (allowing faith in falsehood - with no possible mechanism for detection - with the only response to valid doubt [to that falsehood] being to "believe harder").
 - Why this Opens the Next Section
     - We've made our prediction of God and the True religion - now we can identify which worldview aligns with it.

5. Alignment with Reality [Atheism, Polytheism, Trinitarianism, and Monotheism]
 - Results
     - Trinitarianism and Monotheism both align with our inferences from reality, with Monotheism aligning more clearly.
     - Trinitarianism requires superior textual authority and preservation over Monotheism to possess greater confidence, as well as an additional inference that "inferring about reality doesn't lead to spiritual truth" (but, if superior textual authority is not established, then note that this inference can be used to justify any false belief).
 - What this Eliminates
     - Atheism, which requires us to infer (contradictingly) that "speculation is proof/refutation".
     - Polytheism, whose narrative of multiple transcendent wills doesn't align with the ordered and singularly universal nature of reality.
 - Why this Opens the Next Section
     - If choosing between the two depends on historical analysis, then we need to comparatively assess the historicity of both the New Testament and Quran.

6. Historical Comparison [New Testament vs. Quran]
 - Results
     - The preservation of the New Testament requires speculation to be true (in the reliability of unknown links over other unknown links).
     - The preservation of the Quran requires speculation to be false (in unsupported possibilities over established historical constraints).
 - What this Eliminates
     - Trinitarianism, as it is not historically superior to the Quran's pure Monotheism.
 - Why this Opens the Next Section
     - If Monotheism is the only remaining possible option, then it should also stand out as the most coherent when compared with Trinitarianism.

7. Non-Fundamental Comparison [Comparative Theology]
 - Results
     - Christianity looks more like a corrupted version of Islam.
     - Islam looks more like a corrected version of Christianity.
 - What this Eliminates
     - Nothing - this only verifies previous results. Islam seems to maximize both internal and external consistency when compared to any other worldview.
 - Why this Opens the Next Section
     - The final step is to verify the project's conclusion for yourself. If you disagree with any result, you can precisely locate the filter you reject and identify why.



------------------
The Sieve of Truth
------------------

This project began with a simple question: How can we distinguish between truth and falsehood?
 
Here is the sieve we built:
 
    0. The Question: in a world where some things are true and others are false, how can we know which is which?
    1. Pursuit of Truth: truth is external to me and anchored in reality, not my preference. If I am unwilling to be corrected, then I'm not pursuing truth but my own affirmation.
    2. Discovering Reality: we observe stable patterns in reality - with limitations. Truth is neither identical to nor blocked by perception: it is found by iterative, consistent inference.
    3. Building Confidence: no claim can justify itself by assuming itself: any method that can "prove" falsehood can't be used to verify truth.
    4. Defining God: if the source of reality is defined as "God", and our purpose defined by "religion", then we can deduce:
          - God possesses ultimate power, transcendence, intelligence, awareness, and will.
          - The True religion corresponds to God's will, distinguishes mysteries from contradictions, and requires faith founded on reason.
    5. Alignment with Reality: comparing various worldviews to these inferences and deductions results in:
          - Rejection of Atheism and Polytheism
          - Possibility of Trinitarianism - only if it has superior historicity to all other candidates, and only when also inferring that "inferring about reality doesn't lead to spiritual truth".
    6. Historical Comparison: comparing the historicity of the New Testament (source of Trinitarianism) and the Quran (source of pure Monotheism) results in:
          - New Testament's historicity requires speculation to be strong.
          - Quran's historicity requires speculation to be weak.
     7. Non-Fundamental Comparison: comparing the theology of Christianity (religion of the New Testament) and Islam (religion of the Quran) results in:
          - Christianity appears more like a corruption of Islam.
          - Islam appears more like a correction of Christianity.
 
As the end of this process, the conclusion is not simply "Islam is true", but more specifically: if the goal is to distinguish truth from falsehood, and if we refuse methods that make truth indistinguishable from falsehood, and if we refuse methods that make fabrication indistinguishable from truth, then the remaining candidates must be the one that maximizes correspondence with reality, coherence, non-circular confidence, and historically constrained authority. Based on the evidence (shown previously in each section of the project), the only candidate that satisfies all this is Islam.


------------------------
The Cost of Disagreement
------------------------
 
You can disagree with the conclusion. But now you aren't forced to disagree vaguely: you can now validate any disagreement by identifying which filter you reject - and what that rejection forces you to accept instead. Below is the sieve again, but with the cost of disagreement at each step:

0. The Question
 - If you reject this:
     - You reject the relevance, importance, and/or coherence of the question itself
 - What you must commit to:
     - You reject the concepts of truth and falsehood - meaning there is no distinction between them
     - Your own argument can't be true nor false
 - Why this matters:
     - If your own argument is neither true nor false, then it isn't a logical statement, to be addressed

1. Pursuit of Truth
 - If you reject this:
     - You are saying that truth is not defined externally: "reality" is [fundamentally] subjective and not something you have to submit to, nor can anyone else decide what your "truth" is.
 - What you must commit to:
     - If the truth is not decided externally, then it must be decided internally - meaning that conflicting beliefs are equally "true" for different people.
     - You can no longer say other beliefs are "wrong" (except that you "don't like it"): they are simply living their truth, just like you.
 - Why this matters:
     - You no longer distinguish truth from falsehood; you only distinguish what you affirm from what you reject.

2. Discovering Reality
 - If you reject this:
     - Either:
         a. You are separating truth (or its accessibility) from reality and awareness.
         b. You claim that, because we can't observe all things, we can't know anything meaningfully.
 - What you must commit to:
     - Either:
         a. speculation can count as truth
         b. truth is unattainable in principle
 - Why this matters:
     - If either occurs, then a liar has the same [epistemic] authority as a truthful person: both appeal to unverifiable information, and all claims become equally undefeated.

3. Building Confidence
 - If you reject this:
     - You are equating assertion, sincerity, or strength of belief with justification and proof.
 - What you must commit to:
     - You now must accept the validity of:
         a. circular/baseless justification
         b. confidence built on ignorance and speculation
 - Why this matters:
     - Fabrication and lies become indistinguishable from truth, because fabricators and liars can build closed loops too.

4. Defining God
 - If you reject this:
     - You are separating the definition (ie. fundamental understanding) of God from correspondence with reality.
 - What you must commit to:
     - Theological contradictions can now be treated as mysteries, protecting any false belief from being identified as such.
     - Faith can now be separated from reason.
 - Why this matters:
     - If mysteries are indistinguishable from contradictions, then no theological claim can ever be identified as false, for faith can now occur without (or against) reason - leaving deception without a mechanism for detection.

5. Alignment with Reality
 - If you reject this:
     - Either:
         a. you reject a previous inference
         b. you've inferred something not mentioned above that opposes the deduced conclusions
 - What you must commit to:
     - Either:
         a. speculation can refute or prove claims
         b. open, blind rejection of clear observation
         c. reality does not lead to metaphysics
         d. you have knowledge not mentioned here
 - Why this matters:
     - If no additional [non-speculative] inference is found to oppose these conclusions, then both speculation and blind rejection can be used to justify both truth and falsehood.
     - Otherwise, valid inferences are needed to prove these claims.

6. Historical Comparison
 - If you reject this:
     - Either:
         a. the evidence is wrong, or some is missing
         b. you reject the process of building confidence in historical claims, specifically traceability and/or resistance to fabrication
 - What you must commit to:
     - If evidence is wrong, then it requires proof, or a competing explanation that encompasses the same data - without appealing to speculation.
     - Otherwise, you accept the validity of:
         a. trusting or rejecting unknown links, simply based on speculation
         b. circular/baseless authentication
 - Why this matters:
     - True revelation becomes indistinguishable from fabrication, as fabricated sources depend on trusting unknown links and accepting circular/
baseless authentication. 

7. Non-Fundamental Comparison
 - If you reject this:
     - Either:
         a. the evidence is wrong, or some is missing
         b. you reject the relevance or process of assessing theological coherence
 - What you must commit to:
     - If evidence is wrong, then it requires proof.
     - Otherwise: you accept that comparative coherence doesn't apply to theology, as the results only appear that way due to your own ignorance - which can never be resolved.
 - Why this matters:
     - Internal coherence is no longer required as a metric for a religion's truth - meaning that the internal claims of false religions are equally valid, for the falsehood is masked by ignorance of the fullness of the truth.

At every stage, disagreement is possible - but the pattern is consistent, where the cost of disagreement is usually the same. You must grant falsehood the same privileges as truth - or, reject the process itself, which requires the identification of an alternative - which dissolves the original question.



----------
Conclusion
----------

This project is not designed to present a personal "preference" among competing worldviews, but to demystify the whole subject and make navigating between them properly accessible to anyone interested in pursuing the Truth. The central insight is simple: if you want to distinguish truth from falsehood, then you must reject any approach that makes falsehood indistinguishable from truth.

This is why each step of the sieve matters: each step removes one more way of "getting to an answer" that can be used equally well by truth and falsehood, while doing so in a hierarchical way that narrows down the possibilities in an efficient, practical, and easy to understand way - effectively forbidding entire families of answers:

     - It forbids "truth by preference/affirmation", because preference can affirm falsehood - allowing any comforting worldview to be "true".
     - It forbids "truth by speculation", because unverifiable possibilities can always be invented - allowing any claim to be defended with what can't be checked.
     - It forbids "truth by assumption/circularity", because fabricators and liars can vouch for themselves - allowing any system to authenticate itself.
     - It forbids "faith without (or against) reason", because devotion alone lacks the capacity to identify deception - allowing any belief to have equal "confidence".
     - It forbids "no rational distinction between mysteries and contradictions", because falsehood can be protected by hiding behind ignorance or incomprehension.
     - It forbids "historical authority without traceability or resistance to fabrication", because then True revelation becomes indistinguishable from fabrication.

Once these escape hatches are removed, what remains is not an arbitrary standard, but the only standard compatible with the question: truth must be identified by correspondence with reality, coherence, non-circular justification, and historically constrained authority. This standard is not uniquely "religious" - it's the same standard we use (whether implicitly or explicitly) for any field of study, whenever we genuinely care whether something is true.

And under that standard, the conclusion naturally follows. We did not start by choosing a conclusion and hunting for support: we started with the meaning of the question itself - to keep truth distinguishable from falsehood, at every level of the sieve, and without special pleading. If we take the question seriously, then the True religion cannot be merely emotionally compelling; it must be distinguishable from fabrication in principle. It must be the worldview whose fundamentals align with reality, whose authority does not require epistemic blindness, and whose theology remains maximally coherent when compared with its alternatives. The only remaining candidate, after applying all these filters, is Islam.

As a result, the conclusion is not merely that Islam is "appealing", but that Islam is the only remaining candidate that remains distinguishable from falsehood at every level of the sieve - from metaphysical fit with reality, to the nature of faith and contradiction, to the historical constraints of revelation, to the comparative coherence of its theological mechanism.


------------
What's Next?
------------
 
This project does not demand agreement, but it does put the ball in your court. You can now navigate topics that are often seen as overwhelming or inaccessible - topics that are necessary for having certain beliefs, but are normally hidden behind an air of complexity that makes it difficult to navigate for the average person. If you agree with the conclusion, now you know how these beliefs are grounded in reality. And if you reject the conclusion, you can now identify the precise disagreement - at which level - and what commitments that requires. This project gives you a map to navigate as you need:

    1. Locate your exact point of resistance.
         - If you feel unconvinced, identify the precise filter you reject. Then, identify clearly what your rejection commits you to: if you're unwilling to pay that epistemic price, then your resistance is not to the conclusion - it's to the implications of the method.

    2. Test the sieve on yourself.
         - Apply the filters to the beliefs you inherited and the beliefs you prefer. Remember that the question was never "how do I critique others?" but "can I distinguish truth from falsehood - including in myself?"

    3. Separate "mystery" (what you don't comprehend or understand) from "contradiction" (what is not rational) in your own worldview.
         - Ask: what is the difference between a metaphysical truth and a metaphysical falsehood?
         - Answer: the one with a rational definition (and comes from a source you have reason to trust) is closer to truth than the one without a rational definition - which can only defend its coherence using speculation or ignorance.

    4. Reflect on your definition of faith and the role of reason.
         - Ask: do you use reason to establish the truth before placing faith, or does reason only apply after faith has been established?
         - Note: if the first, then that is what this project aims to demonstrate. If it is the second, then you deprive yourself of the mechanism necessary to identify false beliefs (as faith can be placed in falsehood). In that case, the only availble response to resolve valid doubt (in that falsehood) is to "believe harder".

    5. Demand the same standard everywhere.
         - If you require evidence and coherence in every serious domain of life, but suspend those standards only when theology is discussed, identify why.
         - If your answer can be used to defend something you know to be false, or can be used by a liar to defend a fabrication, or aligns with any of the costs of disagreement, then that answer does not allow you to distinguish between truth and falsehood.

    6. If you accept the sieve, follow it all the way.
         - The project's goal is to make the identification of truth easy and accessible to anyone - but along the way, we made profound discoveries that directly impact our lives. If they are shown to be true, and you become aware of their truth, then apply those discoveries into your own life.
         - Note that this project is not persuasively demanding, "Islam is true, therefore become a Muslim". Rather, it is saying, "Islam is the only candidate that remains distinguishable from falsehood at every level; here is the argument to verify for yourself." The rest is up to you.


At the end of the day, the standard is simple: a method that can justify both truth and falsehood cannot distinguish between them. The sieve was built to prevent that collapse, demystify apparent complexities, and allow anyone who disagrees to precisely identify what they reject and what commitments that earns them. If you accept that goal, the remaining work is straightforward: verify each filter for yourself -and then follow the conclusion wherever it leads.
