1. Textual/Historical Claim
 
 - What is the claim?
     - Bible
         - The Bible possesses a textual and historical preservation that is superior to all alternatives, which are not authorized by God (ie. deceptive or demonic in origin).
         - Specifically: if the resurrection of Jesus is shown to be decisively true (without any reasonable alternative), then it incapacitates any argument that Jesus wasn't divine.
     - Quran
         - The Quran possesses a preservation and inimitability that is superior to all alternatives, which are not authorized by God (ie. deceptive or demonic in origin).
         - Specifically: if the Quran is shown to be perfectly preserved and cannot have been produced by a human being, then it incapacitates any argument that Jesus was divine.
 
 - Is it ontologically possible and epistemologically attainable?
     - Bible: Yes
         - Texts and historical events can be preserved over time, and their preservation can be analyzed by eliminating alternatives for fabrication and falsehood .
     - Quran: Yes
         - Texts and historical events can be preserved over time, and their preservation can be analyzed by eliminating alternatives for fabrication and falsehood.
         - Additionally, humans have a hard limit to what they are capable of doing, so anything that falls beyond that limit would verify inimitability.
 
 - What are the criteria for success and failure?
     - Bible
         - Truth is defined/identified by consistency and reality, and falsehood by inconsistency and contradiction with reality.
         - If the Bible is authorized by God, then it must be the epitome of consistency and correspondence with reality. As a result, if all falsehoods necessarily contradict with reality, then the Quran cannot possess the same level of consistency and coherence as the Bible.
         - Therefore, success is showing that the Bible has a better form of preservation than the Quran and is more consistent and coherent. Failure is if the Quran's preservation and inimitability is stronger than the Bible's, as no manmade effort can ever surpass God's power.
     - Quran
         - Truth is defined/identified by consistency and reality, and falsehood by inconsistency and contradiction with reality.
         - If the Quran is authorized by God, then it must be the epitome of consistency and correspondence with reality. As a result, if all falsehoods necessarily contradict with reality, then the Bible cannot possess the same level of consistency and coherence as the Quran.
         - Therefore, success is showing that the Quran is truly inimitable and has a better form of preservation than the Bible and is more consistent and coherent. Failure is if the Quran could have been manmade, or if Bible's preservation and historical reliability is stronger than the Quran's - as no manmade effort can ever surpass God's power.



2. Oral, Written, and Archaeological Presence

 - Who were the eyewitnesses, and what did they bear witness to?
     - Bible
         - Three significant events: Jesus' 3-year earthly ministry, 40 days of Jesus' resurrection, and the vision that appeared to Paul and his companions after the ascension.
         - Thousands of people witnessed Jesus's ministry (about 3 years long), while over 500 witnessed his 40-day bodily resurrection (between 30-33 AD). Finally, Paul and his companions witnessed a spiritual vision of Jesus (between 33-36 AD).
     - Quran
         - Two significant events: the Quran's revelation to the Prophet Muhammad over 23 years, and his character, personality, and ministry in that time period.
         - Thousands of people witnessed the Quran's revelation to the Prophet Muhammad (which were unique, context-specific events that occurred from 610-632 AD), and more witnessed his ministry in that time.

 - How reliable was the cultural context for preserving oral tradition accurately?
     - Bible
         - Core concepts preserved without verbatim transmission.
         - The Jewish culture at the time already had a strong tradition of oral preservation. However, there was at least 35-80 years after Jesus' ministry before the Gospels were written, and in this time cultural and linguistic barriers were crossed - transitioning from Jewish and Aramaic to Greek and Roman.
         - This making verbatim preservation unlikely, but this is in line with classical views, as the tradition is seen as "living" where the core is preserved without significant corruption.
     - Quran
         - Verbatim preservation with the Quran, semi-verbatim preservation with the Prophet's life and character.
         - The 7th century Arabian culture already had an exceptionally strong tradition of oral preservation, as they lacked written and textual presence before the Quran. Moreover, the Quran was memorized verbatim by all witnesses, and daily, audible, and communal prayers served as a living verification by the community.
         - As for the Prophet's life and character, much of what is recorded is considered verbatim without it being necessarily so, such that the core is reliably preserved.

 - When was the text written and standardized, and by whom?
     - Bible
         - Written by multiple authors (not Jesus) sometime 10-70 years after Jesus' ministry, while the canon was standardized (against the apocrypha) in 382 AD by the Council of Rome.
         - The earliest New Testament books written were likely Galatians, Thessalonians (both by Paul), and possibly even James (although this is not a majority view), with the earliest estimates between 7-12 years after Jesus' ministry. However (excluding James), Paul was not a witness to Jesus' earthly ministry nor his resurrection - putting the Gospels (eyewitness accounts of Jesus' ministry) being written later than the non-eyewitness accounts. This also means that none of the texts are authored by Jesus himself.
         - Meanwhile, heresies were prevalent from Paul's ministry and continued into 400 AD, with many apocryphal works being written. This resulted in a gradual process that culminated in the Council of Rome in 382 AD, establishing the New Testament canon.
     - Quran
         - Three significant events: the writing and ordering of the Quran during the Prophet Muhammad's life, the first complete written Quran being standardized 2 years after his death, and the written dialect/accent was standardized 20 years after his death.
         - While the Quran was primarily memorized verbatim by the community, it was also written by the Prophet's scribes - namely, Zaid ibn Thabit. Abu Bakr (the first caliph after the Prophet) compiled the first written text of the entire Quran, as it was textually fragmented so far (because the Quran was revealed in context-specific situations). Finally, as Islam began to spread to the non-Arabian world, the dialect and diacritics were standardized 20 years after the Prophet's death by the fourth caliph, Uthman.
         - As for the Prophet's life and character, this started to be written down within 100 years after the Prophet's death, while the most rigorous works (the 6 Sahih Collections) were written 200 years after his death.

 - What corroborating archaeological finds are there?
     - Bible
         - Strong historical and cultural corroboration, with earliest manuscripts of the New Testament dating to 125 AD.
         - Some non-Christian writers (namely, Josephus and Tacitus, around 100 AD) reference Jesus in their historical works - although, none of them mention his resurrection or divinity (one passage in Josephus is debated). Moreover, the locations of cities, cultural practices of the Jews and Romans, and inscriptions of historical people all corroborate what the New Testament mentions. Meanwhile, the earliest manuscript we have dates to 125 AD, although that is the exception: the vast majority of them are between 300-400 AD.
         - Therefore, as each of the New Testament books are claimed to have been written long before 125 AD, the primary means of dating when they were written is a purely logical analysis, without corroborating archaeological manuscripts to verify.
     - Quran
         - Overwhelming historical and cultural corroboration, with the earliest manuscripts dating to the Prophet's life.
         - The Islamic Golden Age came about as a direct result of the Prophet's legacy, leaving us with overwhelming evidence of Islam's influence. Moreover, every single geographical location relevant to the Prophet's life is well known and corroborated, as well as extensive knowledge into the Prophet's life and the practices of the early Muslims. Finally, the earliest manuscript we have dates to the Prophet's own lifetime (which is verbatim of what we have now), although the vast majority of them are between 100-200 years after the Prophet's death.
         - Therefore, not only is the Quran reliably preserved verbatim by communal memorization, it is also clearly verifiable through archaeological means.


3. Assessment of the Chains of Transmission

 - How many independent streams of transmission are there, leading to the text's standardization?
     - Bible
         - 3 main streams (the synoptic, johannine, and pauline chains) that merge with Irenaeus, leading directly to the Council of Rome.
         - The synoptic stream goes from: Jesus → Peter (and the other Apostles) → Mark → Matthew & Luke → Papias → Polycarp of Smyrna → Irenaeus of Lyons.
         - The johannine stream goes from: Jesus → John the Apostle → Polycarp of Smyrna → Irenaeus of Lyons.
         - The pauline stream goes from: Jesus (post-resurrection vision) → Paul → Clement of Rome → Ignatius of Antioch & Polycarp of Smyrna → Irenaeus of Lyons.
         - From Irenaeus (from all three streams), we get: Irenaeus → Tertullian → Origen of Alexandria → Eusebius → Athanasius of Alexandria → the Council of Rome (led by Pope Damascus I).
         - Notes: Irenaeus was the earliest to list the 4 gospels and Paul's letters as canon, while Athanasius made the first known list of the New Testament canon. Also, Mathew also was an eyewitness, but uses Mark's work (and other unknowns) as a reference.
     - Quran
         - 2 main streams: the Quran and Hadith.
         - The Quranic stream goes from: Muhammad → scribes (led by Zaid b. Thabit) and memorizers (thousands, with hundreds memorizing the whole Quran) → Abu Bakr → Hafsa → Uthman.
         - The Hadith stream goes from: Muhammad → first generation Muslims (who met the Prophet) → second generation Muslims (and their texts) → third generation Muslims (and their texts) → the 6 standardized compilations (by Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Al-Trimidhi, Al-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah).
         - Notes: the Quran is claimed to be authored by God, while the hadith are the works of men (ie. fallible). The Quranic scribes were led by Zaid b. Thabit (who would write almost immediately after each revelation), and that there were thousands of memorizers (with hundreds who memorized the entire Quran). Abu Bakr (with Zaid b. Thabit) was the first to standardize a full, written copy of the Quran (verified by those who directly witnessed the Quran's revelation), with Hafsah (one of the Prophet's wives) being its guardian. Lastly, Uthman was the one to textually standardize the Quran's accent/diacritics.

 - Are there competing claims, and if so, what are their chains of transmission?
     - Bible
         - Yes - the Jewish Christians and Gnostics.
         - The Jewish Christians believed in Jesus' virgin birth and that he was the Messiah, but rejected Paul as a heretic and practiced Mosaic Law. As we don't have their text, it is also debated whether or not they believed that Jesus was divine.
             - Their chain goes from: Jesus → James (with Peter and the other Apostles) → oral transmission → written texts (now lost).
         - The Gnostics believed that Jesus was purely divine, and the god of the Old Testament was a flawed creator, lesser than the true, transcendent God.
             - Their stream goes from: Jesus → secret knowledge to a chose disciple → private line of enlightened teachers → Gnostic gospels.
         - Notes: all Jewish Christian writings are lost, and what is known about them is only by authors who were hostile against them, so most of what is known about them is hearsay and debated (including whether the "human Jesus" belief occurred during early Christianity or was a later phenomenon). The Ebionites were a noteworthy sect within the Jewish Christians, only differing in rejecting the virgin birth. They were expelled from Rome after the Bar Kokhba revolt (along with the Jews living there as well), settling in various parts of the Mediterranean, Africa, and the Arab-world. As such, little is truly known about them, with the only other possible source being 7th century Arab history.
     - Quran
         - No with the Quran, yes with the Hadith (Shia vs. Sunni).
         - There were no competing claims for the reliability nor content of the Quran. However, there still was the Shia/Sunni split, which differed over the nature of Ali b. Abi Talib (the Prophet's cousin) and who was the rightful caliph after the Prophet's death. With this split, the Shias believed that those closest to the Prophet corrupted the religion after he died, fabricating claims in his name. As such, they have their own set of hadith compilations, in which the Prophet entrusted the true interpretation of the religion with Ali and his hereditary lineage (including Fatima, the Prophet's daughter and Ali's wife).
         - The Shia Hadith stream goes from: Muhammad → Ali b. Abi Talib → Hasan & Husain b. Ali → Ali Zayn al-Abidin → Muhammad al-Baqir → Jafar al-Sadiq, etc. (the other twelver imams) → Al-Kulayni (Shia Hadith compiler).

 - What are the strongest, independent chains of custodianship for each stream of transmission?
     - Bible
         - Custodianship converges with Irenaeus, Eusebius, and Paul.
         - Synoptic Chain (Mark)
             - Eusebius → Papias (now lost) →  unnamed "Elder" → Mark → Gospel of Mark → Peter → Jesus
             - Irenaeus → unknown oral transmission (likely Polycarp → Papias) → Mark → Gospel of Mark → Peter → Jesus
         - Synoptic Chain (Matthew)
             - Eusebius → Papias (now lost) →  unknown oral tradition → Matthew → Q/Logia (now lost, written in Hebrew or Aramaic) → Jesus
             - Irenaeus → unknown oral transmission (likely Polycarp → Papias) → Matthew → Gospel of Matthew (written in Greek) → Jesus
             - Unknown author → the Didache → almost identical Lord's Prayer as in Matthew → Jesus 
         - Synoptic Chain (Luke)
             - Irenaeus → unknown oral transmission → Luke → Gospel of Luke → unknown eyewitnesses → Jesus
         - Johannine Chain
             - Gospel of John → unknown disciple whom Jesus loved (self attested) → Jesus's crucifixion
             - Irenaeus → Polycarp (oral, not written) → John → Gospel of John → Jesus
         - Pauline Chain
             - Paul's letters →  Paul (self-attested) → Jesus (post-resurrection vision)
             - Clement of Rome →  Paul →  Paul's letters
             - Ignatius of Antioch →  unknown Antioch oral tradition →  Paul →  Paul's letters
         - Jewish Christian/Nazarene Chain
             - Clement of Rome →  Paul's letters →  Judaizers → Jesus
             - Eusebius → unknown Jewish Christian oral tradition → unknown author of Hebrew/Nazarene Gospel (now lost - possibly Q/Logia) → Jesus
             - Eusebius → Papias (now lost) →  unknown oral tradition (likely via → Matthew → Q/Logia [now lost]) → Jesus
             - Justin Martyr → unknown oral tradition of tolerant Jewish Christians → Jesus
             - Justin Martyr → unknown oral tradition of separatist Jewish Christians → Jesus
         - Ebionite Chain
             - Epiphanus → Ebionite gospel (now lost) → unknown Ebionite tradition → James and/or Peter → Jesus
             - Irenaeus → unknown Ebionite tradition → unknown author of Ebionite gospel → Jesus
             - Justin Martyr → unknown oral tradition of Jewish Christians who believed Jesus was a "man of men (ie. naturally born) → Jesus 
         - Gnostic Chain
             - Irenaeus → Gnostic tradition → secret (unknown) oral tradition → secret (unknown) apostle → Jesus
         - Notes: each chain begins with the earliest preserved source of that chain. Moreover, it is not unusual that the Gospels' authors don't include their name in the text, as their recipients would know who the author is and manuscripts would be tagged with the authors' names (just like books nowadays). The Christian claim is that the Holy Spirit is the primary custodian of the truth, making it so that the truth is preserved regardless of what happened historically. John is the only one of the Gospels to have witnessed Jesus' crucifixion, and Paul's letters seem to have been written before the Gospels.
     - Quran
         - Quranic custodianship from Uthman, Hafsa, Abu Bakr, and those who memorized the Quran, while Hadith custodianship converges with the textual compilations.
         - Quranic Chain
             - 1,000s of named individuals (and 10,000s of unknown individuals) who memorized the Quran → Muhammad
             - Uthman b. Affan → Hafsah b. Umar →  Abu Bakr → Zaid b. Thabit → original scribes and memorizers → Muhammad
         - Hadith Chain (Sunni)
             - 6 standardized compilations (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Al-Trimidhi, Al-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah) and more (Muwatta of Imam Malik, Musnad Ibn Hanbal, etc.) → line of known individuals, each with an authenticity grading → Muhammad
                 - If a link between narrators is unknown, the hadith is given a very weak grading.
         - Hadith Chain (Shia)
             - 4 standardized compilations (al-Kulayni, Ibn Babawayh, al-Tusi) and more (ex. Bihar al-Anwar, Nahj al-Balagha, etc.) → students and companions of an infallible imam → line of infallible, divinely guided Imams → Muhammad
         - Notes: the Islamic claim is that the angel Gabriel (also known as the Holy Spirit: the angel responsible for revelation) is the primary custodian of the Quran between God and Muhammad. Furthermore, as asserted by the Quran, God is the one who ensures the preservation of the Quran, upholding the claim that truth is distinguishable from falsehood. Moreover, one of the fundamental differences between the Sunni and Shia beliefs is that the Shia believe that the lineage of Ali b. Abi Talib is infallible up to 12 generations.

 - What are the weakest links in the strongest chains of custodianship?
     - Bible
         - All chains have unknown transmission or lost works, except for the Pauline chain (regarding his own work).
         - Synoptic Chains
             - While both chains for Mark corroborate that he wrote his Gospel by learning from Peter (assuming Papias isn't who Irenaeus learns from), they depend on lost work (with Papias), an unknown transmitter, and an unknown oral tradition.
             - The chains for Matthew and Luke both rely on unknown transmission, with Luke relying on unknown sources as well.
         - Johannine Chain
             - The chain for John depends on Irenaeus, but his claim for John's authorship relies on Polycarp, who orally testified this to Irenaeus without writing it in his work.	
         - Pauline Chain
             - Paul's letters are self-attesting, and is corroborated by Clement of Rome.
             - Ignatius of Antioch also corroborates, but his chain relies on unknown oral tradition.
         - Jewish Christian/Nazarene, Ebionite, and Gnostic Chains
             - All orthodox-competing chains rely on lost work and unknown oral traditions.
             - The Gnostic chain is the exception, as their gospels are preserved yet their chain of transmission appears to be intentionally hidden (according to Irenaeus).
         - Notes: all chains about the earthly Jesus seem to have some degree of uncertainty.
     - Quran
         - The potential weaknesses of the oral and textual chains are mitigated by the other.
         - Quranic Chain
             - Both chains' weaknesses are mitigated by the other: the single, standardized text safeguarded by Hafsa is corroborated by the memorization of the Muslim community, and their memorization is safeguarded from gradual or intentional change by the standardized text.
         - Hadith Chain (Sunni)
             - The more narrators in a chain increase risk of weakness, relative to the efforts of the compilers to verify their authenticity. As such, single-narrator (known as 'ahad' hadith) narrations with greater degrees of uncertainty are considered more weak.
             - The science of authenticating hadith was established at the time of the compilers, thus allowing hadith narrations to be classified based on their authenticity - and putting more pressure on the compilers for being reliable.
         - Hadith Chain (Shia)
             - Hadith reliability rests on infallibility of the narrators, which is unfalsifiable. Furthermore, there are less corroboration as with the Sunni chains, allowing for greater possibility of weakness.
         - Notes: the Quran seems to be reliably preserved, while Hadith's reliability seems to be relative to the reliability of their compilers.



4. Alignment with Historical Context

 - What was the historical context throughout the chain of custodianship?
     - Bible
         - Three significant time periods: the crucifixion of Jesus, the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple, and the Bar Kokhba revolt.
         - After Jesus' ministry (30-33 AD) up to 70 AD, (destruction of the Jerusalem Temple), The earliest Christian community existed as a small, Aramaic-speaking Jewish sect within the Roman Empire. Oral transmission regarding Jesus and his message starting to spread, was transmitted publicly and privately, and was centered around the Church of Jerusalem.
         - After the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple in 70 AD, the movement expanded rapidly across the Roman Empire, shifting from a primarily Jewish and Aramaic-speaking context to a predominantly Gentile, Greek-speaking one - even amidst persecution. As a result, multiple written accounts and traditions began to circulate, including those later deemed heretical.
         - After the failed Bar Kokhba revolt in between 132-136 AD, the split between Christianity and Rabbinic Judaism was finalized. The Christian movement began to develop a more formal institutional structure, with early Church Fathers primarily tasked with defending and consolidating authentic apostolic tradition against competing claims. After the conversion of Constantine in 312 AD, Rome became the new capital of Christianity.
     - Quran
         - Three significant time periods: the time of Quranic revelation, the time of Quranic textual standardization, and the time of Hadith compilation.
         - The Quran was revealed to Prophet Muhammad over a period of 23 years (between 610-632 AD): 13 in Makkah, and 10 in Madinah. In this time period, the warring, independent tribes were united under a single nation, and his message reached northern Africa and southern Europe.
         - After the Prophet's death in 632 AD, the next 4 caliphs standardized the Quranic text to combat the threat of memorizers falling in battle, as well to ensure its meaning would be consistent among the Arab tribes with different dialects.
         - After Uthman standardized the Quranic dialect (between 560-656 AD), Islam began to establish itself outside of Arabia and it became harder to verify which narrations of the Prophet were authentic. From 684 AD until 915 AD, hadith compilations were written and the science of authenticating hadith was formalized. Additionally, this also was the time period where the Golden Age of Islam began, and many sciences (not just hadith) were explored and expanded upon.

 - What were the standards of testimony and custodianship in this time?
     - Bible
         - Eyewitness testimony, gradually shifting towards scholarly textual standardization.
         - In the 1st-century Jewish context, testimony standards required at least two eligible witnesses (free adult males, not relatives or biased, as per Deuteronomy 19:15 and Mishnaic traditions). Roman standards similarly valued credible eyewitnesses (non-criminals) and oral declarations in courts, with limited writing for formal records.
         - As Christianity spread amid Roman persecution after 70 AD, general standards shifted toward Greco-Roman historiography and Jewish diaspora practices of semi-verbatim oral transmission, with emerging written texts preserving core narratives to counter others. By the 2nd-4th centuries, Roman legal writing increased for contracts and trials, while church standards formalized apostolic linkage, universal acceptance (catholicity), and non-contradiction with faith.
         - As such, the consistent standard of testimony was from credible eyewitnesses, with the goal being the faithful transmission of the message's meaning and core content (rather than verbatim memorization of long prose). This is verified by the number of established textual variants in the earliest manuscripts, as what was copied was intended to be a better at transmitting the truth than the original.
     - Quran
         - Communally verbatim oral transmission.
         - Pre-Islamic Arabia was a predominantly oral culture that lacked literary and textual presence, and so their histories were preserved in memory and recitation competitions, not in books. This skill was seen as a source of immense cultural pride and honor, specializing in developing eloquent poetry and memorizing of complex genealogies that were transmitted across generations. While sparse writings did existed, it was seen primarily as a tool to aid the superior faculty of human memory.
         - This tradition continued during the revelation of the Quran and well after, founding the mass, communal, and verbatim memorization of the Quran (as well as the sciences of hadith later on) - even to the level of daily prayers (which were communal and audible and requires the recitation of the Quran - meaning that anyone praying behind the imam could correct him on mistakes). As such, the oral text became a primary authority that could be verified by the masses, leading to its textual standardization as well.

 - Is there anything anachronistic (historically or logically out of place) about the claim or its custodianship?
     - Bible
         - Many examples against Jewish expectations.
         - Social and Cultural
             - Female testimony was not seen as credible, yet all 4 gospels state that the first witnesses of Jesus' resurrection were women.
             - Jews and Samaritans shared animosity with each other, yet Jesus is depicted as positively interacting with one.
             - Despite Jewish purity laws and social boundaries, Jesus is stated to have dined with, healing, and ministering to society's "unclean" - such as tax collectors, lepers, prostitutes, and other religious outcasts.
             - Despite a culture in which superiors are not subordinate to inferiors, Jesus was baptized by John - an act that physically and spiritually cleanses oneself.
         - Theological
             - Despite deeply embedded Jewish understanding of monotheism (in which God is not a man), Jesus is depicted to be divine - God Himself. However, relative to the Greek/Roman beliefs at the time, this claim is not anachronistic.
             - Despite Jewish understanding that God openly and jealously makes Himself known, Jesus is depicted to keep his divine identity implied and/or hidden.
             - Despite expectations of a triumphant Messiah, Jesus is depicted to be executed by the Romans - a mark of shame, reserved only for the lowest of criminals. This is resolved by his resurrection (defeating death itself), but then his resurrection is not anachronistic to a Greek/Roman audience, as dying and rising gods were common themes.
             - Despite the expectation that authentic messages would be clear and consistent from the start (and orally transmitted as such), the Gospels appear to reflect a rising Christology, with Jesus's divinity becoming more easily identifiable in later-written texts.
         - Notes: Jesus also reinterpreted Jewish understanding of the law (such as being more lenient with stoning, yet more strict with divorce), but these are non-fundamental. Also, regarding the rising Christology: some argue that this sign of changing what Jesus himself claimed, while others defend by defining it as progressive revelation, where different authors highlight different aspects of the complete, unified truth.
     - Quran
         - Many examples against the 7th century Arabian context.
         - Social and Cultural
             - Females were seen as inferior to males, yet the Quran states that men and women are equal under the sight of God. When women weren't able to own property, inherit wealth, and consent to marriage, now they can. Where daughters could be buried alive due to her birth dishonoring her family, now that was considered a crime.
             - Despite deeply embedded tribalism (where an individual's identity, protection, nobility, and honor were entirely dependent on their tribe), the Quran states that the most noble in the sight of God are those who are most righteous.
             - Despite Arabian expertise and history of established poetry and eloquence, the Quran's structure does not fall in any prior literary or oral genres.
             - Despite the Quran being considered the epitome of eloquence (even by its opponents), it came out of Muhammad's mouth - a man known to be illiterate.
         - Theological
             - In a context in which religious scriptures are written by men (and are defended as divinely guided), the Quran claims to be authored by God Himself, where it explicitly states that He will be the one to preserve it.
             - Despite being geographically surrounded by polytheists (who rejected the hereafter and a supreme God), Jews (who rejected the possibility of an Arab prophet) and Christians (who were trinitarian or heretical), the Quran preaches a pure monotheism that is unique from every group. However, the message of pure monotheism was closest to what the Jews at the time believed.
         - Notes: The Quran also reinterpreted Jewish understanding of the law (such as explaining why the laws were so strict) and Jesus' identity as pure human being, but these are non-fundamental. Also, there is a significant argument that the Quran's historical natural claims are entirely (and anachronistically) in line with established history and science (as well as skeptical arguments), but that is also non-fundamental.



5. Resistance to Fabrication

 - What possibilities are there for fabrication and error in the claim?
     - Bible
         - Liar, lunatic, or demonic influence.
         - Since Jesus wrote no texts himself, the claim of his divinity rests entirely on the accuracy of the Gospels' portrayal. Assuming, then, that the Gospels are entirely accurate, then the only potential sources for fabrication and error is if Jesus acted in bad faith (lying and deceiving his followers), was honest but mentally challenged, or was honest but deceived by an unseen/demonic influence.
     - Quran
         - Liar, lunatic, or demonic influence.
         - The Quran (through Muhammad's mouth) claims to be a verbatim revelation from God. As such, all potential sources for fabrication and error must be located within his character and psychology: either acting on bad faith (lying and deceiving his followers), was honest but mentally challenged, or was honest but deceived by an unseen/demonic influence.

 - What possibilities are there for fabrication and error with the claim's custodianship?
     - Bible
         - Bad faith, revisionism, and differing Christologies.
         - If any significant error or fabrication occurred, then mainstream Christianity will differ from Jesus' message on a significant level. As such, assessing this requires exposing the link between the earthly Jesus and the earliest, established form of mainstream Christianity. Considering that Paul's letters were the first texts written chronologically, and his theology aligns with mainstream Christianity, this question reduces to figuring out if Paul's theology corresponds with Jesus' ministry. This exposes the following potential sources of fabrication and/or error:
         - Jesus and Competing Claims' Textual Silence
             - Jesus didn't write any texts himself. Moreover, the works and self-attestation of competing claims to Jesus' identity are entirely lost, making it possible that their claims could be closer to the earthly Jesus' message without us ever knowing.
         - Paul's Trustworthiness
             - Paul never met the earthly Jesus and began preaching 1-6 years after his crucifixion, with the recipients of his letters being 400-900 miles away from Jerusalem (Rome was 1500 by sea, 2500 by land). Additionally, in his earlier letters (Galatians 2:2), he states that he met with the Jerusalem Church in private, where they verified and authorized his message.
                 - If his authorization was private and self-attested, then the strength of his claim rests entirely on his trustworthiness. As such, this opens the possibility that Paul acted in error or bad faith.
                 - If the recipients of Paul's letters were 400-1500 miles away from that private meeting with the Jerusalem Church (requiring weeks or months of travel, and much more time to preach a message), then this significantly extends the time by which an oral tradition can be verified (especially at a time of competing claims).
         - Gospel Revisionism
             - The earthly Jesus is primarily known through the Gospels, which rely on unknown transmission/lost works and were written after Paul's preaching occurred. As such, the possibility arises that some (or all) were written to corroborate what Paul had already wrote and preached.
             - Looking at the Gospels in historical order, their Christologies seem to rise the more time passes on (the earliest Gospel [Mark] doesn't even mention the virgin birth, while the latest Gospel [John] explicitly identifies Jesus as divine and pre-existent). As such, the it becomes possible that the Gospels correspond with a rising, new interpretation of Jesus' message.
             - Looking at the Gospels' earliest preserved manuscripts (in the 2nd and 3rd centuries), they contain a minority of variants that significantly affect the Christology of the affected passage. This makes it possible that an early Gospel copier had a different Christological rating than a later one.
             - Ignoring the synoptic chains' reliance on unknown transmission and lost works, what is known from Q/Logia reflects a lower Christology than later works. This makes it possible that earlier Christianities believed in a lower Christology than later ones.
         - Notes: the books of Acts, 2 Peter, and Jude are omitted here as they are not fundamental to these points. While Acts does give more information on Paul's first visit in Jerusalem, it was written after Paul's own letters were written (10-40 years later), and if it was authored by Luke, then he didn't meet the earthly Jesus either. Moreover, while 2 Peter attests to Paul's writings as scripture, it was written close to the time of Paul's death (15-20 years later) and shares over 70% of content with the book of Jude (and it is unknown which book took from what). As such, none of these address the fundamental claim that Paul's theology differed from Jesus' message, and they could also have been written revisionally.
     - Quran
         - Preservation errors and contextual reinterpretation.
         - If any significant error or fabrication occurred, then the mainstream Quran will differ from Muhammad's teaching on a significant level. As such, assessing this requires exposing the link between Muhammad and the earliest, established form of the Quran. Considering that the earliest manuscripts can be dated to be at the Prophet's lifetime, this question reduces to figuring out if the Quran out of Muhammad's mouth corresponds with the text that the Muslims preserved. This exposes the following potential sources of fabrication and/or error:
         - Muhammad's Textual Silence
             - Muhammad was illiterate and so he didn't write any texts himself. As such, it is possible that what the Muslims preserved could have been different from his message without us ever knowing.
         - Preservation Errors
             - The Quran was only formally compiled into a single, complete, written book after the Prophet's death. Considering that efforts involved the gathering of scattered materials (like writings on palm trees and rocks) and cross referencing them with hundreds of individuals who memorized the text, it raises the possibility of misordering or the omission of fragments or witnesses that they didn't have.
             - After the Quran started to spread internationally (to non-Arab speakers) 20 years after the Prophet's death, diacritical variations were causing disputes as different accents of the Quran's recitation were accepted. As a result, Uthman commissioned a single, diacritically standardized version of the Quranic text, and ordered all other existing written codices to be burned. This opens the possibility that legitimate codices could have been eliminated.
         - Contextual Reinterpretation
             - The Quran was revealed in specific historical contexts that are essential for its interpretation, to which their contexts are generally preserved in the collections of hadith - 150-250 years after the Prophet's death. This long gap in the transmission of the contextual material creates a significant window for error, misunderstanding, or even deliberate fabrication of events to support later theological or political agendas.
         - Notes: even though the "contextual reinterpretation" section is more about how the Quran is understood rather than whether the Quran's text was preserved properly or not, it was included here for completeness: those who witnessed the Quran's revelation were often involved in the context of its revelation.
