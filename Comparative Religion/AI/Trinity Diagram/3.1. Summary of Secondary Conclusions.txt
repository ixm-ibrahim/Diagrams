1. God possesses power and intelligence that encompasses all of reality.
     - God is all-powerful.
     - God is all-knowing.
     - God created all things.

2. God transcends time and space.
     - God is atemporal/eternal: experiencing no past, present, or future.
     - God is aspatial/immaterial: not being composed, caused, or governed by physical processes.
     - God is uncaused.
     - God is limitless/infinite.

3. God wanted the universe to be created exactly as it is.
     - Destruction is an easier task than creation and maintaining order.
     - God could have more easily destroyed this universe than to have created it.
     - God is not surprised by what He creates.
     - God intentionally created us with a purpose.

4. God is consistent.
     - God doesn't change His mind, in the sense of learning of a better approach to something.
     - Reality does not contain contradictions.

5. God's will is the fundamental material of reality.
     - The universe's existence is not a one-time event, but a continuous processes.
     - Reality is not self-sustaining or accidental.
         - The consistent laws of nature we observe are a direct reflection of God's unchanging will.
     - The dependence of reality on God's will is why we can infer His necessary properties its structure.

6. God is intentionally subtle.
     - God is discoverable by reason.
     - God has complete power over us.

7. Truth and proper reason correspond with God's will.
     - To implement proper reasoning is to align our minds with His will.
         - To understand a truth is to understand an aspect of God's sustained, willed expression.
         - Rationalizing falsehood is an act that does not correspond with God's will.
     - God is not the author of confusion.

8. God gave us a will to choose the values that govern how we live our lives.
     - God allows us to pursue good or evil as we wish.
     - God allows us the capacity to pursue falsehood if we choose to use our reason improperly.
     - Our wills "seek permission" from God to manifest in reality.
     - The causal relationship between God's will and ours is paradoxical as we can't comprehend atemporality.
     - Destiny and predestiny refer to the same concept, differing only on temporal/atemporal perspective.

9. Any True religion corresponds to the purpose that God created us for.
     - God has an ontological will and a prescriptive will.
     - God wants us to freely pursue the Truth.
     - Proper reason filters out falsehood and converges towards the true understanding of reality.
     - Some decisions are objectively better than others.
     - Only one set of fundamental claims corresponds to the singular, consistent reality of God's will.
     - A "religion" is a way of life that is governed by a set of beliefs and values.
     - A "True religion" prescribes the way of life that achieves our God-given purpose.
     - Any True religion will be fundamentally founded on using proper reason to believe in God.
     - The True religion transcends comfort and culture, guiding towards truth and what is right.
     - "Faith" is a form of reason in which the foundational premise relies on trustworthiness or knowledge rather than empirical observation.
     - A religion that fundamentally reduces to a leap of faith against/without reason has no mechanism to distinguish itself from a well-crafted falsehood that appeals to the same emotions or desires.
     - Reality corresponds more closely with a test than a punishment.

10. The True religion corresponds with reality.
     - An exception doesn't disprove the rule; it highlights the existence of a "higher" law that encompasses both.
     - A "miracle" is, fundamentally, a unique phenomenon that contradicts human capacity to replicate it.
     - True miracles must be distinguishable from false (manmade, deceptive) miracles.
     - A mystery is a paradoxical gap in our knowledge that is beyond our limited human comprehension.
     - A contradiction is where the distinguishable difference between two things is also shared by them.
     - Both mysteries and contradictions appear as profoundly deep concepts, yet are distinguishable on their correspondence with reality.
         - Mysteries cause reflection on our own limits, relative to God's ultimate power and transcendence.
         - Contradictions require infinite justifications, fundamentally appealing to ignorance or emotion instead of proper reason.
     - The True religion does not demand belief in contradictions.
         - If a religion has no distinguishing factor between mysteries and contradictions, then it is fundamentally indistinguishable from a false religion (as falsehoods now can be portrayed as mysteries without detection).

11. The True religion is distinguishable from false religions.
     - We can use tools like source, logical, and empirical evaluation to filter the True religion from the false one.
     - Humble, iterative reasoning necessarily converges on the Truth, allowing discoverability without coercion.
     - The consistency of the True religion necessarily reflects God's consistency when it comes to Truth.
     - False religions will inevitably contradict reality at a fundamental level.
     - Faith is blind when reason isn't used to verify the source's trustworthiness and reliability.
     - God tests us (in the pursuit of Truth) by means of others (how they treat us).

12. The True religion is accessible to everyone (whether they seek it or not).
     - The True religion must be founded on principles that transcend language and culture.
     - The primary "scripture" of the True religion is embedded in reality itself: universally discoverable and attainable.
     - Humble, accumulative, iterative reasoning necessarily converges on the Truth while filtering out falsehoods.
     - God does not hold us accountable for a capacity that we can't achieve.
     - Reason, reflection, and humility are universally available to anyone who is willing to pursue the Truth.
