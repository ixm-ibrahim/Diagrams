A RECORD OF MY INTELLECTUAL QUESTIONS

This is a summary of the questions I have been working through across many
sessions, reconstructed from the conversations themselves. It covers four big
areas — consciousness and the will, truth and reasoning, religion, and gender
discourse — and ends with the threads that run through all of them.

Quotes in "quotation marks" are my own words, pulled from the original chats.

1. CONSCIOUSNESS, AWARENESS, THE MIND & THE WILL
   (the "phenomenology tree" / DAG project)
================================================================================

This is the largest and most foundational thread: a structured tree of ~168+
nodes that starts from what is absolutely undeniable in direct experience and
builds every later concept (reality, truth, the self, will, and eventually God)
on top of it. The rule was that nothing could be used before it was grounded.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  The starting point: "Something is happening" (Node 1.1.1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The root of everything is the bare fact of occurrence — before any argument,
something is present in experience. I wanted the examples to be concrete and to
include phenomena people don't realize they're aware of:

   "add more senses that people aren't commonly aware of (but still get
    awareness of, whether they realize or not), like proprioception, a logical
    statement, a moral statement, a false statement, etc."

The key move is Cartesian and self-sealing: to doubt that something is happening,
something must happen — the doubt itself. A false or imaginary thought ("a flying
pig") still OCCURS as a phenomenon even though its object doesn't exist. This is
also where I took on eliminativism — the claim that "consciousness is an illusion,
nothing is actually happening" — and argued it operates beneath the level the
objection can reach.

Questions at this layer:
  - What is the absolute, undeniable starting point of all knowledge?
  - Can the bare fact of occurrence be doubted without self-refutation?
  - How wide is the scope of "phenomena" — does it include logic, morality,
    falsehood, and imagination, not just the five senses?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2  The minimal structure of any experience (Node 1.1.x)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Beneath "phenomena occur," five primitives: (1) phenomena occur, (2) they occur
with distinctions, (3) they have qualities, (4) qualities have associations, and
(5) awareness is "the occurrence of a quality or association." I kept pushing to
keep these grounded in observation rather than smuggling in metaphysics — e.g.
stripping an ungrounded "my" out of the root claim.

A deliberate choice: defer the self. No "I," "you," "my," "your" allowed until a
much later node — the subject of experience is something to DERIVE, not assume.

Questions at this layer:
  - What is the minimal structure any experience must have?
  - Can that structure be stated without presupposing a self?
  - Where is the line between what experience GIVES us and what ultimately
    EXISTS (the monism / dualism / hard-problem fault line)?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3  Will, desire & motivation (Node 1.2.5.1) — the free-will territory
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This branch reconstructs agency from the inside. Wanting/willing is treated as a
distinct kind of phenomenon: one that is DIRECTED — toward something. The
signature distinction I built here:

   - DESIRE = a *wanted* want's opposite: an UNWANTED want — an uninvited
     craving (craving while wanting not to crave).
   - WILL   = a *wanted* want — an endorsed resolve.

So desire and will are "wants about wants." The open questions (left as explicit
Unknowns) are essentially the free-will questions held in reserve: mechanism,
physicalism, the self, unconscious wants, and outcome patterns. If you reject the
whole branch, "the entire domain of motivation has no experiential basis" —
hunger becomes indistinguishable from a mere stomach sensation.

Questions at this layer:
  - What is the will, and what distinguishes it from desire?
  - What does it mean to ENDORSE one's own wanting?
  - Does motivation have any purely experiential basis — or is "wanting just
    complex sensations" (reductionism)?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4  Time, change, memory (Node 1.2.5.3) and qualia/perception (1.2.5.2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Node 1.2.5.3 grounds the temporal dimension — that awareness has a "now," a
"before," memory, succession, change, and events — and I treated imagination and
thought as built ON TOP of this temporal vocabulary. Nearby nodes handle
surprise, possibility, and "not-occurring" / absence (the machinery for imagining
what is not the case). The 1.2.5.2 branch covers intensity, sensation, and the
move from raw sensation to perception (qualia proper). Aphantasia came up as a
real objection — what to make of people who report no mental imagery.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.5  How I wanted the mind reasoned about (method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   "Prioritize sound reasoning over my preferences. If something doesn't hold
    logically, say so — don't defer to my judgment when the logic is clear.
    Think WITH me, not just do what I say."

Other standing rules: trace every new term back to previously defined terms (keep
the graph acyclic); challenge ungrounded terms instead of rationalizing them
(defending a known problem is "confirmation bias support, the opposite of the
project's purpose"); use only real objections people actually hold (eliminativism,
monism, dualism), never strawmen; and the standard for every node — "clear enough
for a middle schooler, precise enough for a philosopher."

Core epistemic stance: "Your awareness of X is what any theory of X is built from.
You do not need the theory to have the awareness." Phenomenology comes first.

>> BIGGEST RECURRING QUESTIONS IN THIS AREA
   - Is "something is happening" truly indubitable?
   - What is the minimal structure of experience, without assuming a self?
   - What is the self — and can the subject be derived rather than assumed?
   - What is the will, and how does it differ from desire?
   - How do time and memory ground the higher mind (imagination, thought)?
   - Where exactly does phenomenology end and metaphysics begin?

2. TRUTH, REASONING & METHOD
   (the "Sieve of Truth" argument tree, objection design, meta-observations,
    and the "AI Council")
================================================================================

The engine behind everything else: how to establish what is actually true,
expose hidden assumptions, and reason without fooling myself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  What counts as objective truth (the argument tree)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A deeply nested tree (~176 nodes) that derives foundational truths from the
ground up. Deepest descendants are "atomic operations"; parents SYNTHESIZE their
children rather than repeat them, so a reader can "dive in at the level they
want." It grew out of comparative-religion / metaphysical inquiry and was also
rendered as an interactive page using multiple visual forms (ladder, tree,
matrix, evidence cards), with ideas like "agreement propagation" and a
"cost-of-disagreement" traversal.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2  Logic, imagination & contradiction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A purely philosophical worry I raised about my OWN system's consistency:

   "we observe that 'A contradiction is when imagining a concept's definitions
    does not result in a visualization' (like I can't visualize a square circle's
    definition, but I can visualize 'infinity's definition [but not infinity
    itself]'). I feel like there may be a contradiction here — is there?"

Resolution I accepted: one claim is about imagination vs. the external world, the
other about imagination vs. itself — two different axes, not a contradiction. This
was an attempt to ground logical IMPOSSIBILITY in the structure of imagination.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3  Designing objections — steelmanning & exposing the hidden assumption
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I was unhappy that the tree's objections were weak and "self-resolved." My
signature move: expose the hidden assumption doing all the work. I built a
four-part objection pipeline where each section feeds the next:

   1. Objection Basis      — steelman the objector's argument
   2. Objection Commitments — expose the assumptions they must hold
   3. What's Missing        — show where those assumptions break "logically,
                              not vibes"
   4. Correction           — what the node provides that resolves it

   "by root cause analysis, I meant exposing the assumptions of the objection,
    not my nodes."

   "every section's output is the input of the other."

Objections had to come from real positions (eliminativism, constructivism,
idealism, mereological nihilism, Frankfurt's higher-order desires, sorites
problems, James-Lange, Bergson, Hume, Sellars) — not complaints "nobody holds."
The quality test was experiential: the objector should say "ah, I hadn't
considered that," not "that's not what I was saying." This surfaced retorsion
arguments — positions that self-destruct by needing the very thing they deny
(e.g. hard determinism needing reasoning to argue for determinism).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4  A method for rigorous thinking ("meta observations")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A documented PROCESS for thinking: walk a text clause by clause and, for each
fragment, derive the underlying rule it's an instance of. I cared about the
thought process itself, not just the output:

   "this question was about the thought process, not the meta observations
    themselves."

Moves I wanted made explicit: name the angle before asking "why"; treat "what's
the general form?" as distinct from "why" (abstraction vs. cause); flag claims
that confuse whether something OCCURS with whether it's ACCEPTABLE (a recurring
"dimension mistake"); reject compound claims and paraphrase-pretending-to-be-
insight. A live tension I raised myself:

   "don't people recognize that pathological liars exist?" (i.e. if strong
    feeling is treated as evidence of truth, why does that survive everyone
    knowing feelings can be faked?)

In the end I had the meta-observations cleared to empty templates so I could fill
them in myself — I wanted to do the thinking, not outsource it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5  The "AI Council" — real pluralism vs. the illusion of it
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A multi-agent tool to "ask philosophical questions and demystify complex social,
political, and intellectual topics." I immediately spotted the core problem:

   "I definitely want to resolve that issue with all the models thinking the same
    under the hood."

My proposed fix — an "anti-Claude" agent whose job is exposure, not disagreement:

   "to make every single thing/assumption exposed, so the other AI's can digest
    that."

This became a pipeline: a "Prior Scout" that exposes the model's framings, hedges,
and safe defaults; tradition-based council members (a Muslim scholar, a Christian
scholar, a hard secular skeptic, a historian); an anonymizer + quality assessor
scoring real engagement vs. hedging; and a final synthesizer. Same signature as
the objection work: surface the hidden assumption, then stress-test it.

>> BIGGEST RECURRING QUESTIONS IN THIS AREA
   - What actually counts as objective truth, and can it be derived bottom-up?
   - What is the hidden assumption doing all the work here?
   - Is my own system internally consistent?
   - How do you reason rigorously without fooling yourself?
   - Is multi-perspective deliberation real, or an illusion when every voice
     shares one underlying mind?

3. RELIGION
   (comparative religion, Islam, the LDS/Mormon church, the Book of Mormon)
================================================================================

A long-running project — part devotional, part adversarial — that tests Islam,
Christianity, and (in a major addendum) the LDS church against one fixed set of
epistemic rules: the "Sieve of Truth." The throughline is a refusal to let any
tradition, including a preferred one, win on feeling.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  A rigorous method for judging religions ("the sieve")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The core distinction: the COHERENCE axis vs. the COMFORT axis — is a belief true
because it survives scrutiny, or merely because it's consoling? The website
encodes this as nodes like "Rationalizing Confusion" and the target belief it
argues against: "Beliefs can be safely based on strong feelings, comfort, or
intuition alone." The deepest methodological demand was SYMMETRY:

   "if you read the original project, you'll find that I tested both directions -
    ie. assuming christianity is true and islam is the falsehood, AND in parallel,
    islam is true while christianity is the falsehood. So the same thing needs to
    happen with lds being true and islam/christianity being false."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2  Reading the Book of Mormon firsthand
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I prepared to read the Book of Mormon cover to cover, with my own framework as a
lens, asking for a plan measured in pages and annotated with what should "pop
out":

   "I am about to read the book of mormon. Give me a set of reading goals,
    equidistant in terms of pages (not chapters)... what should pop out to me,
    and when? go in chronological order, as if I was reading with these notes."

Things to watch for: anachronism and authorship (pre-Christ Christology; KJV
phrasing and the Sermon on the Mount appearing verbatim in supposedly ancient
text); internal contradiction (Jacob 2-3 condemning polygamy vs. D&C 132
commanding it); the epistemology of feeling (Alma 32's faith-as-seed; Moroni
10:3-5's "ask God... he will manifest the truth" — the mirror image of my own
rules); and the thesis that the Book of Mormon is MORE orthodox/monotheistic than
later LDS revelation ("early vision + later institutional accretion").

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3  Islam vs. the LDS church — the comparison I found most alive
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Muslim and a Mormon share more surface ground than either shares with creedal
Christianity (both reject the Trinity, both have post-biblical scripture, angelic
visitation, living authority). I wanted it dramatized as a real conversation, and
insisted on steelmanning the other side:

   "What would the main topics be? What rebuttals and challenges would he expect
    from that person who also knows about Islam?"

   "I want you to surface the strongest objections the lds member can leverage
    against the muslim (ideally the ones that are lds-specific)."

Flashpoints:
  - Tawhid vs. henotheism: is "one God we worship" actually monotheism, or a
    chain of gods?
  - The sealing of prophecy: by Islam's own logic (prophets to every nation), why
    does revelation stop in 632 CE — and does Joseph Smith fit Islamic
    prophetology better than Muslims admit? (One of the two objections I judged
    to genuinely "bite.")
  - Pre-mortal existence: Islam has the seed (the mithaq, Q 7:172); LDS builds the
    whole structure. Whose theology does more with the same raw material?
  - Anthropomorphism: the Quran gives God a face, hands, a throne — do Muslims
    allegorize these away, and is that the same move they criticize in others?
    (The other objection that bites.)
  - Witnesses & transmission: Book of Mormon witnesses vs. the Quran's mass
    (mutawatir) transmission.
  - Falsifiability: the Book of Abraham papyri as a cleanly falsified translation
    claim, and whether "catalyst theory" is an unfalsifiable rescue.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4  Theology under stress (the LDS addendum)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The hardest doctrinal work — built, then audited:
  - Euthyphro's second horn: LDS grounds justice in an eternal law external to God
    (Alma 42:25) — does that put something above God? (And does Islam's own
    Ash'arite/Mu'tazilite debate face the same problem?)
  - An embodied, once-mortal God: if God has a body He is in space, extended,
    contingent — does literal scripture-reading buy fidelity at the cost of
    metaphysical coherence?
  - Genesis 3:5 ("ye shall be as gods"): LDS reads it as a true prophecy of human
    destiny; Islam and Christianity read it as the paradigmatic Satanic lie — the
    single most diagnostic finding.
  - Teleology: LDS's infinite forward regress of gods vs. Islam's terminus ("to
    Him we return").
A meta-finding I sought out and accepted: several critiques I leveled at LDS
(continuing revelation as a "doctrinal absorber," esoteric teaching, the founder's
resilience as evidence) generalize uncomfortably to features Islam also has.

>> BIGGEST RECURRING QUESTIONS IN THIS AREA
   - What makes a religious claim true — coherence, or comfort/feeling?
   - Is the LDS church monotheist or polytheist; can an embodied, once-mortal God
     be the uncaused absolute?
   - By Islam's own logic, why does prophecy end — and does Joseph Smith fit it
     better than Muslims admit?
   - What separates a legitimate "restoration" of a faith from a "corruption" —
     and is the test falsifiable in every direction?
   - Am I applying my own standards fairly? (A question I kept turning on myself.)

4. GENDER & DISCOURSE
   (a layered framework documenting asymmetries in how men and women are treated)
================================================================================

A stacked, falsifiable framework cataloguing asymmetries in law, media, language,
emotional life, violence, health — and especially in DISCOURSE itself: how claims
about gender get made, received, and shut down.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  The layered structure (the method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each layer treats the layer below as its raw material:
   Layer 1 — Observations (individually checkable facts/patterns; ~147 of them)
   Layer 2 — Inferences ("the smallest interpretive step from the observations")
   Layer 3 — Structural claims (patterns across Layer 2)
   Layer 4 — Patterns across Layer 3
   Layer 5 — "Governing patterns"
   + a staging file for candidates that failed audit or belong one layer up.

   "basically the same operation, but treating level N nodes as the new
    observations and deriving the higher-level inferences from those."

The hard problem I kept circling — how to generate candidate principles freely
yet keep only the correct ones:

   "I imagine many possible explanations may work - but I'm only wanting the
    'right' ones, if that makes sense. Not sure how to filter that."

Four filters every entry had to pass: both-sidedness; the smallest interpretive
move; the "specialist wouldn't wince" test; and falsifiability (you must be able
to name an observation that would disconfirm it).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2  Discourse mechanics — how arguments get deflected
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The original seed: rhetorical moves that are "structurally available" whether or
not the underlying claim is true. My flagship example:

   "the use of misrepresenting analogies... 'no it's not, B has xyz qualities
    (that are true but irrelevant to the point) that A doesn't have' - especially
    in emotionally charged settings."

I insisted a meta-observation name the REASON, not just relabel the move:

   "why is whataboutism ok for one side but not ok for another? Meta observations
    can be made about consistency, deflection, etc."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3  The unfalsifiability lens (the most important epistemic test)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   "if your system of reasoning can be used to justify a falsehood, it can't
    distinguish between truth and falsehood."

Applied here: if a framework can't tell a real victim/bias/oppressor from a felt
or false one, it isn't producing evidence. This generated a cluster of
"closed-loop" observations (implicit bias where denial confirms it; privilege
where both acknowledgment and refusal confirm it; "lived experience" as a trump
that breaks when two experiences contradict) — and I demanded the SAME lens be
turned on the men's side too (alpha/beta, hypergamy as post-hoc framings),
because flagging only one side "would itself fail the audit."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4  Symmetry — documenting male-disadvantaging asymmetries too
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A throughline was fairness in both directions: Selective Service/conscription,
the "women and children first" protector-disposability norm, ~92% of workplace
fatalities, 3-4x suicide and homelessness ratios, sentencing disparities, the
post-divorce package (custody, support, home, alimony), "deadbeat dad" with no
"deadbeat mom," and the empathy gap. The Layer 3/4 synthesis: mainstream
discourse runs gender equality as a "one-way project" — progress counts only when
it reduces female disadvantage.

I also kept the framework honest about its own gaps — the largest acknowledged
hole being trans / gender-identity discourse ("the biggest single elephant... the
framework currently has nothing on it").

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5  Writing philosophy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   "Writing goal: demystification. Use plain, common language and keep cognitive
    load low, while staying precise enough that a specialist would agree it's
    accurate. Be hard on the topic, soft on the reader."

Drifts to avoid: academic jargon, bland "both-sidesing" that claims nothing,
false accessibility that makes a specialist object, and emotional cushioning that
buries the point.

>> BIGGEST RECURRING QUESTIONS IN THIS AREA
   - Can a gender-discourse framework distinguish a real victim/bias from a merely
     felt one — applied symmetrically to both sides?
   - How do you generate inferences freely yet filter to only the correct ones?
   - Why the asymmetry (the reason), not merely that one exists?
   - Is equality being pursued one-directionally?
   - Can it stay both-sided, falsifiable, AND readable at once?

5. THE THREADS THAT CONNECT EVERYTHING
================================================================================

Across consciousness, truth, religion, and gender, the same intellectual
signature shows up again and again:

  - EXPOSE THE HIDDEN ASSUMPTION. Whether it's a philosophical objection, a
    religious claim, an AI's trained priors, or a rhetorical deflection — the real
    work is surfacing the assumption doing the load-bearing, then testing it.

  - COHERENCE OVER COMFORT. A belief earns its place by surviving scrutiny, not by
    how good it feels. "Strong feeling as evidence of truth" is the error you
    chased through every domain (Moroni's promise, the pathological-liar tension,
    emotionally charged discourse).

  - SYMMETRY / TEST BOTH DIRECTIONS. Every claim gets tested all ways: each
    religion assumed true in turn; asymmetries documented for both sexes; "if your
    method can justify a falsehood, it can't tell truth from falsehood."

  - STEELMAN, DON'T STRAWMAN. Objections must come from real positions people hold,
    strong enough that the objector nods rather than protests.

  - DEMYSTIFY WITHOUT LOSING ACCURACY. Plain enough for a non-expert, precise
    enough that a specialist wouldn't wince — never hedging into saying nothing.

  - DERIVE, DON'T ASSUME. Ground every term in earlier ones; defer the self until
    it can be earned; build parents from children.

  - INTELLECTUAL HONESTY ABOUT YOURSELF. The recurring meta-question — "Am I
    applying my own standards fairly? What have I missed?" — turned on your own
    work as hard as on anyone else's. You repeatedly cleared finished output back
    to blank so you could do the thinking yourself.
