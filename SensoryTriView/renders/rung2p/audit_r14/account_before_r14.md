# The True-Person view, and what I took from your rounds

## Part one. How the True-Person view works today

Three names are used throughout. The glass body is the glass figure the True-Person camera looks at, standing in the dark. The mind's model is everything drawn inside the glass body's skull. The simulated mannequin is the small body the mind keeps inside that skull, the mind's own body, drawn as tiles where the eyes see it and as its known shape where they do not.

### The scene

The room, his body, the window, the trees, the ground, the range and the sky are one Blender scene. Two cameras live in it: the First-Person camera at his eyes, with its lens of 20 mm and its lids, and the Third-Person camera on its road. The True-Person camera stands outside him and looks at the glass body's head. Inside the glass skull sits the mind's model: what his mind holds of the world, drawn as tiles.

### The eyes' scans

Every frame, from the point between his eyes, five pictures are taken with a fisheye of 200 degrees across and 130 high, 1024 pixels square, each pixel sampled at one point so a pixel is one surface and not a blend:

- The scan: the colour of every surface the rays strike, its position, and which thing it belongs to.
- The backdrop: the sky, the ground and the range with everything nearer hidden, so what stands behind the trees and the window frame is known whole.
- The sky: the sky's shell alone, for the sky remembered behind the range.
- The peel: the far side of the first thing every ray strikes, its position and its thing.
- The pane: the window's glass reflecting on its own, with the world black behind it.

All five are at the eye's own exposure that frame. Through a reveal or a dive the model also has a second scan at the road's exposure, so nothing brightens as one picture takes over from the other.

### From pixels to tiles

Every scan pixel becomes a tile in the skull, placed on its own ray from the eyes at the distance the mind believes:

- Within 6 m a distance is held as it is. Beyond that it is compressed, the logarithm of how far it lies past the reach, so the trees, the range, the sky and the sun keep their order but not their scale. What still falls outside nine tenths of the glass radius is pressed onto the glass.
- A thing whose radius is under half its distance is compact: it is held whole, at one believed distance, with its shape. A thing larger than its distance is held piece by piece.
- Until the far field has been looked at for five seconds it lies flat against the sky; that knowledge fades over a minute once he looks away.
- The model's scale is the mind's zoom: one twenty-fourth while attention is in the room, shrinking so that nine tenths of what sight reaches fits inside the glass, down to about one eighty-sixth with the sky in mind. It zooms out at once and comes back in only after nothing far has been looked at for seven tenths of a second. A thought of a thing counts as attention at that thing's distance.

A tile is as long as its cell along the sheet's slope and as wide as its cell across, 1.7 times its pixel's footprint, so neighbours overlap and the sheet closes. A tile is at most four footprints long on a surface held piece by piece and twelve on a thing held whole. A tile the rendering camera would see edge-on turns a third of the way toward it instead of vanishing. A tile nearer the rendering camera than twenty to sixty of its own footprints fades out, so a camera passing through the sheet meets no streaks; from the eyes every tile is a pixel wide and none fades. From the side the eyes never see, a tile is six tenths as bright, clear while the camera is within two model metres and solid beyond three and a half.

### Rims, coins and chords

Where a ray meets the edge of a thing against something deeper, the surface still runs on along the ray to the thing's limb. Every such rim pixel of a thing held whole, and every pixel whose sheet runs within a quarter cosine of the ray, is a coin: a tile facing the eyes at the plain footprint, drawn only for a camera on the eyes' own ray. Each carries a chord: a tile three footprints wide running from its front hit to its far hit along the ray, standing half a footprint inside the thing, its front set by the picture's depth gradient. The seen surface keeps the near half of every chord, the far side the far half, so the seam between a thing's two sides is the line of the chords' middles. A chord takes the colour of an interior neighbour; every other tile keeps its own pixel's colour, since a silhouette pixel's colour is a true mix of the thing and what stands behind it.

### Seen and remembered

The scan of this frame is the live picture, drawn in full colour. The lids wipe it as the First-Person lids do, the upper lid three quarters of the way: a blink dims what it covers to a fifth, never to nothing. When the eyes stay shut longer than 0.35 s the live picture goes over 0.6 s and the last picture they held stands in its place; that memory comes up once the live picture has mostly gone, and goes again over a quarter of a second when the eyes open. For that quarter second the two overlap, which is the knee you saw at 42 s and the crossfade at 89 s.

A picture is what the eyes have held. Until they first open, at 16 s, there is none: the last picture, the backdrop and the remembered sky are not drawn at all, and the eyes being shut light nothing. So while he wakes with his eyes shut the head holds only what a thought or a sense brings.

Every remembered tile has the same look, whatever it belongs to:

- Its colour drains toward its own grey, the grey of its own brightness, half of the way at most; the drain comes with age over two and a half seconds.
- Its brightness fades to 0.82 over six seconds. It is fully opaque: a thing held in memory hides what stands behind it, as a seen thing does.
- It wavers as a mirage does, and this is settled: waves four times wider than tall in the thing's own frame, which change shape where they stand, about one and a half times a second, and travel nowhere. They move each tile across its ray in every direction, sideways as much as up and down, by four percent of the thing's radius in all, never under two footprints nor over twenty. The same wave carries light: a tile is brighter or dimmer by up to four tenths as the wave swells and sinks at it, as smoothly as the wave itself, with no grain. The light is there because the stand-in surfaces are one flat colour each, and a flat colour bent by waves shows nothing inside its outline.
- It is lit by the thought on its thing, times his awareness, or by the eyes being long shut, times his awareness, once they have held a picture. Unlit, it is not drawn. Lit under a tenth, it thins out as it dims, so a thing fading from mind goes, and does not stand as a dark shape in front of what lies behind it.
- A picture from another time is drawn one and a half percent nearer the eyes than the live one, so the two never fight for a pixel.

The backdrop and the sky are remembered the same way, completing the live picture at its own depth behind the trees and the window frame: this frame's while the eyes are open, the last picture's while they stay shut. The sun is a ball a little beyond the sky, six degrees wide from the eyes: the half the rays reach is seen at the sun's strength, the rest remembered at four tenths of white, rippling by a twentieth of its radius, lit by the thought of the outside, by the warmth on his face, or by the eyes shut where the last picture held the sun.

### The known shapes

Every mesh thing of the room and the outside under 50 m across has a shape in the model: the thing as the mind knows it. The ground, the range and the sky have none and are remembered from the backdrop and sky scans alone. A shape is placed point by point exactly as its hits would be, two footprints inside its own surface. A tile of it is not drawn where the eyes see its place this frame: the place is seen where the eyes' own scan has a hit there at the same distance, on the side of the thing that faces the eyes. So from every camera a seen surface shows as seen, and only its unseen parts show as memory. Since today it is drawn as tiles, four per footprint squared scattered over its faces, each the footprint wide, each lit on its own by its smooth normal, each drifting on its own with the same waves as every remembered tile. Its colour is its material's colour under the known light: the sky's level indoors or outdoors, and the sun's share by the normal and by how much of the sun's disc stands above the ridge, nothing before first light at 40 s. How bright that light is drawn is read off the picture beside it: the middle brightness of the eyes' own scan of that frame, over what the same things' known colours would give, indoors and outdoors each from its own part of the scan, times 1.62. So a remembered wall sits as dark or as bright as the seen wall next to it, a little brighter, at every moment of the dawn and whatever the eye's exposure. While no picture stands in the head, before the eyes first open, it is the mind's own light, a fixed level of 0.3. It is shown only while a thought of its thing is on, times his awareness. It stands as the oldest memory there is: fully drained, at an old memory's brightness, opaque as every memory is, a smooth surface of flat colour with no grain; a mirage bends an image and adds no noise, so its unrest is the drift alone, which with the stand-in's flat colours shows at edges and in shading and with real textures will show across the surfaces. The far side of every whole thing is this shape.

### The thoughts

The thoughts come from the timeline in the senses file, each a set of things with a rise of 0.4 s and its own fade, which runs on a clock at one over his awareness, so a thought is gone sooner the less aware he is: the alarm from 4 to 10 s on the phone and the nightstand, joined by the bed from 5 s, fading over 3 s; the rest of the room as he sits up, one thing after another from 17 to 19 s, held to 22 s and fading over 3 s; the view from 68 to 80 s on the ridge, the ground and the sun, fading over 1 s; the chime from 88.8 to 92.3 s on the phone and the table, fading over 6 s. A thought lights a thing's remembered tiles and its known shape wherever its hits lie, and pulls the mind's zoom to that thing's distance. Every thought is multiplied by his awareness, a curve in the same file, so a thought in his sleep lights nothing.

### The soul, the mind's soul, and the sounds

The soul is a white ball of light on the line forty-five degrees behind and above the glass body's head, three hands clear of the skull, lit by the will and never out. Inside the mind's model, behind and above the simulated mannequin's head on the same line, sits the mind's own soul: a white disc sized with the model, never scanned, whose presence rises over 0.3 s at each word of will, holds a second and fades over five to a floor of 0.12, times his awareness. The sounds, and every other sense, are drawn on the simulated mannequin as the next section says.

### The senses on the simulated mannequin

One rule for all of them: a sense lights the part of the simulated mannequin it arises on, whether or not the eyes see that part, and nothing is ever drawn on the real body in the room. A sense is drawn on the outside of the body alone, its skin, the surface a ray from outside would strike. The stand-in mannequin is made of balls and tubes that run into one another, so wherever one piece lies inside another, more than four millimetres deep, nothing is drawn: no tile, no glow. A limb then reads as one limb with one outline, and no ball shows inside a tube. The senses on the skin, touch with its pain and pleasure, proprioception and temperature, are drawn as a glow: one layer of light that stands 1.8 centimetres outside the skin, over the part's own tiles, which keep their own colour under it. The glow's light fades to nothing at the layer's own outline, and that outline lies outside the body's, so a glow has no edge where the body ends. Its colour is six tenths the sense's colour and four tenths the part's own colour, the sense's strength setting the brightness and not the blend. It wavers: a slow pattern over the body, fourteen cells to a room metre and changing about once a second, brightens and dims it by a fifth either way and turns its colour by a fortieth of the colour wheel either way. Nothing moves; only the light changes. The senses on the organs, the ears, the eyes, the heart and the lungs, colour the organ's own tiles instead, seen, remembered and known alike, and those tiles are kept undrained and fully opaque; the red of a hurting ear and of the eyes in the glare stands alone, with none of the organ's colour. The senses may overlap on one part. Where two of them share one patch of skin, the stronger shows in its own colour, and they mix only where their strengths lie within a tenth of each other: mixed by their strengths, touch's magenta and warmth's colour made red, which is pain's. Touch is the one sense that lights less than a part: only the patch of the part that is touched. The strength of every sense is the timeline's strength for the event times the attention the event gets under the budget, as far as he is aware.
