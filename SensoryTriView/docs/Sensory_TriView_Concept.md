# Sensory Tri-View: the concept

The vision. Dated 2026-11-06.

## 1. What this is for

This is the first scene of a larger philosophy project about Truth: what it is, how we identify it, and how we tell it from falsehood. The scene makes one point visible. Every picture we have of reality is a description the mind builds from signals, and the description is not the thing. We see a ball, not its atoms, yet the mind's picture of the ball holds the atoms too. We see the room, not the dark beyond what the senses have touched.

So the work is three things, in order:

- **An engine.** A way to take any scene, a body moving through a world, and show it three ways at once: as it is seen from outside, as the person sees it, and as the person's mind simulates it, body and world both, with the soul that moves the body. The engine runs every sense all the time. What the mind's view shows of each sense is set by how aware the person is of it, and the engine can also show every sense at full strength at once, for looking at the whole of what it runs. Any of the three views can be reached from any other, by way of Third-Person between the eye and the mind, because all three are drawn from the one scene. The engine is the lasting product. It is built once and applied to scene after scene.
- **A first scene.** A man alone at dawn, built with the engine. It exists to prove the engine and the idea on the simplest material: a room, a window, a body, a couple of minutes of ordinary sensation.
- **A showcase video.** Narrated. Each view shown alone, then turned into the next, then all three together, so a viewer learns the language before being asked to read it.

Later scenes extend the idea to other people and to animals, each a body in his model with a mind he can only guess at; to feelings, which arise in the body like any signal; and to things that are never seen and still modelled: atoms, gravity, energy, pressure, number, time, justice, God. Further out, the engine could run live, as a game in which a player moves him and watches the other views respond.

## 2. The claim the piece makes

The finished piece is one video of one moment, drawn three ways. Across the top, Third-Person on the left and First-Person on the right. Across the whole of the bottom, True-Person. All three play at the same time and show the same instant. The Third-Person picture is how we usually describe reality: a man in a room, seen from outside. The First-Person picture is what reaches his eyes: a field of light with hands in it. The True-Person picture exposes where the first two are made: inside a head. A still glass figure of him, the ghost, stands in the dark, and in its head a mind receives signals and builds and runs a model of the world and of its own body from them, bright where attention falls, nearly gone where it does not, and black where nothing is held. Above its head a small soft light, his soul, sends the words of his will into the head. The third-person description turns out to be a thing only a mind holds. His mind holds its own third-person description, a body in a room built from memory and sense, and the viewer's mind holds the one at the top left. What is in the dark is unknown, and it is most of everything.

## 3. Three views, three names

| Place | Name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Top left | Third-Person | Him seen from outside: a camera in the room, a point of view and not the world's. It moves, gently, and never cuts. |
| Top right | First-Person | His eyes. Photoreal, meaning as close to a photograph as can be made. Nothing drawn on it that an eye could not produce, bar the faint edge tint allowed in section 5. |
| Bottom, full width | True-Person | A still glass figure of him in the dark, a soft light above its head that is his soul, and inside the head the mind's own reality engine: a running simulation of the room and of his body, built from the signals that reach it and moved by the words the soul sends in. The view with the most happening and the one that takes the most work to read, so it gets the widest frame and the taller row, with the two top views sharing the rest. The other two are recognisable at a glance. |

The three names share one structure on purpose. "True-Person" names the view that shows what is actually going on: the other two pictures are made here. It is also the picture least like the room, since most of the room is missing from it, and that is the point.

**One world, three drawings.** Any view can be reached from any other, because all three are drawn from one scene, and every change is continuous: surfaces drain and cameras travel, and nothing breaks into dust, grain or points on the way. There are four moves and no others: Third-Person to First-Person and back, and Third-Person to True-Person and back. First-Person never becomes True-Person directly, because the moment the camera leaves his eyes it is a camera outside him, a third person's, so the way from the eye to the mind runs out into the room first and from the room into the dark, and the way back runs out of the dark into the room and from the room in through his eyes. Third-Person becomes First-Person by the camera travelling from its place in the room, round his left side to behind his head and in through the back of it to his eyes, the pass through the head covered by a blink, the frame dark for the length of a blink and opening as his eyes open, the camera arriving already looking where he looks, the room never changing on the way. First-Person becomes Third-Person by the mirror of that move: out through the back of his head on a blink and round his right side back to the place in the room it left, so that the two moves together make one circle around him and not a path retraced. Third-Person becomes True-Person without the camera leaving its place, since a camera outside him is what the two views share: the room goes dark around him from the walls inward, his body drains to white glass and stills into the ghost's standing pose, the soul's light comes up above the head, and in the skull the model comes up, holding him in the posture the room just saw and as much of the room as his mind still has. True-Person becomes Third-Person by the mirror of that: the model sinks into the skull, the light above the head goes with the dark it stood in, the ghost takes his posture and his surfaces come back, and the room comes up around him from him outward. In either direction the camera the viewer is riding keeps its place through the change and becomes the other view's camera, easing from there onto that camera's own road. Taken in the showcase order, Third, First, Third, True, the moves make one circle around him and then the world falling away, and the moves back, each by its mirrored route, bring the world back. The moves are made while he is up, where the way round behind his head is open, and the change from room to mind at a moment when his attention is narrow, so that the ghost stands where he stood and what the viewer has just seen of the room comes back inside the skull as a few faint grey plates around one bright thing, and the lesson is plain.

**The showcase video.** Third-Person first, to set the scene. Then it turns into First-Person, so the viewer learns that the two are one world and sees it from behind his eyes. Then First-Person turns back into Third-Person by the other side of him, and Third-Person turns into True-Person, to expose that the description they trusted is the kind of thing a mind makes and was happening inside one all along, and that what is in the dark is unknown and so much more, and that above the head is a light the other two views never show. Then the full layout, all three at once. In the piece itself all three run from the first frame; the showcase video is where the order is taught.

## 4. The True-Person view

The examples below point at the first scene, described in full in section 7: a man asleep before dawn, an alarm, a phone fumbled and caught, a small pain, the sun cresting the ridge, a walk to the window with a hand up against the glare and a hiccup on the rug, his toe catching its curled edge, a stretch, his eyes closing in the sun, and a chime that takes him back to the bed and the phone.

**The ghost.** A still glass figure of him, white, full size, standing in the dark with his arms out a little from his sides, drawn as glass whose outline is a shade brighter than its fill. It never moves and nothing is drawn on it. It is the person as an object, and everything happens inside its head, the model, the simulated body at the model's centre, every signal, the rays, the waves and the words of recognition, except the soul above it and the words it sends in. The dark around the ghost and the dark inside its skull where the model holds nothing are the same dark.

**The soul.** One thing in the True-Person view is outside the ghost's head: a soft white disc the size of his closed hand just above the crown, a hand's width clear of the glass, brightest at its centre and softening only at its rim, the rim a narrow band, so that it reads as a soft light and never as a haze or a point, a light and not a surface, like the sun's disc but soft where the sun's is sharp. It is his soul. It is there from the first frame to the last, as still as the ghost, and it never dims, because it is not a thing he is aware of; it is the one who is aware. What it does is will. When he chooses to do a thing, a word leaves the light, white and lit from the moment it leaves, short, upright and facing the camera like the words of recognition, and drops through the crown into the skull, through whatever the model holds above the simulated head, the ceiling for most of the scene, which it does not light, to the simulated head, where it goes out as it lands with no flash; and the simulated body does what the word says, and the body in the other two views does the same, since he acts on the model. One word stands for the whole of the act it names and not for each motion in it, so that "go" carries him to the sill and "back" sits him on the bed: "stop" at the alarm, "up", "look" at the crest, "stand", "go", "stretch", "back" when he turns from the window, "answer" at the phone, and a stream of small words while he types. Between the words the light is quiet. No word comes for what the body does on its own, the breath, the heartbeat, the blink, or for what it does before the will can speak, the head that jerks up in the doze, the hand that snaps after the falling phone, the arms that lift at the hiccup, the hand that comes up against the glare, the head that turns to the chime. Inside the skull the mind keeps its own picture of the soul, and keeps it in a different place: a small white disc at the centre of the simulated body, lower than the heart and smaller than it, its face even and its rim soft but definite as the light above is, and it does not beat as the heart does. It comes up as a word lands and he feels himself the one acting, the part of the body around it coming up grey with it, and sinks between words to the faint light a held thing keeps. The mind puts its soul inside itself; the view puts it outside the glass. The light above the head is the one thing in the piece drawn neither in the room nor in the model: no sense delivers it, no memory holds it, and no camera in the room can see it.

**The True-Person camera.** The view is drawn by a camera in the dark with the ghost. It moves gently and never cuts, one continuous move over the whole scene, like the Third-Person camera's. It starts wide and in front of the ghost, the whole figure in the frame, and comes in toward the head as the mind wakes, until the skull and the light above it fill the frame and the model inside the skull can be read; for most of the scene the body is implied by the shoulders at the bottom of the frame. It is in front of the face and a little above while he sits and dozes, at his right side for the fumble, and it comes round behind the head at the crest, so that it looks through the back of the skull along the rays from the simulated eyes as they sweep the lit room. It stays behind and above for the walk and the window, eases back for the stretch until the ghost's shoulders are in the frame and the lit body inside the skull still reads, and goes wide as his eyes close, the whole ghost in the frame from behind with the warmth ray and the sun ahead of it. The chime brings it round to the ghost's side, where the back of the skull filling again is seen whole, then in behind the head for the walk to the bed and the phone, and at the end it goes wide again and comes round to the front, where it began, the whole ghost in the frame with the screen bright in its head and the glow of the will under it, and settles there. From behind the head the fan of rays goes away from the viewer to the surfaces it lands on, which is the shot the rays are for; from the front or the side the same rays are seen crossing the skull, and nothing is hidden from any angle. Anything the model holds between the glass and the simulated body on the camera's side thins to its edges like a far surface, the ceiling for instance while he lies on his back and the camera is in front of the ghost's face, so that nothing ever hides the simulated head, or whatever of the body he holds, from the camera.

**Inside the head.** The model sits inside the skull with the simulated head at its centre, aligned with the ghost's head: facing where the ghost faces, upright where the ghost is upright. The model is drawn relative to that head, because the mind holds the world the way the head is turned in it. So the simulated head keeps its place and its bearing inside the skull, the simulated body hangs from it in whatever posture he is in, and the room turns and slides around them as he turns and moves. Lying on his back reads, in his head's own frame, as standing against the bed with the ceiling in front of his face, because that is where the room is relative to his head. Sitting up, the room swings until the floor is under him and the window ahead. Walking, the room slides back past him and the window comes to him; turning from the window, the whole room wheels round him until the bed is ahead. The violet plumb line of his balance sense hangs to where the inner ear says down is, so while he lies it points at the bed behind him, and it swings to the floor as he rises. The model is drawn to fit the skull: the simulated body a few times smaller than the ghost's head, the room around it filling the skull, the far wall at the skull's front whichever wall he faces, the window there once he faces it, and whatever the mind holds beyond the skull's reach, in any direction, at the glass, as the ridge and the sun are ahead of him at the window. The words of recognition hang inside the skull too, to one side of the simulated head. The flash of a signal arriving at the head lands on the back of the simulated head as a small thing, not a bloom over the frame.

**The model.** The mind's simulation of reality is its own reality engine. It holds the room and his body in one space and keeps running whether or not a sense is feeding it: what it holds stays in place, and moves as the mind expects it to, while nothing is looking. It is drawn as surfaces: the same walls, floor, bed, table and window the other two views show, in their true shapes and places wherever a sense has had them, and what lies beyond the skull's reach at the glass, at the size it has from the simulated eyes, so that one view can turn into another without anything changing shape. Nothing in it is made of dots, grain or sparks. Each surface is a plate lit evenly from within, in the colour of the sense that put it there, since the mind has no sun. Every plate carries its edges as a thin line of its own colour, a shade brighter than its face, so a wall and a floor of one colour still meet at a visible corner; that line is finish, not attention. Surfaces thin with distance from the simulated body rather than dim, because brightness is kept for attention: near him a plate is whole, farther off its face goes and only its edges remain, like glass, and beyond that nothing. A dim surface is a whole plate at low light; a far one is an outline at any light. So the room is solid and the mountains, when they arrive, are a far ridge line at the glass. Among what sight lays, the sun is the one exception: a light and not a surface, it keeps its face as a disc at any distance, as the cyan disc that marks a sound does.

The model holds only what has entered the mind, and holds it only while it is in mind: what he thinks of, the mind brings up, and what he forgets fades out, to come back when he thinks of it again. In mind means attended to, thought of or acted on: the floor he walks on and the walls he places himself between stay held while he is in them; a thing he has finished with, like the phone on the table, leaves, and what he has turned his back on and stopped thinking of, like the bed and the table while he stands in the sun, goes with it, to come back the moment something calls it to mind. He acts on the model and not on the room: his hand goes where the model puts the phone and his foot where it puts the floor, and when the model is wrong the body finds out. Light reaches an eye along rays, and the mind's picture of seeing is drawn the same way: a fan of gold rays from the simulated eyes out to the surfaces they strike, each ray a continuous line, the fan dense enough to read as a beam and never as a scatter, brightest at the centre of the gaze. Where the rays land the surface comes up gold; where they do not reach, the model keeps what it had. The rays drawn are the simulated eyes' own: the rays the First-Person picture is made of, which in that view produce the picture and are not themselves seen. No other rays of sight appear. The Third-Person camera is a camera in the room and shows only in its own view, and the light of the sun and of the screen is not drawn as rays, since the gold rays are seeing and not light. Touch lays the patch of a surface where skin meets it: the phone is known by touch before it is seen, and the remembered table edge comes up magenta under the knuckle. Hearing arrives as waves. A sound is drawn as cyan waves spreading from where it is judged to be, through the model to the simulated ears, their height following the sound's loudness and their rhythm its peaks and troughs, slowed to what an eye can follow: the alarm as even pulses, the crow as one rising and falling swell, the rap as a single short wave. The source is marked with a flat cyan disc facing the camera in the judged direction, its size growing with his doubt about the direction, its face kept at any distance, and its edge always definite. It sits on a surface when the model has one there, as on the table top at the alarm, and at the boundary of the model when it has none, as for the rooster, heard at the window before anything beyond it is held. Warmth lays a direction: an orange ray from the warmed skin out toward its source, running as far as the model reaches that way, the sun as a ray before it is ever looked at and a disc at the ray's end once it is seen. The rest of the house is not in the model. He does not think of it in this scene, and the model holds nothing he has neither sensed nor thought about, beyond the gaps it fills in around what it holds, the filled-in state below. His own body is in the model on the same terms as the room: under the rays the simulated hand comes up gold like the phone it holds, a touched fingertip is magenta, a limb he feels the place of is mint, a limb he holds from memory alone is grey, and a limb he neither senses nor thinks of is dark. The signals drawn on the body, fields, comets and rivers, are drawn over whatever state it is in there.

**Four states of knowing.** Every region of the model is in one of four states. Colour and finish tell the state; brightness tells attention, down to the faint floor a held thing keeps, and only a thing he has forgotten falls through that floor to dark.

| State | Looks like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Sensed now | A surface in the colour of the sense feeding it, sharp. Where two senses feed one place, the colour of the one he attends to. | A signal is arriving from there right now. |
| Remembered | The same surface drained to grey, sharp while the memory is fresh. It softens as the memory goes stale, toward the look of the filled-in state in the next row, and stays in view, at whatever finish it has reached, while the thing is in mind. When he forgets the thing it fades to dark from that finish, and it comes back grey, at the finish it had, the moment he thinks of it again. | It was sensed earlier and the model keeps it without new evidence: the room on waking, the room at his back, the phone after it leaves his hand. |
| Filled in | A grey plane in the shape the mind gives the thing, flat and even inside, with no detail in it, soft only at its edges, the softening a narrow band no wider than a hand with the corners still readable, so it reads as a surface out of focus and never as a haze in the air. | Never sensed, assumed: the far side of the table, the space under the bed. The mind's gap-filling, shown as what it is, and sometimes wrong. |
| Dark | Nothing. | Nothing is held there: no signal, no memory, no guess, or a memory he has let go. Most of the model, most of his own body most of the time, and it stays in view around and beyond every surface. |

The scene is built to move regions between these states. He wakes in the dark and the room comes up around the simulated body from memory, grey, sharp where he knows it and soft where the memory has gone stale or he has never looked. When the sun crests the mountains and he lifts his head, the rays sweep the lit room and every surface they land on turns gold. The floor, which his narrowed eyes never go to, stays soft grey, remembered and stale, until his toe finds the rug edge. The phone, set down on the table, drains to grey and is gone from the model by the time he reaches the window, forgotten, and the bed and the table follow it while he stands in the sun; one chime brings all three back, grey, at the finish they had. At the crest the model grows past the room for the first time, as his mind lays the sun and the ridge beyond the window, and at the window sight fills what the window frames. With his eyes closed in the sun, the gold drains to grey and the room he saw becomes the room he holds. At the end, with his eyes on the screen, that whole room sinks to faint grey behind one bright rectangle and the glow of the will under it.

**The simulated body.** The mind simulates his body as it simulates the room, and the figure at the centre of the model is that simulation: a figure of him, head to foot, a few times smaller than the ghost's head, and unlike the ghost it moves. It is part of the model and drawn by the model's rules, in the same four states as the room, and no more of it is drawn than he holds. Where his eyes are on it, as on the hand around the phone or the hand he raises against the sun, it comes up gold like any surface the rays land on. Where another sense is on it, it is in that sense's colour: magenta at a touched fingertip, orange on the warmed face, lime in a working muscle, mint along a limb whose place he feels. Where he holds it in mind with no sense on it, as the legs he knows are under the blanket, it is grey, from memory, and where he is not aware of it at all it is dark, which is most of the body most of the time. The heart he never sees appears inside the chest when he thinks of it, and so does any part of him he turns his mind to; what he never attends to is not there, and the whole body is drawn at once only when he feels all of it, in the stretch. The simulated head holds the model's centre whether or not it is lit, since every comet and river ends at it and the rays leave it, and the flash of each arrival shows where it is. The body is placed by proprioception, the sense of where his own limbs are, and corrected by touch, balance, effort and the sight of his own hands. The First-Person view belongs to it: its eyes are the first-person camera, what reaches them is the First-Person picture, and the rays of sight leave them. Every signal that arises in the body is drawn on it at the matching point, and sound reaches it at the ears. It lies, sits, stands, walks and sits again, and inside the skull each posture hangs from the fixed head, so that lying stands it against the bed, and it is as fallible as the model of the room: it is where the mind believes the body is, which is where the body is until the mind is wrong, as it is for a moment when his toe catches the rug. No state of it is white. White is the ghost's and the soul's, and the breath and the heartbeat, drawn over the chest, the glow the mind keeps of its soul at the body's centre below them, and a lit word are the only other white things in the view.

**The signals.** Comets and rivers run along the simulated body from the point where a sensation arises to its head. Sight and warmth run the other way, outward from the simulated body into the model, and hearing comes in from outside to the ears. Balance hangs instead: a violet plumb line, a weighted string from the inner ear to where the inner ear says down is, wherever the head's frame puts that, which swings when the head moves and settles when it is still. Events are comets: a bright pulse that runs along a thin thread of its own colour, the thread drawn from the point to the head for as long as the pulse is on it, a short tail behind the pulse, a flash on arrival, then nothing. Continuous senses are rivers: a steady ribbon of light whose width, not brightness, is the strength of the sensation. Conditions on the skin, warmth, moving air and cloth sliding over it, are fields: a patch of colour on the simulated body where they act, whose spread, not brightness, is the strength of the condition. Effort is a field too, lime on the working muscle inside the simulated body rather than on its skin. All of them are continuous light; none is made of particles. Where two senses act on one patch of the simulated body, as on the model, only the one he attends to shows. Colour says which family of sense it is, the families grouped by where in the body the sensation comes from: gold for sight; blues for what arrives through the air, hearing and airflow; warm hues for what happens at the skin, touch in magenta, temperature in orange, pain in salmon; greens for the body's own motion sense, proprioception in mint and effort in lime; violet for balance in the inner ear, the sense of gravity. Within a family, the hue and the shape tell the senses apart. White is kept for the self.

**The view maps awareness.** This is the rule that organises the whole panel. The view does not show everything that is happening to him; it shows what he is aware of, and how much. A sense he is not aware of is off: no comet, no river, no field, however strong the stimulus. The rule covers his own body: a limb he is not aware of is not drawn, and the simulated body is whole only when he feels all of it, as in the stretch. What the mind holds but is not attending to is almost off, faint enough that the viewer's eye goes to the lit thing and present enough that what he still holds of the room stays readable. Brightness is attention and only attention. A tiny pain can be the brightest thing on screen. The warmth on the backs of his hands at a sill he has stopped noticing fades to nothing while the warmth is still there. Attention is a budget with a ceiling: the light in the view can never exceed it, so at the ceiling nothing brightens without something else dimming, and one thing seizing attention dims the whole model under it. The budget need not be spent. Asleep, drifting or settled, the whole view sinks toward dark. The one thing the rule does not govern is the soul's light above the head, which is not something he is aware of but the one who is, and holds steady at every moment. A word of its keeps that light on its way down and goes out as it lands; the glow it raises at the simulated body's centre is attention, like everything else in the skull, and spends the budget.

**The automatic layer.** Breath and heartbeat run the whole time, and most of the time he is not aware of them, so most of the time they are not drawn at all. The scene makes breath rise into awareness three times and the heart twice. After the pain lands he catches his breath, and for a few seconds the held breath and the quickened heart, a swell of the simulated chest and a throb at its core, white, take the budget from the pain. In the stretch, a deep breath swells the whole chest wide and white. With his eyes closed in the sun, breath and warmth are the only bright things, until the chime. Between those moments they are gone from the view, though they never stop. Blinks are a third automatic process. They happen in the First-Person view, unnoticed moments of darkness, and never reach the True-Person view, because he never notices one. No word of the will comes before any of these, and none before the reflexes either, the head that jerks up in the doze, the hand that snaps after the falling phone, the arms that lift at the hiccup, the hand that comes up against the glare, the head that turns to the chime: the body makes them before the will has spoken, and the soul's silence at those moments says as much as its words. This rising and falling of things that happen on their own is one of the things the piece is for.

**Pain.** A small pain is in the first scene on purpose. It arrives late, along the same path as the touch that caused it, because pain travels slowly. It throbs at the knuckle on the simulated body rather than flashing, in time with the heart, and it holds attention far beyond its size. It stays the whole time afterwards, dim under everything that follows, because a pain is never quite unattended, and it flares once more when the hands take his weight on the sill. That makes it the clearest demonstration of the awareness rule.

**Recognition.** To the left of the simulated head, away from the table and the hand, inside the skull, in the dark, clear of the paths the signals take, hangs a loose jumble of words, grey and unlit: the names of things in this scene and of things that are not in it. The words are fixed to the simulated head, so they keep their place in the skull while the room turns. Each word stands upright, faces the camera, overlaps no other word and is large enough to read; unlit, they sit a step darker than any surface he still holds, under the room. When a sensation is matched to a thing he knows, its word lights white for a moment and fades: "alarm" at the first sound in the dark, "phone" when his hand finds it, "sun" at the crest, "rug" at the toe, and "phone" again when the chime calls it back. Recognition comes a beat after the sensation and never before it, and no word lights for a thing he has neither sensed nor thought about. These are not the words of the will: those come from the soul above the glass, lit from the moment they leave it, and go in; these hang inside, grey, and light when a thing is known. It is drawn sparingly here. The ball scene is where it comes into its own.

**Scale.** In later scenes the True-Person camera can go in through the glass and down into the model, to the atoms a ball is made of, which were never sensed and are in the model anyway. The first scene does not use it.

## 5. The other two views

**First-Person.** Photoreal, and nothing is drawn on it: no icons, text, bars or colour overlays, with the one exception given below. The non-visual senses show only through what a real eye would show: the hands arriving where the body put them, the horizon dipping on the hiccup, the exposure blooming at the sunrise, the whole picture washing out white before the eye adjusts, his own hand black against the glare, the warm dark of lit eyelids when he closes his eyes in the sun, and his thumbs on the screen at the end. The one exception is a faint tint at the frame edge in the colour of whichever sense holds his attention at that moment, laid over the finished picture and weak enough that a stranger could not point at it. Sound is half of this view: the stereo mix, sound placed left and right as his ears would hear it, is part of what reaches him, and it is the sound of the whole piece. When all three views play together, the viewer hears what he hears.

**Third-Person.** Photoreal, and nothing is drawn on it. The camera moves, gently and smoothly, and never cuts: one continuous move over the whole scene. It starts wide in the corner at the foot of the bed on the window side, looking back along the bed, so that he faces the camera when he sits up and when the first light lands on his face. It slides along the window wall as he walks and is close at his side at the sill, his face in the light. When the chime turns him round it lets him walk away from it and slides back the way it came, and it ends where it began, wide at the foot of the bed, with him on the edge of the bed in full daylight, bent over the phone, where he first sat up in the dark. A moving camera also makes a quiet point: the third-person view is itself a point of view, not a view from nowhere.

## 6. Realism and pace

The reference for the look is the naturalism of a film like Avatar: bodies, cloth, hair, light and dust that are entirely simulated and never read as such. That rhymes with the thesis: the mind's model is also entirely simulated, and it reads as real. The bar is high in every aspect that carries the scene. Everything essential obeys physics: the phone falls, the blanket drags, the cloth hangs, the dust drifts, the light bounces. The body has weight and intention. And nothing is decorated: the True-Person view is the only stylised image, and even it obeys rules a viewer can learn.

What does not carry the scene may bend for it. The sun climbs faster than nature so the dawn fits the scene. A rooster crows at the moment the sun crests the mountains, as if on cue. Neither reads as false, because everything a viewer could check against their own body is true.

The pace is unhurried and natural. The scene takes the time its story needs and lingers where the story allows, so long as every second holds the viewer.

## 7. The first scene: a morning

**Setting.** A bedroom before sunrise, its window open and facing east over mountains. He is asleep on his back under a blanket, his head on the pillow. A bedside table stands on his right, beside the pillow and wide enough that its near corner sits at his hip once he is up, a hand's width out from the mattress, its top level with the mattress, with his phone on its near edge. When he sits up and swings his legs out on that side, the window is in front of him. A rug lies between the bed and the window, its near edge curled up a little. The room is dim and blue, the window the only pale thing in it. Both hazards are in the Third-Person frame before they matter: the gap beside the table in the opening frames, the curled rug edge when he sits up.

The scene opens on closed eyes in the dark and closes on open eyes on a screen in full daylight. In between, the senses build the model, he loses half of it, and a sound gives it back.

## 9. Scenes after this one

- **The ball.** He sees a ball, picks it up, bounces it. The True-Person camera goes in through the glass and down into the model while he contemplates it, to atoms he never sensed, and the words light up with what he recognises. The engine of section 1 gains scale here, and recognition comes into its own.
- **Other people.** Someone else comes into the room. His mind builds them as it builds the table, a surface where sight lands and a voice as waves, and gives them what the table never gets: a head with a model of its own inside it that no sense of his can reach, drawn filled in, grey, his guess at what they hold. A mind modelling a mind, and the guess can be wrong like any filled-in thing.
- **Animals.** A dog on the rug, a bird at the sill. Each is a body in the model with an inner life he grants it by guess, the dog's head holding a small grey world and the bird's almost nothing, so the piece can show how much he gives each and how little of it he can check.
- **Feelings.** Fear, grief, joy, missing someone. They arise in the body like any signal and are drawn on the simulated body where they are felt, in the chest, the throat and the gut, and they bend the model around them: a feared thing holds attention and stays bright long after the sense of it has gone, and a person he misses is held in the model with no sense feeding them at all.
- **Unseen things.** Gravity, energy, pressure, number, time, justice, God. Each is a thing never seen and still modelled. Gravity and pressure are already felt in the first scene, as the plumb line and as push; what no sense delivers is the law behind the feeling. The filled-in state holds a guess about a place; for these it holds a thing with no place. The soul is already in the first scene, as the light above the glass head and as the glow the mind keeps at the centre of the simulated body, and these scenes take the same step for the rest.
- **Live.** A version in which a player moves him and the other two views respond in real time. It runs on what the engine already is: a body, a world, what reaches the senses, and where attention goes.
