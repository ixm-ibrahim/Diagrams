# The True-Person view, and what I took from your rounds

## Part one. How the True-Person view works today

Three names are used throughout. The glass body is the glass figure the True-Person camera looks at, standing in the dark. The mind's model is everything drawn inside the glass body's skull. The simulated mannequin is the small body the mind keeps inside that skull, the mind's own body, drawn from the eyes' picture where the eyes see it and as its known shape where they do not.

Two clocks are used. A moment of the piece is named by the video player's second, as you read it off the showcase, which runs 145 seconds. A length of time or a rate inside a rule (a fade, a reaction, a beat) is given in his own time, the scene's clock, on which the rules are written. The film plays his time at eight tenths of its speed, so on the player every such length reads a quarter longer.

### The scene

The room, his body, the window, the trees, the ground, the range and the sky are one Blender scene. The Third-Person camera on its road looks at the room. His eyes are one point between them with a direction of its own: the eyes turn in the head, up to 35 degrees from where the head faces, toward the thing the timeline has him look at, and each look lands and leaves over 0.15 s. The First-Person view is what those eyes take in: their whole field, an ellipse 200 degrees across and 130 high, with the lids closing over it. The True-Person camera stands outside him and looks at the glass body's head. Inside the glass skull sits the mind's model: what his mind holds of the world.

### The eyes' pictures

Every frame, from the point between his eyes and in the eyes' own direction, five pictures are taken with a fisheye of 200 degrees across and 130 high, 1024 pixels square, each pixel sampled at one point so a pixel is one surface and not a blend:

- The scan: the colour of every surface the rays strike, its position, and which thing it belongs to.
- The backdrop: the sky, the ground and the range with everything nearer hidden, so what stands behind the trees and the window frame is known whole.
- The sky: the sky's shell alone, for the sky behind the range.
- The peel: where each ray leaves the first thing it strikes, which gives a round thing its depth along the ray.
- The pane: the window's glass reflecting on its own, with the world black behind it.

All five are at the eye's own exposure that frame.

With the lids fully shut the eyes take nothing in. The five pictures of such a frame are those of the last frame the lids stood open, and the model reads its field, its lids and what is seen from the eyes as they stood then. So nothing that happens in front of shut lids, his own hand rising or his head turning, reaches the model as sight.

### Where a point is placed

Every point the model draws, seen or remembered, is placed on its own ray from the eyes at the distance the mind believes.

- Within 6 m a distance is held as it is. Beyond that it is compressed, by the logarithm of how far it lies past that reach, so the trees, the range, the sky and the sun keep their order but not their scale.
- A thing small against its distance from the eyes, its radius under half the distance, is held whole: it is scaled as one thing and keeps its shape. A thing that spans its distance, a wall, the floor, the ground, the range, is placed point by point. The same thing changes kind as he moves toward it.
- Until he has looked at the far field for five seconds, everything beyond 6 m lies flat against the sky. That knowledge fades over a minute once he looks away.
- The model's scale is the mind's zoom. It puts the distance that holds nine tenths of his attention at the glass: one twenty-fourth of life size while attention is in the room, and far smaller with the range and the sun in mind. It zooms out at once and comes back in only after nothing far has been looked at for 0.7 s. A thought of a thing counts as attention at that thing's distance. Rays that land on his own body do not count, and the senses other than sight do not move the zoom.
- What the zoom still leaves beyond nine tenths of the glass radius is pressed toward the glass.

### How the seen picture is drawn

The scan of this frame is the seen picture, drawn in full colour.

- Sheets. Neighbouring rays that strike one thing are joined into a sheet, a connected surface, where the step between their hits is under 12 percent of their distance from the eyes, or runs evenly with the step beyond it, as on a flat surface seen at a low slant. Rays on two things are never joined.
- A sheet's edge. The edge is set where the picture's pixels say the thing ends, half a pixel farther, so that two things which touch overlap and no gap stands between them. Where two things touch, the edge stops at the other thing. For a thing held whole the edge steps across the eyes' ray. For a thing that spans its distance it steps along the thing's own surface.
- Faces the eyes see nearly edge-on. The picture has only a row or two of pixels for such a face. The thing's known shape draws it instead, in the seen look and in the picture's own colours for that face. Whether a face is turned toward the eyes is read in the room: for a flat-faced thing by the face itself, for a round thing by its smooth surface, so that its rim runs across its facets. While the seen picture goes at a long shutting of his eyes, or comes as they open, the same face is also drawn as memory under its seen drawing, and the two hand over by the share every other seen place does, so the rim of a round thing, or the edge-on side of a flat one, never stands open.
- A round thing's rim. On a round thing held whole the sheet runs to the last pixel clear of the thing's outline, and the known shape's tiles draw the rest. The outline is then the thing's own and not a staircase of pixels.
- Round or flat-faced is read off the thing itself. Its faces are taken together by the way they face, and it is round where no one direction holds 12 percent of its surface. A crown, a bush and a limb of his body are round. The window's frame, the bed, the table and the rug are flat-faced.
- Colour at an edge. A pixel beside an edge is a mix of its thing and of what stands behind it. On a thing that spans its distance it takes the colour three pixels inside its own thing. On a thing held whole the edge colours come from the known shape's tiles, which take the picture's pixels of their own face.
- A pixel no sheet takes, on a thing one pixel thin, is drawn as a small square facing the eyes.
- The window glass's reflection is drawn as squares alone and added as light, since glass passes the rays. Each is cut to its pixel's cell of the glass. At the pane's outline, where a pixel has no neighbour on the glass along one axis of the scan, a square is no larger than its pixel's own footprint.
- The picture's grain. One pixel of the eyes' picture is one point of a sheet. On a face seen at a slant a pixel covers centimetres, so an edge moves by that much as his head moves. This belongs to the stand-in scan of 1024 pixels. The piece uses 2048.

A place the eyes do not see is not drawn at all unless a thought, or the eyes being shut, has him remember it. From the True-Person camera such a place is black. That black is what the view is for: it shows the difference between what he sees and what he thinks is there.

### Seen and remembered

The lids wipe the seen picture as the First-Person lids do. They dim what they cover to a fifth of its brightness, never to nothing.

- Shutting. The eyes must stay shut 0.35 s before the seen picture goes. It then fades over half a second, and the last picture the eyes held rises over 0.3 s and stands in its place for as long as they stay shut. While the one gives way to the other, the last picture and what completes it stand together just behind the seen picture, and they come forward together at the moment the seen picture's fade has run out. So the remembered bushes and range stand before the remembered ground and sky from the first frame, and nothing of the seen picture is hidden at a stroke.
- Opening. The last picture fades over 0.25 s, and the seen picture comes in as the lids uncover it.
- A blink shorter than 0.35 s moves the lids alone.
- Before the eyes first open, at player 20 s, there is no picture. The last picture and what completes it are not drawn at all, and the eyes being shut light nothing. While he wakes with his eyes shut the head holds only what a thought or a sense brings.
- The lids' cover is read at three marks, which answer three questions. Which picture is kept as the last one: the last frame with the lids less than a tenth closed. Whether the eyes still take in the far view: while the lids are less than half closed. When the eyes count as shut: when the lids are fully closed.

Three things stand in the head as memory.

- The last picture: the picture the eyes held when they shut, standing whole while they stay shut. It is lit by the eyes being shut alone. A thought lights nothing of it. It is drawn by the seen picture's own rules: sheets, their edges, and the faces seen nearly edge-on from the known shapes as the eyes stood at that frame. The window glass's reflection is held with it.
- What completes a picture: the ground, the range and the sky behind the near things, and the sky behind the range, from the backdrop and sky pictures. It is drawn only where a thought or the shut eyes light it: this frame's while the eyes are open, the last picture's while they stay shut and until the last picture has gone after an opening. It stands a hair behind the seen picture, and its sheets run two pixels under the seen picture's rim, so the two close.
- The known shapes, described in the next section.

Every memory has the same look, whatever it belongs to.

- Its colour drains toward its own grey, the grey of its own brightness, half of the way at most. The drain comes with the memory's age, over 2.5 s.
- Its brightness falls to 0.82 of the seen picture's over six seconds. It is fully opaque: a thing held in memory hides what stands behind it, as a seen thing does.
- A known shape is always drawn at the oldest look, since the unseen side of a thing and the memory of a thing are one. What completes this frame's picture takes the same oldest look: ground he only thinks is behind a bush does not look like ground he sees.
- It ripples. There is one ripple for every remembered thing, measured as an angle from his eyes and not by the thing's size or the scan's pixels. It moves a point across the eyes' ray by up to 3.9 degrees. Its waves are 5.9 degrees tall and four times as wide. They change shape where they stand, about one and a half times a second, and travel nowhere. The same wave carries light: a remembered tile is brighter or dimmer by up to four tenths as the wave swells and sinks at it, smoothly, with no grain.
  - A thing held whole ripples about its own centre, at its own place in the pattern and at its own time. So each bush wavers where it stands, and two bushes do not move together.
  - A thing set into another, or lying on it, ripples with the thing it belongs to: the window frame with its wall, the blanket with the bed, the rug with the floor, the lamp and the phone with the table. The two then do not part.
  - What spans its distance, the walls, the floor, the ground, the range and the sky, shares the one pattern whole.
  - His own body keeps a smaller ripple, four percent of each part's radius, as its known shape and in the last picture alike. He feels his body at every moment, whether or not he last saw that part.
- It holds still at a seam. Where a memory meets the seen picture it does not ripple, and the hold falls away over about eight degrees. The last picture needs no such hold, since it stands only while no seen picture stands.
- It gives way to sight. A remembered tile is not drawn where the eyes see its place, and a remembered place the eyes see again goes as the seen picture comes to stand.
- It is lit by the thought on its thing, times his awareness, or by the eyes being long shut, times his awareness. Unlit, it is not drawn. Lit under a tenth, it thins out as it dims, so a thing fading from mind goes, and does not stand as a dark shape in front of what lies behind it.
- The lids dim it wherever it lies inside his field of view, as they dim the seen picture. In a blink the remembered back of a tree goes dark with the front he sees.
- The last picture is drawn one and a half percent nearer the eyes than the seen one, so the two never fight for a pixel while one takes over from the other.

### The known shapes

Every mesh thing of the room and the outside under 50 m across has a shape in the model: the thing as the mind knows it. The ground, the range and the sky have none and are remembered from the backdrop and sky pictures alone.

A shape is placed point by point exactly as its hits would be. So that its faces follow the same curve as the seen picture of the same thing, every shape is cut until no edge of it is longer than two degrees as seen from the middle of the room, twelve centimetres at the least. A wall is otherwise one face four and a half metres long, whose middle stays on a straight line while the seen wall bends toward the glass.

A shape is drawn as tiles, four to each footprint of the scan scattered over its faces, each lit on its own by the shape's smooth surface and each rippling as every remembered tile does. A tile of it is not drawn where the eyes see its place this frame. So from every camera a seen surface shows as seen, and only its unseen parts show as memory. The far side of every thing is this shape.

Its colour is its material's colour under the known light: the sky's level indoors or outdoors, and the sun's share by the surface's facing and by how much of the sun's disc stands above the skyline, which is nothing before first light at player 50.25 s. How bright that light is drawn is read off the picture beside it: the middle brightness of the eyes' own scan of that frame over what the same things' known colours would give, indoors and outdoors each from its own part of the scan, times 1.62. So a remembered wall sits as dark or as bright as the seen wall next to it, a little brighter, at every moment of the dawn and whatever the eye's exposure. While no picture stands in the head, before the eyes first open, it is the mind's own light, a fixed level of 0.3.

A shape is shown only while a thought of its thing is on, times his awareness, or, for a part of his own body, while a sense lights that part.

### The thoughts

The thoughts come from the timeline. Each names a set of things, rises over 0.4 s, holds, and fades by its own time on a clock that runs at one over his awareness, so a thought is gone sooner the less aware he is. Every thought is multiplied by his awareness, so a thought in his sleep lights nothing. A thought's things come up as their shapes first, colourless, and their colour arrives 1.5 s after the thought begins, over one second. A thought lights a thing's known shape and what completes the picture there, and pulls the mind's zoom to that thing's distance.

- The alarm, player 5 to 12.5 s: the phone and the nightstand, fading over 3 s.
- The bed, from player 13 s to 20 s, fading over 3 s. It alone rises slowly, over 3 s, with his rising awareness: its shape thickens in over the first two fifths of that time and its light is whole at the end.
- The rest of the room as he sits up with his eyes open: the east wall and the window, the floor and the rug, the ceiling, the west wall, the north wall and the south wall, one every 0.4 s from player 21.25 s, held to player 27.5 s and fading over 3 s.
- The view, player 85 to 100 s: the ridge, the ground and the sun, fading over 1 s. This is where what his eyes cannot see fills in behind the bushes and the trees.
- The chime, player 111 to 115.4 s: the phone and the nightstand, fading over 6 s.

### The sun

The sun is a ball a little beyond the sky, six degrees wide from the eyes. The half the eyes' rays reach is seen, at the sun's strength. The rest is memory.

Nothing of the sun is in his head before its light reaches his open eyes. The rays toward it are followed for 3,000 m, past the range, so the range hides it until it clears the skyline. The sun counts as seen when any of five rays, to its centre and its four limbs, reaches the disc inside his field. How much of the disc stands above the skyline is read from 96 rays spread over it, and that share sets the warmth on his skin, the sun's part of the known light and the glow on his shut lids.

The remembered sun looks as every memory does: a grey ball, opaque, drained toward its own grey, dimmed with age, rippling by the one ripple with the wave's light across it, and thinning out as it fades. It is lit by the thought of the view, or by the eyes shut where the last picture held the sun.

His reaction to the first light follows the light. The sunlight comes down the wall behind him from player 49.8 s and reaches his open eyes at player 50.25 s. His lids begin to shut 0.13 s after that, his head flinches 0.15 s after, and his hand starts toward his eyes 0.25 s after, which on the player read 0.16, 0.19 and 0.31 s. The build measures the scene's own first light from the sun and the range as built, sets it beside the second the script names, and warns when the lids or the hand would move before the light.

### The soul, the mind's soul, and the sounds

The soul is a white ball of light on the line forty-five degrees behind and above the glass body's head, three hands clear of the skull, lit by the will and never out. Inside the mind's model, behind and above the simulated mannequin's head on the same line, sits the mind's own soul: a white disc sized with the model, never scanned, whose presence rises over 0.3 s at each word of will, holds a second and fades over five to a floor of 0.12, times his awareness. While he is not aware it stands four times its distance from the simulated mannequin's head, at the glass, and it comes back to its place as awareness rises. The sounds, and every other sense, are drawn on the simulated mannequin as the next section says.

### The senses on the simulated mannequin

One rule for all of them: a sense lights the part of the simulated mannequin it arises on, whether or not the eyes see that part, and nothing is ever drawn on the real body in the room. A sense is drawn on the outside of the body alone, its skin, the surface a ray from outside would strike. The stand-in mannequin is made of balls and tubes that run into one another, so wherever one piece lies inside another, more than four millimetres deep, nothing is drawn. A limb then reads as one limb with one outline.

The senses on the skin, touch with its pain and pleasure, proprioception and temperature, are drawn as a glow: one layer of light that stands 1.8 centimetres outside the skin, over the part's own tiles, which keep their own colour under it. The glow's light fades to nothing at the layer's own outline, and that outline lies outside the body's, so a glow has no edge where the body ends. Its colour is six tenths the sense's colour and four tenths the part's own colour, the sense's strength setting the brightness and not the blend. It wavers: a slow pattern over the body, fourteen cells to a room metre and changing about once a second, brightens and dims it by a fifth either way and turns its colour by a fortieth of the colour wheel either way. Nothing moves; only the light changes. The waver is on the skin's glows alone, not on sparks, rings, the arrow or the organs.

The senses on the organs, the ears, the eyes, the heart and the lungs, colour the organ's own tiles instead, seen, remembered and known alike, and those tiles are kept undrained and fully opaque. The red of a hurting ear and of the eyes in the glare stands alone, with none of the organ's colour.

Where two senses share one patch of skin, the stronger shows in its own colour, and the two blend only where their strengths lie within a tenth of each other. They are not mixed otherwise, since touch's magenta and warmth's gold mixed make red, which is pain's.

The strength of every sense the timeline names is the timeline's strength for the event times the attention the event gets under the budget. His awareness dims what the engine derives by rule: a touch at rest, warmth at rest, the glare, the organs, the band on a moving part, balance, the sparks from contacts and the rings. It does not dim again what the timeline names, since the timeline's own number already says how strongly he feels that thing at that moment.

- Hearing. A sound sends one ring every tenth of a second while it lasts, in a radar's green. Each source of sound has its own shade of that green, so sounds that ring together can be told apart: the first source to sound in the timeline, the phone, has the green itself, and each later source takes the next shade from a short list, turned a little toward blue or toward yellow, paler or fuller. Each ring is as bright as the sound is loud in that tenth, a little dimmer the less the sound is attended, and gone as far as he is not aware. A ring glows from a bright line at its outer edge softly inward over eight hundredths of its radius. It starts at the place the mind judges the sound to come from, placed in the model as a seen point at that distance is placed. So a bird's rings start out on the ridge, and are as small there as the ridge is in the model, and the phone's rings start at the phone. A ring travels from that place to the nearer ear in three tenths of a second and on past the farther ear, then goes over twelve hundredths. The ear a ring crosses lights green for the moment of the crossing, held eight hundredths of a second so it reads at the film's rate. A sound louder than 0.7 on the timeline's scale, the alarm at 0.8 and the rooster at 0.9, lights the crossed ear red instead, fading over a second. A crossed ear is green or red, never a mix.

  The loudness comes from a stand-in waveform made for each sound until rung 6 brings the sound files: the alarm's beeps, two a second, each on for a fifth of a second; the slap that silences it; the knuckles' knock and the phone's thud; the rooster's crow of three seconds in four syllables, the third long and warbling; the birds' chirps; the phone's chime of one second in two notes ringing out. The same waveforms, laid end to end in the film's seconds, are the stand-in soundtrack under the showcase, so what is heard and what the rings show are one thing.

- Touch. Touch is contact, by one rule for everything that touches him. A point of a body part's surface is touched where it lies within one centimetre of another thing's surface, fading to nothing at four; or inside another thing's closed shape, since a stand-in mattress does not give way under him; or where the middle of the body part at that point lies inside another thing's closed shape, which has then given way and wraps the part there. Another thing is every mesh thing of the room, and every body part except the touched one and the parts joined to it at one joint, whose stand-in shapes overlap at the joint by construction. Three pairs two joints apart are left out with them, the chest with the head and the chest with each thigh: the stand-in trunk runs up into the head's ball and down into the tops of the thighs, so those pairs touch in every frame of the piece. That exception belongs to the stand-in mannequin and goes with it at rung 3. Nothing in the rule knows the bed from the floor or a hand from the head.

  So the back of the head lights where it lies on the pillow, the back, the seat and the legs where they lie on the mattress, the forearms and hands where the blanket's slab holds them, the seat and the soles while he sits, the soles alone while he stands, the hands where they meet the phone, the table, the sill or each other.

  The touched patch alone is drawn, as a glow in magenta over four tenths of the part's own colour. It is strongest where the contact is closest and falls away with the contact itself, to nothing four centimetres from the other thing, so the patch has no cut edge along the skin. The glow gives one and a half times the light of a white surface and covers what is behind it by 1.2 times its strength: a resting touch lets the part and the bed show through, a sharp one stands nearly solid. Its level is the timeline's strength for a touch the timeline names on that part, and a quarter of full, as far as he is aware, for every contact the timeline does not name. A touch lies between the body and the thing touched, so its glow is drawn through the limb.

When a spark strikes is read off the contact itself, by one rule for every case. Before the piece is drawn, every body part is measured against every thing of the room, and against every other body part that is not joined to it at one joint, at every one of the 3,480 frames: the gap between the two surfaces. A pair touches once its gap is 1.3 centimetres or less and has parted once the gap has opened to 3.1.
