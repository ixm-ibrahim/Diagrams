                  +

                    1. Opening

                    So we just read the khutbat al-hajah. That's the opening the Prophet ﷺ taught for every speech: you praise Allah, you ask His help and His forgiveness, testify your faith, and then three reminders from the Quran. Be conscious of Allah. Be mindful of Allah. Be mindful of Allah, and speak what is right.
                    +
                      Now. Today I want to look at one moment in a story most of us know: the story of Sulayman, alayhis-salam — Solomon — and the Queen of Sheba, as mentioned in the Quran. The scholars identify her as Bilqis. There's a part in the middle of it, only four verses, where the story seems to be saying one thing, and then you look again and it's saying something much closer to home. Because it isn't really about a king and a queen three thousand years ago. It's about whether we can see what's actually in front of us. And about what comes out of us when we're put on the spot. And that's a test that happens to each of us, more often than we notice.
                      +

                        2. From the hudhud to the throne

                        pause Most of us know the story. Sulayman, alayhis-salam, had armies of jinn and men and birds. A kingdom that no other person will ever be given after him. And one day he gets some news from the hudhud, one of those birds: "I've seen something you haven't. There's a nation to the south, in Saba — Sheba — ruled by a woman, and she has a great throne." And — subhanallah — instead of worshipping the creator of all things, they instead bow down to one of His creation - the sun.
                        +
                          And the hudhud says this about them:
                          +
                            وَزَيَّنَ لَهُمُ الشَّيْطَانُ أَعْمَالَهُمْ
                            +
                              "Shaytan made what they were doing seem pleasing to them," and that's what kept them away from the truth. It's not that they were unintelligent - in fact, the queen of this nation was actually known for her intelligence - but it was their hearts that prevented them from recognizing the truth. I mean, just like us, they see something beautiful, warm, responsible for their way of life. But they could only believe in what they could see with their eyes, and their hearts were blind as a result.
                              +
                                Now, Sulayman (as) realizes all this. And he isn't just a king, by the way: he is a prophet of God. His job is to lead his people for Allah's sake, but also to spread the truth and invite others into Islam. So he verifies the hudhud's truthfulness and sends it back with a letter, and tells it: "deliver this to them, then step back, and we'll see how they respond."
                                +
                                  Bilqis gets the letter, and she gathers her council for advice. It's in the name of a merciful, loving God, inviting them to Islam, but it came from a king... and she knows how worldly kings are. All they care about is power, and elevating themselves, and she even says in the Quran: "when kings enter a city, they ruin it, and they make its honored people humiliated." So she sees these conflicting messages: what she thinks she knows, and what this letter is communicating to her. And she does something very wise: instead of acting on her assumption, she decides to send a test to find out what kind of king Sulayman truly is. She says:
                                  +
                                    وَإِنِّي مُرْسِلَةٌ إِلَيْهِم بِهَدِيَّةٍ فَنَاظِرَةٌ بِمَ يَرْجِعُ الْمُرْسَلُونَ
                                    +
                                      "I'm going to send them a gift, and let's see what the messengers bring back." If he truly was the kind of king that was after power and riches and controlling other lands, then he would surely accept.
                                      +
                                        But Sulayman actually refuses the gift. He is a prophet of God - he doesn't get paid for what he does, and he certainly can't get bribed to stop fulfilling that responsibility. "What Allah has given me is better than what He's given you," he tells the emissaries, and he sends all of it back with a harsh warning. Now, did they fight after that? We don't know. Did she send word back and say, "fine, we'll come"? We don't know. What happened in between? We don't know. But all we do know is what she learned: this man cannot be bought, and he stands by his word. So, she sets out to see him herself.
                                        +
                                          And we know what happens next: Sulayman asks his court to bring him the queen's throne - the one thing in her kingdom she knows better than anything else - before she even arrives. And before he could even blink, faster than the speed of light, the throne appears before him. But he says something very unexpected after that. He says:
                                          +
                                            هَٰذَا مِن فَضْلِ رَبِّي لِيَبْلُوَنِي أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ
                                            +
                                              "This is my Lord's favor, to test me. Will I be grateful, or ungrateful?" A throne brought across a kingdom in an instant, with a power no other human being has had, and that's his first sentence? Subhanallah, the one actually being tested here isn't Bilqis. It's prophet Sulayman. And how does he pass this test? He doesn't say "look what I can do" or boast about his power - any other normal king would do that. Rather, he remembers that all he has came from Allah in the first place: "This is from my Lord."
                                              +
                                                When we get what we want, when we work hard to earn something, or something just lands in our lap — whether a test result, money, a spouse, a job, a child — what's the first thing out of our mouths? Are we grateful, or ungrateful?
                                                +

                                                  3. The question

                                                  And then it gets even more interesting. She's still on her way, and he has her throne. He then turns to his people and says something strange. "Change how it looks, just a little. Disguise it, just enough." pause
                                                  +
                                                    Why would he do that? Many of us know this story, and it might seem obvious. But what does the throne have to do with anything? What is Sulayman (as) trying to accomplish?
                                                    +
                                                      pause Most of us naturally think: to impress her. To show her what he can do, what kind of power he has — or maybe what kind of power Allah really has. That's the answer most of us think of, and it makes sense. He's a king with visibly miraculous power. How could she possibly reject his message after seeing that?
                                                      +
                                                        But it's not what the Quran says. pause
                                                        +
                                                          قَالَ نَكِّرُوا لَهَا عَرْشَهَا نَنظُرْ أَتَهْتَدِي أَمْ تَكُونُ مِنَ الَّذِينَ لَا يَهْتَدُونَ
                                                          +
                                                            "He said: disguise her throne for her. We'll see whether she can be guided, or whether she's one of those who can't."
                                                            +
                                                              "Impress" actually isn't in there. The word is نَنظُرْ. "We'll see." And by the way, that's the third time in this story someone says it: Sulayman, about how they'd respond to his letter. Bilqis, about verifying Sulayman's values with her gift. And now Sulayman again, about her. But it's strange: the one moment he gets something that would genuinely impress her, he covers it up?
                                                              +
                                                                Sulayman, alayhis-salam, is wiser than all of us. And he's using her own throne — her seat of power — to find out something about her. So what exactly is he watching for? And what does it have to do with guidance?
                                                                +

                                                                  4. Her answer

                                                                  So she arrives. And they ask her one question. It was said to her:
                                                                  +
                                                                    أَهَٰكَذَا عَرْشُكِ
                                                                    +
                                                                      "Is your throne, like this?"
                                                                      +
                                                                        That's the whole test. A closed-ended question. A yes, or no.
                                                                        +
                                                                          Put yourself in her shoes for a second. You're in a foreign court. There's a man in front of you with an army that you've never seen before, and it dawns on you that you're hopelessly outmatched. And the thing sitting there looks like something you left at home - weeks of travel away, under lock and key. If this is really your throne, what kind of power does this man really have? What can you really do, except try to get on this man's good side?
                                                                          +
                                                                            Let's review our options. She could say, "yes", while - in her head - she knows it's impossible for it to be there - letting Sulayman know that she is only saying that to please him, and she is the kind of person to say whatever to gain something she wants - regardless of the evidence. She can also say, "no", while knowing that no other throne could ever look like hers - letting him know that she is too stubborn to engage with evidence when presented to her, despite the evidence right in front of her.
                                                                            +
                                                                              So, what did Bilqis say? What answer did this intelligent person give, in the face of contradictory evidence? pause
                                                                              +
                                                                                قَالَتْ كَأَنَّهُ هُوَ
                                                                                +
                                                                                  "She said: it is as if it is." It looks like it. This is the right answer! And it was not a yes. And it was not a no. This might seem like a little thing, but how did this answer Sulayman's question? How can he know if she is someone to be guided or not?
                                                                                  +
                                                                                    Because she only stated what she knew, and nothing more. She avoided both of these traps subhanallah, and said exactly what she is positioned to say: "it only appears that way". This gives Sulayman exactly what he wanted to see: this person is willing to recognize evidence for what it is. And this is exactly what you need to know about someone before you show them a sign. And for those of you who know the story, he's preparing to show her one.
                                                                                    +
                                                                                      The Prophet ﷺ used that same little word, by the way. كَأَنَّ. "As if." When the angel Jibril — Gabriel — asked him what ihsan is, the highest level of worship, he said:
                                                                                      +
                                                                                        أَن تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ، فَإِن لَّمْ تَكُن تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاكَ
                                                                                        +
                                                                                          "To worship Allah as if you see Him. And if you don't see Him, He surely sees you." We don't see Him, in the same way that we see what is apparent to us. But He sees us - is closer to us than our own jugular vein! - and He hears exactly what we answer.
                                                                                          +
                                                                                            And you already heard this, before I even started. قُولُوا قَوْلًا سَدِيدًا. "Speak what is right." Sadid: a word that hits the mark. It goes exactly as far as what you actually know, and then it stops. That's what she just did.
                                                                                            +
                                                                                              That's actually not an easy test. It's hard to wrestle with the idea that the way you've lived your life - whether on a religious level or a social level or professional level - is actually, wrong. And her whole worldview is about to change.
                                                                                              +

                                                                                                5. The floor

                                                                                                The Quran says:
                                                                                                +
                                                                                                  قِيلَ لَهَا ادْخُلِي الصَّرْحَ ۖ فَلَمَّا رَأَتْهُ حَسِبَتْهُ لُجَّةً وَكَشَفَتْ عَن سَاقَيْهَا ۚ قَالَ إِنَّهُ صَرْحٌ مُّمَرَّدٌ مِّن قَوَارِيرَ ۗ قَالَتْ رَبِّ إِنِّي ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِي وَأَسْلَمْتُ مَعَ سُلَيْمَانَ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ
                                                                                                  +
                                                                                                    "She was told: enter the palace. And when she saw it, she thought it was deep water, and she lifted her dress to wade through it. He said: it's a palace, paved smooth with glass. She said: My Lord, I have wronged myself. pause confused And I submit, together with Sulayman, to Allah, Lord of all the worlds."
                                                                                                    +
                                                                                                      This time, nobody told her it was a test. There was no question to answer. She just looks at the floor and thinks she knows exactly what it is. حَسِبَتْهُ. "It appeared so to her". "She took it for water." She was sure enough to lift her dress. And she was wrong, about the floor she was standing on. The metaphor is strong subhanallah: the very foundation she was on was made of glass, and she couldn't see it for what it truly was.
                                                                                                      +
                                                                                                        And when he corrects her in one sentence, telling her that it's just glass and she's safe, she doesn't say, "you tricked me!" She doesn't say, "well, anyone would have thought that." She says: "My Lord, I have wronged myself." What? By thinking glass is actually water?
                                                                                                        +
                                                                                                          The true answer is actually beneath the surface: she wronged herself in the years of bowing to the sun. Believing only in what was apparent to them. And she had just been taught a subtle lesson in what happens when you believe in the apparent. Her eyes told her it was one thing, but it was really another. Her whole life she believed in what her eyes saw, and in a mere moment, her worldview instantly shatters.
                                                                                                          +
                                                                                                            But she was a unique individual. She was humble and intelligent enough to recognize her mistake for what it is. And the moment she does, the woman who had bowed down to the sun her entire life bowed down to لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ. "To Allah, Lord of all the worlds."
                                                                                                            +
                                                                                                              أَقُولُ قَوْلِي هَٰذَا وَأَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ لِي وَلَكُمْ وَلِسَائِرِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ، فَاسْتَغْفِرُوهُ إِنَّهُ هُوَ الْغَفُورُ الرَّحِيمُ.

                                                                                                                  6. What the surah is about

                                                                                                                  I said at the beginning that this isn't really about a king and a queen three thousand years ago. It's actually about us. And this is actually how the surah ends:
                                                                                                                  +
                                                                                                                    وَقُلِ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ سَيُرِيكُمْ آيَاتِهِ فَتَعْرِفُونَهَا ۚ وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ
                                                                                                                    +
                                                                                                                      "Say: all praise belongs to Allah. He will show you His signs, and you will recognize them. And your Lord is not unaware of what you do."
                                                                                                                      +
                                                                                                                        Subhanallah, look at the order of that verse. First, have gratitude that Allah is one who sends us signs that we will recognize. And then, from responding and engaging with those signs that we recognize, guidance will come. And, if I'm not mistaken - that's the whole point of the story we just read. Because the grateful person is the one who's already looking for the Giver - the Most Bountiful - in everything, so when a sign comes, he recognizes it. Sulayman gets a throne dropped in front of him, and the first thing he says is "this is from my Lord," and he's the one who ends up facilitating the queen's guidance. Her people look at the sun every day and thank the sun. So when the signs come, from beyond the sun, they can't see them; they've already decided how the world works. But for Bilqis: the moment she recognizes a sign, the first thing she does is to turn to the Lord of all the worlds. Imagine how grateful she must have felt to have finally been brought out of that darkness, from worshipping the sun.
                                                                                                                        +
                                                                                                                          Now it gets deeper. Remember the question I asked you at the beginning? Why did he disguise the throne? Most of us had an answer, with most of us having the same answer: "to impress her." With total confidence. That's actually the throne test! We just took it ourselves!
                                                                                                                          +
                                                                                                                            He will show us His signs. We will recognize them. Both of those are promises. What isn't promised is the timing. Razi points out that some people only recognize at the very end, when recognizing changes nothing. Bilqis recognized in the middle of a palace, with one sentence, while it still changed everything. That's the difference the surah is asking us to notice.
                                                                                                                            +

                                                                                                                              7. Gratitude and guidance

                                                                                                                              This lesson applies universally to our lives.
                                                                                                                              +
                                                                                                                                Everything is given to us. And the test is how we react. Sulayman said, "this is my Lord's favor, to test me. Will I be grateful, or ungrateful?" And listen to the Arabic: أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ. The word for ungrateful there is kufr. The same word the Quran uses for disbelief. And that's actually one of the Quran's recurring themes. In Surah al-Insan, Allah describes the whole human situation in one line:
                                                                                                                                +
                                                                                                                                  إِنَّا هَدَيْنَاهُ السَّبِيلَ إِمَّا شَاكِرًا وَإِمَّا كَفُورًا
                                                                                                                                  +
                                                                                                                                    "We guided him to the way: either grateful, or kafur." When guidance comes to you, there are exactly two responses to it. Gratitude, or denial. It's fainating: in the Quran, the opposite of gratitude isn't rudeness. It's kufr - disbelief. And this story is built on exactly that contrast.
                                                                                                                                    +
                                                                                                                                      Because the Quran describes her the same way Sulayman described himself: أُوتِيَتْ مِن كُلِّ شَيْءٍ, "she was given of everything." Two people, given everything. One says, "this is from my Lord." The other's whole nation looks at the sun, the thing that warms them and grows their food, and bows to it. And notice, the hudhud's complaint wasn't that they were ungrateful. It was that they bowed to the sun instead of to Allah, "who brings out what is hidden in the heavens and the earth." The One who actually provides. That's what ingratitude really is. It's not just forgetting to say thank you. But placing your gratitude in the wrong one.
                                                                                                                                      +
                                                                                                                                        And it's the thing that keeps us from being guided. Not because we're not smart enough - mashallah most of us are students and graduates and teachers and professors and professionals. But when something lands in our lap, whether a job, money, a spouse - how we react to that is a test. Sulayman's answer was "this is from my Lord." Her people's answer was the sun.
                                                                                                                                        +

                                                                                                                                          8. The sentence after

                                                                                                                                          And I'm going to end with this inshallah.
                                                                                                                                          +
                                                                                                                                            Her sentence was "My Lord, I have wronged myself." Those weren't new words. The first two human beings said them together, the first time anyone ever got something wrong. Adam and Hawwa, Adam and Eve: رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَا أَنفُسَنَا, "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves" - after eating from the tree. And long after her, Abu Bakr, radiyallahu anhu, the closest companion of the Prophet ﷺ, came to him and said, "teach me a dua I can say in my prayer." And the Prophet ﷺ taught him to say:
                                                                                                                                            +
                                                                                                                                              اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِي ظُلْمًا كَثِيرًا، وَلَا يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ، فَاغْفِرْ لِي مَغْفِرَةً مِنْ عِنْدِكَ، وَارْحَمْنِي، إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْغَفُورُ الرَّحِيمُ
                                                                                                                                              +
                                                                                                                                                "O Allah, I have wronged myself, greatly. And no one forgives sins except You. So forgive me, with a forgiveness from Yourself, and have mercy on me. You are the Forgiving, the Merciful."
                                                                                                                                                +
                                                                                                                                                  "I have wronged myself, greatly." This is a lesson for us Muslims, as well as anyone else who wants to be guided by the Lord of the worlds.
                                                                                                                                                  +
                                                                                                                                                    So, one thing to do this week inshallah. The next time something good happens to you, the next time one of your Lord's favors manifests in front of you - no matter how small - remember: "Alhamdulillah. This is from my Lord." And when you find out you were wrong about something, before anything else: "I was wrong." And in our prayer inshallah, say اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِي. May Allah make us people who can say them, and mean them.

                                                                                                                                                      9. Dua

                                                                                                                                                      رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغْ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَيْتَنَا وَهَبْ لَنَا مِن لَّدُنكَ رَحْمَةً ۚ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ الْوَهَّابُ
                                                                                                                                                      +
                                                                                                                                                        "Our Lord, don't let our hearts swerve after You've guided us, and grant us mercy from Yourself. You are the Giver."

                                                                                                                                                            "My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor which You have given me and my parents, and to do good that pleases You, and admit me, by Your mercy, among Your righteous servants."
                                                                                                                                                            +
                                                                                                                                                              Ya Allah, make us grateful for what You've given us, and let us see that it came from You. When You show us Your signs, let us recognize them before it's too late. When You show us we were wrong, let us say we wronged ourselves, before the Inevitable Day when it will be said for us. And forgive our parents, and our ummah, who carried this deen to us.
