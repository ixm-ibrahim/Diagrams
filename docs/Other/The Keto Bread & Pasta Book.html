Measurements give cups and grams. A scale makes the gluten-blend recipes far more repeatable; the almond flour and egg recipes are forgiving either way. The nutrition strip on each card is computed per serving from typical brand labels for the ingredients as listed (optional add-ins left out), rounded; expect it to move a little with your specific bags and how you slice. Net carbs are total carbs minus fiber. Every recipe assumes large eggs, full-fat dairy, unsalted butter, and halal-slaughtered meat and poultry. Wherever a recipe calls for something you'd normally buy but could make, a homemade link points to the method in the back.

Every recipe here is halal as printed. Nothing uses pork, lard, gelatin or alcohol, and where a conventional version would, the swap is already in the ingredient list: beef tallow or ghee in tortillas, beef bacon in haluski, halal beef pepperoni in calzones and stromboli, chicken or lamb in dumplings, chicken-skin or beef cracklings where pork rinds would be. A few ingredients still deserve a label check because they vary by brand.

Label checks

Cheese and rennet
Parmigiano-Reggiano, Grana Padano, pecorino, and traditional halloumi and feta are made with animal rennet from calves or lambs that were not halal-slaughtered. For parmesan and other hard cheeses, choose ones labelled microbial or vegetable rennet, or halal-certified. Most American mozzarella and cheddar use microbial enzymes; the label will say "microbial enzymes" or "vegetarian rennet". Cream cheese, ricotta, labneh, sour cream and yogurt are acid- or culture-set with no rennet, but some light or whipped versions add gelatin: use full-fat and read the line.
Extracts
Vanilla, banana and corn extracts are usually alcohol-based. Use alcohol-free extracts, vanilla powder, or the glycerin vanilla in the back of the book. Wherever a recipe says vanilla, that's what it means.
Cream of tartar
A purified salt recovered from grape must in winemaking. It's widely accepted as halal, but every recipe that uses it also gives a lemon-juice alternative.
Soy sauce
Naturally brewed soy sauce carries traces of alcohol from fermentation. Use a halal-certified soy sauce, a tamari checked the same way, or coconut aminos.
Meat products
Halal beef pepperoni, beef bacon, beef cracklings and halal deli chicken or turkey cover every place a recipe wants cured meat or crackling. Where buying them is hard, the homemade section has spiced beef crumbles, chicken-skin crisps and roast-your-own deli chicken.
Fine as they are
Yeast, vinegar, baking soda and powder, sweeteners, psyllium, gluten, oat fiber, and konjac. Sugar-free chocolate is usually fine; check it for alcohol-based flavourings.

This is the blend from your guide, with the numbers corrected. It is the only keto base with real gluten, which means it kneads, rises, holds a shape and browns (a little). Make the blend in bulk, then every recipe in this chapter starts from the same loaf dough and diverges at shaping.

Gluten blend
Makes 3 cups (~310 g)Time 5 min
Per ¼ cup (30 g)130 kcal12 g protein7 g fat2.5 g net carbs6 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (120 g) vital wheat gluten
  • 1 cup (100 g) super-fine blanched almond flour (buy this one; home-ground is too coarse for the blend)
  • ½ cup (52 g) golden flaxseed meal (homemade from whole seed, ground fresh)
  • ½ cup (36 g) oat fiber (not oat flour)

Method

  1. Whisk everything together in a large container for a full minute so the gluten is evenly distributed; clumps of gluten make rubbery streaks.
  2. Store airtight. Room temperature for a month; refrigerate or freeze for longer, because flax meal goes rancid.
  3. Scale it up: a 3× batch (3 cups gluten, 3 cups almond, 1½ cups each flax and oat fiber) fills a 4-quart container and makes about four loaves.

Why these four. Gluten gives structure and chew. Almond flour gives fat, tenderness and flavour. Flax gives a multigrain taste and binds water. Oat fiber adds bulk with zero calories so the crumb isn't dense. Vital wheat gluten carries about 4 g of carbs per ¼ cup, which is where most of the blend's net carbs come from.

Gluten blend
Makes 1 loaf, 16 slicesTime 2½ hr (20 min hands-on)
Per slice95 kcal7.5 g protein6 g fat2 g net carbs3 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 2½ cups (255 g) master blend
  • 1 packet (2¼ tsp, 7 g) instant or active dry yeast
  • 2 tsp sugar or honey (the yeast eats it; under 0.5 g net per slice even if it didn't)
  • 1 tsp (6 g) salt
  • 1 cup (240 ml) warm water, 105–110°F, starting with ¾ cup
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 2 tbsp (28 g) butter, melted, or olive oil
  • Optional: 1 tsp baking powder as insurance on the rise

Method

  1. Stir the yeast and sugar into ¾ cup of the warm water. Wait 5–10 minutes for foam. No foam means dead yeast; start over.
  2. Put the blend and salt (and baking powder) in the mixer bowl. Add the yeast water, eggs and butter. Dough hook on low for 1 minute to bring it together.
  3. Knead on medium (speed 4 on a KitchenAid) for 6–8 minutes, and stop when it's smooth and springy; gluten this concentrated develops fast and turns rubbery if you keep going. You want a smooth, springy ball that slaps the bowl and pulls cleanly off the sides. If it tears or looks dry, add the remaining water a tablespoon at a time. If it's a sticky puddle, add blend a tablespoon at a time. It should be tacky, not sticky.
  4. Cover the bowl and rest 10–15 minutes so the oat fiber and flax finish absorbing water.
  5. Flatten the dough into a rectangle as wide as your pan, roll it up tightly, pinch the seam, and set it seam-down in a greased 9×5 pan (8½×4½ for a taller loaf).
  6. Cover loosely and rise somewhere warm for 45–75 minutes, until the dome crowns ½–1 inch above the rim. Don't push past that; overproofed gluten bread sinks.
  7. Brush with egg wash if you want colour. Bake at 350°F for 35–40 minutes, until deep golden and an instant-read thermometer in the centre reads 200°F or higher.
  8. Cool 10 minutes in the pan, then fully on a rack, at least an hour. Cutting warm gives you gummy slices. Slice thin with a serrated knife.

This dough is the base for every recipe in this chapter. When a recipe says "one batch of loaf dough", make steps 1–4 and pick up from there. Egg-free: replace the eggs with an extra ½ cup water; the loaf will be a little denser and paler.

Pullman loaf
Make 1¼× the dough, proof in a greased 9-inch Pullman pan until it's an inch from the lid, slide the lid on, and bake at 350°F for 40–50 minutes, lid off for the last 10 for colour. Square slices, fine tight crumb.
More rise and chew
Swap ¼ cup of the blend for ¼ cup more vital wheat gluten. The blend as written leans tender; gluten-heavier keto doughs rise higher and chew more, up to the point where they turn rubbery.
Softer, rounder flavour
Add 1 tsp apple cider vinegar to the water. It relaxes the gluten slightly and takes the edge off the flax.

Gluten blend
Makes 1 round loaf, 16 slicesTime 3 hr (20 min hands-on)
Per slice95 kcal7.5 g protein6 g fat2 g net carbs3 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough
  • 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar, or ½ tsp citric acid
  • Optional: 1 tbsp plain full-fat yogurt for lactic roundness

Method

  1. Add the vinegar (and yogurt) to the water in step 1 of the loaf dough. Make the dough through the rest.
  2. For real depth, cover the kneaded dough and refrigerate it 12–24 hours before shaping. The yeast keeps working slowly on the sugar and on the almond flour, and the flavour compounds it makes are most of what your nose reads as "bread". Let the cold dough sit out 30 minutes, then shape and proof as usual; the proof takes longer from cold.
  3. Shape and bake as the Dutch-oven boule below. The cracked, scored crust and the tang do most of the sourdough impression.

Honesty note. There's no fermentation here. A real starter needs starch to eat and this blend has almost none, so this is flavour mimicry, and it works better than you'd expect. Skip anything online promising a true keto sourdough culture.

Gluten blend
Makes 1 round loaf, 16 slicesTime 3 hr (25 min hands-on)
Per slice95 kcal7.5 g protein6 g fat2 g net carbs3 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough
  • Oat fiber for dusting

Method

  1. After the 15-minute rest, shape the dough into a tight round: pull the edges under and into the centre, flip, and drag it toward you on the counter a few times to tighten the skin.
  2. Set it on a sheet of parchment, cover with an inverted bowl, and rise 45–60 minutes until noticeably puffed.
  3. Thirty minutes before baking, put a lidded Dutch oven in the oven and heat to 450°F.
  4. Dust the top with oat fiber and score a ½-inch-deep cross or crescent with a razor. Lift the parchment and dough into the pot, spritz the loaf with water, lid on.
  5. Bake 20 minutes covered. Remove the lid, drop to 425°F, bake 12–18 minutes more until deep golden. Internal temperature 200°F.
  6. Cool on a rack for a full hour. The crust will crackle as it cools.

Crust. Without starch there's less to caramelise, so the crust is crisp but not glassy. Brushing with egg white before scoring deepens the colour.

Gluten blend
Makes 3 baguettes (about 36 one-inch slices)Time 2½ hr (25 min hands-on)
Per 1-inch slice40 kcal3.5 g protein2.5 g fat1 g net carbs1.5 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough
  • 1 egg white, for brushing

Method

  1. Divide the rested dough into three (about 200 g each). Flatten each into a rectangle, fold the long sides to the middle, roll into a tight 12–14 inch log, and taper the ends.
  2. Set seam-down on parchment or a baguette pan, cover, rise 30–45 minutes.
  3. Heat the oven to 425°F with a metal pan on the lowest rack. Boil a kettle.
  4. Brush the loaves with egg white and slash 3–4 diagonal cuts with a razor. Slide them in, pour the boiling water into the pan, and shut the door fast.
  5. Bake 20–25 minutes until golden and crackly. Cool 20 minutes before cutting.

Gluten blend
Makes 2 loaves, 24 slicesTime 3 hr (20 min hands-on)
Per slice65 kcal5 g protein4.5 g fat1.5 g net carbs2 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough made with ¼ cup (60 ml) extra water
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Oat fiber for dusting, and plenty of it

Method

  1. Make the dough with the extra water. It will be slack and sticky and won't clean the bowl; that's right for this one. Knead 6 minutes, then scrape into an oiled bowl and cover.
  2. Instead of shaping, do two rounds of stretch-and-fold 20 minutes apart: lift one edge, stretch it up and fold it over the middle, turn the bowl a quarter, repeat four times. Rest 20 minutes after the second round.
  3. Tip onto heavily dusted parchment. Dust the top, cut into two rough rectangles with a bench scraper, and nudge them apart without deflating them. Cover and rise 45 minutes until puffy.
  4. Bake at 425°F for 20–25 minutes with a pan of boiling water on the lowest rack, until deep gold and 200°F inside. Cool 30 minutes.

Expectations. You get the flat slipper shape, the flour-dusted chewy crust and a moist crumb, but not the big glossy holes; those are starch structure, and there's none here. Good for panini and for tearing.

Gluten blend
Makes one 12–13 inch crust, 8 slicesTime 1 hr (20 min hands-on)
Per slice, crust only95 kcal7.5 g protein6 g fat2 g net carbs3 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1¼ cups (128 g) master blend
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ½ cup (120 ml) warm water, plus more as needed
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tbsp olive oil

Method

  1. Make a half batch of loaf dough with these amounts: bloom the yeast in the water, then knead everything 5–6 minutes to a smooth ball. Rest covered 20–30 minutes.
  2. Heat the oven to 450°F with a stone or heavy sheet inside.
  3. Roll the dough out on parchment to 12–13 inches. It will fight you; when it springs back, walk away for 5 minutes and try again.
  4. Dock the whole surface thoroughly with a fork. Slide the parchment onto the hot stone and par-bake 8–10 minutes until set and lightly coloured.
  5. Top with low-carb sauce, cheese and whatever else, keeping toppings dry. Bake 8–10 minutes more until the cheese blisters.

No time for yeast? Skip it, add 1 tsp baking powder to the blend, rest 10 minutes, and roll. Slightly less chew, ready in 25 minutes.

Gluten blend
Makes 8 burger buns, 8 hot dog buns, or 6 hoagiesTime 2 hr 20 min (25 min hands-on)
Per burger bun245 kcal16 g protein18 g fat4.5 g net carbs6.5 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough, plus:
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 2 tbsp (28 g) softened butter
  • 1 tbsp granular sweetener (optional)
  • Egg wash: 1 yolk + 1 tbsp cream
  • Sesame or poppy seeds

Method

  1. Make the loaf dough, adding the yolks, butter and sweetener with the other wet ingredients. Hold back 2 tablespoons of the water at first; the extra fat loosens the dough.
  2. Burger buns: divide into 8 (about 90 g), round tightly, then flatten to 3½–4 inch discs. They rise up, not out. Hot dog buns: roll into 6-inch logs. Hoagies: divide into 6, roll 8-inch logs, slash once lengthwise after rising.
  3. Set 2 inches apart on parchment, cover, rise 45 minutes.
  4. Brush with egg wash, scatter seeds, bake at 375°F for 18–22 minutes until deep gold. Cool fully before splitting; a warm bun compresses.

Sturdiness. These hold a wet burger without dissolving, which almond-flour buns won't. Toast the cut side in the burger fat for the best version.

Gluten blend
Makes 1 loaf (16 slices) or 12 bunsTime 2 hr 50 min (30 min hands-on)Babka slice: 160 kcal, 8 g protein, 2.5 g net. Sticky bun (of 12): 275 kcal, 11 g protein, 3 g net.
Per slice of brioche loaf125 kcal7.5 g protein9.5 g fat2 g net carbs3 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough made with ¼ cup less water, plus:
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 4 tbsp (56 g) very soft butter, in pieces
  • 2 tbsp granular sweetener
  • 1 tsp vanilla (sweet doughs)
  • Babka filling: 3 tbsp melted butter, 3 tbsp cocoa, ¼ cup powdered sweetener, ⅓ cup chopped sugar-free chocolate
  • Sticky-bun pan glaze: 4 tbsp butter, ⅓ cup brown-style sweetener (allulose browns best), ¾ cup pecans, pinch of salt

Method

  1. Make the loaf dough with the yolks, sweetener and vanilla in with the wet ingredients, holding back ¼ cup of the water. Knead 4 minutes, then add the butter a piece at a time with the mixer running and knead 4 minutes more until glossy and soft. Add water back a tablespoon at a time only if it's stiff. Rest 15 minutes. This is also the dough for the brioche buns, challah and cinnamon rolls if you want them richer.
  2. Brioche loaf: divide into 4, round, and set side by side in a greased 9×5. Rise 60 minutes until crowned, egg-wash, bake at 350°F for 30–35 minutes, tenting with foil if it darkens early.
  3. Chocolate babka: roll to 12×16. Spread the filling edge to edge, roll up from a long side, then cut the log in half lengthwise and twist the two halves around each other with the cut sides facing up. Fit into a lined 9×5, rise 45–60 minutes, bake at 350°F for 35–40 minutes.
  4. Sticky buns: make the cinnamon rolls from this dough. Before arranging them, coat a 9×13 pan with the melted butter, sweetener, pecans and salt. Bake as usual, rest 3 minutes, then invert onto a board while hot so the glaze runs down.
  5. Milk-bread rolls: divide into 12, pack into a 9×13, rise, egg-wash, bake at 350°F for 20–25 minutes. Pull-apart soft.

Why the butter goes in late. Fat added before the gluten develops coats the proteins and you get a cakey crumb; added after, you get the feathery pull of brioche.

Gluten blend
Makes 8 bagelsTime 2 hr 10 min (35 min hands-on)
Per bagel205 kcal16 g protein13 g fat4.5 g net carbs6.5 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough made with 2 tbsp less water (a stiffer dough holds the ring)
  • 3 quarts water + 1 tbsp baking soda, for the boil
  • Egg wash: 1 egg + 1 tbsp water
  • Everything seasoning (homemade), sesame, poppy, or coarse salt

Method

  1. Divide the rested dough into 8 (about 80 g). Roll each into a 9-inch rope, wrap it around your hand, overlap the ends by an inch, and roll the seam on the counter to seal. Or round each piece and poke a thumb through the middle, stretching to a 1½-inch hole.
  2. Set on parchment, cover, rest 20–30 minutes until just puffy. Don't fully proof; overproofed bagels flatten in the water.
  3. Heat the oven to 425°F. Bring the water and baking soda to a rolling boil.
  4. Boil 3–4 bagels at a time, 45 seconds per side. Lift out with a slotted spoon onto a rack for a minute, then back to the parchment.
  5. Brush with egg wash, press the tops into a plate of toppings, and bake 18–22 minutes until golden and firm. Cool 20 minutes.

Expectations. The boil sets the skin and gives the dense chew. They'll be a shade paler and less glossy than wheat bagels because there's no malt or starch to caramelise; the egg wash does most of the work. Split and freeze the day you bake them.

Gluten blend
Makes 8 pretzels or ~60 bitesTime 2 hr (40 min hands-on)
Per pretzel190 kcal15 g protein12 g fat4 g net carbs6.5 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough
  • 8 cups water + ¼ cup baking soda, for the bath
  • Egg wash: 1 yolk + 1 tbsp water
  • Coarse pretzel salt
  • Optional: 2 tbsp melted butter to finish

Method

  1. Divide the rested dough into 8. Roll each into a 22–24 inch rope, thinner at the ends. Make a U, twist the ends around each other twice, and fold them down onto the bottom of the U, pressing to stick. For bites, cut the ropes into 1½-inch pieces.
  2. Rest uncovered on parchment 15 minutes while the oven heats to 425°F and the bath comes to a rolling boil. Add the baking soda slowly; it foams.
  3. Dip each pretzel 30 seconds, flipping once, lift with a slotted spatula, shake off, and return to the parchment. It's the alkaline bath that makes it taste like a pretzel.
  4. Brush with egg wash, sprinkle salt, bake 12–15 minutes (bites 10–12) until deep brown. Brush with butter out of the oven.

Darker pretzels. Spread 1 cup of baking soda on foil and bake at 250°F for an hour to make it more alkaline, then use ½ cup of that in the bath. Wear gloves; it's harsh on skin.

Ingredients

Method

Makes 8 pitasTime 1½ hr (25 min hands-on)
Per pita185 kcal15 g protein12 g fat4 g net carbs6.5 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 batch loaf dough

Method

Konjac & store-bought
Makes about 2 lb of noodles, 6–8 servingsTime 3 hr (30 min hands-on)
Per cup (115 g)11 kcal0 g protein0 g fat0 g net carbs3.5 g fiber

Ingredients

  • 1 oz (28 g, about 3½ tbsp) glucomannan powder, or konjac flour
  • 3½ cups (830 ml) warm water, about 90°F
  • ½ tsp (about 1.2 g) food-grade calcium hydroxide, sold as pickling lime for canning
  • ¼ cup water for dissolving the lime

Method

  1. Put the warm water in a blender or a large bowl and, with it running or while whisking hard, sprinkle the powder in slowly. It thickens within a minute. Stop when it's smooth with no dry lumps.
  2. Leave it 1–2 hours, stirring once or twice, until it's a uniform, thick, translucent gel that holds a spoon track.
  3. Stir the lime into the ¼ cup water until cloudy, then pour it into the gel and stir vigorously for 1–2 minutes until it's evenly mixed and turns glossier and firmer. Wear gloves; lime is a strong alkali. This step is what sets konjac permanently; without it you just have thick goo.
  4. For noodles: load the gel into a piping bag with a small round tip, a potato ricer, or a shirataki press, and extrude directly into a wide pot of simmering water. For a block to cut: pour into a loaf pan, smooth, let set 30 minutes, cut into strips or cubes, and slide those into the simmering water.
  5. Simmer 20–30 minutes. This finishes the set and cooks off the alkaline taste. Drain, rinse, then use exactly like store-bought shirataki: dry-fry and sauce. Store submerged in fresh water in the fridge up to a week.

Should you bother? The result is the same near-zero-carb, near-zero-calorie noodle you can buy for a few dollars, and getting the texture right takes a couple of tries. It's worth it if you want noodles with no additives, want to cast shapes (sheets for lasagna, thick udon-style rounds) that aren't sold, or just like the project. Don't taste the gel before the boil, keep the lime away from kids, and stick to food-grade pickling lime. More lime gives a firmer, bouncier noodle with a stronger konnyaku smell; less gives a softer one that may not hold.

Makes a jar goes a long wayTime 5 min
Per teaspoon (3 g)0 kcal0 g protein0 g fat0 g net carbs2.5 g fiber

Uses

  • Binder in gluten-free doughs: ¼–½ tsp per cup of almond or coconut flour in tortillas, crackers and quick breads, in place of xanthan gum. It holds moisture and adds flex. Past ½ tsp it turns gummy.
  • Stretch in psyllium tortillas and egg noodles: ½ tsp whisked into the liquid first makes both more pliable.
  • Egg replacer for binding only: 1 tsp glucomannan whisked into ¼ cup water, rested 5 minutes, stands in for one egg in crackers and flatbreads. It won't replace an egg's lift or structure in a loaf.
  • Thickener: ⅛–¼ tsp per cup of liquid for gravy, pan sauce or soup. Far stronger than cornstarch.

How

  1. Never add it dry to a wet dough; it clumps into slimy beads that won't dissolve. Whisk it into the dry ingredients thoroughly, or whisk it into cold liquid or fat before heating.
  2. For sauces, whisk it into the cold liquid, then heat. It thickens as it warms and keeps thickening for 10 minutes off the heat, so stop early and wait. It doesn't break with reheating and freezes fine.
  3. Store airtight and dry. A small bag lasts a year at these quantities.

Cautions. It's a soluble fiber that swells enormously, which is the point in a sauce and a hazard in a throat. Never swallow it dry (this is why konjac supplements come with drink-a-full-glass warnings and why konjac jelly candies were pulled from shelves). In the teaspoon quantities here it's ordinary food; in large amounts it can cause bloating. What not to try: recipes that use konjac as the main "flour" for bread. It doesn't bake into anything but rubber.

Every bread in this book is higher in fat and moisture than wheat bread and moulds faster. Cool loaves completely, slice, and refrigerate in a bag for up to a week, or freeze slices with parchment between and toast straight from frozen. Buns, bagels and English muffins: split before freezing. Fathead and cheese-based items keep 4–5 days in the fridge and re-crisp in a hot oven or skillet. Cloud bread and egg noodles: fridge, 4–5 days, in a bag with a paper towel. Fresh gluten pasta: dusted and covered in the fridge 2 days, or frozen in nests. Crackers: airtight at room temperature, re-crisp at 300°F for 10 minutes if they soften.

Ingredients that aren't keto no matter how the recipe is framed

  • Semolina: around 69 g net carbs per 100 g. The same as regular flour. Fine for a non-keto dinner, out of these recipes.
  • Chickpea flour: around 48 g net per 100 g, roughly 14 g per ¼ cup. Better than wheat, still far past a keto budget in any useful amount. Lupin flour is the keto legume flour.
  • Oat flour and oat bran: these are not oat fiber. Full-carb.
  • Cassava, arrowroot and tapioca: common in "grain-free" recipes; they're pure starch.
  • Edamame, black bean and lentil pastas: 20-plus grams of net carbs per 2 oz serving.
  • Bananas, honey, maple syrup, dates in "healthy" bread recipes. A banana is around 24 g net.
  • Anything sold as "low-carb bread" with modified wheat starch or resistant wheat starch high on the label; read the total carb line, not the front of the package.

Things this kind of baking can't do well

  • Croissants and other laminated doughs. Without starch the layers don't set and the butter runs. Every keto croissant recipe is a compromise you'll be disappointed by. If you want layers and butter, the paratha and feteer-style breads get there honestly.
  • True sourdough fermentation. A starter needs starch to feed. Use the vinegar trick.
  • Shattering-crisp thin crackers from the gluten blend. They come out firm and snappy, not delicate. The almond and seed crackers are the ones for delicacy.
  • Sandwich bread that tastes exactly like wheat. The gluten loaf is close. Toasting closes most of the remaining gap.
